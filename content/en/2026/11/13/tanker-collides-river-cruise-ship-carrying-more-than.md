A tanker collided with the American Heritage, a river cruise ship carrying more than 130 people, on the Mississippi River in New Orleans on Friday afternoon, according to The Guardian, which reported the incident in a live blog and in a separate news story.

The ship, operated by American Cruise Lines, had docked at the Port of New Orleans about two hours before the collision so that passengers could begin to disembark. It was struck at about 12.30 local time (19.30 CET), according to a statement from Kimberly Curth, the port's press secretary, as cited by The Guardian. The Atlantic Asphalt tanker was the vessel that hit it.

The Guardian reported that the American Heritage was taking on water and later lay on its side in the river, while images circulating on social media showed people climbing onto its exposed side. The newspaper said the ship was docked outside the port's administration building when it was hit.

The sources differ on the number of people on board. Curth told The Guardian that about 129 passengers were aboard, in addition to crew members, while the newspaper's headline puts the total at more than 130. The sources do not give a confirmed figure for the number of people rescued or injured, nor any death toll.

According to The Guardian, New Orleans firefighters, port and city police, the US Coast Guard and local emergency medical services were all at the scene. Video from the local NBC affiliate WDSU, cited by the newspaper, showed tug crew members pulling people wearing life jackets, or holding flotation rings, out of the water. The New Orleans mayor's communication team, cited by a local Fox affiliate, said several people had been rescued and that search operations were continuing. People who left the ship were taken into the port's administration building.

An emergency response source told The Guardian that the tanker may have lost power before it struck the cruise ship. The newspaper did not say whether this had been confirmed by investigators.

The US Coast Guard closed the river to all vessel traffic between mile markers 93 and 96, the stretch that includes the crash site, so that responders could assess the situation and carry out rescue work, according to the agency's statement as reported by The Guardian. Louisiana Governor Jeff Landry said on X that he was in contact with local officials and law enforcement.

Officials said they would hold a news conference at 3pm Eastern Time (21.00 CET) to provide further information. The Guardian said it would update its coverage as more details emerged.
