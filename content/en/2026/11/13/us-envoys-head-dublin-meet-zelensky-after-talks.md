US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff travelled to Dublin on Thursday to meet Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelensky, according to the BBC. The trip followed a new round of talks with Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow, held a day after separate discussions aimed at ending the war wrapped up in Abu Dhabi.

Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022 started the war, and the latest talks were framed around security arrangements for Europe in a post-war scenario. The BBC reports that a settlement still appears distant, because Moscow's demands for territory and neutrality far exceed what Kyiv is prepared to consider.

Witkoff told Russian state television that a new round of trilateral talks had already been agreed with Putin, and that Europeans would take part. “They are our partners and it's important to us that they take part,” he said. Al Jazeera reports that Witkoff said Putin had agreed to let European representatives join. The Kremlin has not confirmed the make-up of next week's talks, according to the BBC.

Putin repeated his position on territory. “For us there is only one line of demarcation: the line of the state border,” he said, according to the BBC. He also proposed a humanitarian truce to evacuate civilians from a front-line area, modelled on a ceasefire in the Russian-occupied city of Oleshky last month, and a five-day ceasefire for Kostyantynivka in Donetsk. Kyiv disputes that Russia fully holds the town. Ukrainian presidential adviser Dmitry Lytvyn told the BBC that the details would need to be clarified.

Zelensky also said Ukraine's military again faced “systematic” restrictions on Starshield, the military-grade satellite service developed by Elon Musk's SpaceX. He said the disruption had lasted several days and was helping Russian forces strike more accurately, according to the BBC.

In Dublin, the European Political Community (EPC), a group of more than 40 European countries set up in 2022, held its biannual summit. Al Jazeera reports that the meeting focused on hybrid attacks, including hoax bomb warnings to schools in Italy. Ireland's Prime Minister, Micheál Martin, known as the taoiseach, said those behind the attacks seek to “create confusion, sow division and weaken public confidence in our democratic systems.” British Prime Minister Andy Burnham pledged his “complete support” to Zelensky, according to Al Jazeera.

The BBC reports that Burnham had said his national security adviser, Jonathan Powell, would join the Dublin talks, but Downing Street later said the trip had been delayed and Powell could no longer attend. It is still unclear who will represent Europe in the negotiations, Al Jazeera reports.
