Norway has said it will bar professional athletes from Russia and Belarus from entering the country to compete, following a similar move by neighbouring Finland, according to The Guardian. The Guardian reported that the Norwegian government made the announcement on Thursday, hours after Finland said it would block visas for Russian athletes.

The government said it intends to keep the restriction in place for as long as the war in Ukraine continues. In its statement, it said Norway already applied strict visa rules to citizens of both countries, but that recent relaxations introduced by international sports federations could allow more athletes and support staff from Russia and Belarus to travel to Norway to compete. Astri Aas-Hansen, the minister of justice and public security, said: “Norway already has a restrictive visa practice for Russian and Belarusian citizens, but is now further tightening who is allowed to travel here.”

The government said it would instruct immigration authorities and the police to refuse entry to Russian and Belarusian professional athletes and their accompanying support staff if they come to Norway to take part in sporting competitions. According to The Guardian, the government said the changes would take effect soon, without giving a precise date. It argued that Russian elite sport has close ties to the Russian authorities and therefore cannot be viewed independently of the war in Ukraine.

The decision could affect a cross-country skiing World Cup that Norway is preparing to host in Trondheim in December. The Guardian noted that Russian and Belarusian athletes have been cleared to compete by the International Ski and Snowboard Federation (FIS), which allows them to take part under conditions such as not displaying national symbols and signing a declaration of neutrality. Norway's move could block them regardless of that clearance.

Finland's measure came first. Elina Valtonen, Finland's minister for foreign affairs, said: “As long as Russia’s war of aggression continues, Russian athletes have no business in Finland.” The Guardian reported that Finland's foreign ministry said the restriction would cover Russians competing under neutral status as well as those representing national teams. The move followed criticism in Finland after Russia was reinstated for the ice hockey world championships.

According to The Guardian, the Finnish change takes effect from Monday, so it should not affect this week's NHL games in Helsinki or the ski World Cup slalom races in Levi on Saturday and Sunday. The figure skating world championships due in Tampere in March could be affected. The International Skating Union (ISU) said it was studying the implications and seeking clarifications from officials in Finland. It also said it believed athletes should not be punished for the actions of their governments.

The Guardian also reported that Finland is among a group of European teams that have vowed not to play against Russia at the men's ice hockey world championships, which Germany is weighing whether to host. In athletics, World Athletics maintains a blanket ban on Russian participation, a rule that is facing a legal challenge. Finland is due to host the European athletics championships in 2030.
