Microsoft's Outlook email and Teams messaging services were disrupted for thousands of users around the world over Thursday and Friday, and the company was still struggling to restore them, according to The Guardian.

The outage affected Microsoft 365, the company's subscription suite of apps, which includes Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and Teams. The Guardian reported that the Down Detector outage-tracking site recorded a spike of nearly 130,000 problem reports from 74 countries around 1pm Eastern Time on Thursday. Access to enterprise services such as SharePoint and OneDrive was also hit, as was Copilot, Microsoft's AI assistant.

A Microsoft spokesperson told the newspaper that the company "had no indication of a cyber-attack". The company has suggested the problem lay in the settings applied to servers that keep Microsoft 365 running. According to The Guardian, Microsoft tried several remedies, including rolling back updates and limiting the traffic reaching affected servers. When some servers were brought back, they became unavailable again once traffic returned.

As of 3.30am GMT on Friday, Microsoft said its Exchange services, the back end of Outlook email, were the most affected, with knock-on effects for other products that depend on them. Teams chats and meetings were less affected, although the company later said it had restored Teams problems in North America, while some users still reported connection issues. In an update on Friday afternoon, Australian time, Microsoft said its fixes were not 'taking as expected'.

Microsoft's executive vice-president of Copilot, Charles Lamanna, wrote on X: "We regret that today's outages caused disruptions for our customers, and we are working to address the issue." The Guardian also reported that the outage hit connectors used by the AI services of OpenAI and Anthropic. Microsoft had reported an outage of its Azure cloud platform the previous month.

In Australia, the impact reached public bodies. Service NSW, the state agency that handles residents' and businesses' government services, asked customers to delay visits to its centres and set up a priority callback system. Some hospitals and clinics reverted to paper prescriptions, according to the Australian Medical Association, which said patient care had not been affected. Schoolbytes, an administration platform used by New South Wales schools, lost access to email temporarily. The Western Australia government said classroom teaching was continuing as normal, while the Victorian government urged residents to be wary of scammers exploiting the disruption.

The outage also disrupted businesses in Taiwan, where more than a hundred reports were recorded between 3am and 9am local time on Friday, according to The Guardian. The Australian Computer Society called on the federal government to review which critical IT systems depend on a single provider. The assistant treasurer, Daniel Mulino, said he could not quantify the economic cost.

Mark Gregory, an associate professor at RMIT University, told The Guardian that the fault appeared to lie in the network layer of Microsoft's systems and could be similar to a configuration problem that brought down Optus's network in 2023. He explained that when some servers are misconfigured, the remaining ones absorb the traffic and can also fail. "And so you end up with a cascading problem," he said. He added that businesses relying on cloud services should have backup options.

It is not yet clear when services will be fully restored. According to The Guardian, Microsoft said users may experience a gradual recovery as their applications reconnect.
