Manchester United have confirmed Mauricio Pochettino as their new head coach, ending a pursuit that The Guardian says dates back a decade. The Argentinian has signed a two-and-a-half-year deal and replaces Michael Carrick, who was sacked on Thursday, according to The Guardian's live coverage.

Carrick's dismissal came after United made their worst start to a season in four decades, the Guardian reports. Pochettino, a former manager of Chelsea, Tottenham and Southampton, will first lead the United States men's national team through two Concacaf Nations League matches, on Saturday and Tuesday, before joining United.

The Guardian says United's own statement uses the title "head coach" and says he will begin work on 18 November. His staff will join once they have finished their duties with the US team. The paper names his long-time assistants as Jesus Perez and Miguel D'Agostino, with Antonio Jimenez as goalkeeping coach.

The appointment brings an end to a series of near-misses. The Guardian reports that Pochettino was linked with the United job as far back as 2016, and came close to being appointed in 2018, 2021 and 2022. The paper also recalls that Sir Alex Ferguson praised him in a 2016 interview, saying "you never see him ruffled" and stressing the value of young English players in his team.

In a statement on leaving the US job, quoted by The Guardian, Pochettino said that representing the USA crest "was one of the greatest honours of my career". He thanked the federation and named Cindy Parlow Cone, JT Batson and Dan Helfrich among those he had worked with.

The Guardian reports that the club's search had been widely discussed by supporters, many of whom were critical of ownership in the paper's reader emails. The paper's live blog did not describe any formal response from the owners to the appointment.

United's next fixture under Pochettino will be away at Liverpool. The Guardian reports that the match is scheduled for next Sunday.
