The Trump administration has reported a record number of deportations and immigration arrests in 2026, according to Al Jazeera, which cited a statement from the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) on Wednesday. The department said Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), the federal agency responsible for immigration enforcement, carried out 483,200 deportations this year and more than 491,000 immigration arrests.

According to Al Jazeera, the deportation total is the highest in ICE's 23-year history and surpasses the previous record of 442,640 deportations carried out in 2025. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said in a statement that “Fiscal Year 2026 saw the highest number of ICE deportations and arrests for any year in ICE's history.”

ICE said it made more than 48,000 arrests in September, which Al Jazeera reported as ending a two-month run of record highs. The agency's arrests peaked at 50,925 in August, the outlet said.

The announcement comes days after President Donald Trump acknowledged, in an interview with CBS's programme 60 Minutes, that his immigration crackdown could be seen as too harsh. Al Jazeera reported that Trump said some people would call the policy great, while others would consider it too tough. Trump defended his record, saying he received high marks for removing people who had committed crimes, and said the policy also affected people with jobs who had entered the country illegally.

Steven Camarota, director of research at the Center for Immigration Studies (CIS), a Washington-based think tank, told Al Jazeera that the administration had increased enforcement more than any previous president. He also said there was evidence that the undocumented population had fallen significantly because people were leaving the country out of fear of enforcement. However, Camarota said the DHS figures were ambiguous. He said he could not determine how many of the cases were arrests inside the country, how many were removals from the interior, and how many were people turned back at the border or at airports.

Al Jazeera also cited Reuters/Ipsos polling showing that Trump's approval rating on immigration had fallen to 38 per cent from 50 per cent at the start of his presidency. The outlet described the crackdown as a political vulnerability for the president.

Democrats are preparing to take control of Congress next year and have said they will target ICE tactics and seek to expand routes to legal status, although Al Jazeera noted that their room for action is limited. Bennie Thompson, a Mississippi Democrat who will chair the House Homeland Security Committee, told Reuters that the party would investigate the administration's use of DHS and its immigration policies. Henry Cuellar, a centrist Texas Democrat, said he had already discussed with Trump officials the idea of focusing deportations on criminals.

Al Jazeera also recalled that federal immigration agents shot dead two US citizens in Minneapolis during an enforcement surge in January. Both incidents were captured on bystander video, and the footage has prompted international criticism of the agents' conduct.
