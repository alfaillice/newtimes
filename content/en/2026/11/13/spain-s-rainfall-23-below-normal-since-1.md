Spain has recorded 79 litres per square metre (l/m²) of rain since 1 October, the start of the hydrological year. That is 23 per cent below the normal figure for the period, which stands at 102 l/m², according to data from Spain's State Meteorological Agency (AEMET), as reported by the news agency Europa Press.

The picture varies widely from region to region. Catalonia and parts of the Mediterranean coast, especially the Levante coastline in the east, are above normal, and in some places the totals are more than three times the normal value. In the west of the peninsula, rain over the past week has reduced the deficits, and some areas of western Castile and León and northern Extremadura now exceed normal levels.

Deficits remain larger in Galicia, in the far north-west, and in western Andalusia, in the south. Wide stretches of the interior of the peninsula are also still below normal.

In the Balearic Islands, rainfall is above normal in Ibiza and much of Mallorca, particularly in the west of the largest island, but below normal in Menorca. In the Canary Islands, totals are generally above normal on El Hierro, La Palma and La Gomera, and below normal on Lanzarote and Fuerteventura, except in the south of Fuerteventura. On Tenerife and Gran Canaria the situation depends on the area.

Between 4 and 10 November, rain fell mostly in the western half of the peninsula. Totals exceeded 40 l/m² across wide areas of Galicia and the rest of the west, and 60 l/m² in western Galicia and parts of Extremadura. The highest figures were recorded on the western section of the Sistema Central, a mountain range running across the centre of the peninsula. A broad band covering northern Cáceres and the area around the Gredos mountains passed 100 l/m², with some points above 200 l/m².

Among the main weather stations, the report cites 89 l/m² at Badajoz/Talavera la Real, 78 l/m² at the Navacerrada pass, 64 l/m² at Santiago de Compostela/Labacolla, 61 l/m² at Vigo/Peinador, and 57 l/m² at both Sevilla/San Pablo and Pontevedra.

Rain also fell across much of the peninsula on Wednesday, 11 November. The heaviest totals again clustered in the western Sistema Central, with more than 100 l/m² in points of northern Cáceres and the western Gredos area. In parts of western Andalusia, totals exceeded 60 l/m².

The report does not include a forecast for the coming weeks, nor does it say what the deficit means for reservoirs or water supply.
