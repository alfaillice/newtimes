Maryland Democrat Chris Van Hollen has said he will run for Senate majority leader, challenging his party colleague Chuck Schumer for the post. He announced the bid on Thursday in a post on X, the social media platform. The leadership vote would take place when Democrats regain control of the Senate, the upper house of Congress, in January.

Schumer currently leads the Democratic minority in the Senate. Van Hollen’s move follows a strong showing for Democrats in this month’s midterm elections, which Al Jazeera described as a Democratic landslide. The Guardian reports that Schumer has been widely criticised by Democratic voters for failing to stand up to President Donald Trump. Al Jazeera adds that he has faced criticism over his handling of the Democratic caucus during Trump’s presidency.

In his post, Van Hollen said voters had “called on us to unrig an economy rigged against working people” and linked the message to political corruption. “To deliver, we need new leadership,” he wrote, adding that the decision was “not about any one person”.

Schumer responded on X within minutes. He said he welcomed “any discussion about the future of our caucus”, pointed to what he called “55 seats and counting”, and said he had the votes to keep serving as Democratic leader.

Van Hollen has sat in Congress since 2003. Born in Karachi, Pakistan, in 1959, he is the son of a US diplomat and is known for his opposition to Trump’s policies and his support for progressive causes. Al Jazeera says he has repeatedly challenged Washington’s relationship with Israel and has called Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu a “chronic liar”. In September he forced a Senate vote on a resolution on the killing of nine Americans in the occupied West Bank; it failed 47-53. The Guardian notes that he took on Trump’s mass deportation effort by travelling to El Salvador to seek information on Kilmar Ábrego García, a Maryland resident wrongly deported to the country. The two outlets differ on the date of that trip: the Guardian places it in early 2025, while Al Jazeera dates it to April 2025.

Michigan senator-elect Abdul El-Sayed, who said he would not support Schumer as majority leader, endorsed Van Hollen’s bid. The Guardian reports that El-Sayed defeated Schumer’s preferred candidate, Congresswoman Haley Stevens, in Michigan’s Democratic primary in August.

Al Jazeera reports that some progressives at a two-day Democratic conference in Denver, Colorado, which began on Thursday, have argued for a change of direction and leadership. The Washington commentator James Butler said that, while he respected Schumer’s service, veteran Democrats should make room for younger members.

The Guardian describes the contest as a choice between two approaches to Trump: Schumer’s cautious style and Van Hollen’s more confrontational one. The sources do not say when Democratic senators will hold the leadership vote, or whether other candidates will enter the race.
