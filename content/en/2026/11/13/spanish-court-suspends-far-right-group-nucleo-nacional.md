A court in Gijón, a city on Spain's northern coast, has suspended the activities of the ultra-right group Núcleo Nacional as part of an investigation into alleged hate crimes and illegal association, according to Al Jazeera. The court announced the ruling on Thursday, ordering the group's office to be shut and its social media content and bank accounts to be blocked.

Al Jazeera reports that the measures follow the arrest last week of 14 members of the group on suspicion of inciting hatred against vulnerable groups and people with opposing ideologies. Police also searched the group's headquarters in Madrid and other locations. They said they seized more than 20 firearms, some of them unlicensed, along with large quantities of ammunition and other illegal weapons.

The group describes itself as a political movement. Its website, which has now been blocked and suspended by court order, says it seeks a "national awakening". Spanish authorities have scrutinised the group over its anti-immigration rhetoric, particularly after thousands of migrants crossed in July into Ceuta, Spain's North African enclave.

The court said key members of the organisation had already been placed under precautionary measures, including a ban on leaving Spain and a requirement to report weekly to a court. Al Jazeera does not say how many members are subject to these measures.

In a video posted on X, an unnamed representative of the group, wearing a black shirt bearing its logo, said the movement was a victim of "persecution and political repression", according to Al Jazeera.

The ruling comes ahead of Spain's general election on 29 November, which will elect the national parliament. It also came a day after the first nationwide general strike in 14 years. Al Jazeera notes that the Spanish government has said far-right activists, some performing Nazi salutes, tried to reach demonstrators camped in the Puerta del Sol square.

Al Jazeera also cites a Reuters report from last month, which said Spanish police arrested three members of a far-right group in Madrid on 3 October, after tens of thousands of people marched across the country over the housing crisis. Al Jazeera's report is the only source for the details of the court ruling in this story, and the group's claims of persecution have not been tested in court coverage available to us.
