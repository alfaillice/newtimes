Cricket Australia (CA) has widened the independent forum into the 2018 ball-tampering scandal known as "sandpapergate" so that it covers the entire 2017-18 season, including the Ashes series against England. The terms of reference, published on Friday, say the forum will consider "ball-management practices" across that season, not only the four Tests of the 2018 tour of South Africa.

The Ashes, the Test series between Australia and England, consisted of five matches in 2017-18, which Australia won 4-0. According to the sources, there were no reports of Australia manipulating the ball illegally during that series.

The scandal returned to the headlines this month after former batter Usman Khawaja said the whole team had known about the cheating in South Africa, not only the three players found culpable by CA's integrity unit in 2018. Those three were former captain Steve Smith and opening batters David Warner and Cameron Bancroft. Smith and Warner were banned for 12 months, and Bancroft was suspended for nine. The sources say Khawaja made the claim in his autobiography.

Warner, Bancroft and Chadd Sayers have backed Khawaja's version. Captain Pat Cummins, along with bowlers Mitchell Starc and Josh Hazlewood, has denied any prior knowledge, and Cummins has described Khawaja's recollection as nonsense. CA backed Cummins to remain captain of the Test and one-day sides last week and rejected any suggestion that his integrity had been compromised.

The six-week forum will be overseen by Australian barrister Fiona McLeod. Participation is voluntary, and the forum will not make adverse findings or determine any individual's responsibility. Players and staff from the 2018 tour, as well as CA management and directors at the time, have been invited to take part, but no retrospective sanctions will be applied. McLeod will give CA's board a report of findings but will not share the accounts given by participants. CA has pledged to publish the full report within 30 days of receiving it.

Bancroft has said he will take part, and former head coach Darren Lehmann has said he would be open to it. Sayers said last week he was unsure whether he would participate, and Warner declined to confirm whether he would. The Australian Cricketers' Association, the players' union, said it had not been consulted on the terms and did not yet have a position on whether it supports the forum. Its chief, Paul Marsh, said the union was reviewing the terms.

The Ashes is also part of the wider story. In his 2019 autobiography, former England captain Sir Alastair Cook, who led England in the 2017-18 series, wrote that Warner had told a group of England players, during drinks after the final Test in Sydney, that he had used substances attached to the strapping on his hand to speed up the deterioration of the ball during a first-class match. Cook also wrote that his suspicions had first been aroused in Perth. These are Cook's own accounts, and the forum has not yet made any findings.
