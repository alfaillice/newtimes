European Union leaders left Brussels on Thursday night without agreeing the bloc’s budget for 2028 to 2034, after a special two-day summit collapsed a day ahead of schedule. The breakdown exposes deep splits over how large the seven-year budget should be and over how to fund Ukraine’s war effort.

The multiannual budget sets the EU’s spending for the period. The European Commission’s original proposal, of about €1.786 trillion, has already been cut by €204bn, or 10.2%. European Council President António Costa, whose job is to mediate the talks, told leaders he plans to remove a further €220bn to €230bn from his next proposal.

Talks broke down late on Thursday. Chancellor Friedrich Merz of Germany and the so-called frugal countries walked out of negotiations led by Costa. Separately, the prime ministers of Denmark, Sweden, Belgium and Austria staged a walkout during smaller group meetings, saying the atmosphere was not conducive to a deal. Some governments are pushing for Costa to give up his role as broker. Leaders did not attempt to resume discussions on the planned second day.

The positions are clear. Germany and six other countries that pay more into the EU budget than they receive, which call themselves the “Sensible Seven”, want cuts. They face 17 member states, led by France and Italy, that call themselves the “Friends of Cohesion” and argue the total should grow. Cohesion funds move money from the bloc’s richer regions to its poorer ones. Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis said: “We cannot talk about a strong Europe with a weak budget.”

The current proposal would reduce the share of agriculture, fisheries and cohesion spending from 59.1% to 42.4%, while money for competitiveness, security and defence would rise from 17.7% to 27.7%. Germany has made this rebalancing a key demand. After the summit, Merz said “hundreds of billions” still needed to be cut. He added: “The proposals that have been on the table so far are not a basis for an agreement.”

Ukraine is the second flashpoint. Berlin has said it will approve the budget only if all other member states back an additional €45bn ($49bn) loan for Ukraine covering the country’s needs through 2027. Hungary, Czechia, Bulgaria and Slovakia oppose the loan, arguing that money for weapons prolongs the war. Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico accused Germany of blackmail.

The proposal also includes a 3% levy on revenues from online advertising, online marketplaces and the monetisation of user data, which would raise an estimated €5bn a year for the EU budget. Ireland, where Google, Meta, Apple, Microsoft and LinkedIn have their European headquarters, is alarmed. Irish Prime Minister Micheál Martin, whose country could still veto the measure, has said it sends the wrong messages.

Some capitals are also concerned about the bloc’s borrowing. The EU has about €860bn in outstanding debt from its pandemic recovery programme and its support for Ukraine. Some governments fear that, without its own revenue sources, the Commission could lose its triple-A credit rating, which would raise borrowing costs for all members.

Leaders have set a final deadline of a summit one week before Christmas. They are expected to return to Brussels in late December, when the bloc’s self-imposed target of agreeing before the end of 2026 falls due.
