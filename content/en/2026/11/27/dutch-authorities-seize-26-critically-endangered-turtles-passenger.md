Dutch authorities have seized 26 Vallarta mud turtles, a critically endangered species, from the checked baggage of a man travelling from Mexico to Hong Kong through Amsterdam's Schiphol airport. The Netherlands Food and Consumer Product Safety Authority (NVWA), the Dutch government agency that handles cases of this kind, said the reptiles were found in a passenger's luggage. Its statement was published on 27 November.

Customs officers found the animals on 21 November during a routine inspection. Once they realised the luggage contained live animals, they alerted the NVWA. At the agency's request, police arrested the man. He remains in custody, and prosecutors are continuing the investigation, the NVWA said.

According to the agency, most of the turtles were packed in a box with a toy car, while the rest were inside a sealed sock in a suitcase. The animals had been wrapped in duct tape to stop them moving. All of them survived the journey, the NVWA said. They are now being cared for at a specialised rescue facility, where the tape has been removed. The agency said the turtles were "presumably taken from the wild in Mexico".

The Vallarta mud turtle is endemic to a small area of western Mexico, in the state of Jalisco. Scientists only formally described it in 2018. It is thought to be one of the smallest turtle species in the world, with an adult length of around 10 centimetres. Its habitat has been reduced by urbanisation and tourism, and roads pose a particular risk to the slow-moving animals. The conservation group Turtle Island estimates that no more than 300 of the turtles survive in the wild. The NVWA cites a similar figure of about 300, and says the 26 animals may have represented nearly 10 per cent of the wild population. The species is listed as Critically Endangered on the IUCN Red List and receives the highest level of protection under the Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species (CITES).

The seizure is not an isolated case. Hong Kong customs authorities were reported to have seized eight turtles suspected to be endangered in October. Earlier, three months before that, they had detected two separate smuggling cases involving a total of 42 live turtles.

The NVWA has urged people to report any suspected illegal trade in protected animals or plants. It named online forums, marketplaces, bird fairs and antique or curiosity markets as places to watch.
