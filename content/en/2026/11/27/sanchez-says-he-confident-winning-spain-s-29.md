Spain’s prime minister, Pedro Sánchez, said on Friday that he was confident his Spanish Socialist Workers’ Party (PSOE) would secure a left-wing majority in Sunday’s snap general election, despite public polling that points in the opposite direction. He made the remarks as the parties prepared to close their campaigns before the vote on 29 November.

In an interview with a Spanish radio station, Sánchez said that his party’s internal data suggested it would win the most votes and that he would be re-elected with the support of smaller leftist allies. “I can’t conceive of us losing,” he said. He argued that left-leaning voters were increasingly mobilising and that a turnout surge was needed to secure a progressive majority capable of tackling the housing crisis.

Spain has a legal ban on publishing new opinion polls in the days before an election, so Sánchez’s claim cannot be checked against fresh surveys. Nearly all the polls released before the ban, however, showed a clear advantage for a potential coalition between the conservative People’s Party (PP), led by Alberto Núñez Feijóo, and the far-right Vox party, led by Santiago Abascal.

The election was called on 5 October, after the PP, Vox and the rightwing, pro-independence Catalan party Junts voted down emergency housing measures put forward by the government. Affordable housing has become a central issue in the campaign, and it has prompted large protests across the country. More than 3 million people took part in a recent general strike over soaring housing prices. It is Spain’s sixth general election in 11 years.

All the polls point to the PP finishing first but without an absolute majority, which would leave it needing Vox to govern. Vox is polling at almost 20%, according to the surveys published before the ban. A coalition of that kind would mark the first time the far right has held government office in Spain since the return to democracy after the death of General Franco.

In a televised pre-election debate, Sánchez clashed with Feijóo, Abascal and the left-wing former Barcelona mayor Ada Colau, who is associated with the Sumar alliance. He warned voters that Spain faced a choice between regression and progressivism. Feijóo, whose party won the most seats in 2023 but failed to form a government, told supporters in Barcelona that “this time we will pull it off.”

Campaigning, rallies and political advertising are banned on Saturday, the “day of reflection”, so voters can weigh their choices before the polls open on Sunday.
