The Med Emre, a vessel flying the Maltese flag that sank about 20 miles off Ibiza in the Balearic Islands, will be towed to the port of Cartagena in the Murcia region of south-east Spain. The decision was taken by Spain's Merchant Marine Directorate-General, the national maritime authority, which issued a resolution imposing the tow and authorising Cartagena as a place of refuge.

Elena Delgado, the maritime captain for Valencia, announced the decision on Friday afternoon after technical meetings in Valencia. She said inspections that day showed the ship remained stable and that its structural condition and buoyancy had not changed since Thursday. Towing had already started, and the operator's plan puts arrival at Cartagena next Monday.

The authorities say the port is the only one in the area that meets the requirements for the work. The Escombreras dock, in the outer part of the port, has a depth of 35 metres just seven metres from the quay, and calm water that keeps swell, wind and current to a minimum. Cartagena also has a base of Salvamento Marítimo (Sasemar), Spain's maritime rescue and anti-pollution agency, and local petrochemical firms that can receive and treat the cargo and fuel directly, without ship-to-ship transfers.

On board are about 7,000 tonnes of cargo and around 160 tonnes of fuel. Officials say the ship cannot be emptied or righted at sea, and leaving it at anchor in late autumn and winter weather would make sinking and pollution unacceptably likely. Removing the cargo and fuel, then righting and refloating the vessel, will take several weeks.

Earlier on Friday, Antoni Costa, spokesman for the regional government of the Balearic Islands, rejected any plan to tow the ship into a Balearic port to empty its tanks. The regional government had offered the port of Sant Antoni for analysis only. "We will not accept it," Costa said in Spanish.

Delgado said a slick of just over one mile was detected on Friday. She described it as very minor and said it would probably disperse soon through wave action and evaporation.

The cause of the capsizing is under investigation by Spain's independent authority for technical investigation of maritime accidents. Delgado said it appeared that hatches giving access to the ballast tanks on one side had been left open, and that heavy seas allowed large volumes of water in, causing a sudden list to starboard and the capsize. She said several other factors could be involved and that any question of crew error would be for the courts to decide.

A coordination group led by the director-general of the Merchant Marine has been set up to manage the operation. It includes the regional governments of Murcia, Valencia and the Balearic Islands, the central government's regional delegations, the ministries for the ecological transition and for the interior, which oversees civil protection, and the Cartagena Port Authority.
