Spain's main political parties held their final rallies on Friday, closing campaigns ahead of the general election on Sunday 29 November. Around 39 million people are eligible to vote. Under Spanish electoral law, no opinion polls have been published since Monday, and the surveys that were published had mostly pointed to a close finish.

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez, leader of the Socialist Party (PSOE), held a rally with a festive character at the Caja Mágica arena in Madrid. Reported figures put attendance at more than 12,000, with about a thousand more people turned away for lack of space. The programme included live music, and the film director Pedro Almodóvar appeared on stage to ask for votes. Sánchez said he was confident of defying the polls. "I cannot imagine us losing," he said, posing for selfies with supporters in red T-shirts.

Sánchez warned that the conservative People's Party (PP), led by Alberto Núñez Feijóo, and Vox, led by Santiago Abascal, wanted to repeal the Democratic Memory Law and reduce protections for women, gay people and migrants. He also said the government's pensions would remain public and guaranteed. He urged supporters to turn out in large numbers, saying that rights could disappear "in a single day" if people did not vote.

Feijóo's PP, which is narrowly ahead in most polls, ended its campaign by travelling to Barcelona and then Seville. In Seville he insisted he wanted to govern with a majority, supported by parliamentary agreements with Vox, and said he had never discussed giving Vox ministerial posts with Abascal. Andalusia, where Seville is located, has 61 of the 350 seats in Spain's Congress, the lower house of parliament, more than any other region.

Vox held its closing rally in the bullring in Valencia, on Spain's east coast. Reported figures put the crowd at about 14,000. Abascal entered the ring to a playlist that included the party's campaign song, and walked around greeting supporters. Speaking on stage, he said: "I aspire to come out on Sunday and say: mission accomplished, Pedro Sánchez is leaving" (translated from Spanish). He also raised suspicions of electoral fraud, which he said his supporters should guard against, and said the sum of PP and Vox seats "is not yet made".

Ignacio Garriga, Vox's secretary general, drew one of the loudest cheers of the night by calling for fewer burkas and fewer halal products. He said he was acting out of love for the country rather than hatred. A small group in the crowd chanted "Viva Franco", a reference to the dictator whose death in 1975 preceded Spain's transition to democracy. Vox's platform includes "national priority", which would put Spanish citizens first for welfare, and "remigration" for some foreigners.

The final day of campaigning followed a four-way television debate on Thursday night, in which housing was the dominant topic. Feijóo said the PP would build one million homes over four years, while Sánchez said housing should be a right and not a subject of speculation. The election will decide the make-up of Spain's 350-seat Congress.
