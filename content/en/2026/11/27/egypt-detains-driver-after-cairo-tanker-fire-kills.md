Egyptian prosecutors have ordered the detention of a 21-year-old driver suspected of causing a collision that overturned a fuel tanker on Cairo's ring road on Wednesday evening, starting a fire that killed 31 people. The Public Prosecution, the body that handles criminal investigations, said on Thursday that the man would be held while the inquiry continues. He has been sent to the Forensic Medicine Authority for tests to establish whether he had taken drugs. Authorities have not named him.

The tanker caught fire at about 17.30 CET (18.30 Cairo time), at the height of the evening rush hour, on an elevated section of the ring road in al-Waraq. The district is a densely populated area of Giza, on the western edge of the capital. The fire spread to passing traffic and destroyed 27 vehicles, including cars and motorcycles. The Ministry of Health and Population first reported 21 injured on Wednesday. On Thursday it put the final toll at 31 dead and 26 injured. Five of the injured have left hospital. The other 21 are still being treated, eight of them in other hospitals. The bodies of the dead are in the custody of prosecutors, and forensic teams are taking DNA samples from relatives to identify some of them.

According to prosecutors, the driver told investigators he was travelling from al-Marg towards al-Haram at 110 km/h (68 mph), with his father in the car. He said he was changing lanes to overtake when the front left of his car struck the rear right of the tanker. He lost control, and his car veered to the far right of the road. One of the tanker's tyres then burst, and the vehicle hit a concrete barrier and overturned. Leaking petrol caught fire. Prosecutors said they identified the driver after inspecting the scene and interviewing witnesses, and that he confessed when confronted with the findings of the investigation. The inquiry will determine whether he bears criminal responsibility.

The cause of the crash has been disputed. Early local reports, citing sources close to the investigation, said a high-voltage cable had fallen in front of the tanker. The Ministry of Transport denied issuing any such statement and said the cause was a matter for prosecutors. The Ministry of Electricity and Renewable Energy said the overhead lines were taut when the fire started and that two circuits tripped only after it had begun. Its spokesman, Mansour Abdel Ghani, said the lines had no connection to the crash and had melted in the heat.

A 2018 cabinet decision bars trucks carrying more than four tonnes from the ring road between 6.00 and midnight. Prosecutors have not said why the tanker was on the road at the time.

President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi ordered full medical care for the injured and support for the families of those killed. Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly visited the injured on Thursday. He said the government, civil society and businesspeople would provide bank investment certificates to give bereaved families a steady income, as well as replacement vehicles for people whose cars burned. "We realise that no compensation or money can make up for those we have lost," Madbouly said. The cabinet said the Ministry of Social Solidarity had received 24 million Egyptian pounds (about $445,000) from seven charities. A cabinet minister separately gave a figure of 10 million pounds donated to the families of the victims. The sources do not reconcile the two amounts.

The crash has renewed questions about road safety. The Ministry of Transport says it spent about $37bn on transport between 2014 and 2024. Yet the state statistics agency, CAPMAS, recorded 5,829 road deaths in 2025, up 10.8 per cent on the previous year, and about 84,000 injured. Mahmoud Samy, a member of parliament, said the government needed to manage road safety more professionally. Abdel Moneim Imam, who chairs the liberal Justice Party, said the cabinet bears full responsibility and should resign.

Transport Minister Kamel al-Wazir was appointed in 2019, after a train fire at Cairo's Ramses station killed 25 people. He has rejected calls to resign since, including after a lorry crash in the Nile Delta last year that killed 19 people, almost all of them young women. The ministry declined to comment further. Earlier this month, a train crash, also in Giza, killed at least seven people and injured more than 125.
