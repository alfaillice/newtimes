Arsenal have parted company with head coach Renée Slegers by mutual consent after a poor run of results in the Women's Super League (WSL), England's top women's football division. The club announced the decision on Friday. Slegers, a 37-year-old Dutchwoman, leaves with the team sixth in the table after 11 matches, 12 points behind leaders Chelsea.

Assistant coach Aaron D'Antino, who had been on the club's coaching staff since August 2018, has also left. Melissa Clarke-Reed, a former head coach of Brighton and London City Lionesses, has been placed in interim charge while Arsenal begin the search for a permanent replacement. The club say they will not rush the appointment, because they want the right person rather than someone in place for the next match.

Slegers was first appointed on an interim basis in October 2024, after Jonas Eidevall resigned. She had worked under him as an assistant. Three months later the club gave her the job permanently. She had been under contract until 2029 after signing a three-and-a-half-year deal in January.

Her record includes the biggest prize in the club's recent history. On 24 May 2025, Arsenal beat Barcelona 1-0 in Lisbon to win the Women's Champions League, the club's second European title and their first in 18 years. In February 2026 they also won the inaugural FIFA Women's Champions Cup, a competition between the continental champions, which was played on home soil.

The league form has been far weaker. Arsenal have scored 11 goals in 11 WSL matches, less than half the tally of Chelsea, who have 29, and below second-placed Manchester City on 28 and London City Lionesses on 31. The team have gone four league matches without a win and six matches without a victory at the Emirates Stadium. They are eight points behind London City Lionesses, who occupy the third Women's Champions League qualification place. Injuries have added to the problems, with midfielder Mariona Caldentey sidelined by a hamstring problem. On Sunday, fans booed at the Emirates after a goalless draw with West Ham.

Former Arsenal and England forward Lianne Sanderson had publicly called for Slegers to leave before the announcement. After the West Ham game, Slegers acknowledged that performances had fallen short. "I take my responsibilities, because the results haven't been good enough," she said.

Clare Wheatley, Arsenal's director of women's football, said the decision had been "extremely difficult". Wheatley added: "After careful reflection, it is clear that results have been below the expected level, and change is necessary." Chief executive Richard Garlick described Slegers as a "wonderful person" and said the club's ambition was to win major trophies consistently.

Arsenal's next league match is away to Everton on 11 December. Clarke-Reed is expected to oversee the three games before the winter break. The team return to action on 10 January.
