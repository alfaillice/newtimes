Five Levski Sofia supporters were taken to hospital after violence broke out before their Europa League match against Marseille at the Stade Vélodrome on Thursday, according to the Marseille police prefecture. Their injuries included eye injuries, head trauma and a dislocated shoulder. The French and Bulgarian presidents both condemned the scenes, and the match went ahead on time. Marseille won 1-0.

Levski Sofia, the Bulgarian domestic champions, had around 500 supporters housed in the away section. About ten minutes before kick-off, several Marseille fans ran from a home stand and breached the away area, appearing to pursue the Bulgarian supporters. Projectiles and flares were thrown between the two groups, and there were further flare-ups during the half-time break. Security staff restored order, allowing the game to start.

The prefecture said six other people were treated on site for various wounds. Police said all those injured were Bulgarian and that no arrests had been reported at that stage. Marseille, a club in France's Ligue 1, said it was working to establish the precise circumstances of the incidents.

French President Emmanuel Macron, whose office is the Élysée Palace, condemned the attacks on Bulgarian fans in the strongest terms. He spoke to Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev on the sidelines of a European summit in Brussels and assured him that "these acts would not go unpunished", the Élysée said. Radev thanked Macron for his personal involvement and for the medical help given to the injured. Bulgaria's President, Iliana Iotova, called the footage shocking in a statement on social media. She said the attacks had desecrated one of the great games and that everyone should be able to support their team without fear for their safety.

Levski Sofia coach Julio Velázquez described the scenes as "unacceptable for football". Marseille coach Bruno Genesio said the violence reflected a wider problem in society and that it was very regrettable.

Violence also affected other European ties that night, with incidents reported at Besiktas's match in Georgia and at Sunderland's home game against Jagiellonia Bialystok. The focus in Marseille, however, has been on the clashes in and around the away section and on the injuries suffered by the Bulgarian supporters.
