Serbia's student-led movement has nominated Radomir Saičić, a chemistry professor at Belgrade University, as its candidate in the presidential election on 27 December. Saičić announced the choice at a televised news conference in Belgrade on Friday, saying the movement must now focus on strengthening the rule of law and fighting corruption.

Saičić, 65, is also a member of the Serbian Academy of Sciences and Arts. He was among the first university professors to join the protest movement that emerged in 2024, and he now sits in the 250-seat parliament as a representative of the Student List, which won 140 seats in October's parliamentary election. The presidency is largely ceremonial, but the president nominates prime ministers and serves as commander-in-chief of the armed forces.

The movement grew out of protests that followed the collapse in 2024 of a canopy at a recently renovated railway station in Novi Sad, which killed 16 people. The disaster was widely blamed on corruption and led to the largest demonstrations in Serbia since the fall of Slobodan Milošević in 2000. After nearly two years of resistance, Aleksandar Vučić eventually called the snap elections.

Vučić dominated Serbian politics for 14 years. He cut short his second presidential term to run for prime minister, but his Serbian Progressive Party (SNS) was defeated by the student movement in the parliamentary ballot. Vučić is now an opposition MP and cannot stand for president again, because the constitution limits presidents to two terms. It remains unclear whether the SNS will take part in the presidential vote. Vučić has said he personally favours ignoring the election, and a final decision from the party is expected in mid-December. Candidacies must be submitted by 6 December.

Saičić's nomination was not a surprise, as he had been mentioned from the start as a possible candidate for the students. He is described as a reserved and deeply religious man, widely respected in academic circles, who does not seek public attention or political confrontation. His reputation rests on his academic work and personal integrity.

Speaking at the news conference, Saičić said: "Now we have the opportunity to turn the freedom we fought for into lasting change." He added that the assembly, the government and the courts must regain the trust of citizens, and that the same applies to the presidency. He committed to a sovereign state that respects its constitution and allows every citizen to live with dignity.

The campaign is expected to focus on the rule of law, the fight against corruption and restoring public confidence in state institutions. One central promise is expected to be a full review of contracts and agreements signed by Vučić's governments, particularly large public investments.

Meanwhile, the outgoing government remains formally in office until a new administration is formed. Arrests and investigations involving senior figures from the former Vučić administration are continuing. Vučić accuses the new parliamentary majority of political persecution, while the student movement insists that accountability must apply to everyone, regardless of political connections.
