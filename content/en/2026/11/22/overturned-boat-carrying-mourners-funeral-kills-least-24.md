Al Jazeera reports that a boat believed to be carrying hundreds of people to a funeral overturned in southern Nigeria on Friday, killing at least 24 people, according to authorities. The vessel capsized in Bayelsa State after a mechanical failure while travelling from the communities of Ayama to Ukubie, according to police.

Al Jazeera says the boat was overcrowded. Police did not say how many people were on board. Al Jazeera reports that some residents told the Reuters news agency there were at least 300 people aboard, a figure police have not confirmed.

The initial police account from Friday gave a much lower toll. At that point, four bodies had been recovered from the water, and 24 people had been rescued. Of those rescued, 19 were treated at a local clinic and five were taken to hospital in the state capital. On Saturday, Manzo Ezekiel, a spokesman for the National Emergency Management Agency, said 24 bodies had been recovered from the scene and that search and rescue operations were still under way. Al Jazeera's report does not explain the rise in the number of bodies recovered between the two accounts.

A statement from the Bayelsa State Police Command, cited by Al Jazeera, places the accident at the river junction between the Oweikorogha and Otuan communities, in the Southern Ijaw district. The statement said the boat was travelling "for a burial ceremony" and that police were "coordinating efforts with local divers and other responders."

According to Al Jazeera, police say the boat's driver fled the scene and the vessel's manager was taken into custody.

Al Jazeera notes that boat accidents are common on Nigeria's busy rivers and waterways. It says they are often caused by overloaded vessels, poor maintenance or failure to comply with safety regulations. The broadcaster cites two earlier incidents: in August, at least 51 people died when a boat sank in Sokoto State in northwest Nigeria, and in January, at least 26 people drowned when a boat carrying passengers from Jigawa to Yobe State capsized.

The search is continuing, and the death toll may change as operations proceed. Al Jazeera's report is based on information from police and the National Emergency Management Agency and does not include a full account from the boat's surviving passengers.
