At least three people have been killed and 36 others were still missing on Sunday after a fire at a confectionery factory in Banten province, western Java, according to Indonesian rescue officials cited by the BBC and the Guardian. Banten lies about 50km west of Jakarta, the capital.

The blaze broke out on Saturday afternoon at a plant owned by PT Fonusa Agung Mulia. The two outlets differ on the exact location: the BBC places the factory in Serang district, while the Guardian places it in Cikande. According to the BBC, the company describes itself on LinkedIn as a subsidiary of UNIFAM, one of Indonesia's largest food and beverage companies.

Firefighters were called at 16.30 local time (10.30 CET), according to the BBC, which cites Kompas TV. When they arrived, the building was already engulfed in flames. Fire official Tubagus Machfudz told Kompas TV that the company's fire protection system could not be checked: “we could not determine whether the company’s protection system was functioning properly or not,” he said. Authorities have not yet established the cause of the fire. The local disaster agency said in an Instagram post on Sunday that the investigation was continuing.

The Banten governor, Andra Soni, told reporters that about 60 people were believed to be inside the factory when it caught fire, according to both outlets. Drone footage published by the Guardian shows parts of the complex destroyed, with sections of the roof collapsed and smoke still rising. Firefighters were still tackling some flames on Sunday, while rescuers searched the damaged areas where workers were believed to be trapped. The BBC reports that at least one body was retrieved from the wreckage on Sunday morning.

The reports differ on the number of people rescued. The BBC, citing Indonesia's National Search and Rescue Agency, says 15 survivors had been located and four others were taken to hospital and a local clinic with injuries that were not specified. The Guardian, citing search and rescue official Rizky Dwianto, reports that four people were injured and taken to a nearby hospital and clinic. Neither outlet gives a further breakdown of the injuries.

Relatives of the missing workers gathered near the factory entrance, waiting for news, according to footage from local broadcasters that the BBC attributes to AFP.

The incident comes after other deadly disasters in Indonesia. The Guardian reports that in December 2025 a blaze in Jakarta, probably caused by a drone battery explosion, tore through a seven-storey building and killed at least 22 people, according to police. In 2023, at least 13 people were killed in an explosion at a nickel-processing plant in the east of the country.
