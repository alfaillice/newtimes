Red Bull’s Max Verstappen won the Las Vegas Grand Prix, his third win in the race in four years, after a chaotic contest in which the two Ferrari drivers collided and both were given penalties. Verstappen finished 0.639 seconds ahead of McLaren’s Lando Norris, with his teammate Oscar Piastri third.

The race was defined by close, almost constant contact between the leading cars. The drivers ran nose to tail under the floodlights on the Strip, and the lead changed hands again and again. Timing reports counted 35 lead changes over 50 laps, while another tally put the figure at 34. Both described a race more like American oval racing than a typical Formula One grand prix, shaped by the circuit and by the energy limits on this year’s hybrid engines.

Ferrari’s Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc collided again. Hamilton received a 10-second penalty for passing Norris off the track during a battle that also involved Leclerc. Leclerc was given a 10-second penalty for tipping Hamilton into a spin. Hamilton finished seventh and Leclerc 10th.

Mercedes’ George Russell, who started on the front row, had been running in the leading group. With two laps to go he locked his brakes over the bumps before Turn Five, as he tried to pass Kimi Antonelli, and went straight into the barrier. He was able to reverse out and finished ninth. Antonelli, who had come through from sixth on the grid, was fourth at the finish, behind the two McLarens. Alpine’s Pierre Gasly was fifth and his teammate Franco Colapinto sixth. Carlos Sainz was eighth for Williams in his 250th race.

“That was crazy. Really, really surreal and something I’ve never seen before in Formula One myself,” Verstappen said. Norris said: “I think that must have been the most chaotic race ever in the history of Formula One.”

The win was Verstappen’s fourth in six races and puts him 37 points clear of Hamilton in the drivers’ championship. Russell has dropped to fourth overall, level on points with Norris. Mercedes have already won both titles this season. Antonelli, a 20-year-old Italian, became the youngest Formula One world champion in history earlier this month.

The championship moves to the Middle East next weekend, when the Qatar Grand Prix takes place at the Lusail International Circuit from 27 to 29 November.
