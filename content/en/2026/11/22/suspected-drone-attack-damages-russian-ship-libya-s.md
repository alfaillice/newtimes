An explosion on a Russian ship docked at the naval base in the Libyan port of Benghazi was caused by a suspected drone attack, the authorities in eastern Libya have said. No group or government has claimed responsibility, and Russia had not commented at the time of writing.

The blast happened at dawn on Sunday, 22 November. The eastern authorities said the ship had stopped at Benghazi to refuel and take on supplies on its way back to Russia, under maritime cooperation agreements between the two countries. The administration, known as the Government of National Stability (GNS), is based in the east and backed by the military commander Khalifa Haftar. It said the site had been secured and that port and naval facilities, including warehouses, were safe. Normal operations would resume once the fire was under control.

Satellite imagery reviewed by a fact-checking unit shows a boat on fire at the port at 06.47 CET, and a later image appears to show the same vessel partly submerged. Videos geolocated by the same unit show the ship in flames while it was still dark. In its initial account, the GNS said: "Initial investigations indicate that the explosion occurred onboard a Russian ship docked in the port."

The reports differ on casualties. Authorities in the east said there were no casualties. Two medical sources told reporters that no deaths had been reported but that some people had been injured. The number and nationalities of the injured were not yet known. These figures should be treated as provisional.

Libya has been divided since the 2011 uprising that overthrew Muammar Gaddafi. The GNS in the east competes with the internationally recognised Government of National Unity (GNU) in Tripoli, headed by Prime Minister Abdulhamid Dbeibah. The GNU issued a separate statement warning against hosting foreign forces or letting them use port and civilian facilities. It said such activity raised serious fears about the threat to citizens and national assets.

The incident comes amid wider Ukrainian strikes on Russian targets beyond the front line. On Saturday, a Russian cargo ship, the Sparta II, was hit by drones in the Gulf of Finland, an arm of the Baltic Sea. The Finnish military said Ukraine had probably carried out that strike. The vessel, bound for the Russian exclave of Kaliningrad, drifted into Finnish waters and was still smouldering on Sunday. Its 16 crew were evacuated, and one was slightly injured.

Reporting has said that Russia is close to the authorities in eastern Libya and uses Benghazi to supply its forces in Africa and as a base in the Mediterranean. Whether the Benghazi explosion was a deliberate attack, who carried it out and what the ship was carrying remain unknown. The GNS said it is coordinating with Russian counterparts to establish the source of the suspected attack and who is responsible.
