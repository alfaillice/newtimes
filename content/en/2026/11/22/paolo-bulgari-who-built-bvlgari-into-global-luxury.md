Italian jeweller and businessman Paolo Bulgari has died in Rome at the age of 89, according to the BBC. The BBC reports that his family announced the news and, citing Italian media, quotes them describing him as "the greatest jeweller of our time".

Bulgari had been chairman of the Bvlgari Group since 1984. The BBC says his jewellery pieces were worn by Hollywood stars and royalty, including Diana, Princess of Wales.

The company was founded in Rome in the late 19th century by his grandfather, Sotirios Voulgaris, a Greek silversmith who later adapted his name to Bulgari in Italian. According to the BBC, Voulgaris came from Paramythia, a mountain village in the Epirus region of north-western Greece known for its silver jewellery. He left Ottoman rule, settled in Naples and then opened a shop in Rome.

The BBC describes the family's style as bold gemstones, rich yellow gold, generous proportions and unexpected colour combinations, which set it apart from the more delicate aesthetic associated with Parisian jewellery houses. Bulgari joined the company shortly after leaving school, became an expert in gemstones and eventually led the business, overseeing its international growth. The BBC also says he began working with his father in 1960.

Under his leadership, the business moved into luxury markets and expanded beyond jewellery into watches, perfumes, leather goods and hotels. The brand's celebrity clients have included Elizabeth Taylor, Audrey Hepburn, Marilyn Monroe, Ingrid Bergman, Sophia Loren, Anne Hathaway, Zendaya and Rihanna.

In 2011 the family sold its controlling stake to the French luxury group LVMH in a deal worth about €3.7bn, the BBC reports. In 2019 Bulgari and his wife set up the Paolo Bulgari Foundation, which supports young people through schooling and job training in disadvantaged neighbourhoods on the outskirts of Rome.

The company published a tribute on social media, describing him as a "true creative soul" whose work would continue to inspire future generations. The BBC notes that the post also quoted him: "Everything can be a source of inspiration."

The funeral will take place in Rome on Monday, 23 November, at the Basilica of Santa Croce in Gerusalemme.
