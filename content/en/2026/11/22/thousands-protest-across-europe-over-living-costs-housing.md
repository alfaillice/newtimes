Thousands of people across Europe took to the streets over the weekend to protest against rising living costs, unaffordable housing and cuts to public services, according to Al Jazeera. Students and workers are asking their governments for action. The gatherings brought together groups with overlapping concerns, although their demands and their size varied considerably from one country to another.

In France, the Ministry of Interior, the government department responsible for police and public order, counted 82,500 people at demonstrations nationwide on Saturday, including 21,500 in Paris. Al Jazeera reports that this was down from 106,000 the previous weekend. The organisers estimated the turnout at 150,000, a markedly higher figure. The protests are now in their sixth consecutive weekend. They began in September, when students demanded better schools, and later drew in trade unions and the Yellow Vests, a movement that emerged in 2018 over rising fuel prices and living costs. Demonstrators have also criticised police violence and a new law that strengthens protections for officers who use firearms.

In Portugal, thousands marched through Lisbon despite heavy rain. They called for higher wages, affordable housing and relief from rising prices. The march was organised by CGTP, the country's largest trade union federation, which also demanded higher pensions and cheaper essential goods. Al Jazeera notes that property prices in Portugal have more than doubled since 2017. It also cites Eurostat, the European Union's statistics agency, which found that average annual earnings in 2025 were 37 percent below the EU average.

A separate wave of youth protests took place on Friday in Germany and Sweden. In Berlin about 4,000 people gathered, according to Al Jazeera. Police in Helsinki reported roughly 200 participants at a Saturday march organised by left-wing and student groups. Organisers of an international youth strike said demonstrations were planned in at least 25 countries. Their demands ranged from greater education funding and climate action to opposition to compulsory military service. In Germany, the youth campaign Gen Z regelt, meaning 'Generation Z takes charge', announced demonstrations in 73 locations.

In Italy, a nationwide strike called by the CGIL, one of the country's main trade union confederations, disrupted transport, schools and some healthcare services on Friday. Al Jazeera reports demonstrations in more than 100 cities. According to the network, union leader Maurizio Landini put the nationwide turnout at more than one million people.

Al Jazeera points out that some of the protests were linked through the international youth initiative, while others grew out of established national campaigns. The figures reported by the network come from a single outlet, and the turnout numbers differ depending on the source, as in France. The report does not say whether the governments involved plan to respond to the demands.
