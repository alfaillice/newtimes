Spain’s main parties enter the last week of campaigning before the general election on 29 November. The conservative People’s Party (PP), led by Alberto Núñez Feijóo, is asking voters to back it as the “useful vote”, and four opinion polls published on Sunday place it ahead of the Socialists (PSOE) of Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez.

According to party sources cited by the Spanish news agency Europa Press, Feijóo will spend the final week asking voters to consolidate the lead that polls give him. The PP says its mood has improved since a televised head-to-head debate with Sánchez, which party officials described as a clear win for their candidate. Party sources also say the PP has stopped votes flowing to Vox, the far-right party led by Santiago Abascal, and that more than 600,000 voters have moved from the PSOE to the PP since October. The party has not yet measured the effect of the debate with internal tracking polls.

At a rally in Valencia on Saturday, which Europa Press said filled the city’s bullring with about 12,000 people, Feijóo urged supporters not to trust the polls or the “atmosphere”, saying they were “capable of anything”. The PP’s campaign team said: “We are winning and we want more votes and more seats.” The party plans to argue that a clear majority is needed for what it calls the “national reconstruction” of Spain after eight years of Sánchez’s government.

The four polls, published by Ipsos, Data10, GAD3 and Hamalgama Métrica, all place the PP first, but the size of its lead varies. Ipsos gives the PP 30.3% of the vote and 127 seats, against 29.8% and 119 seats for the PSOE. Data10 gives the PP 32.1% and 133 seats, and the PSOE 27% and 111. GAD3 gives the PP 31.1% and 131 seats, and the PSOE 28.4% and 110. Hamalgama Métrica gives the PP 31.8% and 130 seats, and the PSOE 25.4% and 98. The gap between the two main parties ranges from 0.5 points in the Ipsos poll to 6.4 points in Hamalgama Métrica’s.

Vox is forecast to win between 63 and 80 seats, depending on the poll. Taken together, the PP and Vox would reach between 190 and 210 seats in the estimates. The Congress of Deputies, Spain’s lower house of parliament, requires 176 seats for an absolute majority, so all four polls put the two right-wing parties above that threshold. Frente Amplio, a left-wing grouping, is projected to win between six and ten seats in the polls that give figures for it. None of the four polls assigns seats to Sumar.

The PP also expects a televised debate on Thursday 26 November, the first in which Feijóo and Abascal will appear together. Party sources say Feijóo will focus on Sánchez rather than on Vox, and expect Sánchez to try again to link the PP with Vox. The PP says that strategy no longer works, because the two parties already govern together in several regions. Party sources also argue that Sánchez has no room to recover before polling day. Those are the claims of the PP’s own sources, not independent assessments, and the polls show a range of outcomes rather than a settled result.
