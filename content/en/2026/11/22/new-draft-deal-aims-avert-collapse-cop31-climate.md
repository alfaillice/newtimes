UN climate talks in Türkiye were under strain on Sunday after a new draft agreement was published in the early hours, as the summit's president said negotiators were closer than ever to a final deal. The talks are the 31st annual UN climate conference, known as COP31. Türkiye is hosting the meeting in Antalya, and Australia is leading the negotiations.

The two-week summit was due to end on Friday but has now gone into a second day of overtime. The dispute has centred on fossil fuels: coal, oil and gas, which scientists say are the main drivers of climate change. An opening draft from Australia on Monday called for a faster phase-out of them. A shorter version released on Thursday removed every mention after Saudi Arabia and other major oil and gas producers objected. That prompted threats of walkouts from several countries, including the European Union and Pacific states. Some negotiating teams rescheduled Sunday morning flights, a sign that a deal was not yet in sight.

The new text still does not name fossil fuels. It refers instead to a commitment made at a previous COP three years ago, when governments agreed to move away from oil, gas and coal. For the first time at a COP, it also recognises that the world is on course to exceed the 1.5C limit set under the 2015 Paris Agreement. According to Al Jazeera, the draft calls for faster development of carbon-removal technologies. BBC reports that commitments to carbon capture, use and storage have been reduced in this version, which may ease some of the EU's concerns.

The BBC reported that the EU considers the new text a step backwards, although the EU had not yet publicly responded. Before the draft was published, EU climate commissioner Wopke Hoekstra said there would be no deal without what was needed on climate action. The UK's chief negotiator, Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh, said her team was still working towards an agreement.

Money for poorer countries is another unresolved issue. Earlier drafts included raising adaptation finance to $120bn (£91bn) by 2035, but that figure was removed. The new text includes no specific amount and postpones the decision to future meetings. Zanetor Agyeman Rawlings, Ghana's environment minister and chair of the African Group of negotiators, said the group had not "come here to beg".

Kurum, who is also Türkiye's environment minister, told delegates early on Sunday: "We are closer than ever to agreement on the remaining issues." Al Jazeera reported that he believed concerns on fossil fuels and adaptation finance had been addressed. The presidency said delegations had a few hours to raise objections before a session to approve the text, scheduled for 07:00 GMT (08.00 CET). BBC reported that the meeting was delayed on Sunday morning.

Approval requires consensus. If countries cannot agree, the negotiations could be suspended until next year. That has happened only once since the COP process began in 1995. Earlier this week, 100 civil society groups wrote to the co-hosts warning that they were "on course to preside over one of the most disappointing COPs" the UN has held. The United Nations had said before the talks that the world would likely exceed the 1.5C goal.
