Suspected Israeli settler attacks set fire to vehicles and homes in several Palestinian villages in the occupied West Bank overnight on Saturday, prompting a political row in Israel. Gadi Eisenkot, the leader of the opposition, described the arson as “Jewish terrorism”.

Dozens of settlers raided at least four Palestinian communities, setting fire to about a dozen vehicles and several homes and daubing racist graffiti. Israeli police said they had opened an investigation with forensic teams. In Kafr Malik, near Ramallah, a Hebrew slogan on a wall read “This is the first revenge”. A local activist, Jihad al-Qaq, said settlers had entered the outskirts of the village at about 2 am and set fire to around eight vehicles. Other affected villages included Deir Abu Mashaal, Deir as-Sudan and Al-Tuwani, and Palestinian reports said an agricultural building had been set alight in Deir Ballut. The Israeli military said soldiers and officers had been deployed to the four affected villages and condemned the attacks.

The political context is a difficult one. An inconclusive election last month left Benjamin Netanyahu, the prime minister, without a majority in the Knesset, Israel's parliament. President Isaac Herzog has given Eisenkot 28 days to try to form a government, with a possible 14-day extension, although his prospects appear limited.

Eisenkot said the attacks were acts of “Jewish terrorism” and accused the far-right ministers Itamar Ben-Gvir and Bezalel Smotrich of pushing the West Bank towards anarchy. He urged the government and security services to use administrative detention and targeted operations by the Shin Bet, Israel's internal security service, against those responsible. Yair Lapid, leader of the Yesh Atid party, blamed the government for encouraging the violence, saying ministers were supporting it through their silence. Yair Golan, leader of the Democrats party, warned of a possible third intifada and said the West Bank was on the brink of an explosion. Noam Tibon, a retired major general and Knesset member, warned that if Israel did not stop Jewish terrorism in the West Bank, it would destroy the country.

Violence in the West Bank has grown since the Hamas-led attacks on Israel on 7 October 2023 and the war in Gaza that followed. A tally compiled from Palestinian health ministry figures counts at least 1,119 Palestinians killed by Israeli forces or settlers in the territory since then. Official Israeli figures show at least 51 Israelis, including soldiers, killed in Palestinian attacks or clashes over the same period. Around 500,000 settlers live alongside roughly three million Palestinians in the West Bank, where settlements are considered illegal under international law. The United Nations regards the territory as Palestinian land under Israeli occupation since 1967.

The violence comes after new British and Australian sanctions targeting Israeli companies linked to settlement building. The United Kingdom sanctioned eight Israeli construction companies on Tuesday, and Australia sanctioned three people and four Israeli entities, including a construction company, on Wednesday.
