Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has urged the United States to approve the delivery of Patriot interceptor missiles that Ukraine's partners have already paid for, as Russian attacks on several regions continued on Sunday. In Dymer, a village north of Kyiv, a drone strike caused part of a five-storey apartment building to collapse, and the number of people missing depends on which official count is used.

Patriot is a US-made air defence system, and its interceptors can shoot down ballistic missiles. Ukraine is short of them. Zelenskyy said the missiles are financed through PURL (Prioritised Ukraine Requirements List), a programme coordinated by NATO, the North Atlantic Treaty Organization, that was launched in July 2025. Under PURL, allies buy US weapons that are then delivered to Ukraine. According to NATO, more than two thirds of its members have committed to the scheme, and allies and partners have funded more than USD 6 billion of equipment through it. Zelenskyy said the deliveries still need Washington's approval. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte said earlier this month that deliveries under the programme, including Patriots, were continuing.

"It is particularly difficult to understand the delays in supplying anti-ballistic interceptors to Ukraine," Zelenskyy said in a social media post on Sunday.

Zelenskyy said the drone strike in Dymer left 14 people unaccounted for and that nine people had been evacuated, warning that others could still be trapped. Ukraine's National Police gave a higher figure, writing on Telegram that 15 people were listed as missing, including two children. Timur Tkachenko, head of the Kyiv Regional Military Administration, said two bodies had been recovered from the rubble and at least four people injured. Russian media reports gave a different count, of 10 people missing, including two children, and three bodies recovered. Rescue teams were still searching the site.

Other attacks on Sunday caused further deaths. In Vyshneve, south-west of Kyiv, a drone hit a grocery store, killing one person and injuring three, according to the regional governor. In Zaporizhzhia, Mykhailo Semikin, acting head of the regional military administration, said a man had been killed in Russian shelling. Russia's Ministry of Defence said its forces had struck an electricity facility in Zaporizhzhia, an oil refinery, electrical substations and a data centre, and that they had taken control of a village in the Kharkiv region. The ministry's claims could not be independently verified.

Zelenskyy said Russia had launched around 2,700 attack drones, nearly 1,460 guided aerial bombs and 165 missiles against 20 Ukrainian regions over the past week. He said many of the missiles were ballistic, including North Korean-made weapons, and accused Moscow of helping Pyongyang improve their accuracy. Ukraine's air force said Russia had launched 186 drones overnight, of which 161 were shot down or disabled, and recorded strikes at 18 locations.

The Sunday attacks followed a larger assault from Thursday night into Friday, when Russian forces fired more than 400 attack drones and over 110 missiles at Ukraine. At least seven people were killed in Kyiv in those strikes, and key bridges across the Dnipro River were damaged.
