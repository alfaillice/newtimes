Carlos Alcaraz will face Alexander Zverev in the final of the ATP Finals, the season-ending tournament, after coming back from a slow start to beat Ben Shelton, according to BBC Sport. The Spaniard won 3-6 6-3 7-6 (7-1) and will return to the final 12 months after losing to Jannik Sinner. Zverev, the world number one, will play the decider on Sunday after beating Daniil Medvedev 7-6 (7-0) 6-4.

BBC Sport reports that Shelton, a 24-year-old American, was the better player early on. He earned his first break point in Alcaraz's opening service game and took the first set, while Alcaraz, who had injured his hamstring earlier in the week and wore strapping on his upper-left leg, struggled to find his rhythm. Alcaraz took more than an hour to lift his level, and he broke for 5-3 in the second set to force a decider.

In the third set, Shelton kept his composure and reached 4-3, but BBC Sport says he missed two forehands that let Alcaraz go a double break up at 4-1. Alcaraz then converted his first match point with a backhand return down the line. Asked about his physical condition with little more than 24 hours to recover, he said: "Today was a really important test. I think it went great."

The 23-year-old third-ranked Spaniard is in his seventh final of 2026, a total that BBC Sport says no other men's singles player has matched this year, despite a four-month absence with a wrist injury. He is on a 13-match winning streak and has won 20 of his past 21 matches. He won the Vienna Open and the Paris Masters back to back before this event. The head-to-head with Zverev stands at 7-6 in Alcaraz's favour, and he has won four of their last five meetings, including a five-set match on his way to the Australian Open title this year.

Zverev, who has been annoyed by the reaction to his rise to world number one, according to BBC Sport, has had a strong tournament. He has not dropped serve so far and saved all six break points he has faced. Against Medvedev he hit 16 aces. BBC Sport notes that he won both his French Open and US Open titles this year, becoming a two-time major champion. Zverev said of Alcaraz: "For sure, the way he's been playing since his comeback has been one of the best players in the world."

The final will decide the ATP Finals title, and Alcaraz is bidding for his first triumph at the tournament. Zverev is a two-time champion of the event.
