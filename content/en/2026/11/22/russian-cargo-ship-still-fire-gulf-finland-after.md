The Sparta II, a Russian cargo vessel bound for Kaliningrad, was still on fire on Sunday after being struck by drones in the Gulf of Finland, a narrow arm of the Baltic Sea. Finnish authorities said firefighting and rescue work was continuing, and the Finnish Border Guard said it was working to make sure the ship did not sink.

Kaliningrad is a Russian exclave that sits between Poland and Lithuania. According to the Finnish Border Guard, the coast guard was alerted to the attack at around 08.50 local time (06.50 GMT) on Saturday and located the vessel about 50 km west of the island of Suursaari. Al Jazeera, citing the Finnish Defence Forces, reported that the ship was damaged in international waters west of Gogland island. The BBC and Al Jazeera do not give the same reference point, so the exact position is not settled in the reporting.

Finland's military said the strike was likely carried out by Ukraine. The vessel later drifted into Finnish waters, where it remains at anchor, according to Al Jazeera. Kyiv has yet to comment. Pictures and video released by the coastguard on Sunday showed several vessels spraying the ship with water overnight to put out the flames and cool containers stacked on its deck. Finland said Estonian and Russian assets were also taking part in the recovery work.

The crew has left the ship. Al Jazeera reported that 16 crew members were evacuated onto a nearby Russian ship. The BBC reported that the Finnish Border Guard said the crew had been transferred to another Russian vessel, which was already heading back to Russia. Kaliningrad's governor, Alexei Besprozvannykh, said on the basis of preliminary information that two drones had been used, and that there was no threat to the territory's supplies.

The Finnish Border Guard said no environmental damage had been detected and that there was no danger to maritime traffic or people. Finland's defence ministry also said a Russian helicopter was suspected of violating the country's airspace, possibly for rescue or tracking operations. Defence Minister Antti Häkkänen said the Border Guard is leading the investigation and is working with other relevant authorities.

Prime Minister Petteri Orpo linked the incident to the war. "War is spilling over into our immediate vicinity," he said on X, adding that Ukraine "has the right to fight back against the aggressor".

The ship has been under sanctions. According to the BBC, the United States sanctioned it in 2022 and Ukraine earlier this year, and the vessel is run by a Russian company. Al Jazeera, citing data from the London Stock Exchange, reported that the 121-metre, Russian-flagged ship is owned by Oboronlogistika, which provides logistics services for Russia's Ministry of Defence according to its website. The US Department of the Treasury has also sanctioned the vessel, according to Al Jazeera. The ship left St Petersburg on Friday.

The Baltic Sea has become a flashpoint in the war that Russia began with its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, and Ukraine has recently stepped up long-range attacks on Russian territory. The BBC reported that a Ukrainian drone strike hit a Russian warship in the Gulf of Finland earlier this month. Al Jazeera reported that Finland's military said last week it had detected a strike on a Russian warship in the same area. Estonian Navy Commander Ivo Vark told the public broadcaster ERR on Sunday that the risk of further attacks in the Gulf of Finland cannot be ruled out.
