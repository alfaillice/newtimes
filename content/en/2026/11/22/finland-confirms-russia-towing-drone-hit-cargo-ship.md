A Russian cargo ship hit by drones in the eastern Gulf of Finland is being towed away from Finnish waters, Finnish authorities have confirmed. The vessel, the Sparta II, drifted into Finnish territory after the attack on Saturday 21 November left it on fire.

The ship was travelling from St Petersburg towards Kaliningrad, Russia's Baltic exclave that lies between Poland and Lithuania. Kaliningrad is separated from the rest of Russia by sea, which makes shipping an important supply link for the territory.

Finland's Border Guard was alerted to the attack at about 08:50 local time (06:50 GMT) on Saturday. The vessel was located roughly 50 kilometres west of Suursaari, a Finnish island in the Gulf of Finland. The fire spread aboard the ship, and the crew were evacuated. The Border Guard said one crew member had suffered minor injuries, according to a news conference held on Sunday. The crew were moved to another Russian vessel heading back to Russia.

By Sunday, the immediate danger appeared to have been contained. Russian rescue vessels had begun towing the damaged ship out of Finnish waters, after an emergency response involving maritime authorities from Finland, Estonia and Russia. Finland's Border Guard is responsible for the investigation into the attack and is working with other relevant authorities, the country's defence minister said.

Finnish military officials assessed that Ukraine was likely responsible for the drone strike. Ukrainian authorities had not publicly confirmed responsibility, and Kyiv has yet to comment. The identity of the attacker and the circumstances of the strike have not yet been independently established.

The Sparta II is subject to US sanctions, which were imposed in 2022. It is operated by a Russian company, which says it was established within the structure of Russia's defence ministry in 2011 and has operated within the military construction complex since 2015.

The Baltic Sea has become a flashpoint in the war in Ukraine, which Russia began with a full-scale invasion in 2022. Ukraine has recently stepped up long-range attacks against Russia beyond the front lines. Analysts and officials have raised wider concerns about the security of Russian shipping in the Baltic and the risk of further escalation close to NATO territory, although the cargo carried by the ship has not been publicly confirmed.
