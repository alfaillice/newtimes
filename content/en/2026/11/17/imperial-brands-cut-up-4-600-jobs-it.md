The chief executive, Lukas Paravicini, described the cuts as part of a "transformation" of the company, with at least £320m of annual savings targeted by 2030. He said the group wanted to become "more agile, more data-led, more consumer-facing and more efficient". He also said the company was "fusing the organisation with more technologies", including new software and AI.

The company also faces tax increases in Germany and, from next June, a ban on advertising vaping and nicotine products in the UK. Paravicini said Imperial had already dealt with regulation in other countries and that sales of Blu vapes were growing at double-digit rates in the UK. He said the company supported regulation on vapes because it would bring more clarity to the market.
