According to The Guardian, Imperial Brands, the Bristol-based owner of JPS cigarettes, Golden Virginia tobacco, Blu vapes and Rizla papers, is cutting up to 4,600 jobs. The cuts form part of a large cost-saving drive in which the company is relying more on artificial intelligence (AI) and other technologies. The newspaper reports that 2,100 roles will go globally this year, and that the group plans a further 2,100 to 2,500 reductions by the end of 2030. Imperial has a global workforce of about 25,000 people.

The Guardian reports that the company will close its factory in Langenhagen, Germany, next year. It has also sold a plant in Taiwan. Together, these moves lead to 700 job losses. The company intends to outsource 400 roles from its finance and procurement teams in Krakow, Poland, and from its global supply chain centre in Warsaw, to a provider the newspaper names as Capgemini. Consultations on the planned reductions are under way in several countries. The current round affects a small number of jobs in the UK.

The chief executive, Lukas Paravicini, described the cuts as part of a "transformation" of the company, with at least £320m of annual savings targeted by 2030. He said the group wanted to become "more agile, more data-led, more consumer-facing and more efficient". He also said the company was "fusing the organisation with more technologies", including new software and AI.

The restructuring comes as demand for cigarettes falls. Imperial, which also owns the John Player Special and Rizla brands, has been shifting towards newer products such as vapes, heated tobacco and oral nicotine. The Guardian reports that its total revenues fell 1.5% to £31.7bn in the year to 30 September, and that profit before tax fell 12.1% to £2.7bn. Tobacco net revenues rose 1.7% in key markets, as the company raised prices by 5.1%, which offset a 3.4% fall in volumes.

The company also faces tax increases in Germany and, from next June, a ban on advertising vaping and nicotine products in the UK. Paravicini said Imperial had already dealt with regulation in other countries and that sales of Blu vapes were growing at double-digit rates in the UK. He said the company supported regulation on vapes because it would bring more clarity to the market.

Imperial is not alone in restructuring. The Guardian reports that British American Tobacco, one of the largest tobacco groups in the world, said earlier this year that it would cut 5,500 jobs by the end of the year and outsource a further 3,500. The two measures affect 9,000 employees in total, out of a workforce of about 47,000.

This account is based on a single source, The Guardian, and the figures are those it reports. Imperial's own published statement on the restructuring was not among the sources used for this story.
