A truck driver employed by Toll Transport was fired after using smartglasses to take photographs at a liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) terminal in Victoria, according to The Guardian. The newspaper reports it as the first case in Australia of a worker being dismissed for using the devices at work. Its account is based on a ruling by the federal circuit court, which heard the case.

The driver, Ricardo Martins Silva, had worked for the company for about eight months as an LPG tanker driver. He was delivering fuel for Origin Energy from the Long Island Point terminal in Hastings, Victoria. According to the court judgment as reported by The Guardian, on 17 June 2025 he told superiors that the truck allocated to him vibrated too much and was jumping excessively. He was advised to take a different truck. At the terminal he took photographs with his smartglasses and sent them to a superior, with a message saying he was returning the truck to Dandenong and that there was no more time for deliveries that day.

An Esso worker at the terminal told Toll that any use of electrical equipment on site is “intrinsically unsafe”, because of the risk that an electrical spark could cause an explosion. Mr Martins Silva was first stood down on 22 June. At a meeting on 24 June, the company raised what it called “the glasses incident” and described it as a critical safety breach. By 30 June, Toll had decided to suspend him, and on 9 July he was told that taking photographs with the glasses breached the company’s code of practice and his contract. His employment ended on 18 July. Origin Energy had also asked for him to be removed from work, saying his attitude was poor.

Mr Martins Silva took the case under the Fair Work Act, arguing that he was dismissed because of complaints he had made, including about safety and the effect of working conditions on his health. The judge, Karl Blake, found that he was not dismissed for complaining but “because he wore smartglasses” at the terminal, which the employer regarded as a serious safety incident. The application was dismissed. Blake said the incident was “on any objective view a serious incident”, adding: “The glasses incident was not concocted or made to look more serious than what it was.”

The Guardian reports that Mr Martins Silva accepted under cross-examination that he knew at the time he had done the wrong thing. In writing to his employer, he said his judgment had been affected by unresolved trauma from a truck fire in March 2025, in which he had been injured.

The case centres on safety, but The Guardian places it within a wider debate. Since Meta’s Ray-Ban glasses and cheaper alternatives such as the Kmart Anko brand became available, governments have faced pressure to ban the devices over privacy concerns. Councils across Australia have barred them from some public spaces, including gyms and pools, and the federal government is considering bans in government offices.
