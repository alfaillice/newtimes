The Spanish government says that about 5,000 adults remain in temporary facilities set up by the state in Ceuta, the Spanish autonomous city on the North African coast, following a mass entry of migrants in July. Elma Saiz, the government spokesperson and minister of Inclusion, Social Security and Migration, gave the figures at a press conference after the Council of Ministers, the weekly meeting of Spain's cabinet, on Tuesday.

Saiz said the temporary infrastructure that had been set up at the city's port has been dismantled. She said that "little by little, normality is being restored" in the city. The phrase is a translation from the Spanish original.

According to Saiz, 16,475 returns, devoluciones and expulsions have been recorded since 30 July. Of these, 15,692 were voluntary returns to Morocco. Devoluciones is the Spanish term for formal returns of people who are not entitled to stay.

The government also gave figures for unaccompanied minors. Ceuta's child protection system is caring for 3,767 minors. Some 361 have been relocated to mainland Spain: 156 through family reunification arrangements and 205 under a mechanism set out in the recent reform of Spain's Immigration Law, the Ley de Extranjería. Saiz also said that 189 repatriation case files for minors have been sent to Morocco.

Finally, Saiz announced a package of aid to help Ceuta's businesses expand abroad and to strengthen its productive base, according to the same report.

The figures come from the government's own statements. The source does not give an independent count of the people in the facilities, nor does it say when the temporary shelters will close. It also does not report reactions from opposition parties to the government's account.
