Spain will provide €45m this year to three international climate funds, Sara Aagesen, Spain's deputy prime minister and minister for the ecological transition, announced on Tuesday at the COP31 UN climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, according to Europa Press.

The money is split between the Adaptation Fund, which finances projects in developing countries that help vulnerable communities adapt to climate change; the Loss and Damage Fund, which pays for responses to climate-related losses and damages; and the systematic observations financing facility (SOFF) of the World Meteorological Organization (WMO), which helps countries fill gaps in basic weather and climate data. The first two form part of the international climate finance architecture linked to the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change. According to Europa Press, this year's breakdown is €25m for adaptation, €15m for loss and damage and €5m for the SOFF.

Citing figures from sources at Spain's Ecological Transition Ministry, Europa Press reports that Spain is one of the main donors to the Adaptation Fund, having contributed €55m between 2022 and 2025, to which the €25m announced this year will be added. Spain has contributed €55m to the Loss and Damage Fund, which began operating in December 2023, including the €15m announced now. For the SOFF, Spain has given €8m since 2022, plus the €5m announced this year.

Spain's contribution is not new in total. Last year, Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez announced at COP30 in Belém, Brazil, a package of the same €45m, with €20m each for the Adaptation Fund and the Loss and Damage Fund and €5m for the WMO's observations. The split for this year differs.

Aagesen also called for clear roadmaps to phase out fossil fuels and for global electrification of at least 35% by 2035 through renewable energy. She said Spain would keep backing resilience and early-warning systems. She asked for faster mobilisation of climate finance on the basis of existing agreements and for people and nature to be placed at the centre through a just and inclusive transition. In Spanish, she said: "No hay justicia social sin justicia climática ni justicia climática sin justicia social", which translates as: there is no social justice without climate justice, nor climate justice without social justice.

She also asked that the reports of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC), which are meant to inform the 2028 global stocktake, be available in time to guide policy. Bloomberg, cited by Europa Press, reports that negotiators are in dispute over the publication timetable for three reports and are seeking a solution before an IPCC meeting in December. That meeting must also agree the organisation's budget, without which, according to Bloomberg, the IPCC would have to halt its work.

Aagesen thanked UN Secretary-General António Guterres for his "leadership and tireless commitment". Europa Press notes that this is Guterres's last climate summit as secretary-general. Last week, the coalition 'Polluters Pay' gave him an award for calling for an end to fossil fuels and for demanding accountability from the industry for the climate crisis.
