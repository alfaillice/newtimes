European Union foreign ministers have agreed to revive a cooperation deal with Syria that was first drawn up almost 20 years ago, Al Jazeera reports. The move is part of the 27-nation bloc's effort to strengthen ties with the country after its long civil war.

The EU-Syria association agreement was first made in 2008, under then-President Bashar al-Assad. According to Al Jazeera, neither side ever fully signed it, because of concerns over the Assad government's human rights record. The Assad family's 53-year rule ended on 8 December 2024, after an 11-day offensive that caught Syria and the world off guard. The Syrian civil war that preceded it left lasting scars on the country, and about 6.8 million Syrians, roughly one-third of the population, fled abroad, Al Jazeera says.

At a meeting in Brussels on Monday, EU foreign ministers "supported moving ahead" with the 2008 agreement and agreed to launch talks on an updated one, EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said, as quoted by Al Jazeera. A discussion paper prepared by the bloc's diplomatic service beforehand set out what Al Jazeera describes as one of Brussels' most ambitious efforts yet to normalise ties with Damascus, less than two years after Assad's fall.

Kallas said diplomats would now work through the technical and legal details. The agreement would create a framework for free trade, political dialogue and wider cooperation. She argued that Syria needs investment and a successful transition, and that if Europe did not engage, other players would fill the vacuum. Al Jazeera quotes her saying that the EU should set "clear conditionality regarding human rights" for an updated deal. She also called for non-recognition of occupied Ukrainian and Georgian territories that, Al Jazeera reports, Syria recognised under Assad's government.

The agreement is the latest step in the EU's engagement with Damascus and its new leader, President Ahmed al-Sharaa. According to Al Jazeera, the EU removed its economic sanctions on Syria in 2025 to help its recovery, and at the start of 2026 Brussels announced a two-year funding package worth €620m ($680m). More than one million Syrian refugees have returned home since Assad's fall, Al Jazeera says, while millions more in the diaspora are weighing whether to do the same.
