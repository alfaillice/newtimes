Venâncio Mondlane went on trial on Tuesday at the Supreme Court in Maputo, Mozambique's capital, over protests that followed the disputed presidential election of October 2024. Police were deployed heavily around the court and in central streets, as authorities feared a fresh wave of demonstrations by his supporters, the BBC reports.

Mondlane came second in the national vote, according to Al Jazeera, which says President Daniel Chapo of the ruling Frelimo party won. The BBC describes Chapo as the candidate the results declared the winner. Al Jazeera says Frelimo, which has held power for more than 50 years, denies any wrongdoing. Mondlane challenged the result, accused the authorities of widespread electoral fraud and called for nationwide protests and work stoppages, the BBC says.

Prosecutors accuse him of encouraging demonstrations that disrupted public services, damaged property and threatened public security. The BBC lists charges including publicly glorifying criminal acts, incitement to collective disobedience and public instigation to commit crimes. Al Jazeera counts five charges in total, the most serious being instigating 'terrorism'. Both outlets say he could be jailed for up to 24 years if convicted of the most serious charge. Mondlane rejects the accusations and says his calls for protest were aimed at demanding electoral transparency, not at encouraging violence.

The death toll from the unrest is disputed. Amnesty International, citing local civil society organisations, says more than 300 people were killed. Mozambican police put the figure at 96, including 17 police officers. The reports do not say when either count was compiled. Human Rights Watch said after the vote that it had been marred by political killings, irregularities and restrictions on freedom of expression and assembly, according to Al Jazeera. EU election observers also reported irregularities in the vote, the BBC says. Amnesty accused security forces of unlawful use of force and mass arbitrary arrests. Al Jazeera also reports that Mondlane's lawyer and an aide were shot dead while he was preparing to challenge the result. The reports give no further details of those killings.

The opposition party he leads, the National Alliance for a Free and Autonomous Mozambique (Anamola), has described the prosecution as politically motivated. The Supreme Court has denied any political interference in the proceedings. The trial will be heard by five judges, with witnesses called by both the prosecution and the defence. The court has admitted only selected media organisations, a decision that journalists and press freedom groups have criticised. Court officials cite limited seating in the courtroom, while critics say the restriction undermines transparency in a case of major public interest.

Outside the court, Mondlane told reporters: "I arrive calm and confident in my legal team." Mozambican police have appealed to citizens to follow the proceedings calmly and to avoid actions that could threaten public order. Political observers warn that the trial could reignite tensions in a country still recovering from the violence and political divisions that followed the 2024 election.
