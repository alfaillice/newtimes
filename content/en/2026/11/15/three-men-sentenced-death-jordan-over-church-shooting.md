A Jordanian court has sentenced three men to death by hanging over a shooting at a church in the south of the country that killed nine people last month. The Jordanian military announced the verdict in a statement on Sunday.

The attack took place on 23 October at St George's Greek Orthodox church in the village of Hmoud, in Karak governorate, south of the capital, Amman. Those killed and wounded had been attending a Christian funeral. Jordan is a Muslim-majority kingdom, but Karak has a sizeable Christian community. Reports put the country's Christian minority at about 300,000 of its 11.5 million people. The village is also spelled Hammoud in some reports.

The number of injured is disputed. One account reports 34 people wounded, while another reports 25. Neither gives a source for its figure in the material available.

According to reports, the attackers fled after the shooting, which was captured on CCTV showing three armed men firing on the church. Authorities said the suspects were the two Jordanian brothers and their brother-in-law. The three were later found in an abandoned house in Karak governorate. They refused to surrender and opened fire on security forces, who returned fire and wounded all three. The Public Security Directorate said no security personnel were hurt. Reports say the men have not been publicly identified, and it is still unclear why the church was targeted.

The military statement said the State Security Court found the men guilty of committing terrorist attacks resulting in death, as well as weapons offences, conspiracy to commit terrorist acts and promoting the ideas of a terrorist organisation. As quoted in reporting, the statement said that one of the men was also convicted of providing funds to finance terrorist acts.

King Abdullah II wrote on X shortly after the attack: "The terrorist attack against Karak will be met with the harshest retaliation." According to reporting, he offered condolences to the families of the victims and ordered a review of the country's security procedures for responding to attacks.

A week after the shooting, Jordan struck what its military described as terrorist organisations in Syria. Syrian health officials told state media that five people, including women, children and an elderly man, were killed in a strike on a village in the southern Daraa province. Jordan said it had co-ordinated with the Syrian government, but Syria said it had not been notified in advance.

Executions in Jordan are carried out by hanging. The kingdom observed an unofficial ban on executions from 2006 to 2014, and another pause from 2017 until June 2026, according to reports. The reports do not say whether the sentences can be appealed.
