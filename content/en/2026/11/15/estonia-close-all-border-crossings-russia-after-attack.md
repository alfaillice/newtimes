The Estonian prime minister, Kristen Michal, said the closure was needed to reduce the risk of further attacks and to make clear that attacks on the country's defence industry and capabilities were unacceptable. He linked the attacks to Estonia's continued support for Ukraine. Michal argued that Russia wanted to frighten Estonia and weaken that support, and said Estonia would respond by strengthening its security, working more closely with businesses and allies, and increasing pressure on Russia.

Estonia has three crossings with Russia: Narva in the north, and Koidula and Luhamaa in the south. The authorities did not say how long the closure would last.

## Earlier attack

The weekend incident is the second attempted attack on an Estonian defence firm that the authorities have linked to Russia. In August, an arson attack hit Milrem Robotics. In September, Kapo said it was confident that fire was a deliberate, pre-planned attack carried out on behalf of Russian special services. The agency said it fitted a wider pattern of Russian attacks on defence and drone companies, particularly those with links to Ukraine.

Estonia summoned Russia's chargé d'affaires over that attack. The Kremlin denied any link and called the accusation baseless, saying it had no relation to reality.

## Warnings across Europe

Several European intelligence services have repeatedly warned of possible attacks on defence companies. They have also pointed to growing Russian attempts to recruit people in EU and Nato countries through online messaging platforms to carry out acts of sabotage.

Michal said the arrests came after security agencies warned the defence industry of potential risks. "We do not warn about these threats without reason," he said. "Preparedness matters, and this time it clearly paid off."
