Charlotte Church is closing The Dreaming, the wellness retreat she set up in mid-Wales, after five years of operation, the company behind it has announced, according to the BBC.

The retreat is based at Rhydoldog House, near the town of Rhayader in Powys, a county in central Wales. It offered stays designed to help guests reconnect with nature and spirituality. Church, the Welsh singer-songwriter, bought the house in 2021. It was formerly the home of fashion designer Laura Ashley, and its renovation later featured in a Discovery Channel documentary series, the BBC reports.

The retreat's team had said earlier that it would pause operations to reassess what it should offer. In a statement on social media, the team said it had hoped the pause would be a period of hibernation rather than an ending. It has now concluded that the retreat is over. "What a beautiful, wild and wonderful few years it has been," the post added, according to the BBC. The team thanked guests and said that thousands of people had visited the "little corner of the Welsh hills" in that time.

According to the BBC, the retreat hosted more than 3,000 guests and ran a "pay what you can" scheme starting at £1 per night, along with free stays for workers in the UK's National Health Service (NHS). Documents filed with Companies House, the UK's company register, showed five employees at The Dreaming in March 2025.

Earlier this year, Church told the BBC that the retreat was a response to the pressures of modern life. She said the world needed care and support during difficult times, and that the emphasis on capitalist values in society had left less room for care, community and belonging. "There are some very difficult transitional times for us as human beings on this Earth," she said.

Church has previously said she spent most of her savings on the house, which the BBC describes as a £1.5m, seven-bedroom property. The house dates back to the 15th century. It belonged to Laura Ashley and her husband, Bernard, and stayed in the family until his death in 2009. A 2021 planning application for the site said Church wanted to turn it into a carbon-neutral wellbeing and healing retreat, with four log cabins in the grounds offering tourist accommodation.

The BBC's report does not say when the retreat will formally close.
