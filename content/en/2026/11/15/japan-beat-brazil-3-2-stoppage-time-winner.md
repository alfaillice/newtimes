Japan beat Brazil 3-2 in a friendly in Singapore on Saturday, scoring the winner deep into stoppage time, according to Al Jazeera. The match was also marked by the dismissal of Brazil coach Carlo Ancelotti and forward Raphinha, both shown red cards for protesting a refereeing decision.

According to Al Jazeera, Japan went 2-0 up in the opening 19 minutes through Takefusa Kubo and Keito Nakamura. Vinicius Jr then scored twice to erase the deficit before Japan regained the lead. The late winner came from substitute Tatsuhiro Sakamoto, who was in the right place to score after Kodai Sano's deflected effort fell to him in stoppage time. It was his first international goal. The match was played at the National Stadium in front of 38,688 spectators, Al Jazeera reports.

Brazil appealed for a foul in the build-up to the goal. The video assistant referee (VAR) upheld the decision, and both Ancelotti and Raphinha were then shown red cards. Al Jazeera reports that Raphinha was led away by midfielder Marlon Gomes and team officials as he tried to approach referee Jansen Foo. Vinicius and Bruno Guimaraes confronted the official instead.

Ancelotti told a news conference that the referee had sent him off for failing to control the bench, and said the result had been influenced by the official's performance. He added that this was not the referee's fault and that he did not think the official had the experience to handle a match of that level. He also said that some players making their first appearances had shown good quality, and that his side had played well for 70 minutes.

Raphinha apologised for his behaviour and for being sent off, but said he had felt there were many refereeing errors. He said Japan's first goal followed a foul when an opponent took the ball off him with his hand, and that a penalty he believed was due in the first half had been denied. He also pointed to a clear foul on Joao Gomes before the third goal, which Al Jazeera says the referee allowed after consulting VAR.

The result carried extra weight for Japan, who lost to Brazil in the last 32 of the World Cup in June, when Gabriel Martinelli scored in stoppage time to give the South Americans a 2-1 win. Japan head coach Hajime Moriyasu, 58, called Saturday's game "the best match in my eight-year tenure", Al Jazeera reports.

Al Jazeera also reports that Moriyasu is expected to step down after a friendly against Paraguay on Tuesday. He said he was looking forward to handing over to his successor, Go Oiwa.
