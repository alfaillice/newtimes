Two weeks ago the Supreme Court also rejected a challenge from the prosecution service and the State Attorney's Office (Abogacía del Estado), which had called the fees of González Amador's lawyer excessive. The court confirmed that García Ortiz must pay €39,000 in costs.

The Ministry of the Presidency, Justice and Relations with the Courts began processing a possible pardon in January, after two private petitions. The Supreme Court has already reported against it, finding that the facts were serious and had seriously affected the standing of the prosecution service. A pardon is granted by the King on the proposal of the justice minister, after the Council of Ministers deliberates, and is formalised by royal decree in the Official State Gazette (BOE).

Justice Minister Félix Bolaños said last month that the file was complete and that he had it on his desk. On Friday he declined to discuss the merits, saying that “neither decision has been ruled out” (translated from Spanish) and that the government would announce the outcome and its reasons at a press conference once it was resolved.

In January the prosecution service, now led by Teresa Peramato, allowed García Ortiz to remain a prosecutor. It read the disqualification as applying only to the post of Attorney General, not to his wider career. He was assigned to the Social Chamber of the Supreme Court.
