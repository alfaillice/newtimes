Voters in Cape Verde began casting their ballots on Sunday in a presidential election in which the incumbent, Jose Maria Neves, faces Joana Rosa, the first woman to stand for the presidency since independence in 1975, according to Al Jazeera. Polling stations opened at 08.00 local time, which is 10.00 CET, and close at 18.00 local time (20.00 CET). The broadcaster reports that more than 300,000 people are eligible to vote and can choose from six presidential candidates.

Cape Verde is an archipelago off the coast of Senegal in West Africa and a former Portuguese colony. Al Jazeera describes it as a country known for its stability in a politically volatile region. Its system is hybrid, combining parliamentary and presidential elements: the president has veto authority and a mediating role.

Neves, 66, is a member of the African Party for the Independence of Cape Verde (PAICV), the country's oldest political party. He served as prime minister for 15 years and became president in 2021. According to Al Jazeera, he is tipped to be the favourite. His party won 37 of the 72 seats in the national parliament in May, and Neves then appointed Francisco Carvalho, previously mayor of the capital, Praia, as prime minister.

Rosa, a former justice minister, stands for the Movement for Democracy (MpD), which governed for ten years before the May vote. Al Jazeera presents her as Neves's strongest challenger. In her campaign, she called for a government that allows young people "to build their futures here at home and prevent the continued exodus of young Cape Verdeans abroad".

Emigration has long been a problem because of a lack of jobs. Al Jazeera reports that the diaspora is larger than the roughly 500,000 people living on the islands, and that most emigrants live in Portugal and the United States. Nearly 77,000 Cape Verdeans abroad are registered to vote at embassies. Neves has promised to organise a youth summit on emigration if he wins a second term.

Al Jazeera also cites Roselma Evora, a professor of political science and gender studies at the University of Cabo Verde, who told the Associated Press that many voters feel distant from the political class because they believe it does not understand their daily difficulties.

A candidate must win more than 50 per cent of the vote to be elected in the first round. If no one does, the top two candidates will face a run-off on 29 November, according to Al Jazeera.
