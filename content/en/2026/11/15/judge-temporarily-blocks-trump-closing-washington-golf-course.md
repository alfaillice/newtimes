A US federal judge has temporarily blocked the government from closing East Potomac Golf Links, a historic golf course and park in Washington DC, the BBC reported. The closure had been due to begin on 15 November, and US President Donald Trump had announced it as part of a plan to renovate the course.

The ruling was issued by US District Judge Ana Reyes, who has been handling a legal challenge that began in February, when the DC Preservation League and others sued over redevelopment plans for the park. The course sits about 3km south of the White House and is one of three public courses run by the National Park Service in the capital. The BBC noted that the course is 106 years old.

In October, Trump announced that the current course, which he called "Trash Island", would be renovated over 15 months. He said the new facility would be open to the public and built to a high standard for golf tournaments. In a post on Truth Social, he described the existing course as "old, broken, dilapidated" and said its trees and seawall were in poor condition. The BBC said it had contacted the White House for comment.

In a 30-page decision issued on Saturday, Reyes said the administration could not close the park or the course, but that it was not required to manage either of them actively. According to the BBC, she found that the justifications given for the closures were pretextual and that the government had violated at least two federal laws in order to meet what she called "an arbitrary deadline". She also wrote that closing a park used daily by many Washingtonians, indefinitely, ran counter to the public interest, and that the government had not shown it would suffer harm from a temporary restraining order that keeps the situation as it is while the case continues.

The case escalated in October, when the National Park Service began cutting down hundreds of trees. The BBC reported that the court had not been notified until 15 hours before the work was due to start. Reyes then ordered a halt to tree removal until at least 26 October and asked for three business days' notice of any other permanent changes.

Reyes has also opened a separate inquiry into whether Justice Department attorneys made misrepresentations to the court or otherwise breached ethical rules. The BBC said the inquiry will consider whether the late notice was given in bad faith and whether the lawyers corrected earlier false statements or withheld information. Reyes said she will carry out the inquiry with a special master, an independent third party. The BBC's report did not say when the inquiry is expected to conclude.
