Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company that the BBC describes as the biggest buyer of Nvidia chips in south-east Asia, says Alice Huang, a suspected Chinese spy linked to Nvidia's former boss Jensen Huang, left her executive role at the firm in August 2025. The company's statement, reported by the BBC on Sunday, comes amid a wider controversy over whether high-end US AI chips reached China despite export restrictions.

According to the BBC, Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive of Nvidia, the US maker of AI chips, after reports emerged that he had allegedly had affairs with Alice Huang and with one of his employees. The BBC reports that China has denied Alice Huang is a spy. Beijing has also warned the United States not to use the scandal as a pretext for a technology blockade on AI.

Megaspeed said it had started an internal investigation into its business. The BBC quotes the company as saying it was aware of reports “containing assertions about our business, supply chain and global operations that are not supported by verifiable evidence”. It said it conducts legitimate and lawful business and had hired outside legal counsel for the inquiry. The company also said its operations are designed to comply with US export control requirements and local rules in every market where it operates.

The BBC reports that Megaspeed is suspected of giving Chinese companies backdoor access to Nvidia chips through its data centres in Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand. Nvidia is banned from selling its advanced chips to China. Singapore does not prohibit the transfer of advanced AI chips to companies abroad, but such transfers can breach US export controls. Singapore has said that, although it does not enforce unilateral export controls imposed by other countries, it expects companies under its jurisdiction to respect them.

On Friday, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, according to the BBC, blamed the commerce department, led by Howard Lutnick, for failing to oversee the export of high-end chips properly. Bessent told CNBC that several agencies had long wanted to place Megaspeed on the so-called entity list, which restricts US trade with overseas firms and individuals, but that the Commerce Department had the final say. Lutnick is due to testify before the Senate Banking Committee next month about his department's role in allowing Nvidia to export its advanced chips. Other congressional committees have asked Lutnick's department and him to keep all records relating to Nvidia and Jensen Huang.

This account rests on a single BBC report. The claims about Megaspeed's business and its internal investigation have not been independently verified, and the company has not yet published findings.
