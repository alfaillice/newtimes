A fifth Canadian soldier has died from injuries sustained in a grass fire at a military base in Alberta, according to the BBC, which cited a statement from the Canadian Armed Forces. Private William Douglas Dal Bianco had been receiving medical care in Ontario when he died on Friday.

The fire broke out on 17 October at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright, a military training area in Alberta, during a live-fire exercise carried out by members of 1 Canadian Mechanized Brigade Group. The BBC reported that strong winds carried the flames rapidly across the site. Military officials told the broadcaster at the time that the fire spread across a kilometre in a matter of seconds, catching many people on the base by surprise.

Four people were killed in the initial incident and dozens were injured. The Canadian Armed Forces said 38 military personnel had been hurt in total. As of Saturday, 24 were still in hospital and 14 had been discharged, the BBC reported. Firefighters fought the blaze through the night, and nearby hospitals were overwhelmed by injured personnel and staff.

An investigation found the fire was likely started by a grenade launcher fired on the base's grenade range, officials said, according to the BBC. Firefighters on the base were aware of the fire but could not tackle it, because the area was a danger zone that could contain unspent explosives.

Prime Minister Mark Carney, the head of Canada's government, described the incident as "one of the worst tragedies for the forces on Canadian soil in peacetime", the BBC reported. Canada's Chief of the Defence Staff, General Jennie Carignan, said the armed forces were "heartbroken" by Dal Bianco's death. Defence Minister David McGuinty offered his condolences to the soldier's family, friends and colleagues, and said the government remained committed to supporting families and the armed forces community.

The BBC's report does not say whether further deaths or charges are expected, and it does not give details of the investigation's findings beyond the likely cause.
