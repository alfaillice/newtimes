Hundreds of men marched in Bern, Switzerland’s capital, on Saturday to denounce misogyny and violence against women, according to Al Jazeera. The rally, titled “Men Against Violence Against Women”, took place outside parliament and was co-organised by a group called maenner. Its organisers argued that men need to play a more effective role in fighting both problems.

Al Jazeera reports that the march responded to persistent concern that misogyny is spreading online among teenage boys and young men, and that other men need to build a stronger counter-narrative. The outlet links these views to the “manosphere”, a loose network of online forums and influencers that promote male supremacy and hostility towards feminism. According to Al Jazeera, some of these influencers grew bolder after Donald Trump won the US presidential election, using the moment to amplify misogynistic mockery and threats online.

Christoph Gosteli, a 42-year-old social worker who co-organised the rally, told Al Jazeera that men are part of the problem when it comes to violence against women and queer people. He said participants wanted to take a stand: “We no longer want to be part of the problem; we want to be part of the solution.”

Al Jazeera also cites a survey released in June by the University of Zurich and MenCare, an umbrella group of Swiss men’s and fathers’ organisations. According to the outlet, the study found that half of male respondents aged 18 to 24 held a restrictive, dominance-based view of masculinity, and some expressed concern that real men are increasingly marginalised in society. The researchers called for a society-wide debate about ideals of masculinity that promote violence, starting in schools.

The outlet adds that Switzerland’s justice minister, Beat Jans, spoke about the issue at a conference earlier this month on the dangers of violent online abuse of women. In a speech on 2 November, he said men commit four-fifths of violent crimes and that in rape cases the share is as high as 99 per cent. “It is almost always men,” Jans said, according to Al Jazeera, adding that violence by men begins with disparaging remarks and that men all have a duty to act.

Al Jazeera’s account is the only source for these details in this story, so the figures and quotations should be read as the outlet’s reporting.
