Aberdeen have appointed Mario Frick as their head coach, the club announced, according to BBC Sport. The 52-year-old replaces Stephen Robinson, who was dismissed on 9 November with the club second from bottom of the Scottish Premiership, the country's top football division. Frick is the club's fourth manager of 2026, after Robinson, Peter Leven and Jimmy Thelin.

Frick won 125 caps for Liechtenstein as a striker and scored 16 international goals, which BBC Sport says make him the country's all-time leading scorer. His clubs as a player included FC Basel, FC Zurich, St. Gallen and Grasshopper. As a coach he got Vaduz promoted in 2020 and then moved to Luzern in Switzerland in 2021, where he helped the side avoid relegation. The following season Luzern finished fourth and qualified for Europe, although they were knocked out of Conference League qualifying by Lee Johnson's Hibernian. He left Luzern in the summer after five years.

According to BBC Sport, Pittodrie chief executive Adrian Bevington praised the candidate's "clear vision, strong leadership qualities" and said the board was unanimous in its choice. Sporting director Lutz Pfannenstiel described Frick as having "a winning mentality" and said he was the right person for the next stage of the club. The club also said Frick has handed top-flight debuts to more than 30 academy players during his career. Hibernian striker Thibault Klidje and Aberdeen goalkeeper Marius Muller both played under him.

Frick will be assisted by Roman Matter. He is the seventh permanent manager since Dave Cormack took control of the club in November 2019. Aberdeen have three wins from 12 league matches this season, and BBC Sport reports that he will take charge for the first time against Kilmarnock, who are bottom of the table, on Saturday. The club did not disclose the terms of his contract.

The appointment was reported by BBC Sport alone at the time of writing, and the details above come from that report.
