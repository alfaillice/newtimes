Australia won the Women's Rugby League World Cup for a record fourth time and a fourth successive title, beating New Zealand 34-10 in Brisbane, BBC Sport reported. The contest was largely decided in the first half, when the Jillaroos, as Australia's women's team is known, scored six tries to New Zealand's one in the opening 35 minutes.

According to BBC Sport, New Zealand, known as the Kiwi Ferns, gave away possession and penalties early on, and sluggish defending added to their problems. Australia used the field position to score their first three tries. Jaime Chapman opened the scoring after just 125 seconds, and BBC Sport says she went on to complete a hat-trick, including a 60-metre run to finish one of her tries. The heat was intense enough that players had a drinks break in each half and the interval was extended.

By the break, Australia were on course to threaten New Zealand's 58-0 winning margin from the 2003 final, the broadcaster noted. The second half was far more even. New Zealand defended better, and Mackenzie Wiki scored a consolation try in the 70th minute. BBC Sport added that New Zealand kept their record of not conceding after the break at this tournament.

The result extends Australia's dominance. BBC Sport reports that the team are now unbeaten in 16 World Cup matches, a run stretching back to July 2013. Across the tournament they scored 306 points and conceded 30. The only real scare came in the semi-final against Fiji, who became the first side in 13 years to open the scoring against Australia in a World Cup game.

Head coach Jess Skinner chose Jesse Southwell ahead of Jocelyn Kelleher at stand-off, and Southwell kicked five of her six conversions, BBC Sport said. Chapman, 24, who has had a series of injuries, including a spinal fracture in February 2025, was named player of the match.

"She's so young - it's quite scary," said Australia captain Isabelle Kelly, according to BBC Sport.

New Zealand head coach Ricky Henry said his side had come with high hopes. "It was a game of two halves and that's the frustrating part for us," he said. He added that his team could not regroup after a poor start.

BBC Sport's report described the final as a clear demonstration of the strengths of rugby league. Chapman's three tries showed power, athleticism and pace, and the outcome was not in doubt for most of the match.
