According to The Guardian, Gymshark, the British sportswear company that began in a family garage, is in a trademark dispute that could decide the future of a small fitness business. The paper values the firm at £1bn and estimates that its founder, Ben Francis, is worth £800m. Francis, who is 34, started sewing his own gym clothes in his parents' garage after dropping out of university.

The dispute centres on The Conditioning Club, a name Gymshark is trying to add to its trademarks. The Guardian reports that the same name belongs to a gym and fitness business on an industrial estate in Fairford, Gloucestershire. Its founder, John Watson, registered the name with the UK Intellectual Property Office (UKIPO), the government body that handles trademarks in the United Kingdom, five years ago, covering gym and fitness services. According to the paper, the business has nine staff, three self-employed personal trainers and about 500 members.

Last March, Gymshark applied to register “Conditioning Club” and “The Conditioning Club” for several classes of goods and services, including clothing, retail and entertainment. The Guardian notes that Gymshark did not apply for fitness and training services, but that one of its applications falls in the same class as Watson's registration. Watson had been selling branded merchandise for several years. He opposed the Gymshark application and also applied to extend his own trademark to cover classes such as retail, which he had not thought to register when he set up the business.

Gymshark responded by asking the UKIPO to invalidate Watson's original trademark. It argues that the name is descriptive, too generic and not distinctive enough to be protected. The Guardian points out that Gymshark itself holds several trademark registrations for the similarly generic “Lifting Club” brand. Watson is now waiting for the UKIPO's decision.

Watson told The Guardian that his legal costs are approaching £30,000 and that he has launched a crowdfunding campaign on CrowdJustice, which has raised just over £5,000. “They can afford a prolonged trademark battle,” he said. He added: “I'm happy to agree a sensible arrangement with them.” The paper says the dispute has already lasted almost 18 months, which it describes as at odds with the brand image Gymshark has built.

The Guardian also reports that earlier this week Gymshark donated £10,000 to the owner of a gym in Redditch that burned down, and almost £9,000 to a British long jumper trying to reach the Olympics. The paper gives Gymshark's valuation as £1bn in its introduction, while noting a £1.25bn valuation when the US private equity firm General Atlantic took a stake in 2020. The extract of the report available to this newsroom does not include a statement from Gymshark on the dispute.
