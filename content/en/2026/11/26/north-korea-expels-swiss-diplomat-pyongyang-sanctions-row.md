North Korea has ordered the head of Switzerland's diplomatic office in Pyongyang to leave the country within 48 hours, in a dispute over sanctions that Bern imposed on a children's camp.

North Korea's foreign ministry declared Sylvain Astier persona non grata, a diplomatic term meaning that his presence is no longer accepted, and urged Switzerland to close the office. In a statement carried by state media on Wednesday, the ministry said Astier had been summoned and handed a memorandum to that effect.

The row centres on the Songdowon International Children's Camp in Wonsan, a city on North Korea's east coast. The camp is attended by Russian and Chinese children, among others. On Tuesday, Switzerland added it to its sanctions list over its alleged role in transferring children from Russian-occupied areas of Ukraine. Swiss authorities said the facility exposes children to propaganda and to activities designed to promote pro-Russian narratives. The United Kingdom in May and the European Union in September had imposed similar sanctions, freezing the camp's assets and banning financial transactions with it.

Kim Yo Jong, sister of leader Kim Jong Un and one of Pyongyang's senior foreign policy officials, issued a statement on Tuesday describing the sanctions as an "open hostile act" and an infringement of North Korean sovereignty. She said the camp supported legitimate cooperation between North Korea and Russia, but did not directly address Western accusations that some Ukrainian children abducted by Russian forces were sent there to be re-educated. State media later quoted the foreign ministry calling the sanctions "a blatant hostile act".

Switzerland said it regretted the decision. Its foreign ministry said relations with Pyongyang would continue through its embassy in Beijing and through North Korea's embassy in Bern, and that it was arranging for Astier to return to the Beijing embassy. It said the closure of the office did not call into question diplomatic relations between the two countries. Switzerland's foreign affairs department also said the country distinguishes between its sanctions policy and its commitment to dialogue with all countries, and that reopening the Pyongyang office had not affected its implementation of UN sanctions linked to North Korea's nuclear programme or of EU sanctions over Russia's war against Ukraine.

The expulsion comes weeks after Ignazio Cassis, Switzerland's foreign minister, made a rare visit to Pyongyang last month. There he officially reopened the office and held talks with his counterpart, Choe Son Hui, on promoting dialogue and cooperation. Switzerland is one of the few Western countries with diplomatic relations with North Korea, which were first established in 1974. Its cooperation office, opened in 1997 to run humanitarian programmes, closed in 2020 when Pyongyang shut its borders during the COVID-19 pandemic and remained shut for six years.

The dispute also takes place against the backdrop of North Korea's support for Russia's war in Ukraine. Thousands of North Korean soldiers have been deployed to help Moscow, and in August Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelensky accused Russia of using North Korean missiles. Neither source reports a response from Pyongyang beyond the 48-hour deadline, nor any further step by Bern beyond the arrangements it has announced.
