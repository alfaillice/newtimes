Two Italian technicians working for Rheinmetall, the German arms manufacturer, have died after an Iranian drone attack on a site in Kuwait, Italy's foreign ministry said. Kuwait's army confirmed the deaths and described the attack on a "vital site in the country" as "heinous Iranian aggression".

Kuwait's defence ministry earlier said that a number of drones from Iran had been detected and intercepted, and that falling shrapnel had injured four people. Those four included the two Italians, a Kuwaiti national and a Pakistani national, according to the ministry's earlier account. The ministry did not give details of the damage beyond describing it as material. The Italian foreign ministry said in a statement that an explosion caused by a drone attack had occurred at a site where two Italian technicians from Rheinmetall Italia were working. Reports based on the ministry's account said the men were working at an air defence detection station and maintaining systems used by Kuwait's armed forces.

Italy's Prime Minister, Giorgia Meloni, expressed her condolences to the families of the victims on X. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, the head of the EU's executive, called for more pressure on Iran to end what she described as unacceptable attacks against its neighbours and partners in the Gulf. She added: "Today's events are a further reminder that security in the Gulf is a security issue for us all."

The attack came the same day that flights on a key air corridor linking Europe, the Gulf and Asia were redirected south on Thursday afternoon, amid reports that Kuwait's airspace had been closed. Since the war in Iran began in late February, most international flights crossing Europe have used a route through Turkey, Iraq, Kuwait, Bahrain and the United Arab Emirates to reach Gulf hubs such as Doha, Abu Dhabi and Dubai. Aircraft on the corridor were given a new heading and turned sharply before Kuwaiti airspace, moving the route towards neighbouring countries. Flights to the Gulf usually carry extra fuel so they can hold in the air if airports or airspace close because of military activity.

The conflict began when the United States and Israel attacked Iran in late February. Iran responded with missiles and drones aimed at Israel and at Gulf Arab states that host US military assets and personnel. Kuwait said about a week earlier that it had repelled overnight Iranian drone attacks on an airbase that hosts US forces, the first strikes on the country in more than two months. A day before the latest attack, the US Virtual Embassy in Iran warned that Tehran, Yemen's Houthis and other aligned groups could target US interests and citizens in the region. Washington has urged citizens across the Middle East to remain vigilant and prepare for possible flight cancellations and airspace closures.

The US and Iran are also in a standoff over the Strait of Hormuz, the strategic waterway through which Iran has tried to block shipping. US forces are escorting vessels through it. Negotiations between the two sides are deadlocked, and recent exchanges of ffire have raised fears of a wider escalation.
