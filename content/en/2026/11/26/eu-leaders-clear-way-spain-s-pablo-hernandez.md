EU leaders have agreed a political package that would put Pablo Hernández de Cos, a Spanish economist, at the head of the European Central Bank (ECB), the institution that sets interest rates for the eurozone, the bloc of countries that use the euro. The arrangement was agreed at a summit in Brussels on Thursday, after the Netherlands stepped back from its own candidate, former Dutch central bank governor Klaas Knot.

Hernández de Cos, 55, heads the Bank for International Settlements (BIS), an international body that works with central banks. He was governor of the Bank of Spain, the country's central bank, from 2018 to 2024. If formally appointed, he would be the first Spaniard to lead the ECB since it was created in 1998. Spain's Prime Minister, Pedro Sánchez, and the opposition leader, Alberto Núñez Feijóo, have both backed his candidacy, which is Spain's first bid for the ECB presidency.

The deal is wider than one post. France, Germany and Spain drew up the political arrangement over recent weeks, and the Dutch Prime Minister, Rob Jetten, agreed to back it at the summit. Christine Lagarde, the current president, will step down on 7 February 2027, nearly nine months before her term ends. In return for the presidency, Germany will take the post of chief economist, with Markus Brunnermeier replacing Philip Lane, whose term ends on 31 May 2027. France's Laurence Boone, a former Europe minister under Emmanuel Macron, is expected to take over market operations on the six-member Executive Board from Isabel Schnabel, who leaves on 3 January 2027 to join the International Monetary Fund.

The process has several steps before the appointments are final. EU ambassadors have formally recommended the three candidates. The European Parliament will hold a hearing for Hernández de Cos on 7 December, followed by a committee vote, which then needs approval in the plenary the following week. The EU's heads of government are due to confirm the appointments at their next Brussels meeting on 17 December, after consulting the Parliament and the ECB's Governing Council.

The incoming president takes over at a difficult time. The ECB has raised interest rates twice this year, and eurozone inflation reached 4% in October, twice the bank's target of 2% over the medium term. One economist at Jefferies described Hernández de Cos as slightly more dovish than Knot, meaning more inclined to favour lower rates, but said a change of leader alone was unlikely to alter the ECB's approach. An economist at MUFG called him a pragmatic centrist who is expected to broadly maintain Lagarde's approach. MUFG forecasts a quarter-point rise in March, which would be his first rate-setting meeting as president.

Lagarde said in October that leaving the ECB would give her more freedom to speak on issues beyond monetary policy. Speaking at a financial conference on 18 November, she said a leadership role at the World Economic Forum was "one of the options" but declined to say what she would do next, adding: "I'm not retiring."
