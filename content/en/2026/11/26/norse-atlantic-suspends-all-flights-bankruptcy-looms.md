Norse Atlantic Airways, a Norwegian low-cost long-haul carrier, has suspended all of its flights while it looks for urgent financing. The company says it will file for bankruptcy if it cannot secure a short-term solution. The announcement came on Thursday, a day after the airline described its financial situation as "critical".

In a filing to the Oslo stock exchange, the board said it had not yet found a short-term financing solution and that it was in discussions with investors about a possible takeover that would include one. It did not name the investors. The company added: "There can be no assurance that a viable solution will be found." All of Thursday's flights were cancelled while the airline clarifies whether such a solution can be put in place.

The market reacted sharply. Shares fell by about 85 per cent on Wednesday and dropped further in early trading on Thursday before recovering into positive territory. At 0.04 kroner a share, the airline's market value stood at $9.7m.

Norse was set up in 2021 to take over the transatlantic routes that Norwegian had abandoned, and it operates a fleet of 12 Boeing 787 aircraft from airports including London Gatwick, Rome and Oslo. Before the suspension it was selling flights from London to Orlando, Cape Town, Bangkok and Phuket. The airline's problems have deepened this year as jet fuel prices rose sharply because of the war involving Iran. Unlike many European carriers, Norse does not hedge its fuel costs by buying supplies in advance at fixed prices, so it was fully exposed when prices roughly doubled at one point. Prices remain well above their levels before the conflict began.

The airline made a loss of $94.6m in the first half of the year. In the summer it launched a cost-cutting programme called Project Falcon, aimed at saving $50m, which included cutting about 75 jobs. It also moved its headquarters to Latvia and its founder and chief executive, Bjørn Tore Larsen, left the company. Norse has apologised to passengers for the disruption.

The suspension also affects Pakistan International Airlines (PIA), which leased aircraft and crews from Norse. PIA cancelled its London to Islamabad and Manchester to Lahore services on Wednesday night and said its UK flights from Thursday would be operated by its own aircraft.

Norse's difficulties follow a wider run of failures in the sector. The US low-cost carrier Spirit Airlines collapsed in May, and the Spanish airline Plus Ultra announced last Tuesday that it was ceasing operations. Latvia's Air Baltic filed for US Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in September. If Norse fails, it would be another setback for the low-cost long-haul model, which passengers tend to like but which has proved hard to sustain financially, particularly on transatlantic routes.
