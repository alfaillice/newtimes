Spain's four leading candidates for prime minister met in a televised debate on Thursday evening, three days before the general election on Sunday 29 November. The debate was organised by the Academy of Television, whose president, María Casado, moderated it. It brought together the incumbent prime minister, Pedro Sánchez of the Socialist Party (PSOE), the conservative People's Party (PP) leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo, Santiago Abascal of the Vox party and Ada Colau, candidate for Frente Amplio (the Broad Front) and a former mayor of Barcelona.

Feijóo opened by accusing Sánchez of avoiding questions. He said the prime minister had not answered his question, put in last week's face-to-face debate, about how Sánchez financed the PSOE primaries. He added that Sánchez had also ignored questions on the Adamuz and Toledo rail tragedies, on the abandonment of Ceuta despite warnings from Spain's intelligence service, and on Morocco. “He does not answer anything, he just runs away,” Feijóo said. He promised what he called truth, decency and good management if he reaches the Moncloa palace, the prime minister's residence. Earlier, he said the PP condemns gender violence but that Sánchez's government had defended women the least.

Sánchez framed the election as a choice between two paths: the cuts and “chainsaw” approach he said the PP and Vox would apply, or the social gains he said the left promotes. He accused the PP and Vox of pushing an “involution” project and of spreading hoaxes. He also said that the supply of rental housing had risen by 476,000 homes since the housing law was passed.

Abascal rejected the idea that Vox represents an involution. He said that “they will say that we represent an involution, but the real regression has been what has happened over these eight years”, referring to Sánchez's government. He accused Sánchez of promoting “massive immigration” and called him the “great vulture” of Spain. He also cited declared rental contracts in Barcelona, which he said had halved between 2021 and 2026, even as demand for homes grew. Sánchez replied that this was “a lot of hoax, a lot of lies”, and said rental supply had increased after the housing law.

Colau opened by calling on women to vote and condemning gender violence a day after 25 November, the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women. She said 49 women had been killed in gender-based violence so far this year. On housing, she argued that the right's approach had failed and said Sánchez's government had also failed in that area.

The debate follows a face-to-face encounter a week earlier. The general election on 29 November will decide the next government, and the sources do not indicate which party is likely to lead it.
