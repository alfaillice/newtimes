FIFA, the Zurich-based body that governs world football, has confirmed it was the victim of a criminal cyberattack. The attack gave unauthorised access to a limited set of personal and business information, the organisation said in a statement on Thursday.

FIFA said it was working closely with the relevant authorities, including law enforcement. It condemned what it called the unlawful acquisition and publication of information, particularly personal data, and described such actions as "a serious and unacceptable violation of privacy". It said they could have significant consequences for the people concerned.

The statement left several questions open. FIFA did not say how many people or organisations were affected, when the attack took place or what the business information contained. It also did not say whether any data had appeared online, or where.

Last week, a group calling itself the Handsome Boy Hacking Club posted on X a screenshot of what it said were FIFA files. Three days later, it appeared to claim the attack in reply to a post saying more than 12 gigabytes of internal documents had been leaked. FIFA has not explicitly said whether it was referring to the same incident.

A FIFA source said the person or group behind the attack gained access by impersonating staff, rather than through a failure of its systems or technical defences. Other reports have also described people posing as FIFA officials.

A person with direct knowledge of the matter said that less than 0.1 per cent of FIFA's total data had been affected. That figure did not come from FIFA's written statement, although some Spanish-language coverage has attributed it to the organisation itself. The same person said no files from FIFA Forward Enterprise had been accessed. That project was a plan to sell a 20 per cent stake in a new unit running FIFA tournaments, including the World Cup, to private investors, raising up to $4.2bn. FIFA President Gianni Infantino said in July that the organisation had scrapped it after a widespread backlash.

The breach comes at a difficult moment for FIFA, which is still dealing with the fallout from the abandoned stake sale. The organisation has not said when the attack happened, and the investigation by the authorities is ongoing.
