UniCredit has received the last regulatory approval it needed for its takeover bid for Commerzbank. Poland's financial supervisory authority, known as the KNF, approved the transaction on 26 November, completing a process that had already seen clearance from the European Commission and the US Federal Reserve. The Italian group has now moved to reshape the German lender's leadership, asking for an extraordinary general meeting to replace nine of the ten shareholder representatives on Commerzbank's supervisory board.

The Polish decision concerns the indirect change of control of Commerzbank's operations in Poland, including mBank, in which the German group holds 69.1 per cent. mBank has around 5.9 million customers and is active in mortgages, investment and payments. The KNF needed to review the deal because taking control of Commerzbank also means taking control of its Polish subsidiaries.

The European Commission cleared the deal on 16 November without conditions on competition grounds. Brussels assessed the effect of combining Commerzbank with HypoVereinsbank, UniCredit's German subsidiary, and found no negative impact on the market. The Federal Reserve granted its authorisation in the same period.

The political hurdle was Berlin. In mid-November, UniCredit's chief executive, Andrea Orcel, reached an agreement with the German government, under which the bank made eight binding commitments on the future of Commerzbank and its presence in Germany. These include keeping Frankfurt as the base for German operations, retaining Commerzbank's stock market listing and its status as a German joint-stock company, and ensuring adequate representation of both banks on UniCredit's executive committee. The group also pledged to support small and medium-sized German businesses, the so-called Mittelstand, and to protect existing agreements for employees.

The German state holds about 13.3 per cent of Commerzbank's share capital through the SoFFin stabilisation fund. It did not tender its shares in UniCredit's offer and has not announced a sale. According to the reports, Berlin has also given up its right to appoint members to the supervisory board.

UniCredit said it held 47.59 per cent of Commerzbank when the acceptance period closed. Because the bank's treasury shares carry no voting rights, that stake translates into 49.65 per cent of the votes.

The next step is the board overhaul. UniCredit has asked Commerzbank to call the extraordinary general meeting. Its proposals would see the nine shareholder representatives removed and the new members serve until the ordinary general meeting of 2031. Among the candidates put forward are Orcel himself, to chair the supervisory board, along with UniCredit's finance director, Stefano Porro, its legal director, Rita Izzo, and its head of digital and information, Ali Khan. UniCredit has also said it will seek a vote against certain members of Commerzbank's management board if they do not agree to leave beforehand. It has not said how many executives could be affected.

Commerzbank has confirmed that it received the request and said it will call the meeting provided the legal requirements are met. It will also review the proposals to ensure they comply with regulatory and corporate governance rules and protect all shareholders. The current chair of the supervisory board, Jens Weidmann, has pointed to the management team's record, which delivered the highest net profit in the bank's 156-year history. Chief executive Bettina Orlopp has stressed the need to preserve the bank's operational capacity during the transition.

The share exchange is due to be completed by 7 December 2026 at the latest. UniCredit estimates the deal will absorb between 200 and 225 basis points of CET1, the main measure of a bank's capital strength, in exchange for a significant expected boost to the group's profits.
