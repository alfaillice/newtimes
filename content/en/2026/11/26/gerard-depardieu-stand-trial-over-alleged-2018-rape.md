A court in Paris has ruled that the French actor Gérard Depardieu, 77, will stand trial for the alleged rape of the actress Charlotte Arnould at his home in the French capital in 2018. The appeals court rejected his attempt to block the case, but it dismissed a second allegation of rape.

Arnould was 22 at the time. She says she went to Depardieu's townhouse to informally rehearse a play, and she reported the alleged assault several days later. The appeals court said the trial will concern what happened on 7 August 2018. A second alleged rape, said to have taken place at a hotel on 13 August, will not be tried.

The case has a long history. It was initially dismissed for lack of sufficient evidence. After Arnould filed a civil complaint, a judicial investigation was opened in the summer of 2020. An investigating magistrate then ruled that Depardieu should stand criminal trial. His defence appealed, arguing there was no evidence of sexual penetration and denying any "criminal intent" on the part of the actor.

Depardieu has denied wrongdoing. In a letter published in the newspaper Le Figaro in 2023, he said "there was never any coercion or violence between us". Arnould's lawyer, Carine Durrieu Diebolt, welcomed the ruling. "She has been waiting for this moment for a very long time," Durrieu Diebolt said, adding that Arnould was "fully prepared" for the trial.

Depardieu's lawyer, David-Olivier Kaminski, described the decision as "a first victory for Gérard Depardieu", adding that the other half of the legal process still had to be completed. The defence is considering an appeal to the Cour de Cassation, France's highest court. It rules only on whether the law was applied correctly and does not retry cases.

The ruling comes after a separate conviction. Earlier this month Depardieu dropped his appeal against a 2025 conviction for sexually assaulting two women on a film set. He received an 18-month suspended prison sentence, was placed on France's sex offender register and issued a public apology. The sentence now stands.

Depardieu is also under investigation, together with the writer Michel Houellebecq, over allegations of a sexual nature. French authorities confirmed the inquiry last week. It follows reports of an alleged gang rape on the set of the film Thalasso in 2018. Houellebecq has not responded publicly to the accusation. These are allegations only, and no charges have been brought in that case.

Depardieu is one of France's best-known screen actors, with more than 200 films to his name, including The Last Metro and Jean de Florette. He was nominated for an Oscar for Cyrano de Bergerac and won a Golden Globe for Green Card in 1990. In recent years, however, accusations of sexual misconduct have increasingly overshadowed his career, and he has become the most prominent figure caught up in France's #MeToo movement, known locally as balance ton porc, or "call out your pig".
