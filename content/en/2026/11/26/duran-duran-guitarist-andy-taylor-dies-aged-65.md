Andy Taylor, the guitarist who helped shape the sound of the British pop group Duran Duran, has died at the age of 65. In a statement, the band's members said he died on Monday 23 November at his home, surrounded by his family, after a long battle with prostate cancer.

Taylor was born in 1961 and grew up in Cullercoats, a coastal village in Northumberland, in north-east England, where many families worked in the fishing industry. He began playing young and left school early to join bands on the working men's club circuit. In 1980 he answered an advertisement in the weekly music paper Melody Maker for a guitarist and travelled to Birmingham to audition. He later recalled that he was the only one of around 40 applicants who knew how to tune a guitar, let alone play one.

The band, one of the defining acts of the New Romantic movement, rose quickly. Girls on Film, released in 1981, carried Taylor's tight, rhythmic playing and made the group stars in Britain and the United States. The album Rio, released in 1982, went into the top 10 in both countries, and Seven and the Ragged Tiger reached number one in the UK. Taylor's guitar lines, from the opening of Girls on Film to the grinding riffs of Hungry Like the Wolf, were widely seen as one of the group's defining features.

After a break in the group's activity, Taylor formed the funk-rock band the Power Station with the bassist John Taylor, who is no relation, and the singer Robert Palmer. He later left Duran Duran in the mid-1980s, after a difficult period that included the band's performance at Live Aid in 1985. He released a solo album, Thunder, and worked for years as a writer and producer for other artists, including a hit with Rod Stewart. He rejoined Duran Duran in 2000 and recorded the album Astronaut, released in 2004, before leaving again.

In 2022, when the band was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, Taylor did not attend. Simon Le Bon read a letter from him in which Taylor disclosed that he had been diagnosed with stage four metastatic prostate cancer. He had received the diagnosis in 2018. He still contributed to the band's Halloween-themed album Danse Macabre, which was his final recorded work with the group.

In a personal statement, Le Bon described his bandmate's influence: “There’s a massive Andy Taylor shaped hole in my heart.” The band's statement praised Taylor's “dry wit” and his role in keeping the group grounded.

Taylor's wife, Tracey, whom he married in 1982, said he had been “the rock of our family”. The couple had four children. In a statement quoted by the wider press, she said the musician had faced his final years with courage and that fans around the world had given him comfort during his illness.

Taylor also worked with the British rock band Reef as a touring guitarist and helped produce their 2022 album, Shoot Me Your Ace. The band's members have asked that the privacy of Taylor's family be respected at this difficult time.
