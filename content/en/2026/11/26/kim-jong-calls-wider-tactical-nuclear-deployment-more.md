North Korean leader Kim Jong Un has called for his country to deploy tactical nuclear weapons more widely and has vowed to expand its nuclear arsenal. The state news agency KCNA reported his remarks on Thursday, after he toured nuclear facilities on Wednesday. The statements came days after Pyongyang left unanswered an offer of talks from US President Donald Trump.

According to KCNA, Kim visited two nuclear material production plants, one newly opened and one still being expanded, as well as a base that produces nuclear-powered equipment and a nuclear research institute. The agency did not say where any of them are. Photographs it released showed Kim and senior officers walking past rows of what appear to be centrifuges, which are used to make weapons-grade uranium, and viewing halls with what were believed to be warheads. Some images were blurred.

Kim described North Korea's nuclear status as an 'irreversible reality' and said the drive to expand the arsenal in both quality and quantity would continue. He said any attempt to deny the country's nuclear status, which it enshrined in its constitution in 2023, would be treated as 'an open declaration of war'. He presented the visit as a response to growing nuclear threats, accusing Washington of encouraging its regional allies to acquire nuclear-powered submarines and of pledging to share its own nuclear weapons with them. He also called for the urgent deployment of low-yield battlefield nuclear weapons, citing danger on the border with South Korea.

Tension on that border has grown since a landmine exploded on 21 September in the Demilitarized Zone (DMZ), the heavily fortified strip separating the two Koreas, injuring three South Korean soldiers. In October, Seoul demanded an apology from Pyongyang after a joint South Korea-United Nations investigation concluded that North Korea had planted the mines more than a year earlier. Pyongyang responded with a letter to the UN Security Council accusing Seoul of fabricating the explosion. Less than a week later, it fired about 10 short-range ballistic missiles into the Sea of Japan.

South Korea's Defence Ministry said it takes Kim's remarks on battlefield weapons seriously and remains militarily ready, backed by its alliance with the United States. The Joint Chiefs of Staff declined to analyse the facilities, saying the information was confidential and had been shared with Washington. South Korea estimates that North Korea possesses between 80 and 120 nuclear warheads.

Trump raised the prospect of resuming talks while in China last week for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit, but no meeting took place and Pyongyang did not respond publicly. In comments published this week, Trump rejected the suggestion that Kim had ignored him, saying the timing was not right during a busy two days. He conceded that the two leaders had not written to each other recently, but said their exchange of letters could resume at any moment.

Kim has previously said he could return to negotiations if Washington abandons what he calls its hostile policy and accepts North Korea as a nuclear state. Experts say he is seeking that recognition so he can demand an end to international sanctions. Last week, the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) chief, Rafael Grossi, said North Korea had been expanding its original enrichment facility at Yongbyon, which he described as a cause for serious concern.
