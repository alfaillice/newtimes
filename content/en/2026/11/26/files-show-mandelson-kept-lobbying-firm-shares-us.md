Newly released UK government files show that Lord Mandelson, the former ambassador to Washington, kept his shares in the lobbying firm Global Counsel after taking up the post, proposed a lunch meeting between a Trump economic adviser and a former colleague, and tried to influence a cabinet reshuffle led by Keir Starmer. The documents were published on Thursday under a motion passed by Parliament in February, which requires the government to release all papers relating to his appointment.

Mandelson was sacked by Starmer in September 2025 over his past friendship with the late convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein. Starmer was himself replaced as prime minister by Andy Burnham in July. Global Counsel, Mandelson’s lobbying firm, collapsed in February 2026 after losing a series of clients amid coverage of his links to Epstein.

The files show that Mandelson did not sell his stake when he became ambassador. According to an official’s letter quoted in the documents, the firm had not made an offer he considered commensurate with fair value. A person with knowledge of the situation said that Mandelson was not asked to divest before taking up the post and that the Foreign Office ruled out placing the shares in a blind trust. Senior Foreign Office officials had also suggested a “light-touch” arrangement to protect him from the perception that he was benefiting Global Counsel while in office.

In April 2025, Mandelson emailed an economic adviser to Trump to propose a lunch on alternatives to the World Trade Organization and on how like-minded partners could deal with China. He suggested bringing “a former trade colleague” and wrote: “It would be best to keep this under the radar.” A person familiar with the matter said the colleague was a former Global Counsel employee and that the lunch never took place. Mandelson also forwarded the emails to a Global Counsel employee to check availability. While in post, he said he would soften his China language in a speech after feedback from a Global Counsel director.

On 5 September 2025, Mandelson texted Morgan McSweeney, then chief of staff at Downing Street, suggesting that Darren Jones be moved to the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology as a way to “solve your Darren issue”. Jones did not get that post. He was instead given a newly created role as chief secretary to the prime minister. The messages show McSweeney did not reply until the reshuffle had concluded. In April, McSweeney told MPs he had not acted on the suggestions.

Security Minister Dan Jarvis told the House of Commons that Mandelson’s appointment raised questions about government appointments, vetting and conflict-of-interest processes. He said ministers would respond to a lobbying review by the Ethics and Integrity Commission before the Christmas recess. Burnham’s spokesperson said: “we are clear nothing like this can ever happen again.” Burnham has also said reforms to address vested interests would be set out next month in a ten-year plan.

The Metropolitan Police is investigating Mandelson on suspicion of misconduct in public office. He has not been charged. His lawyers have said he is cooperating with the investigation and that his priority is to clear his name. Some documents, including his conflict-of-interest declaration and a vetting summary, remain withheld at the request of the force, which says its investigation is ongoing.
