Elon Musk, the world's richest person, has promised that every child in the United States will receive two shares in SpaceX, the space and artificial intelligence company he runs. He announced the plan in a short post on X, the social media platform he owns, on Wednesday.

The pledge has two parts. SpaceX said it would give one newly issued Class A share, a type of common stock, to each currently active Trump Account. Musk would then match that number with shares of his own. Trump Accounts are tax-advantaged investment accounts for children. The US government provides a one-off $1,000 (€914) contribution for eligible American citizens born between 2025 and 2028. The money is generally invested in low-cost funds that track the US stock market, and withdrawals are usually restricted until the child turns 18.

To receive the shares, parents or guardians must claim and activate their child's account. The White House said in October that nearly 70 million accounts had been created, but it is unclear how many have been activated. The number of children who will actually receive shares is therefore not known.

The two donors will transfer their shares at different times. Musk said SpaceX would transfer its shares in December. His own shares will only be transferred in June 2027, when the restrictions on moving his stock, known as a lockup, expire.

The value of the pledge depends on the share price and on how many accounts are eligible. At Wednesday's closing price of $127.44, two shares were worth $254.88. If about 70 million accounts each received both shares, the total would be roughly $17.8bn (€16.3bn). Other reports put the figure at almost $18bn on the same basis. Neither number is a firm sum, because it assumes every account is activated and the share price does not change.

This is not the first promise of its kind. In October, Musk said every child in the US would receive one SpaceX share, calling it "the first of many". The same month, SpaceX president Gwynne Shotwell and her husband pledged a share of their SpaceX stock to each of more than 2 million children under the Trump account initiative.

Musk also advised recipients to keep their shares rather than sell them. SpaceX raised about $85.7bn (€78.4bn) when it listed in June. On Wednesday, the US Federal Communications Commission gave the company permission to deploy up to one million satellites for a project to use solar power to run data centres in orbit. In September, the company's Starship rocket reached orbit for the first time.

The announcement comes as technology companies debate how to share the gains from the AI boom. Anthropic, which listed earlier this month, said it would commit 5% of its shares to a new fund worth around $54bn at current prices.
