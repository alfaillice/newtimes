Kilmar Ábrego García, the Salvadoran man whose mistaken deportation became a symbol of US President Donald Trump's immigration crackdown, arrived in Madrid on Wednesday after leaving the United States of his own accord. Reporters at the airport said he walked to a waiting vehicle wearing sunglasses and a baseball cap, without answering questions.

His case dates back to 2025, when he was deported to El Salvador even though a court order barred his removal there. He was sent to CECOT, a maximum-security mega-prison built under President Nayib Bukele to hold alleged gang members. Officials later described the removal as an "administrative error", and the US government acknowledged it had been a mistake, although it initially resisted arranging his return. Ábrego García has said he was beaten and deprived of sleep during almost three months in the prison.

He was eventually brought back to the US after the Supreme Court ordered the administration to facilitate his return. He was then charged in a migrant-smuggling case, but a federal judge dismissed all the charges, ruling that the indictment was motivated by a wish to retaliate against him for opposing his deportation. The government went on trying to remove him, proposing several African countries, including Uganda, Ghana and Eswatini, which did not agree to receive him. His lawyer said an ICE flight to Liberia had been planned for Tuesday. ICE is the US Immigration and Customs Enforcement agency.

This week, the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), the US department responsible for immigration enforcement, said he had "self-deported" to Spain. In its statement it said: "We are delighted to announce that Kilmar Abrego Garcia has self-deported from the United States to Spain." The DHS also used strongly negative descriptions of him, which his lawyers dispute and which the courts had questioned.

His lawyer, Simón Sandoval-Moshenberg, said the authorities had agreed to let him leave, remove his ankle monitor and take him off the no-fly list, as long as he left by 24 November. A second lawyer, Chris Newman, said Ábrego García had landed as a tourist, intended to stay only a few days and had no plans to meet politicians in Spain. Sandoval-Moshenberg described the move as temporary. He said his client would decide where to settle over the next two years, and that he hopes to return to the US, where his wife and child are American citizens.

At the airport, Ábrego García did not speak to reporters. In a video call later, he said: "The government does not decide where my story continues." His advocacy group, We Are CASA, said he planned to take part in international speaking engagements.

Democratic Senator Chris Van Hollen of Maryland, who visited him in El Salvador's CECOT prison last year, welcomed the outcome and said he had briefed Spain's ambassador to the US, Ángeles Moreno Bau, on the case. The Senate is the upper house of the US Congress. Van Hollen said he was pleased that Ábrego García was now able to tell his story.
