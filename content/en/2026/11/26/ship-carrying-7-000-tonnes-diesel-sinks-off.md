A cargo ship carrying around 7,000 metric tonnes of diesel sank in the early hours of Thursday about 25 nautical miles from Ibiza, one of Spain's Balearic Islands in the Mediterranean. One crew member died. The other 16 people on board survived.

The vessel, the Med Emre, was built in 2019 and is registered in Malta. It was sailing from Algeciras, in southern Spain, to Barcelona when it began taking on water. Local reports say the ship was 120 metres long and 17 metres wide. Authorities said it had suffered a breach that eventually caused it to sink. The alert reached the emergency services at around 01.20 CET.

The Balearic government's director general for Emergencies and Interior, Pablo Gárriz, said the dead crew member was taken to Can Misses Hospital in Ibiza, where he was pronounced dead. Six other survivors were brought to Ibiza airport. Rescue operations involved Salvamento Marítimo, Spain's state maritime rescue service, which deployed ships and aircraft, and several merchant vessels that were nearby.

Reports differ on the ship's exact position from the island. Some place it 25 to 30 nautical miles north, others 25 miles north-west.

The main concern is the risk of pollution. Spain's Directorate General of the Merchant Navy, the national maritime authority, activated the national maritime pollution contingency plan at level 2. The Balearic government activated PLATERBAL, the regional civil protection plan, at level 0. Gárriz said: "We are monitoring the possible environmental consequences that this accident may have."

The full extent of any environmental damage is not yet known. Gárriz said authorities were waiting for assessments and for any change in the situation before deciding whether further measures were needed.
