Italy's Jannik Sinner has been ruled out of the Australian Open in January after knee surgery, Tennis Australia confirmed on Thursday. The world number two, who has not played a match since winning Wimbledon in July, will miss the first Grand Slam of the 2027 season at Melbourne Park.

Tennis Australia, the body that runs the tournament, said in a statement that it had been in regular contact with Sinner's team, which had confirmed he would not play. Its chief executive, Andrew Abdo, said that "Jannik loves competing here in Melbourne" and that Sinner was looking forward to returning for the 2028 edition. Abdo also wished him a full recovery.

Sinner announced the operation in a social media post on Tuesday. He said that he and his team had spent several months trying to treat the problem without surgery. When his knee did not respond as they had hoped, he had what he described as minor surgery to improve the condition of his iliotibial band, a thick band of tissue that runs along the outside of the knee.

Experts have speculated that his absence could last around three months. If so, the earliest he could compete again would be the American hard-court swing, with the Indian Wells and Miami Masters tournaments in February and March.

The Australian Open is the first Grand Slam of the year, and Sinner won it in 2024 and 2025. He lost in the semi-finals this year to Novak Djokovic, who was then beaten in the final by Carlos Alcaraz.

The 25-year-old had been in outstanding form before his knee problem. He won 44 singles matches and lost three in the first half of the season, and between March and May he won five Masters 1000 titles in a row. That run made him the youngest man to complete the so-called career Golden Masters, and he reclaimed the world's top ranking from Alcaraz. He lost in the second round of the French Open, then recovered on grass to beat Djokovic in the Wimbledon semi-finals and come back from a set down against Alexander Zverev in the final.

Sinner ended his season in early October. As a result, he could not defend the points he had earned from his titles in China, Vienna, Paris and the ATP Finals at the end of 2025. Zverev went on to win the US Open and finish the year as world number one. He has also taken over the top ranking in Sinner's absence.

Alcaraz, the world number three, who has had his own injury problems, responded on social media: "Tennis misses him. I miss him."
