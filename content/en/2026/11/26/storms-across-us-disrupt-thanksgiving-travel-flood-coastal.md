Storms across large parts of the United States disrupted travel over the Thanksgiving holiday on Thursday. Nearly 400 flights had been cancelled so far that day, and about 1,200 more had been delayed, according to FlightAware, a flight-tracking service. The disruption coincided with one of the busiest travel periods of the year. The American Automobile Association (AAA) had predicted that 81.5 million Americans would travel at least 50 miles from home over a seven-day period.

The National Weather Service (NWS) forecast storms along both the eastern and western coasts, bringing heavy rain, strong winds and flooding. On the East Coast, the NWS warned of disruption at airports through Friday morning because of poor visibility, turbulence, strong winds and ice forming on aircraft. The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), the US aviation regulator, had earlier warned that delays could occur across the country throughout the week.

The New Jersey shore was among the worst-hit areas. Streets were flooded in Sea Isle City and Ocean City, and water spread across parts of Avalon. The borough's business administrator, Scott J. Wahl, said the town was seeing some of the worst flooding in its history. A few drivers who drove around barricades into floodwaters had to be rescued. Wahl said no injuries had been reported. New Jersey's governor, Mikie Sherrill, declared a state of emergency across the state, and New York Governor Kathy Hochul declared one for parts of New York. More than 600,000 homes and businesses were without electricity on Thursday afternoon, mostly in East Coast states, according to the outage tracker poweroutage.us.

In Manhattan, Macy's postponed its 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade until Saturday. The company said it had acted with caution after consulting city officials, given concerns about how the wind would affect the parade's giant balloons.

Further inland, rain and snow affected the St Louis area in Missouri, where up to 33 centimetres of snow was forecast. In Wentzville, Missouri, heavy snow partly collapsed the roof of the Wentzville Ice Arena.

On the West Coast, coastal flood warnings were issued from Southern California to the Oregon and Washington coasts because of king tides. These high tides occur when the gravitational pull of the moon and sun align, raising water levels. The NWS said water levels along parts of the California coast could reach 2.4 metres on Thursday, breaking records set last month. A wave tore down part of a pier in Pacifica, California, on Wednesday. The NWS also forecast storms for the Pacific Northwest, including the Canadian province of British Columbia.

In Miami, some delays lingered after an incident on Wednesday evening. The FAA briefly halted flights heading to Miami International Airport after a FedEx aircraft overran a runway. No injuries were reported, and the FAA is investigating. FedEx said in a statement that one of its flights "experienced an issue upon landing" and that its crew was safe.

Both coasts were affected, although air travel is usually busier in the days before Thanksgiving than on the holiday itself.
