Former Malaysian prime minister Najib Razak has left prison and arrived at his home in Kuala Lumpur to serve the rest of his corruption sentence under house arrest, the BBC reported. According to the broadcaster, a large media presence gathered at his residence late on Tuesday night to watch the 73-year-old arrive by SUV, escorted by a heavily armed police convoy.

Najib, who led the country from 2009 to 2018, was convicted at two separate trials over the 1Malaysia Development Berhad (1MDB) scandal. 1MDB is a state-owned sovereign wealth fund that he co-founded. The BBC, citing investigators, estimated that $4.5bn (£3.3bn) was siphoned from the fund into private pockets, including his own. Prosecutors alleged that more than $1bn ended up in accounts linked to him. The affair brought down his government when it came to light about a decade ago and drew in figures from Goldman Sachs to Hollywood, the broadcaster said.

In the first trial, Najib was sentenced in 2020 to 12 years in prison for abuse of power, money laundering and breach of trust, over 42 million ringgit ($10m; £7.7m) transferred from SRC International, a former unit of 1MDB, into his private accounts. He was jailed in 2022 and the term was later halved on appeal. In September, the King of Malaysia granted him a conditional pardon that allowed him to serve the remaining two years at home. According to the BBC, his party paid a $12m fine as part of the terms.

The transfer was initially due to take place on Friday but was put on hold after the attorney general sought clarification of the royal decree. On Tuesday, the high court ruled that the house arrest was effective immediately. The BBC said Najib's mansion, in one of the capital's most exclusive neighbourhoods, had been declared a federal prison ahead of his return.

Najib's lawyers said he had been misled by his advisers, in particular the financier Jho Low, who has maintained his innocence and remains at large, the BBC reported. Najib has denied wrongdoing. Critics quoted by the broadcaster said his release undermined efforts to fight corruption and questioned its legal basis, arguing that Malaysian law makes no provision for house arrest.

Najib is also appealing a 15-year sentence handed down at a second trial in December 2025, for misappropriating nearly 2.3 billion ringgit ($569m; £422m) from 1MDB. He is due to start serving that sentence once his house arrest ends, which is expected in August 2028, according to the BBC.
