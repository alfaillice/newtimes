The Saudi-led coalition fighting Yemen's Houthis said it had intercepted two missiles fired towards Riyadh and a third aimed at the south of the kingdom, according to Al Jazeera, which reported the announcement on Tuesday. The claim came hours after Saudi officials reported damage to a key oil pipeline.

Major-General Turki al-Maliki, a coalition spokesperson, blamed the Iran-backed Houthis for the attacks in a series of posts on X. He said a ballistic missile and a cruise missile were intercepted and destroyed over Riyadh, while a second ballistic missile was downed over the southern part of the country, Al Jazeera reported.

Al Jazeera said that multiple explosions were heard in Riyadh, citing the AFP news agency. Residents received text-message alerts telling them to take shelter because of an "aerial threat". A later message from the country's civil defence agency said the danger had passed.

## Strikes reported in Sanaa

Al Jazeera also reported, citing the Houthi-run Saba news agency, that coalition air strikes had hit Sanaa, the Yemeni capital that the Houthis control. According to Saba, the strikes hit the al-Mufazir gas facility in the Jadar district, north of the capital, and a building belonging to the state oil company in southern Sanaa. Civil defence teams were working to put out a fire in gas trailers at the al-Mufazir station, Saba said.

The exchanges are part of wider fighting in Yemen between the Houthis and the internationally recognised government, which is backed by Riyadh. Al Jazeera reported that the coalition has supported a government offensive to retake territory with air strikes, while the Houthis have repeatedly struck Saudi oil infrastructure, including refineries and storage facilities, with drones and missiles.

## Pipeline and energy exports

On Monday, the East-West oil pipeline in Saudi Arabia's Medina region was attacked again, Al Jazeera reported. A Saudi Ministry of Energy official told the state-run SPA news agency that the strike caused a "limited fire" that was extinguished, adding that "pipeline operations were unaffected".

Al Jazeera said the pipeline is a vital route for Saudi oil exports because Tehran has restricted traffic through the Strait of Hormuz following the US-Israel war against Iran. The outlet reported that the pipeline has come under attack several times as the war fuels a global energy crisis.

On Sunday, the Houthis also said they had attacked Riyadh's business district, according to Al Jazeera. Saudi officials said falling debris from an "attempted attack" had caused minor damage to the Aramco Tower. Images released by SPA showed the deputy governor of the Riyadh region and other officials inspecting the building, with damage visible on several floors.

The account of these events comes from Al Jazeera's reporting. The Saudi and Houthi statements cited in the piece were not included in other sources available to this story, so the details should be read as Al Jazeera's account of official and Houthi-run media claims.
