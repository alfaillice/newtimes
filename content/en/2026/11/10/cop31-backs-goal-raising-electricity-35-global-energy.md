The presidency of this year's UN climate conference, COP31, has launched a commitment to raise electricity's share of global final energy consumption from 23% to 35% by 2035. According to Europa Press, the initiative, called the "35 for 35" Global Electrification Commitment, was announced on Tuesday through COP31's account on X and presented at a joint press conference.

Three officials spoke at the event. Murat Kurum is Turkey's minister of environment, urbanisation and climate change and COP31 president. Chris Bowen is Australia's minister for climate change and energy and chairs the negotiations. Simon Stiell is executive secretary of the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change. They argued for faster electrification of transport, buildings and industry, using efficient technologies and clean energy to cut emissions, pollution and economic and social costs.

Kurum said the effort combined political will and investment to deliver more reliable energy, cleaner transport and lower-emission production. Bowen went further, warning that the world has no chance of keeping warming to 1.5°C unless the proposed level of electrification is reached. He said, in a translation of the Spanish-language report: "Getting to 35% will be a huge effort, but it is something that must be done."

Stiell called on countries to restructure their economies around efficient electric technologies powered by clean energy. He framed the choice as "electrification or shock", contrasting it with the continuing cost of fossil fuels for households, businesses and national budgets. He also stressed the benefits of lower pollution and lower household costs.

The Europa Press report does not say which governments have endorsed the commitment, how progress would be tracked or how much investment it would require. Its account rests on the figures and statements released by the COP31 presidency. Quotations in this story are translated from the Spanish-language original.
