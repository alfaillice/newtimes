Anthropic, the artificial intelligence (AI) company behind the Claude chatbot, could be valued at around $1.3 trillion when it sells shares to the public, the BBC reports. The company plans to list on the Nasdaq stock exchange in New York under the symbol ANT on 19 November. The listing is going ahead even though the firm warned last month that AI could pose "catastrophic or existential risks to humanity".

According to the BBC, Anthropic will offer its shares at between $70 and $78 each and hopes to raise up to $64bn when it floats this month. That would value the company at between $1.1 trillion and $1.3 trillion. The broadcaster notes that this is below the $2 trillion that some of the firm's backers predicted in the summer, a figure it attributes to the Financial Times.

Anthropic is still loss-making, but its revenue is rising fast. In its most recent results, covering the six months to the end of June, revenue reached $16.2bn, up from $1.1bn in the same period a year earlier, the BBC says.

The timing is difficult for the sector. After the US midterm elections, the Nasdaq fell sharply on concerns that a Congress led by the Democrats could slow the development of AI and the building of data centres. Investors are also worried about the huge sums being spent on AI and whether they will pay off. Fiona Cincotta, a senior market analyst at City Index, called the $64bn fundraising "fairly bullish" given recent volatility. She said it suggested the company remained optimistic about demand for AI, the BBC reports.

For comparison, SpaceX, the rocket, satellite and AI company run by Elon Musk, listed its shares in June and was valued at nearly $1.8 trillion. The BBC describes Anthropic as set to become the first fully AI-focused company to go public, which it says makes the listing a test case for the future of the technology.

Anthropic was founded in 2021 by siblings Dario and Daniela Amodei and five others, all of whom had worked at OpenAI, the rival company behind ChatGPT. OpenAI has also said it will list its shares, and that is now expected early next year. The BBC notes that after the listing announcements, a series of incidents involving AI agents, bots designed to operate with some autonomy, went wrong. Current and former staff at both companies have warned that AI was being developed without adequate means of protecting humanity from possible future harms.

Anthropic has itself said that advanced AI systems could pose catastrophic or existential risks if they were not developed and run with proper safeguards, oversight and security, particularly if they were misused or allowed to act with a high degree of autonomy. The company says it remains committed to developing AI that benefits people. It has also told investors that its future success depends largely on building the most advanced AI models, and that any failure to do so could harm its business.
