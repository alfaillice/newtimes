Spain’s government has declared a migration emergency in the Canary Islands and set aside €15.18m to create more places in state-owned reception centres and to move migrants between the islands, according to Europa Press.

The announcement followed a meeting in La Moncloa, the seat of the Spanish government in Madrid, of the Interministerial Commission on Immigration. The commission is a body that brings together several government departments. It was chaired by Ángel Víctor Torres, the minister for Territorial Policy and Democratic Memory, who spoke to reporters afterwards. Ministers from the Interior, Foreign Affairs, Finance and Inclusion departments also attended, along with the government spokesperson.

The package includes an extra €1m for the Red Cross, which will strengthen its health and first-care service. The government has also added the Guardamar Calíope to its sea rescue fleet. Once it is in service, three of the five “guardamares” operating in Spain will be based in the Canaries. Since November 2024, a second rescue vessel, a salvamar, has been stationed on El Hierro, where the Guardamar Urania also operates. The rescue boats Menkalinan and Acrux are also active in the islands.

Torres said the Canaries had seen a “relevant” rise in arrivals and boat rescues in recent weeks. Most of the people on these boats are from sub-Saharan Africa, which the government puts at 92%. El Hierro is currently receiving the largest number of migrants. Torres said that 92% of those arriving on the island are sub-Saharan, many of them possibly eligible for international protection, and that about 8% are from the Maghreb, the North African region.

He also said that migration “is cyclical”, and called for a response based on “institutional loyalty” and cooperation between administrations. Torres said he was satisfied with the meeting.

Other measures cover staffing and money. The Ministry of Youth and Children will reinforce staff at the Government Delegation in the Canaries, the Spanish state’s representative office on the islands, to process the relocation of unaccompanied migrant minors across the territory. The Ministry of Finance will study what is needed to cover the extra costs linked to migration pressure on El Hierro, and to keep local councils financially sustainable. The Ministry of Economy will monitor the impact of the situation on the island’s productive economy.

The Foreign Affairs and Interior ministries pointed to diplomatic efforts with countries of origin. They highlighted recent trips by the interior minister to Mauritania and Senegal.

The details were reported by Europa Press, citing Torres’s statements to the media. The announcement did not include a timetable for when the new places and transfers would start.
