Qantas will cut 850 local corporate jobs and outsource the related work to the professional services firm Accenture, with much of it moving to Accenture's team in India. Chief executive Vanessa Hudson announced the plan on Tuesday in an internal email to staff, according to the BBC.

The two reports differ on how the work will be split. The BBC says the roles will move "mostly" to India and "some" to Australia. The Guardian reports only that some services will be transferred to India. The first changes will hit finance, procurement, people and marketing roles, with the cuts starting in mid-2027. Qantas says it will consult staff and unions before final decisions are made, and that affected workers will be redeployed where possible, including into new roles in Australia.

The airline says the deal is about modernising its back-end systems. Hudson told staff that Qantas spends more than AU$1bn a year on technology and runs more than 1,000 legacy applications that were never designed to work together. The company says the work handed to Accenture will be simplified and that automation and AI will be applied to it over time.

Hudson also described the pace of change in strong terms. In the Guardian's report, she said: "The scale and speed of change in this space is unprecedented and unlike anything businesses have seen in generations."

The Australian Services Union has responded sharply. Its assistant national secretary, Scott Cowen, said the union wanted Qantas to explain itself, given that the airline had earlier promised it had no plans to move any jobs offshore. "Sending more jobs overseas is not in the spirit of Australia," he said. The BBC said it had contacted the union for comment.

The headcount figures also differ. The BBC says Qantas employs about 28,000 staff in Australia and another 4,000 worldwide. The Guardian, citing the airline's most recent annual report, puts its total workforce at about 32,000.

The announcement follows a market update last week in which Qantas flagged higher fares and fewer flights because of rising fuel costs. According to the Guardian, the airline said customer demand was holding up, partly because some travellers had switched to Qantas after cancelling flights with carriers that use Middle East stopovers. Oil prices have risen because of the conflict involving Iran, which the Guardian says is adding to cost pressures across the Australian economy.

The Guardian also reports that Qantas is the third large company in Australia to announce corporate job cuts in three weeks, after Canva and National Australia Bank (NAB). It says NAB has targeted 2,500 roles, Canva 1,200 and Qantas 850, a total of about 4,550 corporate jobs cut, relocated or restructured in the past month. The same outlet notes that Australian living costs are rising and that unemployment has reached its highest level in nearly five years.

For now, the plan is not final. The job losses depend on the consultation with staff and unions, and the first changes are not due until mid-2027.
