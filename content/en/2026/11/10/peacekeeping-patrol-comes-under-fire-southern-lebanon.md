A patrol of UNIFIL, the United Nations peacekeeping force in southern Lebanon, came under fire on Tuesday, according to Al Jazeera. The force said that roughly a dozen rounds were fired at one of its patrols near Wadi Jilu, in Lebanon's Tyre district, but did not identify who was behind the shooting.

According to Al Jazeera, the patrol returned fire, after which the shooting stopped. UNIFIL reported no casualties and called for an investigation. In a statement, the force said: "Deliberate attacks on peacekeepers are violations of Security Council resolution 1701 and international humanitarian law." It also reiterated the obligation under international law for all actors to ensure the safety and security of UN personnel and property at all times.

UNIFIL was set up by the UN Security Council in March 1978, days after Israel invaded Lebanon. Since then it has acted as a buffer between Israel and Lebanon, but it has not been able to prevent several rounds of conflict, including the most recent one between Israel and Hezbollah, Al Jazeera reports.

Al Jazeera also says that Israel continues to occupy large parts of southern Lebanon, despite a so-called ceasefire and a framework agreement brokered by the United States. Under that deal, the Israeli military would withdraw from those areas and the Lebanese army would move in to disarm Hezbollah.

The same day, France's foreign minister, Jean-Noël Barrot, said that G7 foreign ministers had agreed on "the need to avoid a security void" in Lebanon after UNIFIL's mandate expires at the end of 2026. Speaking after the group met in Paris, Barrot said the ministers also backed "the need to move quickly toward a new security architecture". He acknowledged, however, that there had been no breakthrough on replacing UNIFIL, which has some 7,500 personnel from nearly 50 countries, according to Al Jazeera.

The UN Security Council voted last year to end UNIFIL's mandate on 31 December 2026. Al Jazeera reports that the vote was taken under US pressure, and that Lebanese officials have called for the mandate to be extended.
