Mary Morgan received a payment of £378,000 when she retired as chief executive of NHS National Services Scotland (NSS), according to accounts published by the health board and reported by the BBC. The broadcaster describes the sum as a near £400,000 redundancy pay-off.

The BBC reports that Morgan retired on 3 May after a 44-year career in the health service. Her exit followed the merger of NHS NSS in April into a new body, Public Services Delivery (PSD) Scotland. According to the BBC, PSD Scotland was set up to drive change across the health and care system and the wider public sector, and it replaced both NHS NSS and NHS Education for Scotland. Its chief executive, Professor Karen Reid, who previously led NHS Education for Scotland, is now in charge of PSD Scotland.

The BBC says Morgan earned between £160,000 and £165,000 a year and had led NHS NSS since 2021. The accounts for 2025/26, cited by the broadcaster, show that she retired under contractual redundancy terms from PSD Scotland and received a related amount of £378,000. The same accounts put her pension pot, known as the Cash Equivalent Transfer Value, at just over £2m.

A PSD Scotland spokesperson told the BBC that Morgan had retired after an 'exceptional career' and that all payments were in line with the terms and conditions of her employment. The BBC also reports that, when its Scotland desk asked NHS NSS on 10 March what would happen to Morgan's role, which the broadcaster describes as defunct, the board said she was 'considering her options and next opportunity' in line with national workforce policies. After a follow-up query three weeks later, Keith Redpath, then chairman of NHS NSS, confirmed her retirement date.

The BBC links the payment to a planned major overhaul of Scotland's NHS that could cut the number of health boards sharply. It also points to doctors who argue that the plan to merge the boards should be scrapped. The figures reported here come from the BBC's reading of the published accounts and from statements by the two organisations; the BBC's headline refers to a near £400,000 pay-off, while the accounts figure it cites is £378,000.
