Emmanuel and Brigitte Macron have filed a new defamation lawsuit against Candace Owens in Tennessee, the US state where she lives, according to The Guardian. The complaint was filed on 4 November in Nashville and extends a transatlantic legal battle that began in July 2025.

Brigitte Macron is the wife of President Emmanuel Macron of France, and The Guardian refers to her as the country's first lady. Owens is a US commentator whom the newspaper describes as a far-right podcaster. She has millions of followers across her podcast and social media channels.

The couple first sued Owens and her media companies in Delaware, responding to what they called “verifiably false and devastating lies” repeated on her podcast and on X. According to that original complaint, the claims included that Brigitte Macron, 73, was born a man named Jean-Michel Trogneux, who is in fact her older brother, and had committed identity theft to hide a gender transition. The complaint also alleged that Emmanuel Macron was elected as part of a CIA mind-control programme, and that the couple are blood relatives committing incest.

The Delaware case has since been narrowed. A judge limited the claim to Owens’s companies registered in that state, after she argued that the court did not have jurisdiction over her personally because she does not live there. The case against the companies remains pending, The Guardian reports.

The new complaint contains 88 counts of defamation, false light and defamation by implication, and is directed at Owens personally. It builds on the couple’s original demand for punitive damages, arguing that she has persisted with the claims. According to the filing, Owens has since expanded them through an eight-part podcast series called “Becoming Brigitte” and through posts on X.

The lawsuit also alleges a financial motive. It says Owens uses her podcast to sell merchandise mocking the Macrons and collects donations based on her coverage of them. It cites a “Brigitte doll” that appears in her videos, which the complaint describes as a grotesque, alien-like figure resembling the character in the film ET, and says the doll caricatures Brigitte Macron’s body. The filing argues that this shows Owens’s campaign is driven by hostility towards Brigitte Macron’s perceived gender identity, gender and sexuality.

The Macrons are seeking actual, presumed and punitive damages, and an injunction barring Owens from republishing the statements they consider defamatory. The lawsuit states that there is nothing shameful or defamatory about being a man, being transgender or being gay. It argues that the statements are defamatory because they accuse the couple of lying to France and the world for decades and of committing crimes to conceal that supposed lie.

The Guardian contacted Owens for comment. In a post on X, she confirmed that the Macrons had filed a new set of claims against her, totalling 88, and said the couple wanted justice over her “Brigitte-Doll”. She said the doll had never sought the limelight and that describing it as grotesque was hurtful. She added that the doll would make no further statements at this time.

The report does not give a timetable for the Nashville case.
