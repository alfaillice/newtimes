France has summoned Iran's ambassador to Paris to “lodge a very strong protest” over a cyberattack that the French government blames on Tehran, according to Al Jazeera. The attack targeted supermarket refrigerators last month.

France's foreign minister, Jean-Noël Barrot, said on Tuesday that the attack was the first large-scale cyberattack aimed at sabotage on French territory, and that it amounted to a serious assault on the country's interests and the security of its people, Al Jazeera reported. He told France Info television that Paris “rules nothing out” and would take measures, some of which it may not disclose, to deter further attacks.

On Monday, Barrot had said that Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) was responsible for attacks on more than 700 French supermarkets on 11 and 12 October. According to his statement, as reported by Al Jazeera, the refrigerators were accessed remotely in order to start abnormal, indefinite defrost cycles. The higher temperatures forced the stores to throw away all their perishable food.

According to the retailers, the losses amount to millions of euros. In some cases the heat caused fires or melted equipment, Al Jazeera reported. The French supermarket groups E-Leclerc, Intermarché, Carrefour, Monoprix and Cooperative U were all affected.

Paris prosecutor Laure Beccuau said on Monday that she had opened an investigation into Shahid Kaveh, which the report describes as a cyber unit of the IRGC. Her office has asked for international arrest warrants for two officials linked to that unit. Barrot said France would push for European Union sanctions in response to what he called an attack of unprecedented scale.

Al Jazeera also reported that similar incidents have occurred elsewhere in Europe. On 2 November, a suspected cyberattack caused temperature spikes in shops in Belgium, Estonia, Ireland, Luxembourg and Sweden. Jody Westby, chief executive of Global Cyber Risk LLC, told the broadcaster that tracing the group behind such attacks is very difficult, because international cooperation on cybercrime investigations is weak.

The same report noted that false bomb alerts in recent days have disrupted European air traffic, affecting dozens of flights. The origin of those threats has not yet been established.

The Al Jazeera article did not include a response from Iran's government.
