Negotiators from Russia, Ukraine and the United States are due to meet in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday, in the first trilateral talks aimed at ending the war in Ukraine since February, according to the BBC. Al Jazeera describes the meeting as the third round held in the capital of the United Arab Emirates this year, after security consultations in January and February. The two outlets differ on the recent history: the BBC says the last trilateral round took place in Geneva in February and ended without a breakthrough.

The talks come almost five years after Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022. The United States is acting as mediator. Its envoys, Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, are leading the American side. Kushner is a son-in-law of US President Donald Trump. Trump promised to end the war quickly after returning to office in January 2025, but no settlement has been reached. Last week in Washington, he met Kirill Dmitriev, a Kremlin envoy, and then spoke of a forthcoming agreement on Ukraine that he said might involve all of Europe.

A US official, speaking on condition of anonymity, said Moscow and Kyiv had shown some flexibility in private, according to both the BBC and Al Jazeera. In public, however, the two sides remain far apart. According to the BBC, Moscow wants Ukraine to hand over the parts of the Donetsk region that Kyiv still holds. Kyiv wants the front line frozen so that it keeps control of those areas, which it says are crucial to its security. Al Jazeera describes the gap differently: Ukraine wants Russia pushed back to the border between the two countries as it stood before the war, while President Vladimir Putin wants all of the Donbas region. Russia occupies roughly 20 per cent of Ukraine, according to Al Jazeera. The international community has not recognised Russia's move.

The US official told reporters that the territorial question would not be settled before a post-war security framework was agreed. The framework under discussion includes security consultations, crisis communication hotlines and reciprocal de-escalation measures, the BBC reports. It could also include the reactivation of the NATO-Russia Council, which has been largely paused since 2022. Al Jazeera adds that a defence commitment modelled on Article 5 of the NATO treaty is also under consideration.

European leaders have largely been left out of the process. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen called for NATO allies and EU members to be included in any discussions on Europe's security architecture. Al Jazeera reports that France, Germany and the European Commission will send officials to Abu Dhabi, but Russia does not want Europeans to take part in the negotiations. Dmitry Peskov, the Kremlin spokesman, said the talks would be trilateral, with US mediation, and that the Americans and Ukrainians have their own channels with Europeans. The BBC reports that the US side intends to propose a multilateral format that would bring Europeans in.

The Russian delegation will include the Kremlin aide Vladimir Medinsky and the deputy foreign minister Sergei Ryabkov, along with military and intelligence officials. Kyiv's team will be led again by Rustem Umerov, head of the Foreign Intelligence Service, according to Al Jazeera, citing The Kyiv Independent.

Zelensky said in his nightly address that Ukrainian forces were strengthening the country's negotiating position and that Russia was suffering record losses. Moscow disputes the claim. Russia has continued deadly strikes on Ukrainian energy infrastructure, the BBC reports. Oleksandr Merezhko, head of the foreign affairs committee of Ukraine's parliament, told the BBC that he believes Trump wants a quick deal at Ukraine's expense, which he said Kyiv would reject.

Kushner wrote on X that the aim was to address the "postwar security concerns" of both countries, and that "an important part of this process is encouraging all parties to begin thinking seriously about what comes after the war". He cautioned that the Abu Dhabi talks could at most build momentum for the next phase of negotiations.
