The final two Formula 1 races of the season, in Qatar and Abu Dhabi, will go ahead as planned despite tensions in the Middle East, according to Mohammed Ben Sulayem, president of the International Automobile Federation (FIA), the sport's governing body. Ben Sulayem said so on social media on Tuesday, writing: "We are ready for Qatar and Abu Dhabi." The Qatar race is scheduled for 29 November, within a race weekend running from 27 to 29 November, and the Abu Dhabi race for 6 December, within a weekend from 4 to 6 December.

The season has already been disrupted by the war between the United States and Israel with Iran. The Saudi Arabian Grand Prix was cancelled after the conflict broke out, and the Bahrain race was delayed and moved to Malaysia, where it was held at the Sepang circuit in October, BBC Sport reports. Al Jazeera also says several events in Saudi Arabia, which borders Qatar and the United Arab Emirates, have been cancelled or postponed in recent weeks following attacks by Yemen's Houthis.

BBC Sport reports that Formula 1 has told the Imola circuit in Italy that it will not be needed as a back-up for the two Middle East races. Imola had been asked to prepare a possible grand prix on the Abu Dhabi dates in case the Middle East events could not go ahead. On Tuesday it was asked to stand down those preparations. Formula 1 says it would still cancel the two races if the situation deteriorates, and that the safety of everyone involved remains the priority.

According to BBC Sport, Formula 1 says the decision to press ahead was always the default option. It argues that the situation in both countries has not developed negatively in recent months. Government travel advice still allows travel, insurance remains valid, events are taking place and business has returned to normal levels, the sport said. It added that all the teams support finishing the season as planned. A MotoGP race was held in Qatar last weekend. MotoGP is owned by the same US group that owns Formula 1.

Not everyone in the sport is comfortable with the plan. BBC Sport reports that several senior figures in Formula 1 have expressed disquiet to it about racing in the Middle East, as both countries are close to areas of the Strait of Hormuz where missile strikes are still taking place. Al Jazeera reports that drivers have mixed feelings about the races, and that four-time world champion Max Verstappen is among those who have voiced reservations. Ben Sulayem's comments echo those made last month by Formula 1 chief executive Stefano Domenicali.

BBC Sport also notes the financial stakes. Both countries pay large fees for their events and are deeply involved in the sport as sponsors and investors. Qatar's sovereign investment fund is a part owner of the Audi team, and Abu Dhabi holds the same position at McLaren.

The championship is already decided at the top. Al Jazeera reports that Italian driver Kimi Antonelli has won the drivers' world title, and that his Mercedes team clinched the constructors' crown last weekend in Sao Paulo.
