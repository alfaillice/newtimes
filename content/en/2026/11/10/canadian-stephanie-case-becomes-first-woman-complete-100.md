Canadian ultra-runner Stephanie Case has become the first woman to complete a 100-mile race in Antarctica, finishing second overall in the Antarctic Ice Ultra, according to BBC Sport. The 43-year-old crossed the line in 17 hours, 31 minutes and 39 seconds.

The event consists of 38 loops of a 4.2km course in the Schirmacher Oasis in Queen Maud Land, in East Antarctica. Because of the harsh weather, organisers brought the race forward to a 1am start on Sunday. BBC Sport reports that runners faced strong wind and temperatures as low as -35C, the coldest the organisers had ever recorded. Case told the broadcaster that the challenge was as much about survival as about performance. She said that running for 17 hours or more in such conditions takes a huge toll on the body, because runners must spend a great deal of energy just to stay warm. She also described the need to run fast without sweating, since sweat would make her cold almost instantly.

BBC Sport says only four of the eight runners who started the 100-mile race finished. Case was one of three women on the start line and the only one to cross the finish. The race had previously been attempted only by men.

The Chinese runner Yusheng Ni, who has completed more than 500 ultramarathons, crossed the line 29 minutes before Case. According to BBC Sport, he also beat his own world record, set last year, by more than five and a half hours. Case had been chasing the world record herself. She told BBC Sport that at times she was 60 seconds or 200 metres behind Ni, and that the two of them had pushed each other. In her words, "It was a real race."

The broadcaster also reports that Case had not anticipated some practical difficulties. Toilet breaks were a problem: biosecurity rules in Antarctica mean that runners must not leave any trace, and removing gloves for even a few seconds in extreme cold risks frostbite. Case, who describes herself as a mountain goat, called it "a real challenge".

Case, a human rights lawyer, went viral last year after breastfeeding her daughter Pepper during a victory at an ultramarathon in Snowdonia. BBC Sport reports that she took on the Antarctic challenge partly in response to the criticism she received, aiming to show that motherhood is not the finish line. She said that doing things that make her feel happy and whole makes her a better mother, and that she wanted to answer those who believed she should stay at home with her baby by attempting the record in what she called the most extreme place on Earth. BBC Sport notes that Antarctica has historically been described as the "womanless continent", which she said made the achievement fitting. Asked about the finish, Case said: "It was just an outpouring of emotion."
