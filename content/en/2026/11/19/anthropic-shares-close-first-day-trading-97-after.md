Shares in Anthropic, the artificial intelligence company behind the Claude chatbot, closed at around $97 on their first day of trading on Thursday, according to The Guardian. That was about 17% above the initial public offering (IPO) price of $83 a share, which was itself a record. An IPO is the first sale of a company's shares to the public. The paper reported that the debut was the largest stock market flotation in history.

The shares opened at $111.50 shortly before noon in New York and briefly valued the company at about $2tn. The Guardian said the momentum faded after that, and the valuation at the close stood at around $1.7tn. The newspaper reported that Anthropic had sold 980m shares, raising $81.3bn. That surpassed the $75bn that SpaceX raised in June, which had been the previous record.

The Guardian gave different figures for the gain depending on the time of day. Its live coverage put the close at about 17% higher. An earlier report from the same paper said the shares were up 15%, at nearly $96, by early afternoon in New York.

The rise did not lift the rest of the market. According to preliminary data cited by The Guardian, the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 2.2%, the S&P 500 lost 2% and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 2.3%. The paper described the S&P 500's losing run as its longest since April 2024. It linked the fall to doubts about how long the boom in artificial intelligence can last. Shares in the chipmaker Nvidia also declined, after the departure of its chief executive, Jensen Huang, the previous week.

Anthropic's chief executive, Dario Amodei, rang the Nasdaq opening bell. The Guardian quoted him saying: "Today puts the company in a stronger financial position, and in doing so, only strengthens our resolve to do the right thing." Earlier he had warned of the risks of AI, which he said were not inevitable.

The paper also looked at the company's finances. Its investor prospectus, filed at the end of October, showed revenue of $16.2bn in the first six months of 2026, compared with $4.6bn for all of 2025. The same document reported losses of $104bn in the first nine months of the year, although the company's losses in the first half were largely due to a one-off charge. The Guardian said compute, the computing power needed to run AI models, was its largest single expense.

The Guardian also reported that the seven co-founders' stakes were together worth more than $180bn at the opening price. The founders have pledged to give away 80% of their shares to philanthropic causes, which the paper estimated at about $150bn at the current price. Anthropic has also committed 5% of its shares to a public benefit fund.

Analysts at Yorkville Ives told clients that the listing was "a big near-term win for the tech sector". The Guardian noted that investors will now watch whether the company's growth can be sustained. Anthropic is the first frontier AI lab to be listed on a stock exchange, and rivals such as OpenAI, the maker of ChatGPT, will be watching its performance closely.
