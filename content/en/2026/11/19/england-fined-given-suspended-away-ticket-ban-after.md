England have been fined and handed a suspended one-match ban on selling tickets to their away supporters by Uefa, European football's governing body, after fans sang anti-Catholic chants during their 3-2 Nations League win over Spain in Madrid on Sunday.

Uefa's control, ethics and disciplinary body fined the Football Association (FA), England's governing body, €15,000. The Guardian reports the sum in euros, while BBC Sport gives it as £12,572 (15,000 euros); the two outlets quote the same amount in different currencies. The sanction also includes a full restriction on selling tickets to England supporters for one away match, but that restriction is suspended for a probationary period of two years from the date of the decision.

Uefa acted over "racist and/or discriminatory behaviour" by supporters. BBC Sport understands the charge relates to chants deemed anti-Catholic and anti-Irish. The Guardian reports that the chant believed to be at the centre of the case was directed at the pope and the Irish Republican Army (IRA). The regulations say that when supporters insult a group on grounds including religion, the association responsible faces at least a fine and either a partial stadium closure or a restriction on selling away tickets to its supporters.

The FA said it "will not tolerate this unacceptable and deeply offensive chanting" and would work with police, who attend all England home and away matches, to identify those responsible and take action.

Before the game, England head coach Thomas Tuchel said he would remind his players of Uefa's anti-racism protocol, and he said he did not expect any incidents. The visit was England's first to the Spanish capital since 2004, when a match at the Bernabéu was overshadowed by racist chanting aimed at England's players, according to BBC Sport. Spain's head coach, Luis de la Fuente, responded to those concerns, saying "in Spain, there's no racism."

England's victory ended Spain's unbeaten run of 43 matches and put England top of League A Group 3 in the Nations League, Uefa's international competition, BBC Sport reports.

The Guardian also reports that Spain were fined £15,000 last month and ordered to play their next friendly with a partial stadium closure, after discriminatory chants about Muslims at March's match against Egypt in Barcelona.

The punishment could have further consequences. According to The Guardian, a further charge within the next two years could trigger a ban on away fans for a competitive match. BBC Sport reports that a further offence in that period would mean a ban on away fans is enforced.
