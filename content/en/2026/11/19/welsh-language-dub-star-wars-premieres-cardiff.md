A Welsh-language version of the 1977 film Star Wars, retitled Star Wars: Gobaith Newydd (New Hope), was shown for the first time at a red-carpet screening at the Cineworld multiplex in Cardiff on Thursday evening. The dub was produced by Lucasfilm and Disney in collaboration with the Welsh-language public broadcaster and the Welsh government's Creative Wales agency. It will be broadcast on Christmas Day.

Osian Morgan, well known in Wales for his role in the Welsh-language soap opera Pobol y Cwm, voices Luke Skywalker. Mark Lewis Jones, who has appeared in The Crown and Game of Thrones, voices Darth Vader. He said he thought Welsh suited the character, and that he gives all his characters north-east Wales accents. Llŷr Ifans voices the droid C-3PO. His brother Rhys Ifans, who plays Obi-Wan Kenobi in the dub, did not make it to Cardiff. Matthew Rhys is named as Han Solo and Aimee-Ffion Edwards as Princess Leia.

Morgan said the role was a "dream job" and that he loved the films as a child. He said his youngest brother had never seen any of the films before and would now see one in Welsh. Llŷr Ifans said he was confident the dub would be well received, and expected many Welsh-speaking parents to watch it on Christmas Day.

The Welsh-language version was first announced at the National Eisteddfod in Pembrokeshire in August. Lucasfilm co-president Lynwen Brennan, who was born and raised in Wales, said that seeing the audience react to famous scenes in Welsh had been a moment of great pride, according to reports.

The film also has a Welsh connection: a version of the Millennium Falcon was built in south-west Wales. Mark Williams, who oversaw an exhibition about the ship, said the Welsh version was long overdue.

Adam Price, the Welsh government's cabinet minister for enterprise, connectivity and energy, attended the screening. He said it was an opportunity for Welsh actors and production staff to work with a major Hollywood studio. The Welsh government aims to have one million people speaking Welsh by 2050. A spokesperson for the Welsh language commissioner's office said that one of its main goals is to increase the use of the language in communities, and that a high-profile film could help attract new audiences.

The chief executive of the Welsh-language broadcaster, Geraint Evans, described the screening as a "hugely proud moment". He said the dub would give Welsh-speaking children the chance to hear some of cinema's best-loved characters speak their language for the first time.
