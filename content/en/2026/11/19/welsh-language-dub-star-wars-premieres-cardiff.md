A Welsh-language version of the 1977 film Star Wars, retitled Star Wars: Gobaith Newydd (New Hope), was shown for the first time at a red-carpet screening at the Cineworld multiplex in Cardiff on Thursday evening. The dub was produced by Lucasfilm and Disney in collaboration with S4C, the Welsh-language broadcaster, and the Welsh government's Creative Wales agency. S4C will broadcast it on Christmas Day.

Osian Morgan, well known in Wales for his role in the Welsh-language soap opera Pobol y Cwm, voices Luke Skywalker. Mark Lewis Jones, who has appeared in The Crown and Game of Thrones, voices Darth Vader. He told The Guardian that he thought Welsh suited the character, and said that he gives all his characters north-east Wales accents. Llŷr Ifans voices the droid C-3PO. His brother Rhys Ifans, who plays Obi-Wan Kenobi in the dub, did not make it to Cardiff, according to The Guardian. The BBC also names Matthew Rhys as Han Solo and Aimee-Ffion Edwards as Princess Leia.

Morgan told the BBC that the role was a "dream job" and that he loved the films as a child. He said his youngest brother had never seen any of the films before and would now see one in Welsh. Llŷr Ifans told the BBC that he was confident the dub would be well received, and expected many Welsh-speaking parents to watch it on Christmas Day.

The Welsh-language version was first announced at the National Eisteddfod in Pembrokeshire in August. Lucasfilm co-president Lynwen Brennan, who was born and raised in Wales, said that seeing the audience react to famous scenes in Welsh had been a moment of great pride, according to the BBC.

The Guardian reported that the film has a Welsh connection of its own: a version of the Millennium Falcon was built in south-west Wales. Mark Williams, who oversaw an exhibition about the ship, said the Welsh version was long overdue.

Adam Price, the Welsh government's cabinet minister for enterprise, connectivity and energy, attended the screening. He said it was an opportunity for Welsh actors and production staff to work with a major Hollywood studio. The Welsh government aims to have one million people speaking Welsh by 2050. A spokesperson for the Welsh language commissioner's office said that one of its main goals is to increase the use of the language in communities, and that a high-profile film could help attract new audiences.

S4C's chief executive, Geraint Evans, described the screening as a "hugely proud moment". He said the dub would give Welsh-speaking children the chance to hear some of cinema's best-loved characters speak their language for the first time.
