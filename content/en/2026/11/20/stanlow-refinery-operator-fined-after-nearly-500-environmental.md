Essar Oil (UK) Limited, which trades as EET Fuels, has been ordered to pay £873,473 after breaching environmental permit limits almost 500 times over three years, according to the BBC. The company runs the Stanlow refinery in Ellesmere Port, Cheshire, which the BBC describes as one of the largest oil refining operations in the UK.

The Environment Agency (EA) said the refinery exceeded its wastewater discharge limits at least 489 times between 2018 and 2020. The company pleaded guilty to 12 charges in March, the BBC reported. At Warrington Magistrates' Court it was fined £800,000 and ordered to pay £73,473 in costs.

Ten of the charges concerned wastewater containing oil, suspended solids and chemicals, including cyanide, discharged into Thornton Brook, the River Gowy and the Manchester Ship Canal. The BBC details several of the incidents. In February 2019, between 15 and 20 tonnes of a chemical leaked into the ship canal after a vent was left open and a written procedure was not followed. A month later, a contractor's vehicle ruptured an effluent pipe. During the repairs, about 15 tonnes of partially treated sewage entered Thornton Brook, which leads to the protected Mersey Estuary.

In January 2020, EA officers reported a sheen of oil on local waterways, and vegetation downstream of the refinery's discharge point was blackened by oil. The court set a penalty of £80,000 for each of the 10 offences, with no additional penalty for the two 2019 incidents. Three breaches from 2022 and 2023 were taken into consideration without further punishment.

Nigel Glasgow, the Environment Agency's area environment manager, said the case showed "a repeated failure to comply" with permit conditions. An EET Fuels spokesperson said: "We take our environmental responsibilities extremely seriously and regret these historic incidents."

The company said it had invested more than £24m to improve its environmental performance. That investment included rerouting wastewater to United Utilities' Ellesmere Port treatment works, which it said had brought fewer discharges and lower freshwater use.

*The figures for the breaches come from the Environment Agency as reported by the BBC. The BBC's own summary of "nearly 500" breaches is based on the agency's count of at least 489 discharge exceedances.*
