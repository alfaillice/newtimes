The top cyber-security official in the US government, Sean Cairncross, has reportedly left his post after a little over a year in the job, according to the BBC. The broadcaster says he was fired after losing the confidence of the White House chief of staff, Stephen Miller. The BBC attributes that account to CBS, its US news partner, and notes that the news was first reported by Axios.

The White House has not officially confirmed the removal. However, Secretary of State Marco Rubio posted on X that Kirsten Davies, currently the Pentagon's chief information officer, is the new national cyber director. The BBC also reports that President Donald Trump, asked about the removal on Friday, said Cairncross was not "good enough".

The move comes after a string of technology outages that hit banking, air traffic control and other sectors. The BBC reports that US officials said the outages were not caused by cyberattacks, but that the blackouts prompted scrutiny from members of Congress, the US national legislature, who asked for a clearer explanation of what went wrong.

Rubio said earlier this week there was "zero evidence" that the outages were connected. The BBC also links the affair to wider security concerns, including the ability of advanced artificial intelligence (AI) models, mostly built by US companies, to find software weaknesses that humans could not identify on their own.

Cairncross, according to the BBC, was previously a lawyer in the Republican Party before becoming chief operating officer of the Republican National Committee, the party's national organisation. During Trump's first administration he worked on the team of the White House chief of staff. He later moved to the private sector and returned to government in August 2025.

The BBC notes that the number of outages in quick succession has left some people worried about the safety of technology that is relied on across almost every part of daily life. The report does not say who will be formally appointed to the post or whether the White House will make an official statement on the change.
