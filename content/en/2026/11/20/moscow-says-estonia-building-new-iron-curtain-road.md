Estonia's government on Friday approved a preliminary six-month closure of its road border crossings with Russia, starting on 1 December, Al Jazeera reported. The government will decide next year whether to extend the measure. According to the broadcaster, rail freight is not affected by the road closure but remains subject to strict customs checks.

Moscow has accused Estonia of planning to build a "new Iron Curtain" and has promised a response. Maria Zakharova, spokesperson for Russia's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, told a news briefing in Moscow that such moves amount to attempts to erect a new Iron Curtain "under the false pretence of some sort of threat from the East". Al Jazeera reported that she said Russia would take the necessary political and diplomatic measures if Tallinn went ahead with its plans.

Estonia is a Baltic state that belongs to NATO and the European Union and is a strong supporter of Ukraine. It first announced the border closure earlier this month, accusing Russia of being behind an attempted arson attack on Threod Systems, a drone manufacturer. The Kremlin denied the accusation at the time. It described the claim as part of a pattern of false allegations that European countries have made against Moscow.

In Tallinn on Friday, Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna described the closure as "an important measure", according to Al Jazeera. He said it would reduce the opportunities to use cross-border movement to recruit people, carry out influence operations, organise acts of sabotage or get around sanctions.

The measure follows a decision by Finland, Estonia's northern neighbour, which shut its Russia border crossings in 2023 and has kept them closed since. Latvia and Lithuania, Estonia's southern Baltic neighbours, maintain open crossings.

Al Jazeera cited Estonian police data showing that 760,000 people crossed the Estonia-Russia border this year. Of these, 307,000 were Russian nationals, 175,000 were Estonian citizens and 54,000 held Finnish passports.
