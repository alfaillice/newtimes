Shares in Anthropic, the artificial intelligence company behind the Claude chatbot, fell sharply on Friday, their second day of trading, as a wider sell-off in technology and AI-linked companies spread through global markets.

The company listed on the Nasdaq on Thursday after a record initial public offering (IPO) that raised $81bn. Its shares rose 17 per cent that day, after peaking at $115 and closing at $97.17, according to the BBC. On Friday they dropped by as much as 12 per cent. The BBC put the price at about $85 by lunchtime, close to the IPO price of $83. The Guardian reported a low of $85.12. Al Jazeera, which quoted a mid-morning price in New York, had the stock at $87.95, down about 9.5 per cent from Thursday's close. It gave the offer price as $83.30, a slightly different figure from the other two outlets. The outlets quoted different moments in the session, so the figures should be read with that in mind.

Wall Street's main indices also fell. By lunchtime the BBC reported the S&P 500 down more than 2 per cent and the Dow Jones Industrial Average down 0.7 per cent, with both touching correction territory, meaning trading 10 per cent or more below a recent high. The Guardian said both indices had briefly entered that territory and that the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite had erased all of its gains for 2026. Its intraday figures were smaller, with the Nasdaq down 2.9 per cent and the S&P 500 down 1.66 per cent in morning trading. The BBC put the Nasdaq more than 3.4 per cent lower in the middle of the session.

Chip and AI stocks led the declines. Nvidia fell by between about 6 and 7 per cent. The Guardian linked its fall to a subpoena from California as part of an investigation into whether its advanced semiconductors ended up in China. Memory chipmaker SanDisk fell 14 per cent according to the Guardian and nearly 15 per cent according to Al Jazeera, while Micron dropped 12 per cent. Meta, Amazon and Microsoft also fell. In Japan, the investor SoftBank had its worst day in more than two years, down 14.5 per cent in Tokyo, the Guardian reported.

Markets elsewhere also fell. The Guardian said Asian markets had recorded their worst week since 2020. Japan's Nikkei index dropped about 5 per cent and South Korea's Kospi fell by between about 5 and 5.5 per cent, depending on the source. Taiwan's stock market fell by roughly 6 to 6.7 per cent, and the chair of its stock exchange pledged to ensure the stable operation of capital markets. In Europe, the BBC reported Germany's Dax down 2 per cent and the UK's FTSE 100 down 0.5 per cent. The Guardian said the pan-European Stoxx 600 fell 1.9 per cent on Friday and that London's blue-chip index hit a five-month low, with a weekly fall of 5.1 per cent, its biggest since the start of the Iran war.

Analysts offered different explanations. Ed Moya, a strategist at the market intelligence firm AlphaSense, told the BBC that the listing had not restored confidence in the wider AI market and that investors were asking how much they were willing to pay for growth. David Miller, chief investment officer at Catalyst Funds, said: "Investors still believe in AI but are becoming more selective about what they will pay for that growth." Chris Beauchamp of the trading platform IG suggested to the Guardian that Anthropic could be draining liquidity from the market, as investors sell other shares to fund purchases of its stock.

The outlook is uncertain. Intropic, a London-based index rebalance analytics firm, estimates that index-tracking funds could buy up to 30 per cent of Anthropic's shares by year-end, Al Jazeera reported. Large debuts are often volatile. SpaceX, which held the record for the biggest IPO before Anthropic, rose nearly 50 per cent in its first three days but fell below its offer price a month later. On Friday it was down about 7.4 per cent.

The Guardian also reported that fears US central bankers might raise interest rates to fight inflation had lifted the dollar to a 19-month high, pushed gold towards its worst week since March and sent bitcoin below $60,000 for the first time since early July.
