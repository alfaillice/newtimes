Shares in Anthropic, the artificial intelligence company behind the Claude chatbot, fell sharply on Friday, their second day of trading, as a wider sell-off in technology and AI-linked companies spread through global markets.

The company listed on the Nasdaq on Thursday after a record initial public offering (IPO) that raised $81bn. Its shares rose 17 per cent that day, after peaking at $115 and closing at $97.17, according to reports. On Friday they dropped by as much as 12 per cent. One report put the price at about $85 by lunchtime, close to the IPO price of $83, while another gave a low of $85.12. A mid-morning price in New York was reported at $87.95, down about 9.5 per cent from Thursday's close, and the offer price was given as $83.30, a slightly different figure from the other accounts. The sources quoted different moments in the session, so the figures should be read with that in mind.

By lunchtime, reports had the S&P 500 down more than 2 per cent and the Dow Jones Industrial Average down 0.7 per cent, with both touching correction territory, meaning trading 10 per cent or more below a recent high. Both indices had briefly entered that territory, and the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite had erased all of its gains for 2026. Other intraday figures were smaller, with the Nasdaq down 2.9 per cent and the S&P 500 down 1.66 per cent in morning trading. Another report put the Nasdaq more than 3.4 per cent lower in the middle of the session.

Chip and AI stocks led the declines. Nvidia fell by between about 6 and 7 per cent, and its fall was linked to a subpoena from California as part of an investigation into whether its advanced semiconductors ended up in China. Memory chipmaker SanDisk fell by between 14 and 15 per cent depending on the report, while Micron dropped 12 per cent. Meta, Amazon and Microsoft also fell. In Japan, the investor SoftBank had its worst day in more than two years, down 14.5 per cent in Tokyo.

Markets elsewhere also fell. Asian markets had recorded their worst week since 2020. Japan's Nikkei index dropped about 5 per cent and South Korea's Kospi fell by between about 5 and 5.5 per cent, depending on the source. Taiwan's stock market fell by roughly 6 to 6.7 per cent, and the chair of its stock exchange pledged to ensure the stable operation of capital markets. In Europe, Germany's Dax was reported down 2 per cent and the UK's FTSE 100 down 0.5 per cent. The pan-European Stoxx 600 fell 1.9 per cent on Friday, and London's blue-chip index hit a five-month low, with a weekly fall of 5.1 per cent, its biggest since the start of the Iran war.

Analysts offered different explanations. Ed Moya, a strategist at the market intelligence firm AlphaSense, told reporters that the listing had not restored confidence in the wider AI market and that investors were asking how much they were willing to pay for growth. David Miller, chief investment officer at Catalyst Funds, said: "Investors still believe in AI but are becoming more selective about what they will pay for that growth." Chris Beauchamp of the trading platform IG suggested that Anthropic could be draining liquidity from the market, as investors sell other shares to fund purchases of its stock.

The outlook is uncertain. Intropic, a London-based index rebalance analytics firm, estimates that index-tracking funds could buy up to 30 per cent of Anthropic's shares by year-end, according to reporting. Large debuts are often volatile. SpaceX, which held the record for the biggest IPO before Anthropic, rose nearly 50 per cent in its first three days but fell below its offer price a month later. On Friday it was down about 7.4 per cent.

Reports also said that fears US central bankers might raise interest rates to fight inflation had lifted the dollar to a 19-month high, pushed gold towards its worst week since March and sent bitcoin below $60,000 for the first time since early July.
