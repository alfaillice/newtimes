Young Germans are due to join nationwide rallies on Friday against conscription and what they see as the growing militarisation of their country, according to Al Jazeera, which spoke to two teenagers ahead of the protests. The demonstrations target the German government's plans to expand the Bundeswehr, the German Armed Forces, and the rising share of public money going to defence.

The Military Service Modernisation Act came into effect on 1 January this year. It sets new targets to grow the army by the mid-2030s, to about 260,000 full-time personnel plus 200,000 reservists. Al Jazeera reports that the expansion began in 2022, after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine, and that Berlin is also increasing defence budgets and the surveillance powers of its intelligence services. The Ministry of Defence says its recruitment efforts are yielding positive results. According to official figures cited by Al Jazeera, the Bundeswehr has already exceeded the statutory force build-up range for 2026, with personnel in October about 6,400 higher than in the same month last year.

The recruitment drive has reached young people directly. Al Jazeera reports that by the end of October, nearly 600,000 letters had been sent to young Germans this year: about 286,800 to young men and 271,700 to women and people of other genders. Only men are obliged to complete the military registration form.

Phil Werring, an 18-year-old high school student from Münster in western Germany, received a letter last month. After ignoring the first one, he got a second, warning him of a €250 fine if he did not respond. He filled in the mandatory questions only, which took around 15 minutes, knowing that this could increase his chances of being called for a military check-up. He told Al Jazeera that the questionnaire had many personal questions and made him angry. "I realised how serious the government is about taking these steps to reintroduce compulsory military service," he said, adding that he may have no choice but to serve.

Al Jazeera reports that the protests are led in part by the Gen Z Regelt (Gen Z Sorts It), a loose alliance that organised a large demonstration and has also been involved in school strikes. Nearly 20,000 people had signed up across 40 cities, including Berlin, Cologne, Frankfurt and Munich, according to the outlet. Friday's events will also include thousands of students taking part in protests organised by the youth-led alliance School Strike Against Conscription.

Linus Schneider, 16, from Munich, Bavaria, which is one of Germany's wealthiest states, said he worried that more money for rearmament would leave less for schools. He described leaking roofs at his school for three years and buckets of water in the sports hall, which sometimes made gym class impossible. He said that he is in trouble with his head teacher for taking part in the protests but still plans to join the rallies. "We don't want our future to be decided by the military or the state," he told Al Jazeera.

Al Jazeera cites the current draft budget for 2027, which is to be agreed later this month, as projecting defence spending of €109.7bn, up from €82.7bn the year before. The Ministry for Education and Family is projected to receive €15.9bn, down from €16.6bn this year.

Sebastian Koos, a sociologist at the University of Konstanz who studies youth and protest movements, told Al Jazeera that the wave of youth protest across Europe had been an important spark for the German movement. He said the cost of living and education cuts were shared concerns, but that conscription makes the situation for young Germans unique. He also said the protesters' many demands, which include economic and social inequality, right-wing extremism and climate change, can leave young people feeling hopeless.

Werring, who is still deciding what to study, said he is interested in the medical sector. "I want to help people, not shoot or harm anyone," he said. He plans to join the rally in his local area on Friday.
