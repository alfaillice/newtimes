Resident doctors are fully qualified doctors who are completing postgraduate clinical training in a hospital or general practice under the supervision of a senior doctor. Their union, the British Medical Association (BMA), held a ballot and its members voted in favour of industrial action after the union rejected a recommended pay rise of 3.5% from an independent pay body.

Dr Steven Montgomery, chair of the BMA's Northern Ireland Resident Doctors Committee, said doctors should not have to strike a second time, but that the issues that first took them to the picket lines remain unresolved. "Our pay is eroding and we need to address that in order to retain young doctors in Northern Ireland," he said. He added that members want full pay restoration, and that the annual rise they are due would not make up for the value their pay has lost over many years. He also said the BMA was willing to discuss with the minister and the Department of Health how that erosion could be addressed.

Consultants and specialist, associate specialist and speciality grade (SAS) doctors will also take 24-hour action from 07.00 GMT on Monday. SAS doctors are specialist doctors employed by health trusts. Both groups will provide what is known as Christmas Day cover, meaning most routine and elective services will be cancelled while full emergency cover stays in place.
