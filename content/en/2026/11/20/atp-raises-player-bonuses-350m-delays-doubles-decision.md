The ATP, the body that governs the men's professional tour, has promised players rewards of at least $350m in 2028, according to BBC Sport. However, the board has delayed a decision on contested changes to doubles until next month, the broadcaster reported.

Under the plan described by BBC Sport, the bonus pool will rise from the current $21.5m to $50m in 2028. Payments will go to players ranked in the world's top 30 at the start of each Masters 1000 event. The world number one will receive the largest bonus, $2m for each Masters event he plays.

A further $25m will be shared among the world's top 250 players at the end of each season, with the aim that everyone receives $100,000. BBC Sport said this would make a significant difference to the finances of lower-ranked players.

The rules on injuries will also change. Players will no longer be penalised so directly for missing tournaments through injury. BBC Sport cited world number two Jannik Sinner as an example. He won five Masters events in a row between March and May, but may qualify for only 25% of his bonus because he has not played since Wimbledon.

The doubles proposals have faced fierce opposition from players. They include halving draw sizes and cutting the share of prize money going to doubles from 20% to 10%. In October, more than 150 men's doubles players wrote an open letter urging the ATP to reconsider what they called “drastic” changes. Doubles players have said the changes would make it “effectively impossible” for anyone outside the top 30 to make a living from the sport.

ATP chairman Andrea Gaudenzi told BBC Sport that the governing body needed “a bit more time to reach consensus”. He said he hoped to find the right balance between the demands of players and tournaments. He still believes that at least some doubles money should be redirected towards singles players, arguing that the tour, like any entertainment business, cannot ignore fans and broadcasters.

Britain's Henry Patten, who received the year-end number one trophy with Harri Heliovaara, was unhappy that Gaudenzi and chief executive Eno Polo were not present at the presentation. According to BBC Sport, Gaudenzi told Patten he had no intention of undermining their achievement. He added that he and Polo had spent twelve hours a day negotiating the deal.

The ATP board also approved the creation of ATP Ventures, which will bring the Tour's media, data and central sponsorship rights under one roof. BBC Sport said this could help the ATP and the women's WTA Tour move towards pooling their resources in a joint venture called “Tennis Ventures”.

BBC Sport described the length of the season as the most difficult issue to resolve. Only 33 days separate this year's Davis Cup Finals from the start of the 2027 season in Australia. The 2028 schedule is likely to be released in late January, but Gaudenzi warned against expecting big changes to the off-season in 2028. He said the off-season would improve but that the plan would take a few years to carry out.
