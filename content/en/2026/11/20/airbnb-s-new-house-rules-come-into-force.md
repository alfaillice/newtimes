Airbnb's revised house rules have now come into force, the BBC reports. The changes, announced last month, limit what hosts can reasonably ask guests to do during a stay. Hosts can choose from seven standard house rules, covering matters such as how many people may stay overnight and whether pets or smoking are allowed. They can then add their own rules, but according to the BBC, guests should not normally be asked to wash up, strip the beds, take out the rubbish or avoid using utilities.

Airbnb has begun reviewing listings and is emailing hosts 'who need to take action', the broadcaster says. A host who wants to keep a rule outside the standard list can appeal to Airbnb and argue that it is necessary. If the platform does not approve the rule and the host keeps it anyway, Airbnb will show guests a warning on the listing page, such as 'includes cleaning tasks', the BBC reports.

Airbnb argues that chores can make customers hesitate before booking and that clearer house rules will attract more clients, the BBC says. Some guests have welcomed the change. Jessica Newhook, who booked a property in Florida for a family reunion of 11 people, told the BBC that they were expected to leave it 'move-in ready for the cleaners', despite paying a $250 (£187) cleaning fee. 'We had rules about everything and it was not relaxing at all,' she said.

Hosts are less convinced. Lisa Clewes, who runs a holiday let in Wales, told the BBC that her guests once left broken glasses, drinks cans, pans of food and fish guts in the garden after a dinner party. She said cleaners typically need four hours to turn around her three-bedroom property, and that extra cleaning could raise her costs, which she may pass on to her customers. Fiona Venter, an Edinburgh-based holiday home owner, told the BBC that she considered the new rules nonsense and that she would not accept bookings from guests who refuse to take their rubbish away. She also argued that higher cleaning costs would be unfair to guests who clean up after themselves.

The new rules come on top of frustration among owners over increases to Airbnb's commission rates last year, the BBC notes. Many hosts are investing in their own websites and social media accounts to win direct bookings and avoid platform fees. Matt Porsz, who hosts and manages other Airbnb properties full-time, told the BBC earlier that the review system encourages both hosts and guests to behave reasonably.

The BBC's report does not say how many hosts will pass cleaning costs on to guests, or how many listings Airbnb is likely to flag.
