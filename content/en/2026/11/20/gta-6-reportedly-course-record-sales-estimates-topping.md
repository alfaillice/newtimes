The BBC reports that Grand Theft Auto 6 is estimated to have become the biggest entertainment launch in history after its debut on Thursday, following a wait of 13 years. The broadcaster cites analytics platform Sensor Tower, which estimates that pre-orders and first-day sales totalled more than 24.5 million units, bringing in about $2.36bn (£1.8bn). The dollar and pound figures are as given by the BBC.

For comparison, the BBC notes that this year's highest-grossing film so far, Spider-Man: Brand New Day, made $2.5bn over its entire run in cinemas.

Rockstar, the New York-based studio behind the game, and its parent company Take-Two have not released sales details. According to the BBC, both said demand has been "unprecedented". The broadcaster says it has contacted both companies for comment. The sales figures are therefore estimates from a third party rather than official numbers.

Sensor Tower says its estimates are based on email receipt data and other statistical sources that track millions of people each day, as well as public profiles on major gaming platforms. According to the firm, around three quarters of sales have been for the PlayStation 5 and the rest for Xbox.

Another market research firm, Niko Partners, expects the game to make more than $3bn in worldwide sales this year, according to the BBC. The BBC adds that this would still fall short of the estimated $4.5bn, adjusted for inflation, earned by the 1939 film Gone with the Wind.

The game is set in Leonida, a Florida-inspired state, and follows the characters Lucia and Jason. The BBC reports that it is estimated to have cost more than $1bn to develop, which would make it the most expensive video game ever made. Reviewers have been positive: the BBC quotes the entertainment site IGN describing it as an "uncompromisingly engineered" masterpiece.

The launch also reportedly broke Virgin Media O2's evening traffic record, as players downloaded the game's 212GB file on consoles. The BBC notes that the game's graphic content has led to censorship in Japan, and that it is not approved for sale in mainland China, as with earlier instalments.

Rockstar has not announced a release date for the PC version or for the multiplayer expansion, which will allow players to take on missions together online. The BBC notes that the multiplayer mode in the previous game, GTA 5 from 2013, brought in substantial extra revenue through in-game purchases, and suggests the same could happen here.
