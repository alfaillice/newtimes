Begoña Gómez, the wife of Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez, addressed the opening rally of the Spanish Socialist Workers' Party (PSOE) campaign on Thursday, according to Europa Press. Speaking in Seville ahead of Sánchez and the regional leader María Jesús Montero, she took the stage without prior announcement. It was the first time she had spoken at a PSOE event.

Gómez is awaiting trial on alleged offences of influence peddling and embezzlement. Her speech did not address the case directly. She said she had been under investigation, that she had received messages of support from across Spain and from the United States, and that she was tired of people who judge her and tell her how to act.

The rally took place at the Fibes congress and exhibition centre. The Socialist organisers estimated the audience at around 4,000 people, inside and outside the hall, many of them wearing red T-shirts with the slogan 'The strength of women'. Gómez described herself as "a rank-and-file militant" and presented her speech as a feminist message. "Nobody decides for us," she told the audience, in a translation of her Spanish remarks, "we define our own path, whatever anyone thinks." She called on voters to back the party at the general election on 29 November and described the PSOE as the party behind every advance for women. She ended by embracing Montero before handing over the microphone.

The same day, Santiago Abascal, leader of the far-right Vox party, criticised the intervention at a rally in Huesca, in the north-east of Spain. "They have no shame," he said, in a translation of his Spanish remarks. He listed the cases around Sánchez's circle, including former minister José Luis Ábalos and aide Koldo García, who are in prison, and Santos Cerdán, the former organisation secretary of the PSOE, who is on provisional release. Abascal also referred to a European Public Prosecutor's Office investigation into alleged fraud with EU funds for electronic bracelets used in gender-violence cases, which led to searches at the Ministry of Equality. He said a Vox government would abolish that ministry.

A poll by the Centre for Sociological Research (CIS), a state body, published the same day, gives the PSOE victory and rules out an absolute majority for the conservative People's Party (PP) and Vox. Most other polls, according to Europa Press, point in a different direction. The Europa Press reports do not include a response from the PSOE to Abascal's remarks.
