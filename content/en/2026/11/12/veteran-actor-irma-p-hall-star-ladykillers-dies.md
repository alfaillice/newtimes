Irma P Hall, the veteran actor best known for her roles in *Soul Food* and the Coen brothers' *The Ladykillers*, has died at the age of 91, according to The Guardian. The newspaper reports that Hall died on Tuesday and that her family confirmed the death in a statement describing her as "a beloved actress, author, poet, educator and public speaker whose contributions touched countless lives throughout her remarkable career". The Guardian does not give the place of death.

According to the same report, Hall was born in Texas in 1935, the daughter of saxophonist Samuel Player, and grew up with a strong interest in the arts. She spent 27 years as a high school teacher before moving into acting in the early 1980s. The Guardian's headline calls her a Dallas schoolteacher, but its main text places her teaching career in Houston. The article does not resolve the discrepancy.

Her early screen work in the 1980s included *On Valentine's Day* and *They Still Call Me Bruce*. She then appeared alongside Dolly Parton in *Straight Talk* and John Goodman in *The Babe*. The Guardian says her career moved to a new level in the mid-1990s.

That breakthrough came with the 1997 drama *Soul Food*, in which she played Big Mama Joseph, the strict matriarch of three daughters played by Vanessa Williams, Vivica A Fox and Nia Long. The Guardian reports that she won the outstanding supporting actress award at the NAACP Image Awards for the role. She returned to the character in a television spin-off that ran from 2000 to 2004.

The article also lists her work with Robin Williams in the comedy drama *Patch Adams* and with Clint Eastwood in the crime film *Midnight in the Garden of Good and Evil*. In 2004 she received a special jury prize at the Cannes Film Festival for *The Ladykillers*, in which she played the sharp-tongued landlady Marva Munson. The same year she appeared in Michael Mann's thriller *Collateral* as the no-nonsense mother of Jamie Foxx's character. Her later credits, according to the Guardian, include *Bad Lieutenant: Port of Call New Orleans*, *My Son, My Son, What Have Ye Done* and the Sundance TV series *Hap and Leonard*.

The Guardian also highlights her work for the arts community. She helped establish the Dallas Minority Repertory Theatre, and since 2015 the Irma P Hall Black Theatre awards have been presented each year to recognise artistic achievement in the Dallas-Fort Worth area. For many years she was also church mother at the Inspiring Body of Christ church in Dallas.

Her daughter, Irma Hall Crayton, told the Guardian: "She started as a teacher in the classroom but ended up being a teacher to the world." The article does not say when or where a funeral or memorial will take place.
