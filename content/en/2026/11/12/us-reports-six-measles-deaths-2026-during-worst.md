The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) has reported six measles-related deaths in the country so far this year, according to figures posted on its website on Thursday, The Guardian reports. The total is twice the number recorded in 2025, as the United States faces its most severe resurgence of the disease in decades. Measles was declared eliminated in the US in 2000.

The Guardian cites data from the Johns Hopkins University International Vaccine Access Center tracker, which counts more than 5,400 cases nationwide. That makes this the country's largest outbreak since 1991. Active outbreaks have been reported in almost every US state.

Pennsylvania has been the hardest hit. Officials there have confirmed six measles-associated deaths this year, according to The Guardian. The CDC did not say which states accounted for the six deaths in its national count, so the two sets of figures cannot be matched directly from the published data. The Guardian also reports that a child died in New York on election day and that an infant died days later in Wisconsin. The Wisconsin Department of Health Services confirmed the infant's death. New York recorded its first known measles-related death in more than three decades.

The figures follow a change in how the CDC tracks fatalities. The agency, which operates under US Health Secretary Robert F Kennedy Jr, now uses data from the National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS), a body that tracks mortality trends over time. According to a note published on the CDC's website on 4 September, this is the new method. The Guardian, citing Reuters reporting, says Kennedy argued without evidence that two of the Pennsylvania deaths may not have been caused by measles. It adds that he asked Erica Schwartz, the CDC director, not to include those deaths in the official tally. The CDC changed its data source on measles deaths the day after the Reuters report was published.

Measles can be prevented with the measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) vaccine, which offers about 97% protection after two doses. The Guardian reports that the US could lose its measles elimination status this week. Losing that status would place the country alongside Canada and several countries in Europe and Asia that had eliminated measles before it returned.
