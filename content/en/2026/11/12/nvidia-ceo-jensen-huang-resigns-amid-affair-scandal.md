Jensen Huang has resigned as chief executive of Nvidia, the chipmaker whose processors power much of the artificial intelligence (AI) boom, after reports of extramarital relationships with an alleged Chinese intelligence asset and a senior employee. The board said on Thursday that Colette Kress, the company's chief financial officer, will serve as interim chief executive.

In its statement, the board said Huang had also left his seat on the board and would not remain a director. Kress will keep her finance role while leading the company until a permanent successor is found. The board is searching for candidates both inside and outside Nvidia and has named Harvey C. Jones as its first-ever chair. The statement paid tribute to Huang, saying: "Jensen's contributions to Nvidia are profound."

Huang, 63, co-founded the company in 1993 with Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem. He owns about 3.6% of Nvidia, a stake that Bloomberg values at $145bn, according to the Guardian.

The affairs came to light over several days. Huang admitted in an email to employees on Sunday that he had had an affair and said he would cooperate with an internal investigation. He did not name the woman, Alice Huang, who is no relation to him and was a former executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based firm that is a key Nvidia partner in Southeast Asia. US intelligence services reportedly suspect her of being a Chinese asset. Huang denies knowing she allegedly worked for the Chinese government. The board hired the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to investigate.

Late on Wednesday, the New York Times reported that Huang also had an affair with a senior Nvidia employee. The board met that night. Earlier in the week, President Donald Trump, a close ally of Huang, said Huang had told him he did not know the woman was an intelligence operative. Trump has praised Huang, calling him "an honorable guy".

Nvidia's shares fell more than 10% in premarket trading on Thursday, according to Al Jazeera and the Guardian. The Guardian reported that they had already slid about 7% after the Wall Street Journal said the board was preparing to oust him. The sources differ on the size of the loss: Al Jazeera put the fall in market value at more than $600bn this week, while the Guardian's live coverage said more than $575bn had been wiped off at the start of trading.

Analysts gave mixed views. Gil Luria of D.A. Davidson said Kress is well respected by investors, but the task ahead is daunting because Huang made much of the company's decisions. Ben Barringer of Quilter Cheviot said it was unclear whether Huang would keep any ceremonial or non-executive role, since the outcome of the investigation is not yet known. Lindsey Stewart of Morningstar said governance and succession planning would now matter more for other founder-led companies.

Kress joined Nvidia in September 2013, after nearly 25 years in finance at technology firms including Cisco and Microsoft. The board said she had played a key role in the company's growth.
