Spain's population stood at 49,935,642 on 1 October 2026, the highest figure in the country's historical series. It rose by 130,223 people during the third quarter of the year, according to provisional data from the National Statistics Institute (INE), Spain's official statistics agency, published on Thursday in its Continuous Population Statistics (ECP).

Migration accounted for almost all of the increase. The INE says people born abroad made up 98.8% of the growth. The foreign-born population, at 10,433,091, is larger than the foreign national population because many people born abroad have acquired Spanish nationality. The number of foreign nationals rose by 86,883 during the quarter, to 7,518,237, while the number of Spanish nationals grew by 43,340.

Over the past year, the population grew by an estimated 435,551 people. The INE's series for 1 October of each year shows 48,486,665 residents in 2023, 48,999,880 in 2024, 49,500,091 in 2025 and 49,935,642 in 2026.

The population grew in every autonomous community and autonomous city. The largest increases were in the Valencian Community (0.48%), the Community of Madrid (0.46%) and the autonomous city of Melilla (0.36%).

The most common nationalities among immigrants arriving in Spain during the quarter were Colombian, with 33,200 arrivals, Spanish, with 23,600, and Venezuelan, with 19,000. Among those leaving the country, the largest groups were Colombian, with 9,100 departures, Moroccan, with 8,900, and Spanish, with 7,500.

The number of households also reached a record, rising by 69,162 in the quarter to 19,953,529 on 1 October.

The INE stresses that the figures are provisional. Each quarter the agency revises the series until a new population census is published at the end of the year. That census then becomes the definitive base for population figures, which will be included before the next ECP release in February of the following year.
