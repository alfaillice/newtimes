Democrat Scott Colom has won Mississippi’s race for the US Senate, defeating the Republican incumbent, Cindy Hyde-Smith. The contest was too close to call for more than a week after election day. The Associated Press (AP) called the race on Wednesday, after Colom’s lead had grown to 4,669 votes as local officials counted absentee and provisional ballots.

The US Senate is the upper chamber of Congress in Washington. Mississippi is one of the most reliably Republican states in the country, and Hyde-Smith had been running for re-election there. According to the BBC, President Donald Trump, a Republican, won the state in the 2024 election by more than 22 points. Hyde-Smith was first elected to local office as a Democrat and joined the Republican Party in 2010, according to the Guardian.

According to figures from CBS, cited by the BBC, Colom took 49.2% of the vote, Hyde-Smith 48.7% and an independent candidate 2.1%. Hyde-Smith conceded in a video posted on social media on Wednesday. “I respect the decision of Mississippi voters,” she said, adding that she wished Colom well.

Colom is a local prosecutor. In 2015 he unseated a nearly 30-year incumbent to become the first Black district attorney for the state’s 16th circuit, according to the Guardian. The Guardian also reports that he is the first Black man popularly elected to the Senate from Mississippi. During Reconstruction, the period after the Civil War, state legislatures chose senators. In 1874 the Mississippi legislature elected Blanche Kelso Bruce, who had been born into slavery; he served from 1875 to 1881. The Guardian gives 1881 as the last time the state had a Black senator, while the BBC describes a gap of more than 140 years. Mississippi has not elected a Democrat to the Senate since 1982, when John Stennis won a seventh term. The Guardian notes that Stennis was a segregationist from a very different Democratic Party.

Colom ran a campaign built on viral social media videos. One ad, released in mid-October, used Hyde-Smith’s remark that voters had “so many proteins to choose from” when she was asked about the price of beef. The Guardian reports that the ad was viewed more than 20 million times. The BBC says Colom also gave her the nickname “Cindy High Prices” and attacked her support for Trump’s tariffs, and that he visited all 82 counties in the state. Hyde-Smith’s campaign described Colom as a puppet of the billionaire donor George Soros, according to the BBC. The BBC also reports that she was criticised for making few public appearances.

Colom’s victory is part of a wider Democratic result in the midterm elections. The BBC reports that Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate. The Guardian reports that Democrats will hold at least 55 Senate seats, their largest midterm gain since 1986. The BBC says the race in Alaska has not yet been called. Polls cited by the BBC suggest voters were frustrated by the economy and the war with Iran.

Biden had nominated Colom to be a federal judge in the northern district of Mississippi, but Hyde-Smith blocked a confirmation hearing, the Guardian reports. Mississippi has no automatic recount law. A recount can only take place if a court orders one after a lawsuit.
