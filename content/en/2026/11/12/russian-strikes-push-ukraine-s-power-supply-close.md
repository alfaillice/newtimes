Ukraine's electricity system is close to breaking point after months of Russian strikes on energy facilities, the BBC reports. Rolling power cuts have left many households in darkness and cold as winter sets in.

The BBC says Russia has stepped up attacks on the energy grid since October, and that Ukraine has responded by striking Russian oil refineries. Since August, the broadcaster reports, there have been almost daily attacks on power facilities, and several energy workers have been killed each week.

A single mother living on the seventh floor of a Kyiv block told the BBC that in recent days her home had had power for only four or five hours, at different times of day, with two hours on and eight off. The broadcaster reports that some families in Kyiv face outages of up to 20 hours. Officials are concerned that daily life will quickly become unsustainable for millions of Ukrainians if supplies cannot be kept at a minimum of four hours a day, and some officials privately say that threshold is already close, according to the BBC. The cuts began earlier than last winter and are lasting longer.

Kyiv has been the worst affected city. According to the BBC, metro trains and trolleybuses have at times been brought to a halt. Across the country, hundreds of thousands of people have been without power in recent weeks, the broadcaster says.

The BBC visited one of Ukraine's largest power stations, which it did not name for security reasons. The broadcaster describes the plant as barely standing after a recent ballistic missile strike, with its turbines buried in rubble and the site completely offline. Volodymyr Kovalenko, who was on duty in the control room when the missiles hit at 3am, told the BBC: "It was a very powerful attack. We'd never experienced anything like it."

Repairs are being slowed by air raid alerts that the BBC says came every 15 to 30 minutes, seven times during its four-hour visit. Workers stop and take cover, sheltering in the main bunker or, when there is no time, in the foundations of the plant's chimneys. Vadym Bobyr, who is leading the recovery effort, described the difficulty of repeatedly stopping work.

The BBC reports that Maxim Timchenko, chief executive of DTEK, Ukraine's largest private energy company, said rebuilding the station would take at least until next summer, because workers under sustained attack have only one or two productive hours a day. He also said Russia is putting the degraded grid under greater pressure than last year and that air defences cannot protect all critical infrastructure.

President Volodymyr Zelensky has said that Ukraine still has enough generating capacity to meet demand, according to the BBC. The main problem, the broadcaster reports, is delivering that power to homes and businesses, since many power lines and substations have been hit. The BBC also reports that Russia is using so-called "waiter drones" that attach to pylons and cables and stay undetected until Moscow chooses to detonate them.

The BBC says many residents are leaving Kyiv or Ukraine altogether to avoid months of bitter cold. Its report did not say when the power cuts are expected to end.
