The United States Department of State has released a 53-page report on how Washington has handled more than $12bn in revenue from Venezuelan oil and mineral resources, Al Jazeera reports. The document was published on Thursday, almost a year after US forces raided the Venezuelan capital, Caracas, in January and captured the country's leader, Nicolás Maduro. He and his wife now face charges related to drug trafficking and torture in New York City. According to the report, cited by Al Jazeera, the aim is to "promote transparency and accountability in the management of these funds".

Al Jazeera explains that Delcy Rodríguez, who was Venezuela's vice president at the time, took over after Maduro's removal. She has since reached agreements with Washington under which the United States took a direct stake in profits from Venezuelan oil and mineral exports. Since then, the Trump administration has sought to control directly who buys Venezuelan natural resources and who earns the profits from those sales. In January, President Donald Trump signed an executive order creating special bank accounts to hold this revenue, which would later be paid back to Venezuela in a controlled way.

The network is also politically sensitive. Al Jazeera notes that Trump highlighted the expected oil revenue during his campaigning before the midterm elections held earlier this month. Venezuelan rights activists, the broadcaster reports, have voiced concern that Washington may give too much weight to oil income and too little attention to supporting democratic reform after Maduro's removal.

## What the report says

The report covers flows into and out of US-controlled accounts between 3 January and 30 September 2026. According to Al Jazeera, the United States received more than $12.26bn from "verified and approved entities" carrying out authorised activities in Venezuela in that period. Most of this came from crude oil and petroleum-based products, but deposits also included transactions involving gold, other minerals and fertilisers.

According to the same account, more than $12bn had been paid out by 30 September, leaving about $169m in the account. The first account was opened at Qatar National Bank in January to receive payments from buyers of authorised Venezuelan oil and minerals. About $500m was initially deposited there, and almost all of it was distributed back to Venezuelan entities by late January. A separate account, controlled directly by the US Department of the Treasury, was then set up to handle later transactions.

That Treasury account received more than $12bn from about 20 depositors, whose names are redacted in the report, Al Jazeera says. Around 99 per cent of the money was linked to oil purchases. By the end of September, nearly all of it had been transferred to a group of Venezuelan banks for foreign exchange interventions. In these operations, banks sell dollars to reduce the amount of local currency in circulation and support the value of the bolívar, Venezuela's currency.

The report gives a split of the money paid out in Venezuela: about $9.4bn went to "legal entities" and $2.9bn to "individuals". Al Jazeera reports that the document is heavily redacted and does not identify the final recipients.

## Oversight

The State Department has contracted KPMG, an auditing firm based in Washington, DC, to examine the transactions, according to Al Jazeera. The broadcaster's account does not say when that review is expected to conclude or whether its findings will be published.
