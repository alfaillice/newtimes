Pope Leo XIV has urged Peru's Catholic bishops to respond to abuse committed by members of the clergy, or in a Church context, with truth, justice, forgiveness and reparation. He also called for a stronger commitment to preventing abuse and protecting the most vulnerable.

The Pope made the remarks on Thursday morning in the chapel of the archbishop's palace in Lima, Peru's capital, during his sixth international apostolic trip. According to Vatican News, he had landed in Lima on Wednesday evening after visiting Uruguay and Argentina.

Speaking about abuse, which he described as a reality that has caused great pain, Leo said the Church must act as Jesus would, “with truth, with justice, with forgiveness, with reparation” (translated from Spanish). He called for a culture of prevention and protection of those most exposed to such crimes.

The Pope also asked the bishops to preserve the unity of the Church. He recalled sitting in the same hall in 2018, during the visit of Pope Francis, and echoed his predecessor's call that bishops should not be trapped in divisions that distort and diminish their vocation. He said communion is the foundation of the credibility of the Gospel message.

Building bridges, encouraging dialogue and helping to heal the wounds that divide Peruvian society were, in his words, part of the bishops' mission. The address was built around the theme of the visit, opening hearts. The Pope said the Church must cross the mountains of indifference, the deserts of individualism and the distances that separate people.

He also reviewed the legacy of saints linked to Peru, including Toribio de Mogrovejo and Rosa of Lima, whom he presented as a school of holiness and examples of closeness to the peripheries and the most disadvantaged.

In a separate address at the Basilica of St John the Apostle and Evangelist, which faces Lima's Plaza Mayor, he called for humility so that people could acknowledge their own failings, ask forgiveness and take responsibility for the wounds they have caused.

He set out five attitudes for being the Church and building what he called a civilisation of love rooted in the common good: looking to Christ, reconciling, being true artisans of peace, building communion and being missionaries. Quoting Francis, he spoke of a “disarmed and disarming” peace.

He closed his address to the bishops with three requests: not to stop walking with their people, to walk together in episcopal communion as witnesses of the unity they proclaim, and to keep opening their hearts to God, to their brothers and sisters, and to the people they serve.
