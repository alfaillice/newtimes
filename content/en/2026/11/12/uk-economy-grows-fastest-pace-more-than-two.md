The UK economy grew by 0.7% in the three months to September, its fastest expansion in more than two years, according to official figures from the Office for National Statistics (ONS), the country's statistics agency. The rate matched economists' expectations and was the strongest since the first quarter of 2024, when growth was also 0.7%.

Almost all of the expansion came from services, the largest part of the economy, which includes consultancy, computer programming, hotels and restaurants. The information and communication sector grew by 2.1% in the quarter, mainly through computer programming and consultancy. The ONS said this sector had been the largest driver of growth so far in 2026, and that businesses linked to artificial intelligence (AI) and cloud computing had reported the biggest rises in turnover.

Construction was the main drag. It fell sharply and accounts for about 6% of economic activity. Work on new projects has dropped by more than 7% since mid-2025, and private housebuilding is down 30% from its 2022 high, according to The Guardian. Production grew by 0.1%.

Monthly figures were also stronger than expected. The economy grew by 0.2% in September, beating forecasts of no growth, while August was revised down from 0.1% to zero.

Chancellor John Healey, the UK's finance minister, pointed to the 'remarkable resilience' of the economy in the face of global conflicts, and said he hoped the figures would 'bring back confidence in Britain', the BBC reported. The Guardian reported that he also said growth was the fastest in almost ten years, excluding the Covid-19 pandemic years. The BBC reported that Prime Minister Andy Burnham called the figures 'welcome news'.

The ONS's chief economist, Grant Fitzner, said growth could have been even stronger without the war in the Middle East. He also warned that households may not yet feel better off, and that prices could soon rise faster than wages, the BBC said.

Other economists were more cautious. Jake Finney of the consultancy PwC expects growth to slow in the coming months unless energy markets stabilise, and a sharp rise in energy bills is forecast for January. The ONS said wholesale electricity prices were about double their level a year earlier, and petrol in the UK has risen above £1.70 a litre. The Bank of England raised its main interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point to 4% last week, according to The Guardian, and some analysts think it could raise rates again in December. The Resolution Foundation think tank said the economy's resilience 'will be severely tested' over the coming months.

The Office for Budget Responsibility, which forecasts the public finances, had expected growth of 1.2% this year. The Guardian said that forecast already looks out of date.

The TUC, the umbrella body for trade unions, said working people are still under pressure from rising energy bills linked to the conflict in Iran. Its general secretary, Paul Nowak, said that despite the good news, households were still struggling with those costs.
