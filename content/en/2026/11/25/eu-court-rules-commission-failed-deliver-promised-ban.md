The General Court, part of the Court of Justice of the European Union, has ruled that the European Commission, the EU's executive, failed to follow up on its promise to phase out cages for farmed animals. The judgment, delivered on Wednesday, means the Commission must now set a timeline for ending cages for calves, rabbits, ducks and other animals. The court did not say by when it must do so.

The case goes back to a European Citizens' Initiative (ECI), a mechanism that lets EU citizens petition the bloc for new laws or changes to existing ones. The campaign, called End the Cage Age, was submitted to the Commission on 2 October 2020 with 1,397,113 statements of support, above the one million needed for a formal response. On 30 June 2021 the Commission replied that it would table a legislative proposal by the end of 2023 to phase out, and finally prohibit, cages for all the animals covered by the initiative. That deadline passed without a proposal.

In 2024, a group of non-governmental organisations (NGOs) led by Compassion in World Farming, an animal welfare charity, took the Commission to court. The court found that the Commission must honour the commitments it made in response to an ECI, even when the college of commissioners changes. It rejected a separate request from the NGOs for access to internal Commission documents on the delays.

The ruling has limits. It does not set a date for action, and the Commission could still avoid acting if it formally withdrew its promise. Compassion in World Farming said the Commission had told the group in a letter that it would not withdraw the commitment, according to its director, Debbie Tripley. The coalition has decided not to appeal the case to the Court of Justice.

The Commission's animal welfare commissioner, Olivér Várhelyi, told a group of EU lawmakers on Tuesday: "the Commission will study the judgment and we will comply with it in the best possible way."

The Commission's livestock strategy, adopted on 7 July 2026, plans to propose welfare rules for laying hens and broiler chickens by the end of 2026, and new rules for pigs by June 2027. The plans include phasing out cages for chickens and replacing pig crates with pens. They give no clear timeline for rabbits, calves, ducks, geese or quail, which are all covered by the 2020 initiative.

Annamaria Pisapia, a spokesperson for End the Cage Age, a citizen-led campaign backed by more than 170 organisations, said: "This is a historic victory for animals and for democracy in Europe." The campaign wants the ban to cover all these animals and is seeking financial aid to help farmers go cage-free.
