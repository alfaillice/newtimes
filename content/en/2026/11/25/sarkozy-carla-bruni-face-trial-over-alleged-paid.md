Former French president Nicolas Sarkozy and his wife, the Italian-born former supermodel and singer Carla Bruni, are to be tried before the Paris criminal court over an alleged paid retraction by a witness, a source close to the case said on Tuesday, 24 November. The hearings are expected to take place between 3 November and 2 December 2027.

The case centres on Ziad Takieddine, a businessman who had accused Sarkozy of taking money from the Libyan leader Muammar Gaddafi to fund his 2007 presidential campaign. Takieddine died in Lebanon last year. Some reports describe him as Libyan and others as Franco-Lebanese.

Sarkozy, who lost his bid for re-election in 2012, is already facing a separate appeal over the Libyan financing allegations. In September 2025 he was sentenced to five years in prison, becoming the first former French president to be jailed. He spent 20 days behind bars before being released pending appeal. The appeal court is expected to rule within days. Sarkozy has always denied wrongdoing.

Takieddine's own account changed several times. In 2016 he said he had helped deliver up to €5m in cash from Gaddafi to Sarkozy and to the former president's chief of staff in 2006 and 2007. He had also said he held written proof that the campaign was heavily financed from Tripoli, and that payments of €50m continued after Sarkozy took office. In November 2020, in an interview with French media, he withdrew his accusations, before changing his position again. At the time, Sarkozy was reported to have said that “the truth is finally coming out”.

The new case rests on allegations that Sarkozy and Bruni asked the photo agent Mimi Marchand, a well-known figure in the celebrity press, to pay the witness to retract. Bruni is suspected in particular of having requested a secret phone line at the end of 2019 so that she could communicate with Marchand about the Takieddine case. Marchand was given an 18-month suspended prison sentence in 2025 for blackmailing a television presenter.

Both Sarkozy and Bruni are to face charges of criminal conspiracy to commit fraud as part of an organised gang, and of concealment of witness tampering. One report describes the second charge as benefiting from witness tampering. Marchand will be tried for witness tampering, criminal conspiracy to commit fraud as part of an organised gang, and acts preparing corruption of foreign judicial personnel. The defendants face a maximum of ten years in prison and a fine of €150,000. All those placed under formal investigation, a pre-trial stage in French procedure, are being sent to trial. Sarkozy and Bruni deny any wrongdoing.

Sarkozy has already been definitively convicted in two other cases. In 2024 he received a one-year sentence, six months of it suspended, over the illegal financing of his 2012 campaign. In 2021 he was found guilty of trying to bribe a judge and sentenced to three years, two of them suspended. He served that sentence at home wearing an electronic tag.
