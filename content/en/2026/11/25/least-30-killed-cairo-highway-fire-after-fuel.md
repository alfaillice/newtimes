At least 30 people have been killed after a fuel tanker crashed on a ring road in Cairo on Wednesday evening, setting off a large fire that engulfed nearby vehicles. Egypt's health ministry said 19 others had been injured. Health Minister Khaled Abdel Ghaffar, who spoke separately, put the number of injured at 15. He said the death toll was preliminary and could still rise, and that he would visit the scene.

The fire broke out on a section of the ring road in the Warraq neighbourhood, in the Imbaba district, north of the old city centre. Hospitals nearby treated people for burns and smoke inhalation. The health ministry said 39 ambulances had been sent to the scene. Egyptian President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi, who holds the country's highest office, expressed his condolences to the victims in a statement. Unverified footage circulating on social media showed flames and thick black smoke rising over the highway, with several vehicles burnt out.

Officials have not explained how the fire started. State media reported that the fuel truck had overturned, spilling petrol over nearby vehicles. Reports citing people familiar with the investigation said a high-voltage cable fell in front of the tanker, causing the driver to lose control. The vehicle then crossed the central reservation, overturned and caught fire. The tanker belonged to a private contractor.

The regional public prosecutor has opened an investigation and has asked that DNA samples be taken from the bodies of the dead. Images from the scene showed a large crowd gathered on the highway after the fire was extinguished, while emergency services blocked access routes. A bulldozer was seen clearing the burnt-out wrecks. State broadcaster NMA later reported that the highway had been cleared and reopened.

Road crashes are common in Egypt, often blamed on poor maintenance and widespread disregard for traffic laws. Official figures show that nearly 6,000 people died on the country's roads last year, several hundred more than the previous year. In January, 21 people were killed and 16 others injured in a multi-vehicle collision on a road near Beni Suef, a province in southern Egypt. Egypt has also suffered several deadly fires in recent years. Fires are more frequent in informal residential areas and where infrastructure is run down, and the response is often slowed by long emergency response times and improvised rescue efforts.
