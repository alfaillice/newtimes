Colombian security forces stormed a supermarket in Cartagena, a Caribbean port known for its colonial walled old town and beach resorts, in the early hours of Wednesday. The operation ended a hostage siege that had lasted about 17 hours. President Abelardo de la Espriella said all those held inside had been rescued and that one of the gunmen had been killed.

The siege began at 08.00 local time on Tuesday (14.00 CET), when armed men entered the supermarket in a shopping centre. Authorities said it followed an attempted robbery of an armoured vehicle carrying money and valuables. Local media reported that the gunmen demanded nearly US$2m (£1.5m), two armoured vehicles and safe passage to Barranquilla, about 130km north-east of Cartagena. Those reports could not be independently verified.

During the standoff, the gunmen freed 10 hostages, including several elderly women. Sources differ on the number still held. One report put it at around 20 after those releases, while another said about 30 people had initially been held. Supermarket employees were among those feared trapped.

As a police helicopter hovered over the complex and special forces set up a perimeter, residents reported explosions. Police later said officers had used stun grenades to overpower the gunmen. Soldiers were then seen inside the building.

De la Espriella, who appeared at the scene in fatigues, described the operation as "an extraordinary joint operation" between the National Police and the armed forces. He said the gunmen had been offered the chance to surrender and had refused. The mayor of Cartagena said the hostages were "safe and sound" and would be reunited with their families before medical checks.

The president ruled out any link to guerrilla or paramilitary groups. He described the gunmen as criminals who had been caught off guard after their robbery failed and had then taken hostages.

The siege coincided with the tenth anniversary of Colombia's peace accord with the Farc guerrilla group. Despite that deal, dissident armed groups and criminal organisations still compete for control of parts of the country. On Monday, authorities blamed a dissident Farc faction for a car bomb in Villavicencio that wounded 25 people. Separately, the presidency said a "terrorist attack" on electrical infrastructure had cut power in several south-western departments, Colombia's regional divisions, and prompted extra measures to protect energy facilities.
