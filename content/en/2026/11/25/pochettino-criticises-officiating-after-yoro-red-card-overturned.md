Manchester United head coach Mauricio Pochettino has questioned the officiating in English football after the Football Association (FA), the sport's governing body in England, overturned the red card shown to defender Leny Yoro in his side's 4-0 defeat at Liverpool on Sunday. It was Pochettino's first match in charge, and he said the video assistant referee (VAR) system should have stopped the sending-off from happening at all.

Yoro was dismissed eight minutes into the match for bringing down Aleksander Isak just outside the United penalty area. On Tuesday the FA ruled the dismissal was wrongful. United insist they have conclusive evidence that Isak could not have reached the ball before goalkeeper Senne Lammens, and that Yoro should not have been punished for denying a goalscoring chance. The club also believe the VAR review was too quick and superficial, and it plans to raise the matter with the Premier League and Pro Ref, the organisation that runs elite English refereeing.

Howard Webb, the head of Pro Ref, defended the original decision before the card was rescinded. He said most people would see it as correct, because Isak was moving quickly towards the ball. He added that Lammens might have got there first, but that this was debatable and “certainly not clearly wrong.” Webb had earlier apologised to United after Erling Haaland’s winning goal for Manchester City at Old Trafford in September was allowed to stand. Pro Ref has since accepted that the goal should not have been allowed, because the VAR had wrongly judged that Enzo Fernández was not interfering with play.

Pochettino said he understood that referee Sam Barrott could make a mistake in the heat of a match. “I am very disappointed,” he said. “We have an unbelievable car, but we need to be at the level to drive [it].” He asked what the VAR officials had been doing and whether the game would now be replayed. He also said Liverpool’s opening goal, scored by Dominik Szoboszlai from the free-kick that followed the red card, should have been disallowed, because Bradley Barcola was standing too close to United’s wall.

The defeat leaves United 19th in the Premier League, England’s top division, one place above the bottom. The club has won only three games this season and faces the prospect of relegation for the first time since 1973-74. Pochettino rejected the idea that the squad is fragile, saying outsiders wanted it to look that way, and insisted the team is strong and can recover its level. Captain Bruno Fernandes said talks over a new contract were on hold until results improve. His current deal expires in the summer, although the club has the option to extend it by a further 12 months.

United’s next match is on Wednesday, away to Sporting in the Champions League, the fifth group game of the competition. Their previous group match was a 4-1 defeat at Como, which came under the previous head coach, Michael Carrick.
