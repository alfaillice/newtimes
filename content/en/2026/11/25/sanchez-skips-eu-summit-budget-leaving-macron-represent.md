Spain's prime minister, Pedro Sánchez, will not attend the European Council summit in Brussels this week. He will leave French President Emmanuel Macron to represent Spain's position, according to sources at Moncloa, the prime minister's office, and an EU diplomat. The meeting opens on Thursday 26 November, while the country is in the final days of campaigning for its general election on Sunday 29 November.

The European Council brings together the heads of state and government of the EU's member states. Its main item this week is the bloc's multiannual budget, the long-term spending plan for the Union. Sánchez has been pressing for a more ambitious budget with more expansionary European accounts. Moncloa said he would leave Macron to defend those positions in the talks.

The agenda also includes a €45 billion loan to Ukraine, the diplomat said. On Friday morning, leaders are also expected to confirm Pablo Hernández de Cos as the next president of the European Central Bank (ECB), the institution that sets monetary policy for the euro. Macron will cast Spain's vote on that appointment too, because Sánchez cannot attend.

Sánchez chose a liberal leader from another large member state rather than a centre-left colleague such as Denmark's prime minister, Mette Frederiksen, or Malta's Robert Abela, the diplomat's account indicates. Relations between Sánchez and Frederiksen have been strained over migration. Frederiksen has teamed up with Italy's prime minister, Giorgia Meloni, to demand tougher border controls after a crisis in Ceuta, the Spanish enclave on the Moroccan border. Frederiksen has also backed deportation hubs in Rwanda, a position Europe's Social Democrats are due to discuss.

The absence comes at a busy point in Spain's campaign. On Thursday evening, Sánchez is due to take part in the final televised debate with Alberto Núñez Feijóo, leader of the conservative People's Party (PP) and of the opposition, Santiago Abascal, leader of the far-right party Vox, and Ada Colau, who heads the left-wing alliance Frente Amplio. On Friday, Sánchez is scheduled to close the campaign first in Plasencia, in the province of Cáceres, and then at a final rally in the Caja Mágica in Madrid.

The sources do not say whether Sánchez's decision will affect the outcome of the summit. The diplomat said that Macron, by casting both his own vote and Spain's, will have extra leverage in bilateral negotiations among leaders.
