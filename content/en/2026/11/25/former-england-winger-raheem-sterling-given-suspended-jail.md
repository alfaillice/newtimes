Former England winger Raheem Sterling has been spared an immediate prison sentence after admitting dangerous driving while inhaling nitrous oxide, commonly known as "laughing gas", at the wheel of his car. Magistrates at Basingstoke Magistrates' Court, in southern England, on Wednesday sentenced the 31-year-old to 12 months in prison, suspended for 18 months, and ordered him to complete 100 hours of unpaid work.

The offences took place on the morning of 28 May, during the rush hour. Witnesses reported Sterling's black Lamborghini weaving between lanes on several motorways in the south of England, including the M25 and the M3, while he was seen inhaling from a balloon. Reports differ on the exact list of roads: some accounts name the M40 as well. After leaving the M3, the car left the carriageway on the A327 and hit the central reservation. The damage was described in court as substantial and the vehicle could no longer be driven. No other vehicles were involved and no injuries were reported.

Sterling pleaded guilty in September to dangerous driving, possession of nitrous oxide and failing to provide a blood specimen. The judge, Kirsty Allman, said he had shown "conscious disregard for the safety of other road users". She said the offences were serious enough that neither a fine nor a community order was appropriate, and that custody was unavoidable in principle. However, she accepted substantial personal mitigation. This included his charitable work, his MBE for services to racial equality in sport, which he received in 2021, and his continuing commitment to his foundation and his football academy. She also noted that he had voluntarily entered addiction treatment, spending 12 days at a residential centre in June. Allman said nitrous oxide had become a "maladaptive coping mechanism" during a difficult period in his career.

Sterling was also banned from driving for two years. A sentence of two months for failing to provide a blood sample was ordered to run at the same time as the main sentence. No separate penalty was imposed for possessing the gas, and the canisters were seized and destroyed.

Sterling, who most recently played for Feyenoord in the Netherlands on a short-term deal, is currently without a club. He won 82 caps for England, the last at the 2022 World Cup in Qatar, and four Premier League titles with Manchester City. His defence counsel, Jason Bartfeld KC, described the incident as "extremely sad" and said Sterling regretted it deeply. Sterling left the court without making any comment.
