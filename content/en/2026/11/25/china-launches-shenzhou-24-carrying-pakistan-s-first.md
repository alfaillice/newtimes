China launched its Shenzhou-24 mission on Wednesday, sending three astronauts to the Tiangong space station. One of them is Pakistan's Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, the first foreign national to join a Chinese crewed spaceflight. The spacecraft lifted off from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in the Gobi desert at 08.18 Beijing time, which is 01.18 CET. Minutes later the mission was declared successful.

Ali, a former fighter pilot, is expected to remain in orbit for five to 10 days. Chinese astronauts usually serve six-month stints aboard Tiangong, so his visit is far shorter. The commander of the mission, Tang Hongbo, and the flight engineer, Wu Yi, will spend six months on the station along with Zhu Yangzhu, a member of the Shenzhou-23 crew that launched in May this year.

Speaking to reporters before the launch, Ali said he was “carrying the name of Pakistan on my shoulder”. He put the number of people he represents at 250 million Pakistanis. In a post on X, Pakistan's Prime Minister, Shehbaz Sharif, referred instead to 240 million Pakistanis and called the mission living proof of the friendship between the two countries. Xi Jinping, China's president, described the joint mission as a clear expression of long-standing ties between the two countries. The two governments are marking 75 years of co-operation in space.

Ali and Khurram Dawood, the other candidate who reached the final stage of selection for the Shenzhou-24 crew, trained in China and had worked on Chinese space stations under the supervision of Chinese experts. Ali said that mastering the Chinese language was one of his biggest challenges. Pakistan has no spacecraft of its own, which makes the invitation to join this mission significant for Islamabad.

During their time in orbit the crew will carry out experiments and test technology in aerospace medicine and combustion, and study how space affects living organisms. Research will include how the space environment influences ageing, with the aim of finding new ways to prevent and treat age-related vascular diseases. Ali will study photosynthesis in bacteria and the effects of radiation and microgravity on seeds.

China has invested billions of dollars in its space programme. Tiangong is staffed by teams of three that rotate about every six months, and Beijing now wants to show that the station can become an international research platform rather than an exclusively Chinese facility. The mission also fits Beijing's wider effort to position itself as an alternative to the US-led space programme. China aims to land its first astronauts on the Moon by 2030, while the United States has set 2028 as its own target, the first US lunar landing since Apollo 17 in 1972. China also plans to build a research base on the Moon with Russia by 2035. Both Beijing and Washington insist their activities in space are peaceful, but the US has warned that China could seek to dominate resource-rich areas of the Moon.
