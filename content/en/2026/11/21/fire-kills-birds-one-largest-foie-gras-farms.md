A fire at one of the largest foie gras farms in the United States killed an unknown number of birds on Thursday, according to the BBC. The blaze engulfed several barns at Hudson Valley Farms in upstate New York before firefighters could get inside. The BBC reports that no cause has been given for the fire.

Crews rescued hundreds of young birds and hatchlings, the BBC said, citing local news outlets. No workers or first responders were injured. The company does not disclose how many birds it holds at any one time, although commercial poultry farms can typically house thousands. The BBC did not say how many birds died.

Marcus Henley, manager of Hudson Valley Foie Gras in Ferndale, New York, told the local outlet Radio Catskill that the fire destroyed two buildings housing ducks and geese. "The building isn't there anymore," he said. He suspected an electrical malfunction may have started the blaze, although this has not been confirmed.

According to Westchester News 12, cited by the BBC, the emergency response in the early hours of Thursday involved 37 fire departments and 200 first responders. The BBC reports that the fire spread rapidly, and that the metal-clad, wood-frame structure made it difficult for firefighters to stop the flames spreading inside the barn. The BBC's own report refers to the company both as Hudson Valley Farms and as Hudson Valley Foie Gras.

Foie gras has long drawn protests from animal rights groups, which say the birds are force-fed through plastic tubes, allowing them to gain over a third of their body weight in a short period. New York moved to ban the product in 2019, and Hudson Valley farms have been in litigation with the state since then. At a city council hearing in 2019, Henley argued against the ban, saying that journalists who visited the farm found "nothing inhumane" about its practices. The BBC also notes that voters in Washington DC and Denver backed bans on foie gras sales in November's midterm elections.

Hudson Valley Farms and the local fire department did not respond to requests for comment, the BBC said. The animal rights group PETA told the BBC that it will erect a billboard nearby in memory of the birds that died, and it urged the farm to donate surviving birds to local sanctuaries.
