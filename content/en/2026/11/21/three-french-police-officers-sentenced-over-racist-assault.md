Three French police officers have been sentenced for assaulting Michel Zecler, a Black music producer, in Paris in 2020. In a judgement delivered on Friday, the Paris criminal court found that racism was an aggravating factor in the attack, Al Jazeera reports. The network describes the ruling as rare in a country where guilty verdicts in alleged police brutality cases are seldom handed down.

According to Al Jazeera, the officers attacked Zecler outside his studio in Paris on November 21, 2020, during the COVID-19 pandemic. The officers pushed their way into the entrance of his office and beat him. They justified the assault by saying he had failed to wear a mask, which was mandatory at the time, and that he had allegedly grabbed one of their weapons. Zecler's security cameras recorded the attack. Al Jazeera reports that it left him with head trauma, wounds to the lip and scalp, and a complete rupture of his biceps tendon.

The court sentenced Aurelien Lapersonne, 29, to five years in jail, including three suspended, Al Jazeera says. Philippe Tondeux, 50, received five years, including three and a half suspended. Pierre Plantier, 37, was sentenced to five years, four of them suspended. He will serve the remaining year with an ankle tag rather than in prison.

The court found Zecler consistent in his account, Al Jazeera reports. He told a Black police officer who was handcuffing him after the beating that he had been subjected to racial slurs. He repeated the claim at the police station, on the way to hospital and to his loved ones. The court also said the officers' falsification of the police report damaged their credibility when they denied using racist insults.

Al Jazeera, citing AFP, reports that all three officers apologised to Zecler earlier on Friday, with Plantier in tears. A crowd applauded Zecler as he left the hearing. His lawyer, Serge Money, said: "Justice has been done. It has restored a man's dignity and allowed him to begin rebuilding himself."

Al Jazeera notes that French victims and researchers say police violence is systemic, but that guilty verdicts are rare. It cites a 2024 case in which a judge handed suspended prison sentences to three officers who caused permanent rectal injuries to a Black man, Theo Luhaka, during a stop-and-search in 2017. It also refers to a report by France's rights chief, published last year, which found that young men perceived as Arab, Black or North African are four times more likely to be stopped than the rest of the population.
