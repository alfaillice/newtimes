Friday's action by resident doctors affected non-emergency services, and many planned outpatient appointments were cancelled. Urgent surgery and most GP services continued as normal because senior doctors remained on duty.

The Western and South Eastern health trusts have asked patients with Monday appointments to attend as planned, unless their trust tells them otherwise. The South Eastern Trust said the urgent care centre at Lagan Valley Hospital would close on Monday and that postponed appointments and procedures would be rescheduled as soon as possible.

Butler said more than 2,200 appointments were cancelled during similar strikes in June. He warned that the latest action would have "real consequences for patients, waiting lists and service delivery".

The BMA says years of below-inflation pay rises have made it harder for Northern Ireland's health service to recruit and retain doctors. Dr Steven Montgomery, who chairs the BMA's Northern Ireland Resident Doctors Committee, said his colleagues were facing burnout and emotional distress. He said the union was not asking for the full amount in one lump sum, but wanted the minister to set out a plan to restore pay over time.

Butler said cumulative pay rises over the previous three years had averaged 30% for resident doctors, 25% for SAS doctors and 24% for consultants. He argued that the recommended rise was higher than that for other healthcare staff, and warned that going beyond 3.5% would have significant repercussions for nurses, teachers, police officers and the wider public sector.
