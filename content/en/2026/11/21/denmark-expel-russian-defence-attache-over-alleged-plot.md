Denmark is to expel the defence attaché at the Russian embassy in Copenhagen after the Danish government concluded that Moscow was behind an attempted killing of Kyivstoner, a US-Ukrainian rapper, according to The Guardian. Lars Løkke Rasmussen, Denmark's foreign minister, said the Russian ambassador had been summoned to the foreign ministry and told of the decision. The expulsion is due to take place within seven days. Rasmussen told a news conference: "we have exposed you".

The rapper, whose real name is Albert Viktorovich Vasiliev, holds both US and Ukrainian nationality. Danish police said they had prevented an attempt by Russian intelligence services to kill him in September, shortly before a planned performance in Denmark. The concert, due on 25 September, was cancelled. According to prosecutors cited by The Guardian, three men with links to the Danish criminal underworld were arrested as they drove towards the venue north of Copenhagen with two loaded pistols and two knives, about five minutes before the concert was due to begin. A fourth suspect was arrested later that evening. One of the suspects had bought a ticket.

The suspects face charges under Section 108 of Denmark's criminal code, which covers assisting a foreign intelligence service, as well as attempted murder. They denied wrongdoing in court, the broadcaster DR reported, as cited by The Guardian. Rasmus Kieffer-Kristensen, head of Denmark's national security and intelligence service, said his agency considered the assassination attempt to have been planned by Russian intelligence. He described it as a sign of a more aggressive and more risk-taking Russia, and said Russian attempts of this kind had not previously been seen in Denmark.

The Russian embassy in Copenhagen said that Denmark's accusations were not supported by proof, according to The Guardian.

Rasmussen also said Denmark was considering suspending Schengen visas for Russians. Schengen is the zone of 29 countries that allows passport-free travel between its members. According to Reuters, as reported by The Guardian, he sent a message on Friday evening to senior European diplomats proposing that the EU halt tourist visas for Russian citizens and stop recognising Russian non-biometric passports across the zone. The note also urged member states to offer Russians more limited visas that do not allow travel elsewhere in Schengen, and to apply tougher entry rules to serving and former Russian soldiers. "There is clear momentum to act decisively now," the message said.

European Commission president Ursula von der Leyen said the EU stood in full solidarity with Denmark and with all member states targeted by Russian hybrid attacks. She said the bloc would soon propose a "new European security strategy" to strengthen preparedness and deterrence.

The Guardian's report did not say when the expulsion would be completed beyond the seven-day timeframe announced by Rasmussen, nor whether the proposed EU visa measures had received a response from other member states.
