Mercedes' Kimi Antonelli set the fastest time in qualifying for the Las Vegas Grand Prix, but a grid penalty means Red Bull's Max Verstappen will start from pole position, according to BBC Sport.

BBC Sport reported that Antonelli posted a lap of 1:33.893, beating Verstappen by 0.258 seconds. He ran at the back of the pack of cars and used the slipstream of those ahead, a tactic known as towing, which BBC Sport said was especially powerful on the Las Vegas circuit.

The Italian will not keep the top spot. BBC Sport said he has a five-place grid penalty for using more engine components than permitted, so he will start sixth. Verstappen, who has won two of the three Las Vegas Grands Prix, moves up to pole, the broadcaster said.

According to BBC Sport, Mercedes' George Russell was 0.073 seconds behind Verstappen and will start alongside him on the front row. McLaren's Lando Norris, who qualified fourth, will start third, ahead of Red Bull's Isack Hadjar. Racing Bulls' rookie Arvid Lindblad, sixth fastest, is the last driver promoted as a result of Antonelli's penalty. BBC Sport listed Racing Bulls' Liam Lawson in seventh, Alpine's Pierre Gasly in eighth and Franco Colapinto in ninth.

## Ferraris knocked out early

Both Ferraris were eliminated in the second session and qualified 11th and 12th, BBC Sport said. Charles Leclerc was ahead of Lewis Hamilton, the two separated by 0.056 seconds. Leclerc told BBC Sport that the team was losing a second on the straights and that a mistake on his lap in the second session was his own responsibility. Hamilton described the session as a nightmare, citing low grip, damage to the floor and a deployment issue that cost the team 0.6 seconds, according to the same report.

BBC Sport also reported that Antonelli's final run did not start well. He and Russell became tangled with the Alpine and Racing Bulls cars while seeking the best track position for the slipstream. Antonelli was the last driver to run and crossed the line with only one second to spare before the chequered flag. Normally, sitting so close to the car ahead would be a disadvantage, but in Las Vegas it boosted the tow effect and carried him to the front. "That was all the tow," Antonelli said over the radio after learning he was on pole, according to BBC Sport.

Russell said he had wanted pole but was happy with a solid session and day, and BBC Sport quoted him saying the performance pleased him more than his front-row start in Mexico. Verstappen told the broadcaster that he had been struggling with unusual issues that also appeared in Brazil, but that the team had made the car work in qualifying.
