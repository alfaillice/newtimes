New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani has unveiled a plan to close the Rikers Island jail complex by 2032 and replace it with several smaller facilities, the BBC reported on Friday. According to the broadcaster, the plan also aims to sharply reduce the number of people held in the city's jails. The BBC said the proposal is set out in an 88-page report, in which Mamdani describes Rikers as a product of "deteriorating infrastructure" and says officials have a "moral imperative" to close it.

Rikers occupies a 413-acre (167-hectare) island between the boroughs of Queens and the Bronx. The BBC noted that the complex has earned nicknames such as "Gladiator School" and "Torture Island" because of its dilapidated conditions. It has held high-profile inmates awaiting trial, including Tupac Shakur, DMX and Harvey Weinstein. According to the BBC, it currently houses more than 6,500 inmates at any given time.

Plans to shut the facility are not new. The BBC said the city council, the New York City legislature, first voted in 2019 to close it. Legislation required the jail to be shuttered by 2027, but the BBC reported that progress was slowed in part by difficulties in finding places to house inmates. Mamdani blamed delays under his predecessor, former mayor Eric Adams, and the Covid pandemic. The BBC also reported that last year, under Adams, a federal judge removed Rikers from city control and appointed an independent outside official to make decisions about the facility. The jail has long faced criticism over staffing shortages, crumbling conditions and inmate deaths, and the BBC said nine people have died there this year.

Under the plan, the city would open four new jails: one in Brooklyn by 2029, two in the Bronx and Queens by 2031, and one in Manhattan by 2032. The mayor's office argues that smaller buildings would give detainees daylight, fresh air and cleaner space, the BBC said. It would also make visits easier for families, as trips to the remote Rikers can take hours.

Dana Kaplan, whom Mamdani appointed as the city's "Close Rikers Czar", told the BBC News channel that the proposal was a "generational opportunity" to build a different detention system. She said the plan could lead to "a moment of transformation" in how people are detained in the largest city in the US. According to the BBC, Kaplan also said that the administration had already opened 100 therapeutic beds at Bellevue Hospital in Manhattan, moving 100 people off Rikers.

The BBC reported that the New York City Correction Officers' Benevolent Association, the union representing correction officers, has criticised the target. In a statement, the union said that a fixed cap on jail beds was ill-conceived and could endanger New Yorkers and officers, and that keeping the population at around 4,100 would require releasing people charged with violent felonies.

Other measures in the plan include alternatives to detention for some people awaiting trial, easier online bail payments for families and faster pretrial legal processes. The BBC also reported that Mamdani wants to open hundreds of therapeutic beds for prisoners with serious mental health problems. The proposal includes $146m (£111m) for mental health and substance use treatment, as well as vocational and education programmes inside and outside the jails.

The New York City Council must approve the revised timeline. According to the BBC, its members, including the city's speaker, have indicated that they support the plan.
