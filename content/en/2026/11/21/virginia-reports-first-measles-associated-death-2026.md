Virginia has recorded its first measles-associated death of 2026, the state's health department said on Friday, according to The Guardian. The child was aged four or younger and had tested positive for the infectious disease. The department did not release further details, including the child's vaccination status, citing the family's privacy.

The death comes amid a worsening outbreak across the United States, which recently lost its measles elimination status after 26 years, The Guardian reports. Virginia has reported 220 measles cases so far in 2026, state health department data showed as of Thursday. Of these, 40 were among children aged four or younger and 75 among those aged five to 12. Most of the cases, 179, involved unvaccinated people, 39 involved people whose vaccination status was unknown, and two involved vaccinated people.

Activity has been concentrated in the state's central region. Buckingham county has reported 127 cases, the most in Virginia, while neighbouring Charlotte county has recorded 34.

Frederic Bertley, an immunologist and chief executive of the Center of Science and Industry, said: "Virginia has not had a measles-related death since 1978." He described the infection as alarming and as evidence that measles is gaining a foothold and becoming a growing problem for the US, according to the same report.

## A national picture with conflicting counts

The Guardian reports that Pennsylvania, the hardest-hit US state, has recorded an eighth measles-related death and confirmed 1,631 cases as of Friday. Wisconsin has reported two deaths, and New York state has recorded its first measles-associated death, involving a child.

The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) has confirmed six measles-associated deaths so far in 2026, but it does not specify the states where they occurred. The Guardian notes a discrepancy between state and federal tallies. It arose after the CDC, under the Trump administration's health secretary, Robert F Kennedy Jr, began using National Center for Health Statistics mortality data instead of state figures.

The Guardian's report does not set out any further steps by Virginia's health authorities.
