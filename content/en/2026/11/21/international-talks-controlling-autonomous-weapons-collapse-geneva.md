Week-long talks at the United Nations in Geneva on a legally binding treaty to control autonomous weapons have collapsed, according to The Guardian. The negotiations, held at the Review Conference on Conventional Weapons (CCW), ended just before 5am on Saturday after an all-night session failed to overcome objections led by Russia.

The CCW is a UN forum that works by consensus, meaning all 128 parties to the convention must agree unanimously before a statement can pass. The Guardian says that this high threshold has stopped the forum from passing any new laws regulating weapons in the past 30 years. States have since looked for other routes, such as the 2008 ban on cluster munitions.

The Guardian reports that a large majority of states wanted a mandate leading to a legally binding treaty. Because of the consensus rule, the conference adopted a much weaker option: a mandate for countries to elaborate an instrument by consensus, which may carry no legal obligations. More than 100 states had pushed for a new law, arguing that the weapons raise entirely new challenges. Others argued that existing international humanitarian law is sufficient.

A group of seven countries, Austria, Belgium, Brazil, Ireland, Mexico, New Zealand and Panama, had pushed for a treaty to be negotiated within one year. According to The Guardian, the United States proposed five years to agree a mandate, while Human Rights Watch's senior arms adviser, Bonnie Docherty, said treaties could be negotiated within 15 months.

The Guardian says the United States, South Korea, Japan, Turkey, the UK, France and Germany, all investing in AI-enabled weapons, were reluctant to be bound by a new law and preferred a non-binding agreement. The US said human control over autonomous weapons had come up in this week's meetings between President Xi Jinping and Donald Trump, but it still resisted a binding treaty. European and Nato states, Ukraine, China and Iran expressed support for or openness to a new protocol.

Diplomats inside the negotiations told The Guardian that Russia had deliberately filibustered and was not working in good faith, while US delegates had sought a consensus statement. On the final day, the Egyptian delegation shouted at the conference chair in a rare protest over the exclusion of several non-member countries from the talks.

Max Tegmark, chair of the Future of Life Institute, told The Guardian that world leaders had failed to meet the moment. “Slaughterbots are already here,” he said. The Guardian also reports that Israel and the US have used AI-enabled targeting systems in Gaza and Iran, and that investigative media reports say the Pentagon struck more than 1,000 targets in Iran on the first day of the war.

Critics quoted by The Guardian said the outcome was a missed opportunity, noting that states had discussed the issue for 13 years and that large parts of a treaty had already been agreed.
