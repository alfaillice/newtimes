British maritime authorities reported two incidents on Saturday in which tankers were struck by unidentified projectiles in the Strait of Hormuz, according to Al Jazeera. The reports came as Iran continues to restrict shipping through the narrow waterway, which links the Gulf with international sea routes.

The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) centre, a British agency that monitors threats to commercial shipping, said one tanker was hit at its rear about 20km north of Khasab, a port town on Oman's northern coast. A second tanker was struck in its engine room about 31km east of Khasab, the agency said. No casualties were reported. Al Jazeera said it remained unclear whether the two incidents involved separate vessels.

Iran's navy gave a different account, according to Al Jazeera. In a statement carried by Iranian state television, it said an oil tanker had caught fire after striking a naval mine in a restricted area east of the strait. The navy accused the vessel of ignoring warnings and sailing with its identification and navigation equipment switched off. Al Jazeera said the Iranian account could not be independently verified and that it was unclear whether the burning tanker was one of the two vessels UKMTO described.

The strait is one of the world's most important oil routes. Before the current conflict, it carried nearly a fifth of the world's crude oil supplies. The incidents take place in a war involving Iran, the United States and Israel, which began in late February when the United States and Israel attacked Iran. Tehran has since moved to tighten its control over the waterway.

Al Jazeera's correspondent in Tehran, Resul Serdar, said incidents involving tankers had risen sharply over the past week. Citing statements from Iran's armed forces, he said about 25 incidents had been reported since the previous Saturday, roughly three a day, a trend he described as “quite dramatic”. He said Iran is telling shipping that the strait is under its control and that any vessel passing through must coordinate fully with Iranian authorities and follow the lanes Iran sets. “This is becoming a pattern,” Serdar said.

According to Serdar, Iranian forces have warned that ships which do not comply will either be attacked or hit sea mines laid by the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) or other Iranian forces. He said Tehran was deliberately escalating tensions to signal that if Iran cannot export its oil, other countries will not be able to do so either, and to show that it still controls the strait.

Al Jazeera reported that the United States has responded by blockading Iranian ports and deploying naval vessels to escort tankers through the waterway. The confrontation has raised concerns about the security of a major route for global energy supplies, with commercial vessels caught between Washington and Tehran.
