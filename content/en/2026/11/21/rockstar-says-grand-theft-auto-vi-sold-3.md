Rockstar Games says Grand Theft Auto VI generated $3.79bn in retail sales on its first day, which it describes as the biggest launch for any entertainment product in history, according to The Guardian.

The Guardian reports that the figure is based on the publisher's own estimates covering the end of Thursday, the day the game went on sale on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. Rockstar also said it had sold 32.5 million units on pre-release, which it presents as a record across platforms. The game launched first in New Zealand and was rolled out market by market as each reached midnight local time.

Sam Houser, Rockstar's president and founder, said in a statement: "We are incredibly humbled by the response from players around the world." The company said the sales set "a new record for entertainment across any medium."

The Guardian compares the figure with the first 24 hours of the previous game, Grand Theft Auto V, which took roughly $800m in 2013. On that basis, the new figure is nearly five times larger. The newspaper also notes that it exceeds the entire box office run of Avengers: Endgame, which at about $2.9bn worldwide became the highest-grossing film of all time last month after a rerelease pushed it past Avatar.

The Guardian also reports that the game has a score of 99 on the review aggregator Metacritic and that it generated more social media posts than this year's World Cup, according to the monitoring platform Visibrain.

Like its predecessors, the game is an open-world action title in which players roam a large city, steal cars, carry out heists and complete missions for criminal gangs. The newspaper says the game's streets, nightlife and beaches are rendered in detail, and that non-player characters go about their lives whether or not the player is involved.

The release has also prompted warnings from authorities. According to The Guardian, police departments across the United States reminded the public that the car thefts, reckless driving and violence shown in the game should not be re-enacted on the streets. The newspaper also reports that the American Psychological Association has said there is insufficient evidence of a scientific causal link between violent video games and violent behaviour. The Atlanta Police Department wrote on social media: "GTA is not a real place, but Atlanta is! Don't confuse virtual entertainment with real-life decisions."

The Guardian's account relies on figures released by Rockstar. Independent confirmation of the sales total was not available in the sources used for this story.
