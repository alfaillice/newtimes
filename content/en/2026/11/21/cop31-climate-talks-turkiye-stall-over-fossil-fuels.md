Negotiations at the COP31 UN climate summit in Antalya, Türkiye, have run into overtime after nearly 200 countries failed to agree on a draft that no longer mentions fossil fuels, according to Al Jazeera. The two-week conference was due to close on Friday 20 November. A late session approved some technical matters, but not the wider political deal, Al Jazeera reported.

COP, short for Conference of the Parties, is the annual UN climate summit. Countries use it to push forward the 2015 Paris Agreement, which aims to keep global warming below 1.5 degrees Celsius. Al Jazeera cited a UN report, published last month, which found the world is on course to pass that threshold.

The draft has changed sharply over the week. An opening text from Australia, the co-host and lead negotiator, on Monday called for a faster phase-out of fossil fuels. A slimmer version on Thursday removed every reference to them after Saudi Arabia and other major oil and gas producers objected, Al Jazeera said. Oil producers also resisted similar language at COP28 in 2023. The revised text additionally dropped a $120bn target for helping poorer countries adapt to climate change.

According to Al Jazeera, the European Union said the draft risked turning the summit into a fossil fuel conference. Panama, which signed a letter with a dozen other countries calling for a clear move away from fossil fuels, threatened to leave the talks. Its environment minister, Juan Carlos Navarro, told a news conference that a summit document failing even to mention fossil fuels was unacceptable.

Other disputes concern electricity and carbon capture. The draft proposes raising electricity's share of the world's final energy consumption to 35 per cent by 2035, but does not say the power must come from renewables. Some countries want the word "clean" included. Others reject expanding carbon capture, a technology that traps emissions from power plants and factories, arguing it is costly, unproven and could allow polluters to keep burning oil and gas. Vanuatu's climate minister, Ralph Regenvanu, wants references to what he called false solutions removed.

Developing countries are angry that rich nations are falling short on climate finance. Ghana's environment minister, Zanetor Agyeman-Rawlings, told the conference that her delegation had not come to beg, Al Jazeera reported.

Al Jazeera also noted that the United States, the largest historical emitter, is absent for a second year after President Donald Trump ended its climate cooperation. The US-Israel war on Iran and higher living costs have drawn attention away from the talks.

Turkish Environment Minister Murat Kurum, who is also COP31 president, told delegates that they were "close to the finish line, but we are not there yet". A third draft is expected on Saturday, the AFP news agency reported, as cited by Al Jazeera. Environmental groups following the meeting said it lacked the ambition needed to address the worsening climate crisis. Mohamed Adow, director of the think tank Power Shift Africa, said: "This has been the worst COP ever."
