The Princess of Wales has spoken about how her cancer diagnosis has affected her in a BBC documentary, according to The Guardian. The film, titled Heal, will be shown on BBC One on 29 November.

In a trailer for the 30-minute programme, Catherine, who is 44, says that cancer “does change you”. She describes physical changes to the body and a deeper shift that, in her words as reported by the newspaper, goes further than the body alone.

The Guardian reports that the film records Catherine taking on the Three Peaks challenge, which involves climbing the highest mountains in England, Scotland and Wales within 24 hours. It also shows her meeting other people who are undergoing cancer treatment. The BBC describes the documentary as a highly personal film.

Catherine was diagnosed with an undisclosed form of cancer in early 2024 and received chemotherapy. By January 2025 she announced that she was in remission. She was treated at the Royal Marsden hospital in London, which the newspaper describes as a world-leading oncology centre. The money raised from distributing the documentary, which will also be broadcast in other countries, will go to the Royal Marsden cancer charity.

The Guardian says patients who finish treatment at the hospital often ring a bell in celebration. In the trailer, Catherine tells her brother, James Middleton, “I didn’t ring the bell.” He replies that taking on the challenge and making the programme may be her way of ringing it.

She completed the Three Peaks in June, 18 months after announcing her remission. The newspaper reports that she became the first royal to achieve the feat, climbing Ben Nevis, Scafell Pike and Yr Wyddfa in less than 24 hours after starting on a Saturday evening.

The documentary will be introduced by the presenter Kirsty Young. According to The Guardian, Catherine says that her recovery has taught her to find hope alongside uncertainty. Kate Phillips, the BBC’s chief content officer, said she hoped the film would resonate with viewers and provide comfort and support to people dealing with their own health problems.

In the same report, The Guardian says King Charles will voluntarily pay the “mansion tax” on relevant royal household properties, despite a crown exemption. The high-value council tax surcharge, which applies to properties valued at more than £2m, is due to take effect in 2028.
