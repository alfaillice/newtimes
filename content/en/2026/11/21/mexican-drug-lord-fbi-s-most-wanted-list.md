Mexican special forces have arrested Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, an alleged cartel leader known as "Chapo Isidro" who is on the FBI's Ten Most Wanted Fugitives list, Mexico's defence ministry said. He was detained on Friday night in Guasave, a town in the north-western state of Sinaloa.

The nickname is similar to that of Joaquín "El Chapo" Guzmán, the jailed former leader of the Sinaloa Cartel, but reports say the two men are not related. Meza Flores was wanted in Mexico on an arrest warrant for organised crime and drug offences, and is subject to an extradition order to the United States, according to the defence ministry.

US prosecutors first indicted him in 2012 and, in a 2019 indictment, accused him of conspiring to manufacture and distribute cocaine, heroin, methamphetamine and marijuana between 2005 and 2019. The FBI added him to its most-wanted list in February 2025, and the US State Department offered up to $5m for information leading to his arrest and conviction. When the FBI announced his listing, then acting director Brian Driscoll accused him of spending around 20 years flooding the US with fentanyl and other drugs. Driscoll also said Meza Flores and his organisation were accused of kidnapping, extortion, torture and murder.

Accounts differ on his organisation. One describes him as the leader of the Meza Flores Organisation, also known as the Guasave Cartel, which reportedly emerged after a split with the Beltrán Leyva Organization. Another, citing Mexican authorities, says he leads the Beltrán Leyva criminal organisation and is an ally of the Mayos faction of the Sinaloa Cartel.

The operation followed coordination between Mexican and US intelligence agencies, according to the defence ministry. Troops used "stealth and surprise" to set up a security perimeter around a house, the ministry said, and came under fire from gunfire and explosives before entering. Accounts differ on the number of detainees. One report says Meza Flores was held with his lieutenant. Another, citing the ministry, says three men were arrested, including a man named as "Serafin", described as the group's alleged second-in-command. All detainees were flown to Mexico City and handed over to prosecutors.

Videos from Guasave showed people fleeing gunfire at a baseball game and cars and petrol stations on fire. Sinaloa's public security agency urged residents to stay indoors and avoid the roads. The defence ministry said roadblocks had been reported in four areas of the state, and that forces were sent to reopen them.

The arrest comes as US President Donald Trump has pressed Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum to do more against cartels. On Friday, Trump said Washington was working with Sheinbaum "because the cartels are running Mexico". Mexico has welcomed closer co-operation but has resisted US forces operating on its soil.

On Saturday, Mexico's security secretary, Omar García Harfuch, said troops would stay in Sinaloa to protect residents. "These detentions are an important step toward restoring peace in Sinaloa," he wrote on X.
