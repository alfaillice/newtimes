Spain's Ministry of Youth and Childhood has asked the State Prosecutor's Office (Fiscalía General del Estado), the country's public prosecution service, to act on reports of child sexual abuse material on TikTok. The ministry has also raised the possibility of halting the platform's services in Spain, according to ministry sources who spoke on Monday.

In its letter to prosecutors, the ministry said it was passing on the information it holds so that prosecutors can take the measures they consider necessary to protect children and adolescents who may be affected.

The reports come from an investigation published on Monday by the Spanish organisation Maldita.es. It said it had identified more than 1,000 TikTok videos containing explicit child sexual abuse material. From those, it reported a sample of 50 videos to the platform as illegal. Together, those 50 videos had 8.8 million views and more than 148,000 "likes".

According to Maldita.es, TikTok removed five of the 50 videos (10%) and blocked 13 others (26%), in the latter case only within Spain. The platform did not respond to 6% of the reports. In the remaining 58%, it considered that the content breached neither the law nor its own rules. Still, more than half of those were taken down within 72 hours of the reports. After that period, 38% of the reported accounts were still active.

Sira Rego, the minister for Youth and Childhood, has asked for an urgent meeting with TikTok's representatives in Spain. She wants to hear the company's explanations, the measures it has adopted and the immediate steps it plans to protect children and adolescents on the platform.

The ministry describes the documented facts as "extremely serious" and says they require "an immediate and coordinated response" from all competent authorities, both national and European. The quote is a translation from Spanish.

Ministry sources also point to "serious shortcomings" in TikTok's systems for detecting and moderating content, as well as in the role of its recommendation systems in spreading this material. In the ministry's view, this could amount to a violation of children's rights by the platform itself.

The reports do not say whether TikTok has responded to the ministry's request, what the Prosecutor's Office will do, or how a suspension of the service would work in practice. At the time of writing, this story does not include a comment from TikTok.
