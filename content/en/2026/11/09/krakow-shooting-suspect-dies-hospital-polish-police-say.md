The man suspected of shooting dead six people in Kraków on Saturday has died in hospital, Polish police said on Monday. The 24-year-old Polish national, who has not been officially named, was wounded in an exchange of fire with police and was taken to hospital in a critical condition. Małopolska police, the regional force, wrote on X that “the perpetrator of the tragic events died in hospital early this morning.”

Kraków is Poland's second-largest city. According to police, the gunman drove at high speed into the Main Market Square in the old town shortly before 15.00 CET on Saturday. The square is usually crowded with tourists. Wearing military-style clothing and a bulletproof vest, he fired at random as he drove around the square, the Kraków police spokesperson Katarzyna Cisło said, as reported by The Guardian. The BBC reported that armed officers reached the scene and that he was shot within three minutes of the attack beginning. The Guardian reported that he kept firing after being wounded.

Six people were killed in the square, according to both The Guardian and the BBC. The victims include a 32-year-old British man, a 51-year-old Austrian woman and four Polish nationals. The two outlets differ on the number of people injured. The Guardian reported 16 injured people, with 15 in a stable condition as of late Sunday. The BBC said 16 people were taken to hospital, including the attacker. Two police officers were among the injured.

Police found the bodies of the gunman's father, 53, mother, 52, and sister, 21, at the family home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, a town about 100 miles north-east of Kraków, according to The Guardian. Officers believe they died of gunshot wounds. Police said the gunman had no criminal record and held a firearms licence. The BBC reported that four firearms, including two AR-15 rifles, were found in his car, along with several hundred rounds of ammunition.

Poland's interior minister, Marcin Kierwiński, said on Saturday that the gunman was fascinated by far-right ideologies and held “very radical” and “ultra-nationalistic” views. The BBC reported that officials initially described far-right ideology as a possible inspiration but have since said they are still investigating his motive. On Monday, Przemysław Nowak, a spokesperson for the National Prosecutor's Office, told a briefing that the motive inquiry remains ongoing.

The justice minister, Waldemar Żurek, said the terrorism investigation would continue after the gunman's death. Its aim is to establish whether he acted alone or was inspired by any group. Żurek called for “restraint” and said evidence was being gathered but would not be disclosed. An interior ministry spokesperson, Karolina Gałecka, said that emblems on the gunman's clothing were being analysed by specialists.

The attack came weeks after several school stabbings in October, which left one woman dead. Officials said those attacks were linked to extremist content online. Poland has declared two days of national mourning, and flags have been flown at half-mast across the country. Mourners have left candles in the square, which reopened late on Sunday.
