Boeing will cut about 5% of its commercial aircraft workforce as it slows the ramp-up of jet production, the company said on Monday, according to The Guardian. The cuts will come through a mix of layoffs and attrition, the process by which staff leave and their posts are not refilled.

The Guardian reports that the company is moderating its production increase to give its factories and supply chain partners more time to reach stability. In a message to staff, Stephanie Pope, Boeing's commercial chief, wrote that the cut in output had left the business overstaffed. She said the company had made substantial progress but had not yet fully recovered, and promised that employees would be treated with dignity and respect as layoff notices begin in December.

Pope credited the workforce with better operations this year, citing US certifications of the 737 Max 7 and 737 Max 10. The Guardian did not report a total number of jobs affected.

The changes follow a late October conference call in which Boeing executives said the company would no longer meet its target for 2026 deliveries. They also pushed back the timeframe for raising 737 Max production from 47 to 52 until the second half of 2027, the Guardian reports.

Investors have treated higher 737 Max output as central to growth in revenue and profit, after a long period of financial losses linked to safety problems. Two deadly 737 Max crashes, in 2018 and 2019, were a major part of that period.

Chief executive Kelly Ortberg said in October that the company was making progress but still had more work to do, and that at the current rate it would take into next year to become fully stable. He pointed to the need for greater predictability in wing production and in fuselage production at its Kansas operation, according to the same report.
