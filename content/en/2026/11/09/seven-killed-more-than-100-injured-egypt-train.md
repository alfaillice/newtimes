At least seven people have died and more than 100 have been injured after two trains collided near the Egyptian city of Giza, according to the BBC, which cited the country's railway authority. The broadcaster reported that several carriages derailed and fell down an embankment.

According to the BBC, the first train was travelling from Giza to Fayoum, south of Cairo, when it crashed into the rear of a stationary service heading south from Cairo to Aswan. The BBC said images from the scene showed some carriages overturned, with at least one appearing to have split in two. It also reported that the driver of one of the trains was among those killed, and that the ministry had confirmed this.

The collision happened between Kafr Ammar and Al-Raqqa stations in Al-Ayyat, close to the site of Egypt's deadliest rail disaster. In February 2002, a fire engulfed a passenger train travelling from Cairo to Luxor. Official figures put the death toll from that incident at more than 370, although the BBC noted that some estimates were considerably higher.

The BBC described a large emergency response at the scene, with people looking inside the overturned carriages for injured passengers. Egypt's health minister, Dr Khaled Abdel Ghaffar, said people were being treated for broken bones and head injuries. According to the BBC, the government put hospitals in the area on "heightened alert" as they received a large number of patients. The Egyptian Red Crescent said it was providing aid and psychological support to victims and their families at the scene.

President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi said he had ordered state authorities to "investigate the circumstances of the accident, and hold accountable anyone found to have been negligent", the BBC reported. The public prosecutor's office warned that the death toll could rise, the broadcaster said.

The railway authority has suspended trains between Giza and the Upper Egypt region, and cranes have been sent to remove the wrecked carriages. The BBC said the authority had not given an estimate of how long this would take. The BBC also reported that on Sunday there were calls in Egypt for the transport minister to resign over the crash.

Foreign governments and officials have sent condolences. The BBC said Saudi Arabia's ministry of foreign affairs expressed "solidarity" with Egypt and wished the injured a speedy recovery. Turkey's ambassador to Egypt wrote on X that he extended condolences to the victims, their families and the Egyptian people.
