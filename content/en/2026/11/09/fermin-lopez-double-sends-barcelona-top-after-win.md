Fermín López scored twice as Barcelona came from behind to beat Atlético Madrid 2-1 at the Metropolitano on Sunday. The win puts Barcelona three points clear at the top of La Liga, Spain's top-flight football league, after 12 games.

Atlético, coached by Diego Simeone, took the lead in the 10th minute. According to Al Jazeera, Giuliano Simeone turned in a close-range volley from Julián Álvarez's cross from the left. The home side, seeking an eighth straight win, had several early chances, and The Guardian described Atlético as the more aggressive team in the opening minutes, with Barcelona struggling to settle after two recent defeats, one of them to Real Madrid.

Barcelona, coached by Hansi Flick, improved as the first half went on. López equalised on the cusp of half-time after a ball broke to him in the box, according to Al Jazeera. The Guardian said the move began with a pass from Pedri and that a deflection off Marc Pubill helped the ball reach López, though it praised the finish.

In the second half, Lamine Yamal set up López's winner with a pass that Al Jazeera put in the 66th minute. López then beat goalkeeper Jan Oblak. Al Jazeera described a first-time finish, while The Guardian described a shot cut past Oblak. Both reports credited the goal to López, his second of the night.

Speaking to La Liga TV after the match, López said: "It was a very difficult match." He added: "I'm very happy." He said the equaliser before half-time had lifted a weight off Barcelona's shoulders.

The result leaves Barcelona first, three points ahead of second-placed Real Madrid. Atlético are third, five points behind Barcelona, according to Al Jazeera. Atlético had several chances late in the match, including a shot by Álvarez that went over the bar, but Barcelona held on.
