Meta is extending automated age estimation for teenage users to all countries, the company said on Monday, following legal settlements in the United States and mounting criticism that its platforms do not do enough to protect young people. According to Al Jazeera, the change will first reach Instagram accounts this month, and Meta plans to extend it to Facebook and Threads later in the year.

The system uses artificial intelligence to estimate whether a user is a teenager, even when the account was opened with an adult birthday. Al Jazeera reports that the technology analyses user behaviour and posts to make that estimate. Once an account is flagged, it is moved into Teen Account protections, which limit who can contact the user, what content they see and how long they can spend on the app.

Al Jazeera notes that teenagers often get around these restrictions by entering a false birthday when they sign up. That, the outlet reports, has raised concern that minors can bypass parental controls and see harmful material. Meta said in its announcement that it was “encouraged by the results” so far, and that it was now “expanding this technology globally”.

The technology has been tested in several countries, including the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada and Australia, Al Jazeera reports. Parents in the United States who have set up Instagram for their children will receive notifications about the new measures this month.

Teen accounts already come with a range of limits. Children under 13 are banned from the apps. Teenagers are reminded to leave Instagram after 60 minutes, and notifications are muted at night. Teen accounts also face a two-hour daily limit and cannot see “social comparison” features such as likes on other teen accounts.

The changes follow legal action. In August, Meta agreed to pay $18bn to US states that had sued it over alleged harm to children, Al Jazeera reports. The states argued that Meta’s platforms encourage addictive behaviour, fail to verify users’ ages and allow harmful content to spread. As part of the settlement, Meta agreed to change how its apps treat under-18 users. The changes include a midnight-to-6am curfew, push notifications blocked between 8am and 3pm, and prompts after 15 minutes of continuous use. Parents will be told when a teen first messages an adult, and searches on topics such as suicide, self-harm or eating disorders will trigger an alert to them.

Meta has also urged other platforms to follow suit. “As Instagram is just one of the many apps teens use, we’re also calling on our industry peers, TikTok and YouTube, to adopt these same practices,” the company said, according to Al Jazeera.

Al Jazeera also reports that several groups, including the advocacy group Digital Freedom Project and Reddit, have filed a lawsuit this month challenging the constitutionality of Australia’s law banning under-16s from social media. The outlet says the challengers also dispute the scientific basis for keeping children off platforms such as YouTube, Facebook and TikTok.
