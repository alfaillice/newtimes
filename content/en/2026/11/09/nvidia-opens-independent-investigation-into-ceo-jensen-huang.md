Nvidia said on Sunday that it is opening an independent investigation into its founder and chief executive, Jensen Huang, after recent reports that he had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort. The US chipmaker's board said a committee of independent directors, supported by outside lawyers, would examine all available information. Huang will keep leading the company while the review runs.

The move follows a report by The New York Times in October. According to the paper, Donald Trump was briefed last year on intelligence that Huang had a close relationship with a Chinese businesswoman who passed information to Beijing. She is Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based datacentre company. The two are not related. The Times, citing unnamed sources, reported that US officials had concluded she was acting as a Chinese intelligence asset. It also said Megaspeed had become one of Nvidia's largest buyers, purchasing about $4.8bn of equipment. The Chinese Embassy in Washington has denied any allegations of spying in the case, Al Jazeera reported.

The accounts of Huang's own position differ. Axios reported on Sunday, citing a draft email from Huang to employees that Reuters had also seen, that he had admitted a romantic relationship with a suspected Chinese spy. Al Jazeera described the message as one he had sent to staff, and said he had confirmed an intimate relationship with a woman accused of working for Chinese intelligence. In the email, Huang wrote: “There is no excuse for what I did.” Nvidia did not immediately respond to a request for comment on the Axios report.

The board said it had no reason to believe that US national security or Nvidia's secrets had been compromised, but that its inquiry would look at all the information available. It said it had confidence in Huang and his leadership team, and that Huang fully supported the review and would cooperate with it.

Republican lawmakers in the House of Representatives, the lower chamber of the US Congress, have expressed more alarm. According to The Guardian, they asked Huang for a closed-door briefing next week to the House foreign affairs and intelligence committees on Nvidia's protections against foreign espionage. Al Jazeera reported that they also called on him to testify before Congress on November 18. Democratic Senator Elizabeth Warren said Huang and the board owed Congress answers, and urged passage of bipartisan legislation to keep advanced American technology out of China.

Trump played down the reports. Speaking to reporters on Sunday, he described Huang as an honourable man who probably had an excuse, and said: “I haven't heard he's done anything wrong.” Huang is an influential adviser to the president on artificial intelligence. Trump was noncommittal when asked whether Huang would accompany him to the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in China in November, though he said he would be happy to take him.

The Times also reported, according to The Guardian, that a memo drafted by Secretary of State Marco Rubio last spring cited intelligence that the businesswoman was a spy for China's Ministry of State Security. Huang was still invited on Trump's trip to China in May, and he and his wife sat at the top table with Trump and Xi Jinping at a White House state dinner in September.

The investigation comes during a difficult week for AI stocks. Nvidia, previously worth about $5tn, lost $450bn in a single day last week, according to Al Jazeera, and the tech-heavy Nasdaq fell by nearly $1tn in a week, The Guardian reported. US export controls effectively bar the company from selling its top-tier processors to customers in China. The board has said it will provide further updates as appropriate.
