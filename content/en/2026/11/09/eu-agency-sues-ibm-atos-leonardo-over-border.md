The European Union's IT agency has taken legal action against IBM and two European companies over delays in building the bloc's new border system, according to The Guardian, which reported the case from the agency's latest annual accounts.

The agency, EU-Lisa, manages large-scale EU IT systems in the fields of justice and security. It filed the claim last year against IBM, the French IT company Atos and the Italian defence contractor Leonardo. The three firms form a consortium contracted to implement and maintain the Entry/Exit System (EES). The Guardian reports that the claim was submitted on 22 December 2025 to a court in Estonia, where the agency is headquartered.

The EES launched in October 2025. Non-EU travellers entering the Schengen area must now have their faces photographed and their fingerprints scanned. Schengen, the passport-free travel zone, covers 25 of the EU's 27 member states, plus Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland. According to The Guardian, the checks apply to people who are not citizens of a Schengen country, Ireland or Cyprus.

The Guardian says the rollout has run into serious difficulties. Some passengers at peak times have missed their flights because of delays that were blamed on the new technology and on a shortage of border staff. Airports have complained of faulty EES kiosks and repeated outages of the system, which The Guardian says have led to waiting times of three hours at busy periods.

The agency's annual accounts say that, after the delays in delivering the EES, it used its contractual and legal remedies to protect its financial interests. EU-Lisa declined to discuss the details of the case. It said the proceedings concern the system's development phase and have no impact on its current operation. "EES is fully operational," the agency said, according to The Guardian.

The European Commission, the EU's executive arm, said it was not a party to the case. It described the dispute as concerning delays in delivering the EES during development, and said it was "not related to the operation of the EES", according to the same report.

The Guardian said it had contacted IBM and Leonardo. Atos declined to comment. The article does not report a reply from IBM or Leonardo, and it does not say when the Estonian court is expected to rule.
