The 2030 Rugby League World Cup will be held in the United States and the Pacific Islands, according to BBC Sport, which reported the decision from the annual general meeting of the sport's world governing body, International Rugby League (IRL), in Sydney.

The BBC said the five host cities have not yet been confirmed. The men's and women's tournaments will run at the same time, as happened at this year's World Cup in Australia, and the 2030 event will be played over five weeks.

The format will include three pool rounds designed to mimic a "Magic Round", in which a complete round of fixtures is played at one venue, in the style of Super League's Magic Weekend. The semi-finals and finals will then take place in different locations. The BBC noted that the 2026 tournament had been criticised for jumping from the group stage straight to the semi-finals, and said that structure appears to have been kept for 2030.

The IRL chairman, Troy Grant, linked the decision to the growth of the event. According to BBC Sport, he said at the meeting: "This is a strategy to maximise the interest of broadcasters, government partners and fans in the current World Cup, which has regularly broken records for crowds and the number of television viewers." He added that the organisation wanted to take advantage of openings in "new frontiers where the Rugby League World Cup hasn't previously been played".

The men's competition will grow from 10 teams to 12, and the women's from eight to 10, Grant said, according to the BBC.

The IRL has also proposed that Samoa and Tonga be promoted to Tier One nations, alongside Australia, England and New Zealand. The BBC recalled that Samoa became the first Pacific country to play in a World Cup final, in 2022, and that Samoa and Tonga both reached the semi-finals of this year's tournament.

BBC Sport also reported that eligibility rules have been changed. A player may now represent the nation of their heritage if they, a parent, a grandparent or a great-grandparent were born there. The change followed a detailed submission from the Cook Islands.

The 2030 Wheelchair World Cup will take place at the same time as the men's and women's tournaments, but will be held in France. The BBC added that the 2034 tournament will be hosted in Europe.

Australia's men have won the past three World Cups, according to the BBC, and the women's team is contesting its fifth successive final. The BBC's report did not give details of the host cities or of the venues for the 2030 matches.
