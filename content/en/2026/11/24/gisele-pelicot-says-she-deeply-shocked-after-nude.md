Gisèle Pelicot has said she is “deeply shocked” that nude photographs of her were used without her consent to promote a book by a French philosopher and psychoanalyst. She said she will take legal action against the publisher and the author.

In an interview with French television, the 73-year-old said she refused to let her privacy “become an advertising tool”. She said she felt “humiliated, exposed and hurt”, adding that it was as if the ordeal of the trial had not been enough.

Pelicot became a global symbol in the fight against sexual violence during the trial in 2024 of her former husband, Dominique Pelicot. He was jailed for 20 years for drugging and raping her, and for allowing other men to rape her while she was unconscious. Dozens of men were convicted alongside him. Pelicot chose to waive her right to anonymity so that shame would move from victims to perpetrators.

The book, which questions the notion of coercive control, was written by Sabine Prokhoris, who has previously criticised the Me Too movement. It was published by Tinbad, a small publishing house in Paris. One chapter deals with the Pelicot case. The back-cover blurb describes coercive control as central to what it calls #MeToo orthodoxy. Pelicot’s lawyer, Christophe Bigot, said the book calls into question her status as a victim.

French media reported that Tinbad sent nude photographs of Pelicot, taken by her ex-husband, to some news organisations to promote the book. Accounts differ on whether videos were included as well. The publisher said it had not sent images of rape or pornographic videos, but did not deny that nude photographs had been distributed.

Tinbad apologised to Pelicot and to the author, describing the episode as an unfortunate communication error. It said no further print runs of the book would be produced beyond the 280 copies already in circulation, and that it would no longer print or promote the work. Prokhoris said she placed full responsibility for what she called an inappropriate promotional campaign on the publisher.

It is not yet clear when Pelicot’s legal action will be filed or what form it will take. Her lawyer and the publisher have not been reported as commenting on any proceedings beyond the statements above.

Pelicot said she had decided to speak publicly about the case so that shame would change sides. Her remarks this week suggest she sees the promotion of the book as a continuation of that struggle.
