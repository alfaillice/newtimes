Police in Manchester are trying to establish what an unknown substance was after it was released into a crowd at the Co-op Live arena on Sunday night. The incident, at about 23.00 CET (22.00 GMT), forced around 12,000 people to leave a concert by the US rapper Don Toliver, and fans have since questioned the venue's security.

Greater Manchester Police (GMP), which declared a major incident, initially believed the substance was tear gas. Detectives are working to establish what it was, where it came from and whether any crime was committed. Further testing was under way on items found at the venue, and no arrests had been made. The force described the event as “an isolated incident” with “no wider threat to the public”.

The number of people hurt is reported differently in different accounts. One woman was taken to hospital on the night, and 88 people were assessed by paramedics for eye and breathing symptoms. Police said those who were decontaminated at the scene were looked after inside the nearby Etihad Stadium, home of Manchester City. The ambulance service said one patient was taken to Manchester Royal Infirmary for further assessment. On Monday, a 16-year-old boy was taken to hospital with burns to his nose and throat, his mother said. She described a makeshift quarantine area at the tennis courts opposite the venue, where people changed into white boiler suits before being moved to a VIP area inside the arena until they had been medically checked.

Fans have criticised security at the arena, which is owned by the US entertainment company Oak View Group and opened in 2024. Several concertgoers said bags were not checked and that a single metal detector was easy to walk around. One fan, Jobe O’Connor, 18, described the checks as “really shocking”. Others said people who had climbed over the ticket barriers were given wristbands without their tickets being scanned. Fans also said that about 40 minutes into the show Toliver left the stage abruptly and the lights came on, and that it was nearly another hour before the concert was cancelled, with little information given in the meantime.

The comments were made in the context of the 2017 Manchester Arena attack, in which 22 people were killed at the end of an Ariana Grande concert. One parent said venues appeared to have learned little from that attack. Fans at Toliver’s earlier concerts in Zurich and Paris also reported that a spray or gas had been released into the crowd, though these claims have not been confirmed by police.

Co-op Live said in a statement that it was working closely with GMP to establish the cause and that it would release more information once police provided an update. On its website, the venue says it uses a range of security measures, some of them not visible to visitors, including screening that detects prohibited items without people having to empty their pockets. Toliver’s planned show at the O2 in London on Monday was postponed because of production delays caused by the abandoned Manchester concert.

Greater Manchester Mayor Bev Craig said the major incident had ended. She thanked emergency services and venue staff, and said that enquiries into the cause were continuing so that those affected could get answers.
