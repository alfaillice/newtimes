Italy has told Ukraine that it is concerned about Kyiv's recent naval drone strikes on Russian vessels off Libya, warning that the wider war could spread into the Mediterranean.

Italy's foreign minister, Antonio Tajani, raised the issue in a phone call on Tuesday with his Ukrainian counterpart, Andrii Sybiha, according to a readout from the Italian foreign ministry. The ministry said Tajani stressed that Rome wants to stop the conflict between Russia and Ukraine from reaching the Mediterranean, and that "for Italy, respecting Libya's sovereignty is necessary." Tajani is a senior figure in Italy's government, which is led by Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni.

Ukrainian naval drones struck two Russian vessels on Sunday. One was docked at the naval base in Benghazi, in eastern Libya, and the other was just off the city. Ukraine has confirmed that it used the drones. The attacks appear to be part of a wider campaign against Russian ships outside the Black Sea.

Libya has been divided since the civil war that followed the fall of Muammar Gaddafi in 2011. The internationally recognised government in Tripoli controls much of the west. The east is dominated by the military strongman Khalifa Haftar and his Libyan National Army, which is backed by a rival administration in Benghazi. Russia supports Haftar, while Italy backs the Tripoli government. Turkey has also expanded its military presence in the country since 2019.

The Tripoli government said on Tuesday that it had not condoned Ukraine's attack on the two Russian ships near Benghazi. Italian officials, according to reporting on the episode, fear that the strikes could reignite fighting between Libya's rival factions, possibly after Russian retaliation on Libyan soil. For Rome, such a scenario would recall the chaos that followed Gaddafi's death.

Meloni has not commented publicly on the incident, apparently to avoid escalating the situation. Domestically, the government is also wary that the episode could weaken political support for aid to Ukraine.

Lucia Annunziata, an Italian member of the European Parliament in the Socialists and Democrats group, said Kyiv should have informed Rome before the attacks. She added, however, that Italy cannot recognise Ukraine's right to self-defence while setting geographical limits on how that right is exercised.

Meanwhile, the wider Russia-Ukraine war continues to spill into European waters and airspace, and the Italian concern adds to unease elsewhere in Europe. Libya's own position, as stated by its government, is that it does not want to be drawn into the conflict.
