Former Bangladeshi prime minister Sheikh Hasina plans to return home from exile on 16 December, her spokesperson said on Tuesday. In a statement, Syed Mozammel Ali asked the government to arrange her flight from Delhi to Dhaka. He argued that she had been flown to India on a Bangladeshi aircraft in August 2024, and that the state should bring her back by the same means.

Hasina fled to India on 5 August 2024, when tens of thousands of protesters converged on Dhaka and crowds stormed her official residence. Her 15 years in power ended after a student-led movement, which had started as protests against quotas for government jobs, grew into a mass uprising. The United Nations estimates that up to 1,400 people were killed during the unrest.

The date she chose is symbolic. Bangladesh marks Victory Day on 16 December, the anniversary of the end of its 1971 war of independence from Pakistan. Hasina’s father, Sheikh Mujibur Rahman, led the independence movement and became the first leader of the new state. He was killed in a military coup in 1975. Abu Obaidha Arin, an aide to Hasina, described the timing as deeply symbolic and said he believed she would mark the day among her people and once again lead the country.

The government has rejected her proposal. Abu Abdullah M Saleh, press secretary to Prime Minister Tarique Rahman, said by phone that the government’s position was clear: Hasina had been convicted, and Dhaka had requested her extradition. At a briefing on Tuesday, Bangladesh’s state minister for foreign affairs, Humayun Kabir, said she would not return “as an ordinary commercial passenger” and would be “brought back in handcuffs”. He also alleged that Hasina and Delhi were backing “terrorist activities” in Bangladesh. India’s foreign ministry did not immediately respond to a request for comment. In April, it said it was examining the extradition request and wanted to engage constructively with the new government.

A Bangladeshi court sentenced Hasina to death in absentia last year for crimes against humanity, over the crackdown on the protests. She denies the charges and has said she will fight the case if she returns. The judiciary has said she missed the deadline to appeal, which leaves no legal basis to halt her execution. Presidential clemency is possible under Bangladeshi law, but she has not sought it. Aminul Islam, chief prosecutor of the International Crimes Tribunal that issued the verdict, said it was unreasonable for a convicted fugitive to expect the government to arrange her return. Hasina is expected to be arrested and jailed on arrival.

Her party, the Awami League, was banned from political activities in May 2025 under the interim government led by Nobel Peace Prize laureate Muhammad Yunus. It was then barred from contesting February’s general election, which was won by its long-time rival, the Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP), led by Tarique Rahman. Awami League supporters continue to hold protests despite the ban and have called for nationwide shutdowns demanding Hasina’s return.

Hasina’s activities from Indian soil have also strained relations between Delhi and Dhaka. India had close ties with Hasina during her 15 years in power and hoped the new government would allow a reset. Rahman has not yet visited India despite an invitation. Talks about a possible visit have resumed, but no dates have been announced.
