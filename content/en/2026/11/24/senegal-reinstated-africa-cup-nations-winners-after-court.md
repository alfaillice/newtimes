Senegal have been reinstated as winners of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations (Afcon) after the Court of Arbitration for Sport (Cas), sport's highest legal authority, overturned a decision that had stripped the West African side of the title. The ruling was published on Tuesday, almost ten months after the final.

On the pitch, the Teranga Lions beat hosts Morocco 1-0 after extra time in Rabat in January, with Pape Gueye scoring the winning goal. The match was chaotic. Senegal walked off in the stoppage time of the second half in protest at a penalty awarded against them, causing a delay of 15 minutes. Senegal's forward Sadio Mané helped persuade his team to return as tensions rose in the stadium. When referee Jean-Jacques Ndala restarted the game, Morocco's Brahim Diaz had his penalty saved.

Almost two months later, an appeals panel appointed by the Confederation of African Football (Caf) overturned the result. It ruled that Senegal had breached the competition regulations by leaving the field without the referee's authorisation, and awarded Morocco a 3-0 default win. Caf cited a rule under which a team that refuses to play or leaves the pitch before the regular end of the match without permission is considered the loser and eliminated from the competition. Football's laws state that referee decisions are final, but the appeal judges relied on the tournament regulations.

The Senegal Football Federation (FSF) appealed to Cas. A three-man panel heard the case behind closed doors in Lausanne on 8 October, in a one-day hearing. Its verdict, published on Tuesday, restores Senegal's title. Cas verdicts can be challenged before Switzerland's supreme court, but only on limited procedural grounds.

Separately, Caf disciplinary judges imposed fines of more than $1m (£770,000) on the two federations, the players and Senegal's then coach, Pape Thiaw. The sources do not say whether the Cas ruling affects those penalties.
