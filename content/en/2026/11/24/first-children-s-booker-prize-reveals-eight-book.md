The first Children's Booker Prize has announced its shortlist of eight books, and four children have been named as judges who will help choose the winner. The £50,000 award is run by the Booker prize foundation, which also runs the adult Booker prize, and is aimed at fiction for readers aged eight to 12. The organisers call the list the “Great Eight”.

The shortlist includes five novels, two graphic novels and an illustrated collection of short stories. Its authors and illustrators come from the UK, Ireland, Australia and Italy. Shaun Tan, the Oscar-winning artist, is on the list with Hometown, about a girl on an alien planet who learns that her real home lies somewhere else. Eoin Colfer, the former Children's Laureate of Ireland best known for the Artemis Fowl series, is included with Andrew Donkin and the graphic novel War, illustrated by Giovanni Rigano. War is the third book in that series and follows 14-year-old Kat through an unnamed European city under siege.

The other titles are The Night I Borrowed Time by Iqbal Hussain, a time-travel adventure; The Teller of Tall Tales by Liz Hyder, illustrated by Tom de Freston; The Rambler's Curse by Mikey Please, a comedy featuring a goblin waiter; Séamus Almost Famous by Jessica Scott-Whyte, illustrated by Becka Moor; The Worst Thing That Could Possibly Happen by Guy Bass, illustrated by David Roberts; and Tadpole Summer by Catherine Bruton. Tadpole Summer follows a girl whose younger brother is seriously ill, and several of the books deal with grief, illness and war.

The shortlist was chosen from 89 submissions by the chair of judges, the writer Frank Cottrell-Boyce, along with the actor and comedian Lolly Adefope and the bookseller Sanchita Basu De Sarkar. Cottrell-Boyce said he believed “there’s a book for every child”, and described the prize as a way of bringing books into view that might not otherwise get attention.

The four child judges were picked from more than 4,000 entries. They are Ibrahim, 12, from Hampshire, Lainey, 12, Luna, eight, and Myla, 11. Cottrell-Boyce said the judges had to be enthusiastic about books and willing to speak their minds, and that he was not concerned that adults on the panel would dominate the decisions. Ibrahim said he did not expect the adults to control the discussion.

Cottrell-Boyce defended the range of subjects in the list, saying that the great classic children’s books often deal with serious issues. He pointed to The Railway Children, in which a father is in prison, and to Anne of Green Gables, a book he described as full of joy. Gaby Wood, chief executive of the Booker prize foundation, said the list “respects a wide range of tastes in children”.

The prize is part of a wider effort to encourage reading. Its launch last year followed concern that children’s enjoyment of reading had fallen to its lowest level in 20 years. At least 30,000 copies of the shortlisted books will be given to children aged eight to 12 through schools, libraries, hospitals, youth clubs and foodbanks across the UK, in partnership with the National Literacy Trust and the Reading Agency. From January, a series of short films will show performers including Adeel Akhtar, Dina Asher-Smith, Dafne Keen and Aidan Turner reading extracts from the books.

The winner will be announced at the Young V&A in London on 2 February. The shortlist was published two weeks after M John Harrison won the 2026 adult Booker prize for The End of Everything.
