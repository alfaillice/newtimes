The US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), the federal agency responsible for American air traffic control, has lifted a nationwide ground stop that halted departing flights across the country on Friday. The order was issued at 3.54pm Eastern Time (21.54 CET) after a communications outage affecting the air route traffic control centres, which handle aircraft travelling between regions. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said airspace reopened at 8.15pm Eastern Time, or 02.15 CET on Saturday.

The length of the stoppage is reported differently. The Guardian and the BBC describe it as lasting more than four hours. Al Jazeera gives both "five hours" and "more than four hours of disruption" in different parts of its report. The BBC and Al Jazeera both describe it as the longest nationwide ground stop since the attacks of 11 September 2001, while Al Jazeera adds that it was the second such nationwide halt of all departures since then.

The cause remains disputed in the details. Duffy said that switches and routers, part of an analogue telecommunications system, became overloaded with data and failed. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said that new equipment added as part of patches to the system had overwhelmed the older network, and that there was "zero evidence" of a cyber-attack. According to the Guardian, Duffy denied that the new Smart system, which uses artificial intelligence and was activated at all 22 air route traffic control centres three days earlier, had caused the problem. Al Jazeera cited a Government Accountability Office (GAO) report published in September, which had warned of ageing copper cables dating back to the 1960s and of changes being scheduled at the same time.

The disruption was large. FlightAware, cited by the BBC, counted more than 8,500 delayed flights and nearly 8,000 cancelled on Friday. Al Jazeera, citing FlightRadar24, reported more than 4,700 cancellations and 10,000 delays. The Guardian reported at least 600 diverted flights, while Al Jazeera, citing the analytics firm Cirium, counted at least 827. Eurocontrol, whose 42 member states include EU nations, the UK, Ukraine and Turkey, said that US-bound traffic from its members had been suspended at the FAA's request. Tens of thousands of passengers were stranded.

Duffy said the system needed a complete overhaul and asked Congress, the US legislature, for $50bn. "It's going to take us some time to unwind what's happened this afternoon," he said. He warned that flights would resume slowly and that airlines would need days to restore schedules. He expected operations to be back at full capacity by the Thanksgiving travel period, which begins in less than two weeks. Mullin said that about 3,000 flights had landed safely after the order and that "everybody's safe". Dr Guy Gratton, an aviation academic at Cranfield University, told the BBC that a 2023 failure of the Notice to Air Missions (Notam) system had grounded flights for 1 hour and 52 minutes, a shorter stoppage.
