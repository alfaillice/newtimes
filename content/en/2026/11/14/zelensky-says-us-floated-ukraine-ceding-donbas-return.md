Ukraine’s president, Volodymyr Zelensky, has said that US negotiators have floated the idea of Ukraine withdrawing its remaining forces from the eastern Donbas region in exchange for Nato membership, according to the BBC. He made the comments on Friday, and his office released them on Saturday.

The Donbas is an industrial region in eastern Ukraine made up of the Donetsk and Luhansk regions. Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, and Moscow now controls most of the Donbas, the BBC reports. Ukraine still holds the smaller areas of the region that remain under its control. Ceding them would be symbolically significant for Kyiv, the broadcaster notes.

According to the BBC, Zelensky said US officials had asked whether Ukraine would withdraw from Donbas if it joined Nato. He described this as the first time the question had been raised. He said the proposal made him believe that “Nato is back on the agenda”. He added that if he received a proposal containing security guarantees, no constraints on Ukraine’s military and funds to rebuild the country, he would put it to a referendum.

The US proposal follows talks between Trump’s envoys and both Russian President Vladimir Putin and Zelensky. Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff, the envoys of US President Donald Trump, travelled to Dublin on Thursday to meet Zelensky after a round of talks with Putin in Moscow. Kyiv has long sought Nato membership, which the United States and others had previously opposed. Russia has said another Nato member on its border is unacceptable.

The BBC reports that Moscow has previously proposed limits on the size of Ukraine’s military, territorial concessions and guaranteed neutrality for Kyiv as conditions of a peace deal. Ukraine has rejected these, arguing they would weaken its ability to resist a future invasion. The broadcaster also reports that Russia has now revived its 2021 demands that Nato halt any expansion near Russia and roll back military infrastructure in countries of central and eastern Europe that joined after the Soviet Union dissolved. The US and other Western nations rejected those demands at the time, and Moscow invaded Ukraine a few months later.

Questions of European security were on the agenda of the latest round of talks between Ukraine, Russia and the US in Abu Dhabi this week, the BBC says. Russia has insisted it will not agree to a ceasefire until a final peace deal is in place, including territorial settlements and security arrangements. Zelensky cautioned that “this is an extremely difficult conversation”, adding that Putin had also sent signals.

The BBC analysis of the comments by Putin and Kremlin officials is that Russia’s position has not shifted, and that its demands for territory and neutrality far exceed what Ukraine is prepared to consider.

Relations between Kyiv and Washington have been strained in recent weeks. Trump had asked Ukraine to pause strikes on Russian oil facilities deep inside Russia, saying this would help supply Russian diesel and ease global fuel prices. Zelensky then accused US officials of limiting Ukraine’s Starlink internet service, which it uses to co-ordinate defensive operations. Trump called for fresh elections in Ukraine. Elections are suspended while the country remains under martial law. Zelensky has said he would be willing to hold elections within months of a peace deal and would give up the presidency in exchange for Nato membership, the BBC notes.
