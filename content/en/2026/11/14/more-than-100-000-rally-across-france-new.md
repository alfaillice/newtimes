More than 100,000 people joined protests across France on Saturday, according to Al Jazeera, in another round of cost-of-living rallies organised by the Yellow Vest movement and several youth groups. Al Jazeera reports that France's Interior Ministry counted 106,000 participants nationwide, including 24,000 in Paris. Student unions gave much higher figures, saying 200,000 people took part, almost 100,000 of them in the capital.

Both figures are lower than those of the previous weekend, when the Interior Ministry recorded 146,000 protesters nationwide and 46,000 in Paris, according to Al Jazeera. Authorities described Saturday's demonstrations as largely calm, the broadcaster said.

The Yellow Vest movement, whose name is the French for "Gilet Jaune", began in 2018 over rising fuel prices. It shook the early months of the government of President Emmanuel Macron and lasted about two years. The current wave has a wider agenda. For more than a month, high-school blockades, mass demonstrations and strikes have targeted falling educational standards, the rising cost of living, low wages and police violence. Many placards attacked Macron, who has faced strong criticism from protesters, Al Jazeera reports.

Al Jazeera also reports that some rallies have seen violence at their edges. The first major protest, on 17 October, was followed by looting in cities across France. Since then, police presence at demonstrations has grown, and checks on public transport and the permits needed to protest have been tightened.

On Saturday the city of Marseille, France's second-largest, banned protests, yet the local prefecture said about 3,500 people still went onto the streets. Local media reported that tear gas and a water cannon were used against demonstrators, and shared images of fires in a popular local square. In Lyon, local media estimated roughly 8,000 people took part. They reported masked protesters chanting in front of street fires, and police charges with tear gas. Al Jazeera says tear gas was also used in Nantes, Bordeaux and Paris.

The unions have rejected a government offer to raise the minimum wage by 0.6 per cent, which they called "crumbs". They have called a new day of strikes for Tuesday. If the protests go ahead, they will fall on the eighth anniversary of the movement's start. The Yellow Vest tracking website 17octobregiletjaune.net, cited by Al Jazeera, says supporters plan to mark the anniversary at roundabouts and toll booths across France.
