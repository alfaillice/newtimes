Wales beat New Zealand 35-21 in a nine-try Nations Championship match at the Principality Stadium in Cardiff, ending a losing run against the All Blacks that stretched back to 1953, according to BBC Sport. It was Wales's first win over New Zealand in 73 years, after they had won the first three meetings in 1905, 1935 and 1953.

BBC Sport reported that Wales started quickly, with tries from scrum-half Tomos Williams and captain Dewi Lake inside the first six minutes, and led 14-0. Prop Archie Griffin added a try before half-time. New Zealand responded through Cortez Ratima, Wallace Sititi and Fehi Fineanganofo, and the sides were level at 21-21 at the interval. Early in the second half, centre Eddie James and wing Louis Rees-Zammit scored, giving Wales a 35-21 lead. Flanker Peter Lakai pulled one try back for New Zealand, but the hosts then held the visitors up three times over their own try line in the final quarter.

The defensive effort was led, according to the report, by flankers James Botham and Jac Morgan and lock Dafydd Jenkins. BBC Sport described the performance as one of the finest in Welsh rugby history and said it was far removed from Wales's 28-21 win over Japan the previous weekend. Wales made four changes to that side. Taine Basham made his first Test start in four years, replacing the injured Aaron Wainwright, and Gabriel Hamer-Webb was preferred on the wing to Josh Adams.

The result came despite pre-match doubts. BBC Sport reported that sections of the New Zealand media had belittled Welsh rugby, and that some bookmakers had offered odds of 20-1 on a Wales win. Former Wales players including Sir Gareth Edwards, Alun Wyn Jones and Shane Williams had never beaten New Zealand while wearing the Welsh shirt, the report noted.

The crowd of 55,152 was the lowest in the 14 matches between the two sides at the Principality Stadium, according to BBC Sport, which said those who stayed away would regret it. New Zealand, who had beaten Scotland 43-21 in their previous Test, were led by Beauden Barrett, who was handed the captaincy for the first time in his 149th international. Coach Dave Rennie had said before the match that New Zealand needed to contain Morgan at the breakdown, but the report said they failed to do so.

Despite the defeat, New Zealand still moved above South Africa, the Springboks, at the top of the southern section, after France had beaten South Africa on the Friday night. BBC Sport noted that one weekend of regular matches remains. For Welsh rugby, which has been struggling on and off the field, the report said the victory could help unite the nation, and that players and fans were reduced to tears at the final whistle.
