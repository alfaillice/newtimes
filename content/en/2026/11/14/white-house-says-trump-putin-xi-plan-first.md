The White House has said that US President Donald Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping are planning to meet next week, on the sidelines of an Asia-Pacific summit in China. If it goes ahead, it would be the first time the three leaders have met together. The announcement was made by White House spokeswoman Olivia Wales, according to the BBC, and was confirmed by a White House assistant press secretary, according to Al Jazeera.

The meeting would take place during the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen, a southern Chinese city known as a technology hub. The BBC reports that Trump will arrive there late on Wednesday for a two-day summit of 21 member states. He will also attend a summit of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (Asean) in the Philippines on Tuesday. On Thursday he will hold bilateral talks with Xi, their fourth meeting since Trump returned to office in 2025. Wales said Trump would end his visit to China with the three-way meeting.

The main subject is nuclear weapons. The New START treaty, which limited the arsenals of Washington and Moscow, expired in February. According to Al Jazeera, Trump rejected a Russian proposal to extend it by a year and asked instead for a new agreement that includes China, which has been expanding its arsenal. Wales said Trump would discuss reducing nuclear weapons worldwide. The United Nations has urged both sides to agree a new framework without delay.

Both outlets report that the Iran war is expected to be discussed, though the BBC says this is only understood. Both also report that Ukraine will not be on the agenda. Al Jazeera adds that Elon Musk will travel with Trump but will not attend the meeting, and that Trump is not expected to meet North Korean leader Kim Jong Un during the trip.

A senior American official told Reuters and AP that no agreement was expected from the talks. Al Jazeera quotes the official as saying: "We do not expect to get to any kind of agreement", and describing the meeting as the start of a conversation.

The planned summit has been compared with the 1945 Yalta Conference, where the leaders of the US, Britain and the Soviet Union shaped the post-war order. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov rejected that comparison, saying any agreements reached in Shenzhen could not be imposed on others. Al Jazeera reports that he nonetheless called the meeting "extremely significant". The BBC said at the time of its report that Moscow and Beijing had yet to comment. Chinese state media reported on Friday that Xi would take part in bilateral talks on the sidelines of the APEC summit, citing a foreign ministry spokesman, but did not mention a trilateral meeting. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has voiced concern that the meeting could be used to discuss a deal on Ukraine without Kyiv.

Trump and Xi have met several times this year. Xi hosted Putin and Trump in Beijing within days of each other, and Trump welcomed Xi to Washington in September. Little progress has been reported on trade, AI or Taiwan. Beijing has also become a key trading partner for Moscow since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, which led to Western sanctions against Russia.
