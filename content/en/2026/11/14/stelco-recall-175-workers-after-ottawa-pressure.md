Canadian steelmaker Stelco says it will recall all 175 workers laid off at its Hamilton plant and pay them back wages. Al Jazeera reports that the decision followed a weeks-long standoff between workers, the Canadian government and the company's owner, the US steel group Cleveland-Cliffs.

The company announced the move on Friday, shortly after Canada's Industry Minister, Melanie Joly, said she would ask a court to order the sale of Stelco unless she heard from management by the end of the day. According to Al Jazeera, Stelco said in a statement that it had reached agreements with enough customers to restart its finishing operations. The workers will return on 1 December and receive pay for the standard wages they would have earned while laid off.

The dispute goes back to late September, when Cleveland-Cliffs, which bought Stelco in 2024, said up to 500 workers could be laid off at its plants in Hamilton and Nanticoke, Ontario. The company cited weak demand for cold-rolled and coated steel, which is used to make products ranging from cars to household appliances. It blamed US tariffs on Canadian steel imposed by President Donald Trump, which raised the cost of its product in the United States, and cheaper foreign steel that it said was eating into demand in Canada.

Ottawa, the seat of the federal government, argued that the layoffs broke binding commitments made when Cleveland-Cliffs bought Stelco. These included keeping the same number of unionised Canadian employees until 2029. The government and the United Steelworkers union also said that the company's chief executive, Lourenco Goncalves, had broken a pledge to promote policies that would benefit steel industries in both Canada and the United States. Al Jazeera notes that Goncalves publicly backed Trump's steel tariffs, including those affecting Canada, while also blaming the same tariffs for Stelco's financial problems.

Ron Wells, president of United Steelworkers Local 1005, welcomed the reversal and credited pressure from workers, the union and the government. At a news conference, he described the layoffs as “illegal” and “immoral” and said they should never have happened. Joly, who appeared alongside him, said the outcome showed investors that Canada is “a country of the rule of law and that the law applies to everyone, notwithstanding the size of your wallet.”

In its statement, Cleveland-Cliffs said its customers had committed to supporting its Hamilton operations. It blamed the federal government for failing to respond to shortfalls in its tariff response programme. According to Al Jazeera, the company did not give details of the agreements with customers and did not explain what had changed to make restarting the operations viable.
