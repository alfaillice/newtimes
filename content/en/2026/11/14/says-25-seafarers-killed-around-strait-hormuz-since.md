At least 25 seafarers have been killed in and around the Strait of Hormuz since the US-Israeli war on Iran broke out in February, according to the United Nations, Al Jazeera reported. The figure covers the period up to November 11 and was given by Khaled Khiari, the UN assistant secretary-general for the Middle East, Asia and the Pacific, during a Security Council briefing on Friday.

According to Al Jazeera, Khiari told the council that the situation “cannot be allowed to become the new normal” and called for the full restoration of maritime navigation in the region. He said security incidents involving ships in and around the strait had been rising, with October recording the highest number since the conflict began.

The UN’s International Maritime Organization (IMO) has tracked 136 security incidents involving vessels in Hormuz and the wider Middle East between March 1 and November 11, Al Jazeera reported. The IMO counted 35 of those incidents in October and 10 in November so far. Khiari described the incidents as unacceptable and said civilian vessels and commercial shipping must not be targeted.

In his remarks, Khiari said the current maritime arrangements in the Middle East remain costly, unpredictable and unsafe. He warned that disruption to shipping has wider global effects, pushing up energy, transport and insurance costs, feeding inflation and threatening food security, with fertiliser shipments severely constrained.

On Saturday, Iranian authorities reported that three oil tankers had struck mines while travelling through what they described as “unauthorised” or “restricted” areas of the strait, and had caught fire, Al Jazeera reported. Tehran has told vessels that they need Iranian approval to cross the waterway during the conflict, and has repeatedly attacked ships it accuses of breaking that rule.

Iran’s navy warned in a statement carried by the state broadcaster IRIB that any tanker entering the restricted area east of the strait would be set on fire by naval mines. Iranian officials have said the strait will not reopen until the United States meets its demands. These include ending the US naval blockade of Iran’s ports, lifting economic sanctions, releasing frozen Iranian funds, ending fighting on all fronts and formally announcing an end to the war, according to Al Jazeera.

The US military says it has cleared the main routes of mines and that the waterway is open to commercial shipping, Al Jazeera reported. However, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) said in its latest report that shipping through the strait is roughly 65 per cent below pre-war levels.

Al Jazeera’s report does not say how the 25 deaths occurred or how many of them were linked to vessels struck by mines. The UN figure is the only casualty count cited in the report.
