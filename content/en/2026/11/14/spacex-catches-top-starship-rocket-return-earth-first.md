The flight began on Friday at 16:47 local time, when the 124-metre rocket lifted off. The Ship then completed 14 orbits over roughly twenty-two and a half hours, deploying 26 satellites that form part of Starlink, SpaceX's high-speed internet network.

This was the 15th test of Starship and the first attempt to recover both stages in full. SpaceX has caught the booster on three earlier test flights, but the Ship reached orbit for the first time only in September. The company wants both sections to be fully reusable, which it sees as the route to much lower launch costs. Its Falcon 9 rockets already fly more than 100 times a year.

The result matters for NASA, the US space agency, which has contracted SpaceX to convert the Ship into a lunar lander for astronauts. NASA is aiming for a crewed Moon landing in 2028, and the Artemis III mission is currently planned for summer 2027. Blue Origin, the company owned by Jeff Bezos, also holds a lunar lander contract. Its New Glenn rocket exploded earlier this year, destroying its launchpad in Florida, and its return to flight has been delayed to early 2027.
