Italy's data protection authority, the country's privacy regulator, has fined Meta €805m for accessing photos on users' phones without their specific consent, according to the BBC. The regulator said the company did not properly inform about 20 million smartphone users, many of them on Android, that Facebook had access to their photos and videos through a feature in the Facebook app. Meta also owns Instagram and WhatsApp.

The feature suggests photos and videos from a user's phone gallery as ideas for posts. The BBC reports that Facebook asked for access to users' photo galleries through a single authorisation covering the whole app. The regulator said an earlier notice did not make clear that this would also allow Facebook to generate suggestions based on the photos in people's galleries.

According to the BBC, the authority has given Meta 60 days to disable the feature by default and to seek users' consent separately. The regulator said it had taken into account "the high number of people affected", and that the matter remains open for users who have not updated the app and for those who have already agreed to the system. The BBC describes the fine as one of the largest an Italian authority has ever imposed on a technology giant.

Meta told the BBC that it "strongly disagree[s]" with the regulator's assessment of the facts, its application of the law and the level of the fine. The company said the feature is "straightforward" and helps people post photos and videos to Facebook. It added that people must give permission on their device before the feature can operate, and that they can change this permission at any time in their settings.

The BBC also places the Italian decision in the context of a larger penalty against the company last month. A US judge in New Mexico ordered Meta to pay $25bn after finding that it had spent years lying to the public about how it handled Facebook users' data. The BBC gives dollar and sterling equivalents for the Italian fine; this story keeps the figure in euros as reported. The BBC's account is the only source used for this story, so the details of the Italian decision come from that outlet alone.
