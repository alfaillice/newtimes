A two-hour hearing at Spain's high court on Friday examined a United States request to extradite James "Fergie" Chambers, a wealthy leftwing activist and philanthropist. According to The Guardian, the session was marked by sharply different accounts of the case from the defence and the prosecution.

Chambers, an heir to the Atlanta-based family business Cox Enterprises, was arrested in Ibiza on 10 July. He describes himself as a communist and anti-imperialist. The Guardian reports that the request, issued under the Trump administration, is the first known case of the US seeking to extradite a citizen over alleged support for Hamas. That accusation is apparently tied to an international money laundering charge and to donations to organisations helping Palestinians in Gaza.

Two of the three charges, riot and conspiracy to riot, relate to protests in 2023 against Elbit, an Israeli weapons manufacturer, in Massachusetts and New Hampshire. According to The Guardian, Chambers acknowledged taking part in those protests, including paying bail for four protesters who were later convicted of misdemeanours and served 30 days in jail.

The defence, led by lawyer Aitor Martínez, also addressed transfers of $7.2m from the US to Tunisia in 2024, where Chambers was living. The Guardian says that ten bank transfers mostly sponsored the football club Club Africain and a sports business he set up in Tunisia. Asked whether any money had funded terrorism, the financial expert Albert Deulofeu Schilt answered "None".

The prosecutor, Juan Hernández Villalba, presented a different picture. He argued that the protests were planned over several days and had violent content, and that there were enough indications that Chambers's funding was aimed at terrorism. He did not put any questions to Chambers, according to The Guardian.

Chambers told the court that he had never had contact with a terrorist organisation or financed one. He said his family had been affected and that he had missed his grandmother's funeral. The Guardian also reports that his partner, the activist and actor Stella Schnabel, said his foundation had funded about 100 projects in 20 countries. Irish film-maker Sinéad O'Shea, who is making a documentary about Chambers, told the Guardian she considered the case politically motivated.

The three-judge panel will decide next week whether to grant extradition. If it does, Spain's council of ministers, the cabinet, can overturn the court's decision. The Guardian notes that the matter could be among the last decisions taken by the Socialist prime minister, Pedro Sánchez, before national elections on 29 November.
