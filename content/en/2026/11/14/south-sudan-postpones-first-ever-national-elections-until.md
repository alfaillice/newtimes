South Sudan has postponed its first-ever presidential and parliamentary elections until 11 February next year, Al Jazeera reported on Saturday. The country's election commission announced the new date that day. The vote had been scheduled for 22 December this year, and the commission gave no reason for the change, according to Al Jazeera.

The delay is the latest in a series. Al Jazeera describes South Sudan as the world's youngest country and says it has postponed the vote at least three times since 2022 was set as its election year. The country became independent from Sudan in 2011. Forces loyal to President Salva Kiir and those of Riek Machar, a former vice president who is now detained, fought a civil war from 2013 to 2018 that killed at least 400,000 people, Al Jazeera reports. A 2018 peace deal ended that war, but the outlet says the country still faces violence.

The political situation has become more fragile this year. In September, Kiir dissolved the transitional unity government and appointed a caretaker administration. According to Al Jazeera, this allowed the country to move towards elections without first completing a permanent constitution or a census, two processes that had previously been tied to the transition. Analysts and watchdogs warned that the move dismantled a key power-sharing arrangement, concentrated power in Kiir's hands and raised the risk of renewed widespread conflict.

Yasmin Sooka, chair of the United Nations Commission on Human Rights in South Sudan, said in October: "This amounts to a constitutional coup through legislative reforms." Al Jazeera also reports that Kiir's government forces and followers of Machar have been involved in escalating, deadly confrontations in recent months. Ethnic tensions and cattle-rustling battles add further complications. Machar has been under house arrest since last March and is accused of crimes against humanity, the outlet says.

Under the new timetable, voter registration is due to begin on Saturday 14 November and run until 28 November. A 45-day campaign period will run from 27 December to 9 February, followed by a one-day silence period before the 11 February poll. Gabriel Bol Deng, chairman of the National Elections Commission, said 3,161 registration centres had been set up across the country and more than 22,000 staff recruited, Al Jazeera reports.

According to the World Bank, cited by Al Jazeera, about 90 per cent of South Sudan's 15 million inhabitants have no state-recognised identification, which complicates registration. Deng said people without such documents, particularly in rural areas, will be identified by community leaders and issued an identification form that will allow them to vote.
