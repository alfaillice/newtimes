Sotheby's says it is working to resolve a dispute over the planned sale of a painting by Vincent van Gogh, according to the BBC. The work, *Châtaigniers en Fleurs*, whose title translates as *Blossoming Chestnut Trees*, carries an estimate of $180m (£136m) and is due to go under the hammer in New York on Wednesday, the broadcaster reports.

According to the BBC, representatives acting for heirs of the Fischer family, who were earlier German owners of the work, say it may have been sold under duress during the rise of the Nazis. The auction house says its own "extensive research" has not established this, but it has said it will work with the family to try to reach a solution.

In a statement to the BBC, Sotheby's said: "While our research to date has not established that the painting was sold under duress", adding that it was committed to working toward a mutually satisfactory resolution with the family. The BBC reports that the painting is being sold as part of a set called the Blaquier-Arrieta Collection.

The auction house's website sets out the work's provenance, the history of its owners since 1890, according to the BBC. It states that Samuel Fischer, a well-known German publisher, acquired the painting in 1909, and that it passed down within his family after his death in 1934. The BBC reports that the work was deposited with Paul Cassirer in Amsterdam in 1932 or 1933, and went to Henriette and Marcel Kapferer in Paris in 1936. Four other owners have held it since before it was put up for auction this year, the broadcaster says.

The BBC, citing the New York Times, reports that a lawyer acting for the Fischer heirs has written to Sotheby's. The lawyer is said to have told the auction house that the painting was sold in Amsterdam before the Second World War because the Fischer family's publishing company suffered financial losses under the Nazis. These are the family's representatives' claims as reported by the BBC; the broadcaster's report does not say whether the sale will go ahead as planned on Wednesday.
