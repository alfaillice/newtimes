US President Donald Trump has urged the board of Nvidia, the chipmaker at the centre of the artificial intelligence boom, to bring back its co-founder and former chief executive, Jensen Huang. Trump made the call on his Truth Social platform on Sunday. Live market coverage reported that Nvidia's shares were set to rise almost 7 per cent when Wall Street opened, a move that had not yet taken place when this report was written.

In his post, Trump said that rehiring Huang would be the best thing for the company and for the United States. It follows remarks he made last week, when he said he would not mind seeing Huang reinstated, and on Friday, when he told reporters at the White House that he would like Huang to be given a second chance.

Huang resigned on 12 November after reports that he had two extramarital affairs, one of them with an alleged Chinese spy. Beijing has rejected the espionage allegations, and US authorities have said they have not reached a conclusion. On 17 November, Huang said he would be willing to return as chief executive if the board judged that best for the company, its staff, its shareholders and America.

The board has not changed course. Its chair, Harvey Jones, told analysts on the company's earnings call last Tuesday that the board "unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO". Colette Kress, Nvidia's finance chief, is acting as interim chief executive while the company looks for a permanent replacement, and an outside law firm is carrying out an investigation.

Pressure to bring Huang back has also grown outside the White House. A "Stand with Jensen" petition, an open letter to the board, had more than 206,000 signatures by Monday morning. Tech entrepreneur Elon Musk is among the signatories. More than 10,000 signers say they are current Nvidia employees. Nvidia and Huang have not endorsed the letter. Two shareholders, the investment firms Altimeter Capital and Atreides Management, have asked the board to keep Huang's return open and to delay naming a permanent chief executive until the facts are known.

The share price has fallen sharply. Nvidia closed at around $125.5 on Friday, 48 per cent below its intraday all-time high of roughly $243.3 on 6 October. Its shares fell 14 per cent that day after a Reuters report, citing two people briefed on the matter, said the Securities and Exchange Commission, the US markets regulator, was examining possible accounting issues at the company. Market commentators have linked the wider sell-off in tech shares to Huang's departure, given Nvidia's central role in the AI industry.

Huang is due to answer questions behind closed doors on 1 December from three committees of the House of Representatives, the lower chamber of the US Congress: Foreign Affairs, Intelligence, and the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party. The committees are examining the possible diversion of advanced Nvidia chips to Chinese businesses. The session will be a transcribed interview, not open to the public. Huang is also expected to testify publicly before the Senate Banking Committee, but no date has been set.
