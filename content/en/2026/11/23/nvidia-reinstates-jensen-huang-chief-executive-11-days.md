Nvidia has reinstated Jensen Huang as its president, chief executive and board member, with effect from immediately, less than two weeks after he stepped down. The chipmaker announced the decision on Monday, reversing a choice its own board had made only days earlier.

Huang co-founded Nvidia and built it from a maker of graphics chips for video games into the dominant supplier of processors used to train and run artificial intelligence (AI) systems. He resigned on 12 November after reports of extramarital affairs, including one with a businesswoman whom US authorities suspect of spying for China. Beijing denies the allegation. US Attorney General Todd Blanche, the country's top law enforcement official, said on 12 November that the FBI was investigating reports that the businesswoman had worked for the Chinese government, and that the Justice Department had not yet reached a conclusion.

Last week, Nvidia said it would consider internal and external candidates to replace Huang, who had signalled he was willing to return if asked. When the company reported better-than-expected quarterly results, the board chair, Harvey Jones, told investors that the board did not intend to bring him back.

On Monday, Jones said the board believed Huang was "the right person to lead NVIDIA" and that his return was in the interests of shareholders, employees, partners and customers. He thanked Colette Kress, who had served as interim chief executive. Kress will remain chief financial officer, and Jones will stay on as chair.

The reversal followed public pressure from the White House. President Donald Trump urged the board to rehire Huang, and a petition calling for his return gathered more than 200,000 signatures. The board said it had received input from senior US government officials and was confident that his return posed no national security risk.

Investors reacted quickly. Nvidia shares rose more than 25% in premarket trading, the period before regular stock market hours, after the announcement.

Huang said he was honoured to return and would take on the role with "the greatest humility and renewed commitment."

The company's investigations continue. A committee of independent directors will keep looking into reports that Huang had been targeted by a foreign intelligence effort. It has hired independent lawyers, and Huang supports the inquiry and has pledged to cooperate, Nvidia said. A separate committee of independent directors will oversee trade compliance and national security risks, with help from specialist lawyers and advisers. Nvidia added that it would continue to cooperate with government investigations and inquiries.

Huang is due to face questions from three committees of the US House of Representatives, the lower chamber of Congress, on 1 December.
