Iran has flogged the singer Parastoo Ahmadi and four members of her concert team for performing without a headscarf, according to defence lawyers. The sentence of 74 lashes was carried out in the historic city of Qom on Monday, 23 November, weeks after an appeals court upheld the conviction.

Ahmadi, 29, performed in a sleeveless dress at a concert held at the Deyr Gachin Caravanserai, a historic venue near Qom, on 11 December 2024. No audience was present, but the performance was livestreamed on YouTube and drew around 3 million views in the following days. The judiciary later sealed the venue.

Iran's laws require women to wear headscarves and modest clothing in public. Under the law of the Islamic Republic, women are also barred from singing in public where men are present. Criminal law does not explicitly ban women's singing as such, so female performers usually face charges such as offending public decency, encouraging corruption or failing to observe hijab rules.

In June, a criminal court in Qom convicted Ahmadi and eight others of 'offending public decency' by publishing 'obscene and indecent material'. All of them denied the charges. Besides the lashes, the sentence imposed two-year bans on artistic and audiovisual activity and on leaving the country. In late September an appeals court rejected the appeal, according to the defendants' lawyers.

Accounts of the enforcement differ. One of the defence lawyers said Ahmadi and four male co-defendants were flogged at the third branch of the Criminal Sentence Enforcement Office in Qom, and that the lawyers had tried unsuccessfully to postpone the punishment. Another defence lawyer, Mostafa Nili, quoted by Iranian news outlets, said the Qom prosecutor's office enforcement team carried out the sentence against Ahmadi in the early hours of Monday, and that the sentences against the band members and the concert organiser were carried out the same day.

The Center for Human Rights in Iran, a US-based group, said its director of advocacy, Bahar Ghandehari, regarded the flogging as a flagrant violation of fundamental rights. In June, Amnesty International had called for the sentences to be quashed, describing flogging as a cruel, inhuman and degrading punishment that breaches the absolute prohibition on torture.

Ahmadi described the performance as a 'hypothetical concert' and wrote on her YouTube channel: 'I want to sing for the people I love.'

The case comes against the backdrop of the 2022 Woman, Life, Freedom protests, which followed the death in custody of Mahsa Amini, a Kurdish Iranian woman detained by the morality police over her hijab. That year Ahmadi performed the protest song 'From the blood of the nation's youths', after which security forces searched her home. Images of women without mandatory hijab at pro-government rallies during the US and Israeli strikes on Iran earlier in 2026 have also circulated widely, in contrast with the sentences.
