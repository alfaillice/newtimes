Authorities in Indonesia have confirmed that 24 people died in a fire that gutted a confectionery factory at the weekend, and rescuers are still searching for about 30 people who are missing. The blaze started on Saturday evening at the PT Fonusa Agung Mulia plant in Cikande, in Serang district of Banten province, about 50km west of Jakarta, the capital.

Firefighters reached the site on Saturday afternoon, but a large part of the building was already burning. The figures for how many people were inside differ. Andra Soni, the governor of Banten, said an estimated 60 people were in the factory when it caught fire. The company's chief executive, Steven Wijaya, said about 300 people had been at the complex for Saturday's shift.

On Sunday, Indonesia's National Search and Rescue Agency said 15 survivors had been located and that about 36 people were still missing. By Monday, the figure for missing had fallen to about 30. Al Amrad, head of the Banten provincial search and rescue agency, said 29 people had been confirmed safe, five of them treated in hospital. Rescuers had removed 24 body bags from the site.

Said Iqbal, a presidential adviser on labour issues, told reporters that preliminary reports pointed to a short circuit in an air-conditioner as a possible cause. He said the implementation of safety protocols was being assessed and that he would summon the factory's chief executive. Earlier, authorities had said they had not determined the cause. Wijaya said the factory regularly held emergency and evacuation drills with local firefighters, and that its fire protection systems met the applicable standards and were working at the time of the fire.

The bodies were too badly burned to be identified visually. Wahyu Hidayati Dwi Palupi, who heads the national police division for victim identification, urged families to provide DNA samples and other details that could help identify their relatives. Police said the process could take two to three weeks. Anxious relatives have been waiting for news outside the burnt-out building.

Firefighters have largely contained the blaze, but on Monday they were still carrying out intensive cooling operations before they could reach every part of the factory. Tubagus Mahfud, operations chief of the Serang fire department, said sweeps were under way in areas already under control. "We are conducting sweeps in the areas that have already been brought under control," Mahfud said. He added that parts of the structure had collapsed, that poor lighting required caution, and that the materials stored in the factory could cause the fire to reignite.

The firm employs about 5,000 people and supplies consumer goods to the domestic market. Its products include Milkita, a popular confectionery brand, and Lemonilo, an instant noodle range, according to its website. The company is owned by United Family Food, known as UNIFAM, one of Indonesia's largest food and beverage groups.

Indonesia has had a series of deadly industrial fires in recent years. In December last year, a blaze in a seven-storey building in Jakarta killed at least 22 people. Police said the fire was likely caused by an explosion of a drone battery. In 2023, an explosion at a nickel-processing plant in eastern Indonesia killed at least 13 people.
