Molly Meldrum, the Australian music promoter and broadcaster whose chaotic manner and trademark cowboy hat made him a national figure, has died aged 83 after a long illness, his family said.

Meldrum, born Ian Alexander Meldrum, died at a hospital in Melbourne in the early hours of Monday, according to a statement from his family, which said he died peacefully. The statement called him the heartbeat and champion of Australian music and gave him the nickname "the Guru".

For decades he helped set the country's musical tastes. He hosted Countdown, the music programme of the national public broadcaster from 1974 until 1987. On the show he promoted local acts such as INXS, Skyhooks and Sherbet, and helped international artists including Abba, Madonna and Blondie reach wider audiences. Accounts of his career also credit him with the first Top 10 hits of several bands, including Duran Duran and John Mellencamp. Later he appeared in a segment on the variety show Hey Hey, It's Saturday. He also produced records, wrote for Go-Set magazine and became a familiar voice on radio and television.

His manner was as memorable as his work. Known for his enthusiasm and a habit of stumbling over his words, he was the subject of much mockery, but he was widely loved. In 1977 he interviewed the then young Prince Charles, now King Charles, in an encounter that became one of his best-known moments. In 2014, on Countdown's 40th anniversary, King Charles recalled that nobody had prepared him for Meldrum.

He lived with an acquired brain injury after a fall in 2011. In 1986 he was made a member of the Order of Australia. In 2014 he became the first person who was not a recording artist to be inducted into the Australian Recording Industry Association (ARIA) Hall of Fame. Meldrum was also a devoted supporter of the St Kilda football club.

Arts Minister Tony Burke said on Monday that Australia had lost an icon, and he paid tribute to the generations of Australians who grew up with Meldrum's enthusiasm for music, describing him as a true champion of Australian talent.

Elton John, a friend of Meldrum, also paid tribute, saying: "I don't think anyone has done more for music in Australia than Molly Meldrum."
