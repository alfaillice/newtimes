Compass Group, the catering company that supplies meals to schools, universities, stadiums and some NHS hospitals, is planning to move its primary stock market listing from London to the United States. The move would be another blow to the London Stock Exchange (LSE). A source close to the company has said an announcement could come on Tuesday, when Compass publishes its full-year results. The company has not yet made an official statement.

Compass has been listed in London since 1988 and is one of the largest companies in the FTSE 100, the index of Britain's biggest listed businesses. It is valued at £43.6bn ($57bn), operates in more than 25 countries and employs more than 600,000 people. The group was created during the Second World War, when a wartime order required large factories and munitions firms with 250 or more workers to provide hot meals for their staff. Its brands include Levy, Bon Appétit, Eurest, Morrison Healthcare and Chartwells.

The proposed move is understood to reflect the company's growing business across the Atlantic and the range of investors it has in the US. North America accounts for two-thirds of its business and drove 2025 revenues to $46.1bn. Last year, about three-quarters of its $3bn profit was made in North America. Compass switched its reporting currency from sterling to US dollars in October 2023, and in April this year it changed the trading currency of its London-listed shares to dollars, which is cited as a sign that its listing could change.

According to the reports, the move would not affect the number of UK employees. Consulting shareholders could take more than a year, and Compass is expected to keep a secondary listing in London.

The move would add to a growing list of departures. Arm Holdings, Just Eat, Deliveroo and Flutter, the owner of Paddy Power, have all shifted their listings to the US in recent years, and the equipment rental firm Ashtead and the building materials supplier CRH have moved their primary listings there too. AstraZeneca began listing its shares directly in New York in February, and speculation continues over the future of Shell.

The contrast in market size is stark. Last year London hosted 23 initial public offerings (IPOs), raising £2.1bn. The US had 354 IPOs, raising $44bn, or about £33bn at the conversion used in the source. Professional services firm EY counted seven listings on the London market in the first half of this year, raising £577m in total.

Dame Julia Hoggett, the head of the London Stock Exchange, has recently urged the government to do more to back British companies and make the domestic stock market more attractive to investors. In a podcast interview, she said that if the UK wants to back British businesses, it needs to create structural incentives to do so.
