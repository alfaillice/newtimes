Alberto Núñez Feijóo, leader of Spain's conservative People's Party (PP) and its candidate for prime minister, said on Monday that his party could reach an agreement with Vox, the party led by Santiago Abascal, after the general election on 29 November. He said any such pact would be carried out within the law, as the PP's agreements in four regions have been.

In a television interview, Feijóo was asked about red lines for a possible deal. He said that "compliance with the law is fundamental". He then said that equality laws, which he argued are needed to tackle machismo in a real way, were untouchable, and added: "The LGTBI community and its rights are untouchable."

Feijóo said these matters had already been discussed in the negotiations to form governments in Extremadura, Aragon, Castile and León and Andalusia, and were included in those regional agreements. He said the regional governments in which the PP and Vox share power cover more than 30% of Spain's population.

He described Vox as "still the third force" in Spain and said it was expected to perform well in the election, with more than four million people set to vote for it. If he needed to complete a majority, he said, he would respect the result of the ballot. Asked whether Vox still caused the unease it once did, he said the party had adapted its message to the reality each region faced.

Feijóo also contrasted a Vox deal with what he called a "hooded" pact between Sánchez and EH Bildu, the Basque party, during this parliamentary term. He said a deal with Vox would be open, "with light and shorthand", a Spanish expression meaning transparent and on the record. Sánchez, leader of the Socialist Party (PSOE), has relied on the votes of pro-independence parties and EH Bildu to pass legislation, including the votes that secured his investiture in 2023. The investiture is the parliamentary vote that confirms a prime minister.

The election on 29 November will decide the next Congress of Deputies and whether the PP needs Vox to form a majority. Feijóo has said his priority is a change of government that can start work from the outset.
