Police in Germany are investigating bomb threats against schools across the country, while Danish police are looking into similar warnings in Denmark. Authorities in both countries said on Monday that the threats did not appear to be credible.

The threats were sent by email. Schools in Berlin, the German capital, were among those targeted, as were schools in Baden-Württemberg, Thuringia, Saxony-Anhalt and Rhineland-Palatinate. In Denmark, schools in Copenhagen were also among the recipients, according to reports.

The figures differ. Reports said more than 400 schools in Baden-Württemberg received threats and more than 250 in Saxony-Anhalt. German police, cited in other reports, put the total at 450 schools across the country.

The emails were largely identical. In Rhineland-Palatinate, police said the anonymous senders claimed to have placed explosive devices in school buildings and threatened to set them off. Police said they had no information yet on who was behind the messages. Investigators are treating the emails as threats of criminal activity.

Police in Freiburg, a city in Baden-Württemberg, said there was no evidence that the schools contacted so far faced any specific threat. Berlin police gave similar advice. Authorities in Thuringia told schools to carry on as normal, saying the apparent aim of the senders was to frighten people without taking action.

A spokesman for Germany's federal interior ministry said schools were not being advised to evacuate, because the incident fitted the current overall threat picture. He said schools and the security authorities of the federal states were prepared for such incidents. He declined to say who might be behind the threats.

In Denmark, police said they were investigating bomb threats directed at schools across the country and that there was no danger. Copenhagen Police said they understood that the messages could cause concern and were in contact with local councils and several schools. Schools on the islands of Bornholm and Funen, in Jutland and in the town of Herning were among those reported to have received threats, according to Danish media. Danish police said the threats were not credible but were creating insecurity locally.

The German and Danish cases are part of a wider series of hoaxes and cyberattacks in Europe in recent weeks. Schools, supermarkets, blood banks, air traffic systems and broadcasters have all been affected. Schools in Italy were evacuated earlier this month after thousands received emailed bomb threats. Last week, more than 10 schools in Spain were evacuated after receiving similar emails. Schools in Britain, Hungary and Serbia have also received hoax bomb threats in recent weeks.

For now, the German and Danish authorities have advised schools to keep operating normally while the investigations continue.
