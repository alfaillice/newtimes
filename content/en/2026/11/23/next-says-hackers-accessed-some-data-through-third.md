Next, the British clothing and homewares retailer, says hackers have gained access to some of its data through a third-party technology supplier. In a statement to investors on Monday, the company said the supplier had told it that it had "experienced a cyber incident", resulting in unauthorised access to certain data relating to Next.

The company, which is headquartered in Enderby, Leicestershire, said its current evidence indicates that no customer personal data has been compromised. It described the information involved as limited and said that, on its present assessment, it cannot be used to facilitate fraud against customers or against Next. It also said its own systems had not been compromised, and that the incident was affecting the supplier's environment.

It is understood that the data accessed does not include contact details, addresses, passwords or bank details, which are the kinds of information typically used to commit fraud. Next said its investigations are ongoing and that it is treating the matter "with the utmost seriousness". It said it is working with the supplier, law enforcement and the relevant regulatory authorities.

Next is the latest retailer in Britain to be caught up in a cyber breach. In October, the online fashion retailer Asos said an unauthorised party had gained access to an employee's account. It said the attacker obtained some customer personal data, including names and contact details.

The wider record of recent incidents is severe. In 2025, Marks & Spencer suffered a major cyber-attack that forced it to stop all online sales for about six weeks and left some shop shelves empty. Other British retailers, including the Co-op and Harrods, were hit by damaging incidents that affected their operations for months. The fashion group TFG London, which owns the Hobbs and Whistles brands, has also faced attacks in which customer data was accessed.

The Next incident also comes amid a wider wave of attacks on retailers and other businesses. Last week it emerged that a secret UK government cybersecurity operation had prevented an attack on the fridge and freezer systems used by supermarkets, warehouses, restaurants, petrol stations and stadiums. That followed attacks in continental Europe in recent months on fridge and freezer control systems, which caused many millions of euros of damage.
