England, Northern Ireland, Scotland and Wales will jointly host the 2035 Women’s World Cup, after Fifa, world football’s governing body, confirmed the four nations as co-hosts on Monday. It will be the first football World Cup held in the United Kingdom since the men’s tournament in 1966. The four associations were the only bidders, and the decision was ratified at a specially arranged virtual meeting. The same special meeting of Fifa’s Congress approved the 2031 tournament for the United States, Mexico, Costa Rica and Jamaica, which will be the first Women’s World Cup with 48 teams.

The 2035 finals will feature 104 matches contested by 48 teams over 39 days. The dates have not yet been fixed. Indicative dates of 21 June to 29 July were used informally during the bidding process.

The bid proposed 19 stadiums: 14 in England, one in Northern Ireland, two in Scotland and two in Wales. The English list includes Wembley, Old Trafford, the Emirates Stadium, Etihad Stadium and Tottenham Hotspur Stadium. The Welsh venues are Cardiff City Stadium and the Principality Stadium, while Scotland will use Hampden Park in Glasgow and Easter Road in Edinburgh. If Manchester United’s proposed new stadium is built in time, it could play a role instead of Old Trafford. Three further stadiums could be added: Birmingham’s proposed Powerhouse Stadium, Wrexham’s redeveloped Racecourse Ground and a potential new Chelsea ground. That would take the tournament to 16 cities and 22 venues. The list is expected to be reduced to between 14 and 16 venues.

Northern Ireland missed out on co-hosting the men’s European Championship in 2028 after the UK government said in 2024 that it would not fund the redevelopment of Casement Park in time. A redeveloped Windsor Park in Belfast is set to stage matches in 2035.

Fifa’s evaluation of the bid, published earlier this month, gave it 4.3 out of five. It rated the contractual framework as “medium risk” and said some government guarantees contained material qualifications, particularly on tax and on safety and security. Fifa is in dispute with the UK government and the four football associations over its demands for tax-free status for all football-related activities and exemptions from exchange-rate fees. Fifa has yet to decide whether all four co-hosts will qualify automatically.

The Football Association of Wales chief executive, Noel Mooney, said: “Wales has been a football nation for 150 years and we have never hosted anything on this scale.” Mark Bullingham, chief executive of the Football Association in England, said: “Hosting a World Cup would give us the chance to build on that across all four nations.”

The bid follows a difficult run for British football at the top level. England failed to win the right to host the men’s World Cups in 2006 and 2018, and a proposed UK and Ireland bid for 2030 was abandoned. England and Scotland hosted matches at the men’s Euros in 2021, and England staged the women’s European Championship in 2022, which the Lionesses won. England, Scotland, Wales and the Republic of Ireland will jointly host the men’s European Championship in 2028.
