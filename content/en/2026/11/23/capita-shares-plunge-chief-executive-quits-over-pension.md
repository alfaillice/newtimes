Shares in Capita, the UK outsourcing group, fell by about two-thirds on Monday after its chief executive, Adolfo Hernandez, agreed to leave the company with immediate effect. Reports differ on the size of the fall: one gives 67%, another about 70%. At its lowest point the share price reached 80p, its weakest level since October 1991.

Hernandez will be replaced by Pablo Andres, currently the company's chief financial officer. In a statement, Hernandez said the company had "not delivered the service that members of the civil service pension scheme deserve". He also defended the transformation he had led, which aimed to turn Capita into a technology-led, AI-enabled business services company.

The problems centre on the Civil Service Pension Scheme, which covers 1.7 million public sector workers in the UK. Capita took over the administration of the scheme from MyCSP on 1 December 2025, under a seven-year contract worth £239m agreed in 2023 with the then Conservative government. Since then tens of thousands of retiring civil servants have faced delays in receiving their pensions. Some pensioners have said they had to borrow money from family to pay for food and heating, and some feared losing their homes because they could not afford rent or mortgage payments.

MPs have blamed the failures on operational problems, including overwhelmed revision teams, poor technology and incorrect pension quotes. In October a Cabinet Office minister described the company's progress in improving the service as "abysmal". In July the Cabinet Office said it hoped to bring the scheme back in-house. The National Audit Office, the UK's public spending watchdog, is also examining the contract.

The financial hit is also growing. Capita now expects its adjusted operating profit for 2027 to be between £85m and £95m lower than previously forecast. It had earlier expected a reduction of up to £40m. The company links the change to the extra cost of its recovery plan for the pension contract and to the loss of two other major contracts, in its defence and pension solutions divisions.

To strengthen its finances, Capita plans to raise £190m through a share issue in the first half of next year, in order to reduce its debt. Its biggest shareholder, the activist investor Oasis Management Company, supports the move.

Sir Geoffrey Clifton-Brown, who chairs the Public Accounts Committee of MPs, said the mishandling of the scheme had caused severe hardship, particularly through long delays to bereavement payments. He said the committee would keep pursuing the matter and hoped Pablo Andres would be able to bring the situation under control.
