Thousands of people were evacuated from Manchester's Co-op Live arena on Sunday night after an "unknown substance" was released into the crowd during a concert by the US rapper Don Toliver, police said.

The incident happened towards the end of the show, at about 23.00 CET (22.00 GMT). Greater Manchester Police (GMP) declared a major incident after reports that people were suffering the effects of the substance, which officers initially believed to be tear gas. Footage shared on social media showed emergency services converging on the venue and some concertgoers coughing as they streamed out.

The North West Ambulance Service said more than 50 people were assessed at the scene, some of them for "eye and respiratory symptoms". Decontamination was carried out as a precaution, and several emergency crews, including a hazardous area response team, were deployed. One patient was taken to Manchester Royal Infirmary for further assessment.

In a statement early on Monday, GMP said more than 12,000 people had been safely evacuated and that several had been assessed for side-effects. Its assistant chief constable, Steph Parker, said: "Our priority was to ensure the safety of every person that was attending the concert." She said the major incident declaration had allowed emergency services and partner agencies to coordinate their response. Police said anyone who had left the venue and then become unwell should go to their local hospital.

Parker added that officers would now work to establish what the substance was and how the incident had come about. Co-op Live said in a statement that it was working closely with police to identify the cause and would provide more information once it had an update from GMP. The venue, which describes itself as the country's largest live entertainment arena, said its priority remained the safety of all visitors.

Toliver, whose real name is Caleb Zackery Toliver, was performing in Manchester as part of his Nitrous tour. He is scheduled to play London's O2 Arena on Monday and OVO Arena Wembley on Tuesday. The 32-year-old has received five nominations for the 2027 Grammy Awards, including Best New Artist.
