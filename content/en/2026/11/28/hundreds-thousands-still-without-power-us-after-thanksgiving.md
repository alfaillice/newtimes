About 264,000 households in the United States were still without electricity on Friday evening, a day after a powerful storm brought high winds, heavy rain and coastal flooding to the eastern part of the country over the Thanksgiving holiday. Crews were working to restore power as conditions began to improve, although forecasters warned that the recovery would take some time.

According to the monitoring site Poweroutage.us, the number of outages peaked at more than 900,000 on Thursday. By Friday evening the total had fallen to 264,000. Another count put the figure at just over 260,000 households. Pennsylvania was the state worst hit early on Friday, and Pennsylvania, Virginia, Maryland and New Jersey were all badly affected. Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro said utility companies were working hard to restore power.

In Maryland, authorities asked almost two million customers to limit their water use to essential needs because of intermittent power disruptions. The order was lifted on Friday morning. Elsewhere, the storm downed trees and power lines and led to water rescues in several states, from New York to Virginia. Parts of some states, including New York, declared states of emergency overnight. Bridges in Maryland were closed because of strong winds, and waves washed over the boardwalk in Delaware.

Rain was heavy in the capital and in New York. Nearly 5cm fell on Central Park, which preliminary National Weather Service data showed as the wettest Thanksgiving on record there. Washington also recorded its wettest Thanksgiving on record, and tidal water flooded runways at Reagan Washington National Airport.

Travel was also badly disrupted. FlightAware counted about 1,600 delays and more than 500 cancellations on Friday. A separate tally of flights within, into or out of the US put the figures at more than 3,400 delays and nearly 670 cancellations. US President Donald Trump said he would consider releasing emergency funds.

In New York, organisers of the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade moved the event to Saturday, the first postponement in its 100-year history. The organisers said the same balloons and floats would take part, along with most of the original celebrities.

The National Weather Service warned that "this huge system will take some time to weaken", and said showers and isolated freezing precipitation would linger through Saturday. Coastal flood warnings remained in place from New Jersey to Virginia. The Met Office said the north-eastern US should have a drier weekend, but another deep low-pressure system was expected on Monday, bringing more heavy rain, strong winds and possible travel disruption.

On the West Coast, unusually high tides caused concern. Both San Diego and Los Angeles recorded tides above 2.4m earlier in the week. Amtrak cancelled several trains on its Pacific Surfliner route along the California coast because of "washout conditions from high tides". Record tides prompted evacuations from Humboldt County in the far north to Ventura County near Los Angeles. In Oregon, footage showed a man and a dog being swept into the sea by a large king tide. Police said both escaped unharmed.
