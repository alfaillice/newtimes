Carlos Alcaraz beat Arthur Fery 6-2 6-1 in Bologna, giving Spain a 2-0 win over Great Britain in their Davis Cup semi-final and sending Spain into the final against Italy. The defeat ends Great Britain’s run in the competition.

Great Britain were playing their first Davis Cup semi-final since 2019 and hoped to give captain Leon Smith a fitting farewell. Smith, who has led the team for almost 17 years and guided them to the title in 2015, is stepping down after this year’s tournament.

The tie was settled in the first singles match, when Rafael Jodar, the world number 10, beat Cameron Norrie 6-2 7-5 to put Spain ahead. Jodar, who started 2026 outside the top 150, has made his Grand Slam debut and won his first ATP title in the past year, rising rapidly up the rankings. He has now won all three of his meetings with Norrie.

Fery then faced Alcaraz, the world number three, whom he had never played before. Alcaraz dropped just two points on his first serve in the opening set and kept the pressure on throughout. He had lifted his first ATP Finals title six days earlier, a result that marked his return after four months out with a wrist injury.

“It’s something I have been chasing for a really long time, to be in a Davis Cup final,” Alcaraz said.

Spain will meet Italy in Sunday’s final. Italy, the three-time defending champions, beat Spain in last year’s final, although they will be without world number two Jannik Sinner, who is out with a wrist injury. Italy reached the final with a 2-1 win over the Czech Republic on Friday, in which Lorenzo Musetti featured in both his singles and doubles matches.

Spain’s captain, David Ferrer, is also stepping down after this year’s event. He will have the chance to guide Spain to their first Davis Cup title since 2019. Speaking in Spanish, Alcaraz said the final would be “a spectacular day”, adding that he expected to enjoy it both on court and from the bench, watching his team-mates.
