At least eight people were found dead and six others were seriously injured after a migrant boat, known in Spain as a cayuco, was rescued in Atlantic waters off El Hierro, one of the Canary Islands, a Spanish region off the coast of northwest Africa.

Spain's maritime rescue service, Salvamento Marítimo, said on Friday that between eight and 10 bodies were found on board. The vessel was located at around 06.24 CET after a sailing boat came across it adrift, roughly 39 nautical miles (72km) south of the island. The rescue was complicated by waves of up to two metres. Six people in serious condition, five men and one woman, were airlifted by helicopter to El Hierro's airport for urgent medical care. A Civil Guard officer who fell during the operation was also evacuated.

The boat, which had about 100 people on board according to one report, was being towed by the Civil Guard patrol boat Río Tajo towards one of the island's two ports, with arrival expected late on Friday. One person who was still alive chose to remain on board during the tow.

Separately, 84 people, including three women and one minor, were landed at La Restinga, the island's main port, which has a reception centre and a field hospital for migrants. The rescue service said its Salvamar Navia had picked them up. It was not clear from the initial reports whether these people came from the same boat as the bodies; one report described them as coming from a separate vessel.

The scale of the operation has affected rescue workers. Salvamento Marítimo has set up specialised psychological support for its staff, with a psychologist due on the island over the next 24 to 48 hours. Minister Ángel Víctor Torres said rescuers had seen macabre images.

Fernando Clavijo, the Canary Islands' regional leader, wrote on X: "The humanitarian emergency doesn't start on El Hierro, but on the shores of Senegal and Gambia, where we have to act." He said he felt great pain and a sense of powerlessness over the deaths, and called on the Spanish government and the European Commission to act.

Arrivals in the Canary Islands have picked up sharply since October. A record 2,100 people reached the islands in a single day earlier this week, most of them on El Hierro, which has about 11,400 residents. Local authorities declared an emergency earlier this month, and rescuers brought in nearly 1,500 people in one day on 14 November.

The route is one of the deadliest in the world. Arrivals reached a record of more than 46,000 in 2024 before falling, after tighter surveillance and cooperation with countries of origin and transit. Patrols off Senegal, Mauritania and Morocco have pushed departures further south, to The Gambia, Guinea-Bissau and Guinea, which makes the crossing longer and more dangerous. Crossings usually increase between September and November, when sea conditions are calmer.

The deaths came two days before Spain's general election on Sunday, in which immigration has become a central issue. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez is one of Europe's most vocal supporters of the economic benefits of immigration. The right, which leads in the opinion polls, is calling for much tighter controls.
