The ChatGPT maker OpenAI has filed a countersuit against Apple, accusing the iPhone maker of stealing its trade secrets. The filing was made in federal court in northern California on Friday, and it answers a lawsuit Apple brought in July against OpenAI and two former Apple employees.

Apple's original case alleges a "pattern of theft" of proprietary information. Two of the defendants are Tang Tan and Chang Liu. Tan spent 24 years at Apple, where he was vice-president of product design for the iPhone and Apple Watch, and he was part of the group that launched io Products, the design start-up founded by Sir Jony Ive, the former Apple executive who designed the iMac, iPod, iPhone and iPad. OpenAI bought io Products in 2025 for $6.5bn (£4.7bn). Apple also says at least two long-serving employees who left for OpenAI emailed themselves internal Apple information. OpenAI and the employees deny the allegations. They say Apple's case depended on monitoring Liu's personal messages after he left the company.

In its countersuit, OpenAI says Apple gained confidential information by rehiring a former OpenAI employee who had worked there for six months. According to the filing, that employee gained access to details of a new device and to OpenAI's roadmap for future hardware products, and sent "dozens of OpenAI documents" to his personal phone numbers. He then presented OpenAI's plans to senior Apple staff, the filing says, at Apple's request. OpenAI argues: "Given the timing, it now appears that stealing those documents may have been his reason for joining OpenAI in the first place."

The company also accuses Apple of pressuring hardware suppliers not to work on future OpenAI devices. It says OpenAI is developing AI-enabled personal devices that could "outcompete Apple on what Apple views as its own turf". The suppliers are not named in the filing.

OpenAI further criticises Apple's employment practices. It says Apple encourages staff to mix work files with personal iCloud accounts, and continues to access former employees' personal devices and accounts after they leave. In OpenAI's view, this allows Apple to "monitor and ensnare departing employees" when they join competitors. OpenAI also says about 400 employees have left Apple for OpenAI, and claims Apple wants to deter others from leaving.

Apple denied using the rehired employee to get OpenAI's confidential information, and denied trying to suppress competition in AI device manufacturing. In a statement on Friday, it described OpenAI's counterclaims as "a transparent attempt to distract from the substance" of its own lawsuit.

The two companies have worked together since ChatGPT was integrated into Apple products in 2024. They are now becoming rivals, as OpenAI develops its first connected device, designed with Jony Ive. The competition for AI talent and proprietary technology has intensified their dispute.

The claims are contained in court filings and have not been tested in court. OpenAI and the lawyers for Tan and Liu did not immediately respond to requests for comment on the countersuit.
