Two workers for Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF), the medical charity also known as Doctors Without Borders, were killed in an Israeli strike on one of the organisation’s clinics in Gaza City, the charity said on Saturday.

MSF said the strike was a direct hit on the clinic. It said the site was clearly marked with its logos and that its location was known to the Israeli military. The organisation said the two men killed were watchmen, or security guards, and that their deaths brought to 17 the number of MSF staff killed in Gaza since the war began. It said it was “devastated and outraged”.

The Israel Defense Forces (IDF), the Israeli military, said in a statement that the two men were Hamas operatives acting as security guards at the clinic. It said they belonged to the military wing of Hamas, the armed Palestinian group that governs Gaza, and that they had advanced plans to attack Israeli civilians and soldiers. The IDF said the strike was “not directed at a hospital” and caused no damage to medical infrastructure. It also said that precautionary measures had been taken to minimise harm to civilians, and described the case as another example of Hamas embedding itself within international organisations. The IDF named the two men, but the spellings differ between accounts of its statement, so this article does not publish their names.

Witnesses said the two men were sitting at the clinic entrance when an Israeli drone descended and fired a missile. A close friend of one of the men said he had seen him hours before the attack and that he had no link to any armed group. Video from the scene showed a small crater beside a plastic chair and bloodstains in a courtyard behind the clinic.

The head of the World Health Organization (WHO), Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, called attacks on health facilities and health workers in Gaza “outrageous and unacceptable”. In a post on X, he wrote: “Those risking their lives to save others must be protected, not attacked.”

The deaths come amid a rise in Israeli strikes across Gaza over the past week. Gaza’s civil defence spokesman said eight Palestinians were killed in Israeli attacks across the territory on Saturday, including the two MSF workers.

A ceasefire agreed by Israel and Hamas in October 2025, brokered by the United States, has not stopped violence. Israeli strikes take place almost daily in areas under Hamas control, which are home to 2.1 million people, most of them displaced. The Hamas-run health ministry says at least 1,705 people have been killed in Gaza over the past 13 months, a figure the UN considers reliable. The ministry says more than 74,770 people have been killed since the war began with the Hamas-led attack on southern Israel on 7 October 2023, in which about 1,200 people were killed and 251 taken hostage.

The humanitarian situation is also severe. Heavy rain earlier in November caused flooding and damaged tens of thousands of shelters for displaced families. The UN and its partners have asked Israel, which controls Gaza’s crossings, to allow in more supplies, including tents, heaters, water pumps, vaccines and medicines, ahead of winter. Israel denies accusations that it has committed genocide in Gaza, a finding made by a UN independent commission of inquiry.
