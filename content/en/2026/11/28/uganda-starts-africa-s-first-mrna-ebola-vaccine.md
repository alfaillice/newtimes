Uganda has begun a clinical trial of an experimental mRNA vaccine against Ebola, which the Uganda Virus Research Institute (UVRI) describes as the first of its kind in Africa. The state-run institute announced the start of the study on Friday, 27 November. It is being run in collaboration with Moderna, the United States company that manufactures the vaccine.

The vaccine is designed against the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola, a less common form of the virus. This strain is behind the outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), Uganda's neighbour. No vaccine or treatment is currently approved for Bundibugyo, according to reporting that cites the World Health Organization (WHO).

The UVRI said the trial will involve 224 volunteers and will test the vaccine's safety and its ability to trigger an immune response. For the first time, children aged one and above will be included, alongside adults up to 65. Moderna began its first human trial of the vaccine in Canada in August, in adults aged 18 to 65, so the Ugandan study is the second.

Another candidate is also moving forward. The ChAdOx1 vaccine, developed by India's Serum Institute and the University of Oxford, will begin testing in the DRC on Monday, 30 November. Trials of both vaccines are due to start in eastern DRC, the epicentre of the outbreak, and will target people who have been in contact with infected patients as well as those considered at high risk of infection.

Gavi, the Vaccine Alliance, is preparing for both outcomes. The body, which brings together governments and private donors to help poorer countries buy vaccines at affordable prices, signed a purchase commitment with the Serum Institute last week for 1.5 million doses of ChAdOx1. It plans to have “hundreds of thousands” of doses ready to deploy if the trials succeed and are approved by the WHO and the DRC government. Emanuele Capobianco, Gavi's director of global health security, said he hoped the trials would conclude in early 2027 and that the purchase deal could bring deployment forward by months.

The outbreak has been severe. DRC government data published on Thursday, 26 November, showed at least 12,021 confirmed infections and 5,904 deaths since the outbreak began in mid-May. Uganda, which also recorded cases earlier in the year, has since declared its own outbreak over. The Ministry of Health reported 20 infections and two deaths in Uganda.
