Jared Kushner, the son-in-law of US President Donald Trump and one of his negotiators on Ukraine, has faced sharp criticism in Kyiv and among Ukraine's European partners after remarks he made in an interview released on Wednesday. Kushner, who has also worked on talks concerning Gaza and Iran, spoke with Mathias Döpfner, chief executive of the German media group Axel Springer, for Döpfner's podcast "MD Meets".

In the conversation, Kushner called the Russian president, Vladimir Putin, a "grandmaster of politics" and said Putin had shown restraint by not using nuclear weapons. He also described the war as, in effect, a proxy conflict in which Europe funds and arms Ukraine against Russia. Kushner said the territorial question was "a little bit of a deadlock" and suggested that a new European security framework could eventually settle the future of Donbas. He proposed reviving the Russia-NATO council and said Ukraine could join the European Union with security guarantees similar to NATO's Article 5, though he gave no detail on them. Neither Moscow nor Kyiv has accepted that plan. Kushner also denied that Trump had pressured Volodymyr Zelenskyy. Döpfner pointed to a recent case in which Trump suggested Ukraine should have a new leader.

The strongest reaction came from Ukraine. Oleksandr Merezhko, chair of the foreign affairs committee of Ukraine's parliament and a member of Zelenskyy's Servant of the People party, said the remarks were "extremely dangerous" for Ukraine and for US security. He called for Kushner and Steve Witkoff, Trump's other peace envoy, to be replaced. Another Servant of the People lawmaker, Halyna Yanchenko, said Kushner's description of the war was a misrepresentation that denied Ukraine's standing as an independent actor, and that he had in effect acted as an advocate for Russia.

Criticism also came from Ukraine's European backers. Roderich Kiesewetter, a veteran German lawmaker from the Christian Democratic Union (CDU) of Chancellor Friedrich Merz, said Washington was not acting as an honest broker and was putting one-sided pressure on Kyiv. Marie-Agnes Strack-Zimmermann, a German MEP who chairs the European Parliament's security and defence committee, called the proxy war description "pure Kremlin propaganda". She said Kushner could hardly be seen as a neutral mediator.

The White House did not respond to requests for comment on the criticism. Poland, Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania, which are among the most hawkish European states on Russia, had not replied to requests for comment at the time of publication.

The reactions come at a delicate moment. Zelenskyy is still seeking deliveries of US-made Patriot interceptor missiles, and US-brokered peace talks in Abu Dhabi earlier this month produced no breakthrough. Yanchenko said that talk of warmer relations with Moscow was a story Putin was trying to make the American negotiating team believe.
