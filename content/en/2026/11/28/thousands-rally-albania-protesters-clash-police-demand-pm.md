Thousands of people gathered in the Albanian capital, Tirana, on Saturday, on the country’s Independence Day, to demand the resignation of Prime Minister Edi Rama. Clashes broke out between protesters and police. The health ministry said on Saturday that eight people had been injured, including a police officer and two journalists.

The protest was called by students and opposition figures, and it is the latest in a long series of demonstrations against Rama’s government. The movement has no leaders and is known as the Flamingo Revolution. It began in May with peaceful rallies against a luxury hotel project in a coastal nature reserve. The project was linked to an investment firm founded by Jared Kushner, the son-in-law of United States President Donald Trump. Protesters accused the government of corruption. In October the protests widened to include criticism of poor healthcare and the lack of opportunities for young people.

November 28 is usually a public holiday marked by national celebrations. This year it marks the 114th anniversary of the country’s declaration of independence. Albania is a Balkan nation that aspires to join the European Union. Police set up checks on vehicles on the main roads leading into the capital.

According to the accounts of the event, the day turned violent in the centre of Tirana. Police used water cannon and tear gas against crowds gathered in Skanderbeg Square, the city’s central square. Some protesters threw stones and rocks, and tried to smash street cameras and the windows of government buildings. Video footage showed thick smoke over the main boulevard as protesters and police exchanged objects. Some protesters chanted “Resignation!”

In late October the government gave police the power to temporarily restrict gatherings near public institutions and to impose fines of up to 500,000 leks (€5,573) on people caught looting or concealing their identity during protests. Since then, protests have drawn smaller crowds, although Saturday’s rally was large.

The European Union has called for an investigation into the use of excessive force by police and urged all sides to de-escalate tensions. Further protests have not yet been announced in the sources reviewed.
