Norse Atlantic Airways, a Norwegian low-cost long-haul airline, has filed for bankruptcy for its main operating unit and cancelled all of its flights. The company said on Friday that it had failed to secure the short-term financing it needed to keep flying, and it blamed a sharp rise in jet fuel prices that it linked to the war in Iran.

The board of Norse Atlantic Airways AS, the Norwegian subsidiary that runs the operation, filed the petition on Friday with the Agder District Court. It concluded that there was no longer a basis for continued operations. Chief executive Eivind Roald said the board had taken “the difficult decision” to file, despite extensive efforts across the group to find a solution.

The collapse came quickly. The airline had described its financial situation as “critical” on Wednesday, suspended all flights the following day and said it would file for bankruptcy unless emergency funding could be arranged. The leasing companies that own its aircraft had also terminated their contracts for the whole fleet and demanded the planes be handed back. Norse initially said it would contest that move, which would have left it unable to operate.

Only the Norwegian operating unit is in bankruptcy. The listed parent company, Norse Atlantic ASA, and the UK subsidiary are not part of the filing. Their boards and advisers are still working to find a solution for the rest of the business.

Fuel is at the centre of the crisis. The US-Israel war on Iran began in late February and disrupted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, the waterway that carries much of the Gulf’s oil and refined fuel exports. Global jet fuel prices more than doubled at their peak after the war began. They dipped in mid-summer, when hopes of a resolution to the conflict rose, and then climbed again as those hopes faded.

Aviation analyst John Strickland said in an interview that Norse had not hedged its fuel purchases and had no protection against rising prices. As a result, the airline was fully exposed to spot prices on routes that burn large amounts of fuel. Many airlines hedge their fuel and stockpile supplies to guard against exactly this kind of shock. Strickland also said that passengers were hesitant to book in the current climate and that Norse faced stiff competition on transatlantic routes.

Norse had already been shrinking for months before Friday’s announcement. Several other carriers have collapsed, entered restructuring or cut routes since the war began. Industry coverage describes the current situation as the worst crisis for aviation since the Covid-19 pandemic, and small and low-cost airlines are struggling in particular.
