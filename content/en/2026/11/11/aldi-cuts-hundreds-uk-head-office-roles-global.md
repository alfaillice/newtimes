Aldi is cutting hundreds of head office jobs in the UK as part of a wider effort by the German-owned budget grocer to reduce costs across its international business, according to The Guardian.

The newspaper reports that the retailer is consulting its technology team about the possible loss of up to 150 roles. This follows 103 redundancies in the finance and administration team earlier this year and almost 20 more among buying directors. According to The Guardian, jobs in stores have not been affected. Aldi has, however, slightly reduced the number of regional managers, a change that affects a handful of well-paid posts.

The company is moving work away from its local country operations and into central functions. The Guardian says Aldi is outsourcing finance, procurement and administration roles to India, among other countries, under an agreement with Genpact, a firm that helps businesses use artificial intelligence. Sources told the paper that consultation on redundancies in payroll is due to begin in the coming months. The paper also reports that Aldi has recently reduced cleaning hours for agency workers at its head office in Atherstone, Warwickshire.

One affected worker told The Guardian that the cuts were "shortsighted" and showed that upper management were "woefully out of touch with the reality".

An Aldi spokesperson said the company was "making some adjustments to the way parts of our UK business are structured". The spokesperson added that changes were being introduced gradually after a consultation period and that colleagues were being given as much notice as possible. Aldi said it had invested £300m this year in lowering prices on 900 products in the UK.

The job cuts come as Aldi's growth slows. Citing figures from Worldpanel by Numerator, The Guardian reports that UK sales at the chain rose only 0.4 per cent in the 12 weeks to 1 November, and that its market share fell back by 0.1 percentage points year on year. The average unit price at Aldi fell by 0.1 per cent over the period, making it the only retailer reducing overall prices.

Aldi faces tougher competition from the traditional supermarkets, several of which now match its prices on key items while offering much wider ranges. Other grocers have also cut head office roles this year. The Guardian reports that Morrisons cut hundreds of such jobs this spring, Sainsbury's eliminated 300 as it restructured its technology team and Argos delivery network, and Tesco laid off a similar number as it changed how it runs its bakeries. In the summer, Asda said up to 1,000 jobs were at risk as it introduced more automation into its logistics operation.
