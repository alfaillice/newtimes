Indonesia's Vice-President Gibran Rakabuming Raka has submitted his resignation to President Prabowo Subianto, according to the BBC, which cited an aide, Budi Arie. The BBC reported that the letter was handed to Prabowo directly on Wednesday and that it remains unclear whether the president has accepted it. The move could further strain the political alliance that brought Prabowo to power in the 2024 election.

Gibran's candidacy has been dogged by questions about his school qualifications. The BBC reported that he had studied in Singapore and Australia and had used an Indonesian equivalence certificate to demonstrate his educational credentials. Indonesian law requires vice-presidents to have completed senior secondary school or an equivalent qualification. According to the BBC, the Supreme Court found no evidence that Gibran held a foreign diploma or certificate that could serve as an equivalent, and it also cited an earlier Constitutional Court ruling from October that reached the same conclusion. Gibran had argued that university study was sufficient proof that he had completed senior high school. On Tuesday, State Secretary Prasetyo Hadi said Gibran had asked for the chance to challenge the ruling and prove the legitimacy of his credentials, the BBC reported.

The BBC also noted that Gibran was 36 at the time of the election and became eligible to stand only after a controversial Constitutional Court ruling exempted him from the minimum age requirement of 40. The Supreme Court decision prompted more than 100 lawmakers to back a parliamentary initiative that could have opened the way to impeachment proceedings against him, the broadcaster said.

Gibran's place on the ticket was part of an alliance between his father, outgoing president Joko Widodo, known as Jokowi, and Prabowo, once his bitter rival. The BBC says tensions between the two men now appear to have flared. Since taking office, Prabowo has shown little interest in Jokowi's legacy project, the construction of Nusantara, a new capital. He has instead focused much of the state's resources on his own flagship programme of free school meals. In a series of reshuffles over the past year, he has also moved many Jokowi loyalists into new roles, including long-serving finance minister Sri Mulyani Indrawati.

Other signs of strain have emerged in recent days, the BBC reported. Last week, authorities arrested presidential chief of staff Listyo Sigit Prabowo, who has no relation to the president, on suspicion of wiretapping him. Prabowo dismissed Listyo a day later. Listyo, a former national police chief, is widely considered a long-time ally of Jokowi. On Monday, three members of Prabowo's cabinet resigned collectively from the party led by Kaesang Pangarep, Jokowi's youngest son, saying they wanted to focus on their duties in the administration.

The government is also facing wider pressures, according to the BBC. The free school meals programme is struggling for funding, parts of Indonesia have experienced the country's worst wildfires in a decade, and the national stock market faces a crisis over allegations of widespread market manipulation. On Tuesday, students across the country held demonstrations against the government's handling of several issues, including the environmental crisis and corruption.
