Portugal cancelled more than 300 flights and closed schools, businesses and many public services on Wednesday as Storm Charles approached the mainland, according to Al Jazeera. The measures were a precaution against an Atlantic storm that the government had warned could be serious.

According to Al Jazeera, operations stopped at four international airports, and hundreds of supermarkets and all non-essential public services closed across the country. The broadcaster reports that about 8,000 schools cancelled classes, which it describes as the first time in living memory that this has happened on such a scale. Its report also says that all schools nationwide shut down. It does not reconcile these two descriptions of the school closures, so the number of schools affected remains unclear from that account.

The storm had already struck Madeira, the Portuguese island off northwest Africa, on Tuesday with winds of 130km/h (81mph), Al Jazeera reports. On the mainland, the national weather agency, IPMA, recorded gusts of more than 120km/h in some areas. Al Jazeera describes largely empty streets in Lisbon, where buses ran without passengers and some roads flooded because drains were blocked. A national supermarket chain closed its stores, and the Web Summit technology conference in Lisbon, which about 70,000 people attend, cancelled the day's events.

The red alert, the highest level of warning, initially covered more than half of Portugal. By mid-afternoon, only the southern Algarve region, a popular European tourist destination, remained under it. Al Jazeera says the heavy rain and strong winds passed without causing severe damage.

## Criticism after earlier storms

The government's response reflects criticism it received earlier in the year. Al Jazeera reports that in January, Portugal's centre-right government faced harsh criticism over its weak response to Storm Kristin, which felled thousands of trees in central Portugal and caused widespread property damage that is still being repaired. In February, the broadcaster says, a 70-year-old man died in the southern region of Alentejo after floodwater swept his vehicle off a street during Storm Leonardo.

Prime Minister Luis Montenegro chaired a crisis unit with eight government ministers and other officials to monitor Storm Charles. Interior Minister Luis Neves, who oversees public safety, rejected suggestions that the authorities had overreacted. Al Jazeera reports that he said the government had organised its response according to the weather forecast.

## Spain

Al Jazeera reports that the storm was forecast to move later into Spain, where workers held a general strike on Wednesday. Strike organisers cancelled or postponed demonstrations in some southern cities, including Córdoba, Huelva and Cádiz, the broadcaster says. The report does not say whether the weather affected the strike itself.

All the details in this story come from a single outlet, Al Jazeera, and have not been independently confirmed by an agency or official source in our list. Further figures, including damage assessments and the final number of flight cancellations, were not included in the report.
