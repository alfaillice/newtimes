The lineup for the 2027 Coachella festival has been revealed after months of speculation, with Dua Lipa, SZA and Tame Impala set to headline the event in California, according to The Guardian.

The festival will take place at the Empire Polo Club in Indio on 9-11 April and 16-18 April. The Guardian reports that Madonna will headline the dance tent, with Zara Larsson, Robyn, Phoebe Bridgers, Weezer and Daddy Yankee also due to perform.

Rumours about the bill had circulated for weeks. The paper says SZA appeared to confirm her booking earlier in the week through an Instagram story, which also referred to a new album and tour next year. Tame Impala return to the desert site after headlining in 2019. SZA performed as a guest during Justin Bieber's headline set earlier this year.

According to The Guardian, Dua Lipa's slot would be the first time a British female solo artist has headlined the festival. She had previously made a guest appearance during Martin Garrix's set in 2017.

The paper also lists global acts including Adéla, Becky G, Jisoo, Drain Gang, NCT and Måneskin. British performers on the bill include Jorja Smith, EsDeeKid, Underworld, Shygirl and Lambrini Girls. Breakout names highlighted by the paper are the young pop artist Underscores, the rapper Nettspend, the showgaze duo After and the hyperpop artist Tiffany Day.

Madonna's slot marks 21 years since she first headlined the dance tent, around the release of her 2005 album *Confessions on a Dance Floor*. The Guardian notes that she released a sequel, *Confessions II*, earlier this year, and that it reached number one on the US and UK charts. In August, Emily Eavis denied rumours that Madonna would play Glastonbury next year, the paper reports.

This year's Coachella was headlined by Bieber, Karol G and Sabrina Carpenter. The Guardian's Adrian Horton praised Carpenter's performance.

Passes for the 2027 festival go on sale on 13 November at 12pm Pacific time. According to The Guardian, YouTube will return as the livestream partner for the fifteenth year, covering both weekends.

The details in this story come from a single source, The Guardian, and have not been independently confirmed by a second outlet or by the festival's organisers in the material available.
