Tom O'Brien, the Irish jockey who won the Welsh Grand National twice, has died at the age of 39 after being diagnosed with cancer, BBC Sport reported.

According to the broadcaster, O'Brien rode more than 1,000 winners in Britain and Ireland, including two at the Cheltenham Festival. Riding Thyme Hill for trainer Philip Hobbs, he won the Liverpool Hurdle in 2021 and the Kauto Star Novices' Chase in 2022.

BBC Sport also noted that O'Brien was the nephew of the trainer Aidan O'Brien. He joined Hobbs's Minehead operation as a teenager, having come over from Ireland without having ridden in a race, and stayed there until he retired in 2023. Hobbs, who now shares the yard with Johnson White, said the young rider had quickly shown that he was talented.

Hobbs described his former jockey as "very reliable and a very good bloke". He said the two had stayed in touch since O'Brien's retirement, as the jockey lived about half an hour away, and that O'Brien had been at the yard only about three weeks earlier.

Hobbs also said the death was very sad, particularly for O'Brien's wife, Hayley, and their two young children.

The BBC Sport report did not say where or when O'Brien died, or what type of cancer he had. The account is based on that single outlet's reporting.
