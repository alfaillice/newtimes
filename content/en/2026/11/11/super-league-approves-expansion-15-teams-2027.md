Super League has agreed to expand from 14 clubs to 15 for the 2027 season, according to BBC Sport. The league said its shareholders had approved the change. If it goes ahead, 2027 would be the first season in which the competition, Europe's top flight of rugby league, has operated with 15 clubs.

The league grew from 12 to 14 teams for 2026, when Bradford Bulls, York Knights and Toulouse Olympique joined. Salford Red Devils dropped out after what BBC Sport describes as a financially turbulent 2025 campaign. The top flight ran with 12 clubs for much of its existence, with spells at 14 teams in 2000, from 2009 to 2014 and last season.

In a statement, the league said the shareholders' decision had been passed to the Rugby Football League board. The board will now set out criteria and assemble a panel to judge applicants against them, BBC Sport reports.

The change could open a route back for London Broncos. The club won the Championship Grand Final and the 1895 Cup last season, but was ranked 16th on the yearly grading list, below fellow Championship side Barrow Raiders, despite setting the pace in the second tier. It was passed over 12 months ago, when Bradford, York and Toulouse were admitted. The club now has the backing of Australian businessman Grant Wechsel and former Brisbane Broncos star Darren Lockyer.

BBC Sport understands that any completed investment deal with the National Rugby League, the Australian governing body, would require the Broncos to feature in the top flight.

Several matters remain open. Super League has yet to agree a new title sponsorship deal, finalise its broadcast negotiations or set its fixtures for the 2027 campaign, BBC Sport reports. The panel's criteria and the identity of any new club have not yet been announced.
