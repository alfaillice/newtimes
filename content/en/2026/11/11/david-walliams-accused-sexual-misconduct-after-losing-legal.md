On Tuesday, the BBC reported that ITV News and the Guardian had published allegations that the comedian and author David Walliams has been accused of sexual misconduct by several women. According to the BBC, one of the women was 15 at the time of the alleged contact. The allegations were published as part of a joint three-year investigation by the two news organisations, which came out after Walliams lost a legal bid to stop the claims being made public.

Walliams rose to fame in the early 2000s with Matt Lucas in *Little Britain*, the BBC sketch comedy series. He also served as a judge on ITV's *Britain's Got Talent* and published a bestselling series of children's books. The BBC says that some of the women accuse him of grooming them when they were teenagers, and one now describes her alleged encounter with him at 15 as a sexual assault.

According to the BBC, the women, who do not know one another, allege they were targets of unwanted sexual advances. One says she met Walliams at a fan event when she was 15 and that they had sex when she was 17. Another, who was a model in her teens, says he groomed her. A third, who was 19 and working as a model, says she rejected him when he tried to kiss her during a tour of his home. The investigation also claimed that Walliams had a sexual relationship with at least one junior employee at the publisher HarperCollins and pursued another. Other women allege inappropriate behaviour at *Britain's Got Talent*, including being invited to his dressing room.

The BBC reports that Lilly Creightmore, who waived her right to anonymity, was 18 when Walliams contacted her. She told BBC Newsnight that a month after they met he suggested she move in with him as his flatmate, and that she felt pressured to share a bed with him once she arrived. She said she was angry and that "this culture of older men in positions of power needs to go".

In a letter to journalists published by the Guardian, Walliams said he was "not a sexual predator" and had not broken the law. He said he regretted not always taking people's feelings properly into account and that he had casual sexual relationships with people who now feel hurt. His lawyers told the investigation that he denied grooming Creightmore or behaving inappropriately towards her.

The women named in the investigation by the pseudonyms Alex and Jade have different accounts of their ages. The BBC reports that the investigation says Alex was 17 when they first had sex, after she had joked by email that she was "old enough to go drinking". Walliams told the publications he believed she was 18. He denied grooming her. Walliams strongly denied any sexual contact with Jade and said he has never groomed young women.

The consequences have been swift. The BBC says Walliams's long-time literary agent, Independent Talent Group, dropped him after the investigation was published. The BBC described the allegations as "serious" and has removed *Little Britain* from BBC iPlayer. Sky, which described the allegations as "extremely serious", said it was dropping its upcoming adaptation of Walliams's fantasy film *Fing!* and had removed content featuring him from its platforms. The Metropolitan Police said it had contacted the Guardian and ITV News to make sure any potential victims knew how to report criminal allegations to the police.

The BBC noted that help and support is available through its Action Line service for anyone affected by the issues raised in the story.
