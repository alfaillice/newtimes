Poundland and its 11,000 employees are hoping that the discount chain's collapse can be delayed long enough for a last-minute rescue deal, the BBC reports. The retailer has been moving towards administration, the UK insolvency process in which an outside administrator takes control of a company's affairs, after its owners blocked it from accessing cash in its bank accounts.

According to the BBC, a deadline to secure a buyer for the business expires at midnight. Sources close to the situation told the broadcaster that they expect more time to be given once £10m of funding is released. The BBC's sources described that sum as "small in the scale of things", but said it could at least postpone the chain's collapse while a longer-term solution is explored.

The BBC understands that about £50m had been frozen by Gordon Brothers, the US investment firm that owns Poundland. The BBC says the freeze left managers unable to restock shelves across the chain's 600 shops ahead of the crucial Christmas trading period. Gordon Brothers bought Poundland for just £1 last year. A committee of MPs, the members of the House of Commons who scrutinise government and public bodies, is due to question the firm on Wednesday. MPs' concerns follow accusations that the firm wants to profit by selling the stores and stock piecemeal. The BBC reports that a takeover bid was rejected, prompting calls for answers from politicians, including Prime Minister Andy Burnham.

The two sides present very different pictures of the business. Poundland's boss, Barry Williams, told staff last week that like-for-like sales, which exclude new store openings, rose 11.4%. He said the period, which included Halloween, was £1.5m ahead of budget, and that the business made a profit of £18.1m in the year to September, a "strong recovery" from losses of £68m the year before. Gordon Brothers, by contrast, said the chain remains "challenged and vulnerable to any potential downturn in future trading".

The 10-day moratorium that protects Poundland from its creditors expires on Thursday. The BBC reports that if there is a prospect of a solvent deal, that deadline could be extended. Several retailers have shown interest in picking up stores, including Poundstretcher, Tesco and B&M, which could keep some staff employed if the chain is broken up and sold in parts.

The central question, the BBC notes, is whether a piecemeal sale would protect more jobs than a buyer willing to keep Poundland as a national chain. The broadcaster's report does not say which option is more likely, and the outcome of the MPs' questioning and the talks over funding remains open.
