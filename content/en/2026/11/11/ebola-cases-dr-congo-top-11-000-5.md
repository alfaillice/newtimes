Al Jazeera reports that the number of confirmed Ebola cases in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) has reached 11,042, according to government data, with at least 5,387 deaths. The figures were released on Tuesday, as health authorities in the Central African country struggle to contain what the network describes as the largest and deadliest Ebola outbreak in the DRC's history.

Al Jazeera says the outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo strain of the Ebola virus, which it describes as rare. The network reports that the outbreak is now the second-largest and second-deadliest Ebola outbreak on record, behind only the 2014–2016 epidemic in West Africa. That epidemic, according to Al Jazeera, infected more than 28,600 people and killed more than 11,000.

Eight of the DRC's 26 provinces have been affected so far. Al Jazeera reports that many deaths have occurred outside treatment centres. The network attributes the faster spread of the virus, compared with previous outbreaks, to delayed detection, conflict and strained health services.

Containment is especially difficult, according to the same report, because no vaccine or treatment has been developed specifically for the Bundibugyo strain, which kills about half of the people it infects. Al Jazeera says the World Health Organization (WHO) approved clinical trials of two new Ebola vaccines earlier this month and expects results in early 2027.

Cases have now been reported in East, Central and West Africa. Al Jazeera describes this as the widest geographic spread of any Ebola outbreak on the continent to date.

The case and death figures come from government data as reported by Al Jazeera. This story rests on that single outlet, and the other details, including the comparison with the West African epidemic and the vaccine timeline, are also attributed to it. We will update the piece if other sources confirm, revise or add to these figures.
