Consultant doctors in England have paused a planned two-day strike after the British Medical Association (BMA), the doctors' trade union, said formal negotiations with the government would begin. The walkout had been due on 25 and 26 November.

Consultants are the most senior doctors in hospitals. Under the planned action, they would have provided emergency care only. The Guardian reported that this would probably have caused delays to most routine and elective services. The BBC said the strike was due to begin at 06.59 GMT on 25 November.

The BMA announced the action after 18 months of talks with the government failed to resolve key issues. Consultants voted in July to take industrial action. In the ballot, 76 per cent of those who took part voted in favour, with just over half of eligible BMA members casting a vote. The government says this represents only around one in five consultants employed by the National Health Service (NHS) in England, as nearly half of them are not BMA members.

The dispute centres on pay and working conditions. The BMA says consultants' pay has fallen by a quarter in real terms over the past 17 years. The Guardian gives the period from 2009-10. The BBC notes that the BMA uses a measure of inflation that tends to be higher than the one favoured by economists. Consultants received a 3.5 per cent pay rise this year. Government sources told the BBC that a bigger increase this year was not on the table, citing the state of the public finances and current levels of pay.

The sources differ on the size of the union's working-time demand. The Guardian reports a requested 10 per cent cut in working hours, while the BBC says the BMA wants the standard working week reduced from 40 to 35 hours. The BBC also reports demands for higher pay for out-of-hours work and changes to pensions, including more flexibility over when they can be taken.

Health Secretary Yvette Cooper, the cabinet minister responsible for health, welcomed the decision. She called it "welcome news for patients and the wider NHS". She said she had held a constructive discussion with the BMA consultants committee on reforms that could give consultants a stronger voice and more freedom to innovate. She added that the pause would avoid disruption to care and let NHS staff prepare for a challenging winter.

The committee's co-chairs, Dr Helen Neary and Dr Shanu Datta, said they hoped the move would mark a turning point in the dispute. They said they had received assurances that there were no limits on what could be discussed in the talks. "We've always said strikes do not need to go ahead as long as the government is willing to genuinely negotiate, and now is their chance to do so," they said.

The BBC reports that the formal talks with the government's negotiating team could begin as soon as this week. Cooper said the government was committed to taking the reform work forward in the weeks ahead.
