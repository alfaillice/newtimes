A five-year project to restore one of Scotland's rarest habitats has been awarded almost £6m by the National Lottery Heritage Fund, the BBC reports. The fund, which channels money raised by National Lottery players into heritage projects, is backing Our Rainforest Futures, an initiative led by the Woodland Trust Scotland as part of the Alliance for Scotland's Rainforest.

The grant will pay for three hubs on the west coast, in Torridon, Morvern and mid-Argyll. Local people will be offered training and work in habitat restoration, deer management and the removal of invasive species. According to the BBC, the Torridon hub will involve communities in Kinlochewe, Shieldaig, Lochcarron, Applecross and Gairloch. The Morvern hub will cover Lochaline, Strontian and Tobermory on the Isle of Mull, and the mid-Argyll hub will involve Crinan, Tayvallich, Lochgilphead, Ardrishaig and Tarbert.

The woodland at the centre of the project covers about 75,000 acres (30,351 hectares) of rain-soaked land along the coast. It is classed as Atlantic rainforest because of its rare oceanic plant life. The BBC says the forest is home to hundreds of species, including glow worms, slime moulds and rare lichens. Overgrazing by deer and livestock, invasive plant species and disease are among the threats to the areas of woodland that survive.

Other partners include Argyll Countryside Trust, Community Woodlands Association, Plantlife, RSPB Scotland and SRUC. Caroline Clark, Scotland director of the National Lottery Heritage Fund, told the BBC that the rainforest is internationally significant but largely unknown. She said: "A 2025 survey found fewer than one in three people even know it exists." She added that the future of the rainforest and of many west coast communities are closely linked.

Julie Stoneman, manager of the Saving Scotland's Rainforest Programme at the charity Plantlife, said the project could create more than 20 jobs and 15 trainee posts. The BBC report does not give a start date for the work.
