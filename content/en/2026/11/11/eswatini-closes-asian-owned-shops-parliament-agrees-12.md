Businesses owned by Asian nationals are being closed across Eswatini as the government cracks down on foreign-owned enterprises, according to the BBC. The measures follow Parliament's approval of the Citizen Economic Empowerment Act, which aims to increase ownership and participation by emaSwati, the term used for citizens of the kingdom, a small landlocked country in southern Africa.

The BBC reports that the law responds to complaints from local traders, who say foreign-owned businesses are moving into traditional retail markets, increasing competition and threatening their livelihoods. The broadcaster notes that Eswatini faces serious economic hardship, with about 70 per cent of its population living below the poverty line. The government says the law is meant to widen economic opportunities for citizens, amid concern that poverty and hunger could fuel further political unrest.

The debate in Parliament has been heated, according to the BBC, with MPs divided over how quickly foreign business owners should leave. Welcome Dlamini, the MP for Mbabane East, told Parliament on Tuesday that officials from the trade ministry had already started enforcing the law by closing Asian-owned shops. Dlamini questioned whether local entrepreneurs were ready to take over these businesses and argued that owners should be given time to train their successors, proposing three years.

Tanele Magagula, the Hhohho Regional MP, rejected that proposal and called for immediate action against foreign-owned businesses in rural areas. "Why must we wait for three years while emaSwati suffer?" she asked, according to the BBC.

Parliament subsequently agreed that foreign business owners should be given at least 12 months to leave. The BBC points out, however, that unless the law is formally amended to reflect that timeline, the trade ministry could continue enforcing its provisions.

The closures follow inspections launched by the trade ministry in August. The BBC reports that some businesses inspected in areas such as Mbabane were found to be fully compliant. The inspections also raised concerns about working conditions and administrative practices at foreign-owned enterprises, including employee complaints about days off, annual leave and overtime. At least one case remains under investigation.

The BBC places the Eswatini measures in a wider regional context. In South Africa, anti-immigration campaigns have led to raids, arrests, looting and the disruption of foreign-run informal businesses. The BBC reports that the March and March movement intensified its campaign against undocumented immigrants on 30 July 2026, giving them a deadline to leave, and that another protest followed on 30 September. The broadcaster says the campaigns have been accompanied by violence and attacks on foreign-owned businesses, and that two Ghanaian and two Nigerian nationals were reported killed amid the tensions. Kenya has also stepped up enforcement against foreign traders, according to the same report.
