Spain's Interior Ministry reported more than 50 arrests and about 1,000 incidents across the country during the general strike on Wednesday 11 November, according to figures it compiled up to 20.00 CET and that Europa Press published. The strike was called by the main trade unions over access to housing, wages and public services. The BBC reported that it was the first general strike in Spain since 2012.

Europa Press gave a total of 53 arrests and 1,044 incidents. Of the arrests, 39 were made by the National Police and the Guardia Civil, the country's national police force and military-status rural police, with 15 in Madrid and 11 in Andalusia. The Basque regional police, the Ertzaintza, reported 13 arrests, and the Catalan regional police, the Mossos d'Esquadra, reported one. The report says the ministry's count excluded Catalonia and the Basque Country, yet the figures it lists add up to 53 only when those regional arrests are included, so the breakdown does not fully reconcile.

In Barcelona, the BBC said more than 50,000 people joined the march and that footage showed masked men setting fire to rubbish bins and smashing shop windows while police fired tear gas. Europa Press reported that, after the afternoon demonstration ended, groups broke shop windows on the Passeig de Gràcia, burned bins and threw fireworks at officers. The BBC put the crowd in Madrid at about 120,000.

The unions accused police of using excessive force. They denounced the use of pepper spray against pickets at Mercamadrid, the central food market in Madrid, and on the Granada Metro. Frente Amplio and Podemos, two left-wing parties, called for the resignation of Interior Minister Fernando Grande-Marlaska. The United Left (IU) also criticised what it called excessive violence. Before the strike, the ministry had instructed police to allow protests while preventing barricades and pickets that used violence, and to protect critical infrastructure, industrial estates and central markets.

The strike comes weeks before Spain's parliamentary elections on 29 November. Socialist Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez called the early vote after parliament failed to approve his housing reform plan. The conservative People's Party (PP) accused the government of trying to use the strike for electoral gain. Unai Sordo, leader of the union CCOO, described the turnout as "very significant". Miguel Garrido, vice-president of the CEOE, Spain's main employers' organisation, said the strike was political rather than about labour issues.

Red Eléctrica, the grid operator, said electricity demand fell 7.5% in the early hours of Wednesday. During the last general strike, 14 years earlier, the equivalent drop was more than 15%.
