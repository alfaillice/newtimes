The US actor Stacy Keach has died at his home in Magdalenka, Poland, on Tuesday, aged 85, according to The Guardian. The newspaper reports that his wife, Malgosia Tomassi Keach, said in a statement that he had died after a long and difficult fight with a serious heart condition and heart failure. Keach had suffered a heart attack on stage in Chicago in 2017.

Keach was best known on television for playing Mike Hammer, the hard-boiled private detective created by the crime novelist Mickey Spillane. The Guardian describes him as one of America's finest stage actors and notes that he was regarded as a leading interpreter of Shakespeare. He also worked twice with the director John Huston, on Fat City and The Life and Times of Judge Roy Bean.

According to the paper, Keach deliberately avoided repeating himself. His film and television work ranged from a stoner police officer in the Cheech and Chong comedies to a deranged colonel in The Ninth Configuration and a Hemingway role in a six-hour miniseries, which won him a Golden Globe. He also narrated the programme World's Most Amazing Videos and had a part in Alexander Payne's Oscar-nominated drama Nebraska. His final role, The Guardian says, was as George Clooney's bitter father in Jay Kelly.

On stage, Keach earned a Tony Award nomination for playing Buffalo Bill in the Broadway play Indians, and played Edmund opposite Lee J. Cobb in King Lear, also on Broadway. He led the national touring cast of Frost/Nixon. In 2017 he appeared in Pamplona, a one-man show about Hemingway, and collapsed on stage in Chicago, which led to the run being cancelled.

The paper also recalls the low point of his career. In 1984, while he was still playing Hammer in the series later retitled The New Mike Hammer, Keach was arrested at London's Heathrow Airport after trying to smuggle cocaine into Britain, hidden in an aerosol shaving cream can and in other luggage. His secretary was also arrested. He was sentenced to nine months and sent to Reading Prison, a medium-security jail, and was released after six months. In his 2013 memoir, All in All: An Actor's Life On and Off the Stage, he described the sentence as "the lowest moment of my life", according to The Guardian.

After his release, Keach narrated the documentary High on the Job and told a panel of the US House of Representatives how he had become dependent on cocaine in the mid-1970s. He later spoke to students and visited rehabilitation centres to warn about drug use. In the television series Prison Break, which aired from 2005 to 2007, he played a prison warden, modelled partly on a real warden in England whom he admired.

Keach was married four times. He is survived by Tomassi, whom he met when she appeared in the Hammer series, and by his children, Shannon and Karolina, The Guardian reports.
