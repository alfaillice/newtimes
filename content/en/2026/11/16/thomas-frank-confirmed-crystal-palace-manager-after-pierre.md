Thomas Frank has been confirmed as Crystal Palace’s manager after Pierre Sage was sacked last week, according to The Guardian. The Dane, who has been without a job since Tottenham dismissed him in February, has signed a two-and-a-half-year contract at the Premier League club, which is currently bottom of the table.

The Guardian reports that Frank was quickly identified as Palace’s main target. The club first spoke to him in the summer about replacing Oliver Glasner, but Frank decided he was not ready to return to the dugout and wanted a break from football. In recent weeks, Newcastle and Fulham have also reportedly shown interest in him, after difficult starts for Matthias Jaissle and Álvaro Arbeloa. Palace had also considered Sean Dyche and Vítor Pereira.

The club’s chair, Steve Parish, led the negotiations. According to The Guardian, Parish assured Frank that funds would be available for players in January and called him “the perfect fit”. Frank said in a statement: “I will do everything I possibly can to help and support the players and everyone around the place.”

Palace’s results explain the urgency. They have conceded 24 goals in their first 10 league games, the worst defensive record in the division. Their next fixtures are against Coventry and Hull, with a Europa League match against Real Sociedad in between.

The Guardian reports that Palace have not yet confirmed Frank’s full coaching staff. It is understood that Justin Cochrane, an assistant in Thomas Tuchel’s England set-up who worked with Frank at Brentford and Tottenham, is expected to join him. Talks are also under way with Chelsea over Bernardo Cueva, who worked under Frank as Brentford’s set-piece coach before Keith Andrews took over and is tipped for an assistant role at Palace. The club has said that Paddy McCarthy and goalkeeping coach Andy Quy will stay on to work under Frank.

Sage’s departure followed a difficult summer. Palace signed 11 players in the transfer window but did not adequately replace Maxence Lacroix, the France defender. A deal with Club Brugge for Joel Ordóñez collapsed after the Ecuador defender failed a medical because of a fractured foot, and he has yet to return to action.

The Guardian reports that the decision to sack Sage was taken on Sunday evening, although his departure was not confirmed until Thursday. Palace sources told the paper that the compensation bill for Sage is closer to £6m than £10m. They said the upfront fee paid to Lens to release him from his contract in the summer was £2.5m rather than £4m, and that £1.5m in bonuses was never paid. Sage held talks with Lens about a possible return, but the French club appointed Didier Digard on Friday.

Sage wrote on X at the weekend that he wished the club, the team and its supporters well. Palace have not yet announced Frank’s full staff.
