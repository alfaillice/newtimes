The French actor Gérard Depardieu has dropped his appeal against his conviction for sexually assaulting two women and has apologised to them. The move came on Monday, the day the appeal was due to open at the Paris appeal court, according to reports.

Depardieu, 77, is one of France's best-known screen actors. He starred in Cyrano de Bergerac and Green Card and has made more than 200 films and television series. He was tried in Paris last year, the first time he had faced sexual assault charges in court. Several other women have made similar allegations in the media, and the case became a turning point for the #MeToo movement in France. He is the most prominent figure in the French film industry to have been convicted of sexual assault.

The court found that he had assaulted a 55-year-old set dresser and a 35-year-old assistant director during the shooting of the feature film Les Volets Verts (The Green Shutters) in Paris in September 2021. The film is about an ageing actor coming to terms with his declining powers. Depardieu had denied all allegations, and at the trial he said the media had used them to damage his reputation. He admitted using vulgar language but denied being a "metro-train groper", according to reports.

In a written statement, Depardieu said he accepted the verdict. "I address my deep and sincere apologies to those who suffered," he wrote, according to reports. He said that for a long time on film sets he had hidden behind provocation and ribald humour, and that his remarks and some of his gestures had consequences, particularly because of his powerful position in the entertainment industry. He said he had never meant to hurt the women involved, but that intention did not change the harm caused. He also wrote that everything had got out of hand, including his defence, and that he belonged to a world that no longer exists.

The defence had drawn wide criticism during the original trial. The judge ruled that Depardieu's lawyer, Jérémie Assous, had been excessively harsh towards the two women, and ordered Depardieu to pay each of them €1,000 (£835) in compensation for being further victimised during the trial. Outside the Paris appeal court on Monday, the set dresser said she had waited years for the apology, though she would have preferred him to attend court to deliver it in person, reports said. Depardieu's new lawyers, Delphine Meillet and David-Olivier Kaminski, said he wanted to move on from the case, according to reports.

Because he has dropped the appeal, the conviction is final. His suspended sentence of 18 months stands, and his name must be added to France's sex offender register, according to reporting.

Depardieu still faces a separate case. He has appealed against a decision to send him to trial on charges of rape and sexual assault involving an actress at his Paris home in 2018. Reports say appeal judges are expected to rule this month on whether that trial can go ahead. In 2023 he denied all allegations in that case, saying any encounter had been consensual.
