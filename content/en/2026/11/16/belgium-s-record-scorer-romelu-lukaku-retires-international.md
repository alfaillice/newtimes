Romelu Lukaku has retired from international football, ending a 16-year career with Belgium, BBC Sport reported. In a statement posted on Instagram, the 33-year-old Fenerbahce striker said he had "enjoyed every single moment" of playing for his country.

According to BBC Sport, Lukaku made his international debut in 2010 and finishes as Belgium's all-time leading men's goalscorer, with 94 goals in 135 appearances. The broadcaster adds that he is the fifth highest men's international goalscorer of all time, behind Cristiano Ronaldo, who has scored 146 goals, and Lionel Messi, Ali Daei and Sunil Chhetri.

His record at major tournaments also features in the report. Lukaku scored eight goals across four World Cups, including during Belgium's run to the quarter-finals at this summer's tournament. He also scored six goals at three European Championships, BBC Sport said.

In his Instagram message, Lukaku thanked his team-mates and coaches for pushing and trusting him to help the team during his 16 years with the national side. He thanked Belgian supporters for their backing and said he was proud to have represented his country at four World Cups and three European Championships. "I'm at peace with my decision to stop here," he wrote, according to the BBC.

Lukaku missed Belgium's Nations League win over Turkey the previous week because of a hip injury. On Sunday he was an unused substitute when Belgium beat Italy 4-1 to reach the last eight of the competition, BBC Sport reported.

The BBC also recalled his 2023 qualifying campaign for Euro 2024, when he scored 14 times in eight appearances, breaking the record for goals scored by a player in a European Championship qualifying campaign.

His Belgium team-mate Kevin De Bruyne posted a picture of himself with Lukaku on Instagram, saying there were no words to describe what Lukaku had meant to him and to the country.

The BBC describes Lukaku as a former Everton, Chelsea and Manchester United forward. The source does not say whether he will continue at club level beyond his current side, Fenerbahce.
