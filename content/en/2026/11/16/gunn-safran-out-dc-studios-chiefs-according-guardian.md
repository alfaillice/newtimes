James Gunn and Peter Safran have been removed from running DC Studios, the Warner Bros unit that oversees the DC Universe (DCU), according to The Guardian, which reported the dismissals this week. The newspaper said the pair will reportedly remain at the studio as film-makers. Jeremy Latcham, who previously worked at Marvel Studios, becomes the new head, the Guardian said.

The DCU was launched in 2022 to replace the DC Extended Universe (DCEU), a franchise the Guardian describes as increasingly muddled after years of abandoned storylines and shifting tone. Gunn was hired to lead the reboot and, according to the paper, was expected to build a coherent shared universe from DC's characters. Warner Bros Discovery chief executive David Zaslav spoke of a "bold 10-year plan" for the superhero studio after Gunn's *Superman* opened last summer with $618m at the global box office, the Guardian reported.

Results since then have been mixed, the paper said. *Supergirl* underperformed earlier this year. The horror spin-off *Clayface*, released last month, took around $111m worldwide on a reported budget of $40m. On television, the Guardian noted that *Peacemaker* and *Lanterns* linked the studio's films together, though *Lanterns* has not yet been renewed.

The changes come as David Ellison's Skydance has taken control of two major Hollywood studios. According to the Guardian, Skydance acquired Paramount last year and Warner Bros last month. The paper cited Hollywood trade publications as suggesting that Ellison had grown impatient with the DCU's slow pace and wanted to bring forward storylines featuring Superman, Batman and Wonder Woman.

The Guardian also reported that Latcham has spoken about wanting films that feel "redemptive" and "hopeful", and that he has used an informal "grandmother test" at Marvel to judge whether a film is accessible to a broad audience. His credits include *Guardians of the Galaxy* and *Dungeons & Dragons: Honor Among Thieves*. The paper's commentary argues that the change risks losing the distinctive identity Gunn and Safran had given the franchise, but that view is the author's, not a finding.

The sources available for this story do not include a statement from Warner Bros Discovery or Skydance on the dismissals, nor a confirmed account of what the new leadership plans for the DCU. The Guardian is the only outlet on which this report rests, and it is attributed accordingly.
