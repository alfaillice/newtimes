Jensen Huang, who co-founded the US chipmaker Nvidia in 1993 and led it for 33 years, resigned last Thursday after reports that he had at least two extramarital affairs. One was with a woman accused of spying for China and the other with a subordinate. Now a broad group of technology figures wants the board to reverse that decision. According to reporting, an open letter with more than 165,000 signatories asks the board to bring Huang back while an independent investigation establishes the facts. Another report says the letter has more than 172,000 signatures, so the totals differ.

Nvidia makes the advanced chips used to build and run artificial intelligence (AI) systems, and it has become one of the most valuable companies in the world. Among the signatories are Elon Musk, the head of SpaceX; Tim Sweeney of the video game publisher Epic Games; Garry Tan of the start-up incubator Y Combinator; and Travis Kalanick, the co-founder of Uber. Reports also say more than 7,500 signatories identified themselves as Nvidia employees, which is put at 18% of the workforce. Musk wrote on X that "Without Jensen, at least some of those critical deals will be lost", arguing that many supply deals were agreed on a handshake rather than in writing.

One financial daily reports that Huang is discussing a possible return with people close to him. One of his allies told the paper that supporters were building a "war room" of Silicon Valley executives to lobby for his return and to seek backing from the White House. The same report says that the board has already approached Rene Haas, the chief executive of the British chip designer Arm Holdings, as a possible successor. Nvidia's chief financial officer, Colette Kress, is serving as interim chief executive.

Not everyone agrees. The analyst Dan Ives of Yorkville Ives said he saw no chance that Huang would return as chief executive and described the petition as noise and distraction. Anthony Scaramucci, a former White House communications director, argued on X that no director with a fiduciary duty could vote to bring Huang back until the investigations into the alleged spy had closed.

The most serious pressure comes from Washington. Scott Bessent, the US Treasury secretary, said in a television interview on Sunday that Nvidia should look "outside" for its next chief executive. "They need a dramatic shake-up," he said. He also wants the company to explain whether it knowingly allowed its most advanced AI chips to reach China. The US bans sales of the most cutting-edge AI chips to China, although in late 2025 President Donald Trump authorised sales of an earlier model, the H200. Bessent said he wanted answers from Nvidia within 10 days.

US lawmakers have also asked what the Trump administration knew about Huang's relationship with the alleged spy. Reports say Huang has agreed to appear before the House select committee on China, as well as the foreign affairs and intelligence committees. The dates have not been confirmed, and the sessions are likely to be closed to the public. The Singapore-based company Megaspeed, where the woman was an executive until 2025, said on Sunday that it runs a legitimate and lawful business. According to reporting, Megaspeed bought nearly $5bn (£3.8bn) in AI chips and related hardware from Nvidia over the past three years.

Nvidia's shares and those of some partners have fallen sharply since the resignation, according to reporting. The company reports quarterly earnings on Tuesday, which will be the first results since the change of leadership.
