Amazon MGM Studios has confirmed that Robert Pattinson will be the next actor to play James Bond, according to Al Jazeera. The studio released a photograph of Pattinson in the character's signature tuxedo on Monday, 16 November, and posted a message reading "Identity confirmed" on Instagram, the broadcaster reports. Pattinson would be the seventh actor to take on the role of the British secret agent.

He succeeds Daniel Craig, who played 007 in five films released since 2006. Al Jazeera says the casting process was lengthy and that British actors Callum Turner, Josh O'Connor and Jack Lowden were among those considered.

In a statement, Pattinson thanked the studio and described the role as "a dream come true". He also said that being entrusted with the character was an honour of a lifetime, the outlet reports.

Pattinson became internationally known for playing Edward Cullen in the Twilight film series. Since then, Al Jazeera notes, he has taken on more independent and fringe roles. His most recent was in Lance Oppenheim's Primetime, in which he played Chris Hansen, the presenter of the reality television show To Catch a Predator. He has also appeared in Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey, alongside Matt Damon and Anne Hathaway, and in Matt Reeves's The Batman. According to the same report, his film The Drama, with Zendaya, was released earlier this year.

His first outing as Bond is currently untitled but widely referred to as "Bond 26". It will be the first 007 film since the long-serving producers Barbara Broccoli and Michael Wilson stepped back. Amazon took creative control of the franchise after acquiring MGM, the company that owns the series, Al Jazeera explains.

Finally, Al Jazeera reports that reports have suggested Pattinson has committed to making three Bond films. The broadcaster does not say which outlets published those reports, and the figure has not been confirmed by the studio in the material it cites.
