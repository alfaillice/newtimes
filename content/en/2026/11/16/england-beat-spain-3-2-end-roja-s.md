England beat Spain 3-2 in Madrid on Sunday night, ending La Roja's run of 43 matches without defeat and finishing top of Group A3 of the UEFA Nations League, the competition for Europe's national teams. They finished level on 15 points with Spain and went through on goal difference. England had to play the final 25 minutes with ten men after a dramatic finish.

According to Al Jazeera, it was England's tenth successive away win, a record for the team. Spain, the reigning European and world champions, had paraded their World Cup trophy on the pitch before kick-off. Both teams had met at Wembley earlier in the competition, where Spain won 3-2, The Guardian reports.

England pressed high in the first half and Spain did not have a shot on target in the opening 45 minutes, Al Jazeera says. Unai Simón made a reaction save to deny Jude Bellingham early on. England led at the interval after Harry Kane was fouled in the box by Pau Cubarsí. A VAR review of an earlier challenge by Elliot Anderson on Marc Cucurella was completed before Kane scored from the penalty spot, his 93rd goal for England.

England doubled their lead eight minutes after the restart, when Ezri Konsa headed in a Declan Rice corner. Ben White made it 3-0 four minutes later after a counterattack, and England looked to be heading for a comfortable win.

The match changed 25 minutes from time. Jarell Quansah was dispossessed in his own half, and Anderson was shown a red card for a foul on Lamine Yamal as the Spain winger raced into the box. After a VAR check, Mikel Oyarzabal scored the resulting penalty, sending James Trafford the wrong way. Substitute Nico Williams then made it 3-2 around the 75th minute, sweeping in a cross from Yamal that came off the post.

Spain pressed for an equaliser in the closing stages. In stoppage time Trafford, who had been picked ahead of Jordan Pickford, made a double save to deny Fermín López and then Aymeric Laporte. England held on. The Guardian quoted Thomas Tuchel, England's manager, as saying Spain had 'forgotten how to lose' before the match.

The sources differ on how Spain's unbeaten run should be counted. Al Jazeera dates their last defeat to a 1-0 friendly loss to Colombia in London in March 2024. The Guardian says Spain had lost only once in 43 matches, on penalties to Portugal in last year's final of this competition. The draw for the quarter-finals takes place on Friday, and England and Spain will learn their opponents from it, Al Jazeera reports. England will face one of the three other group runners-up, while Spain will face one of the other group winners.
