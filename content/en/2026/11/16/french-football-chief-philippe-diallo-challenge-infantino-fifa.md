According to The Guardian, Philippe Diallo, president of the French Football Federation (FFF), has been confirmed as the candidate that Uefa, the governing body of European football, is backing to stand against Gianni Infantino in the Fifa presidential election in March 2027. The paper reports that the decision came late, with Wednesday set as the deadline for those planning to contest the vote.

The Guardian cites a statement from Uefa issued after a meeting chaired by its president, Aleksander Ceferin. The national associations represented on Uefa's executive committee and the Fifa council invited Diallo to stand and pledged their full support. Uefa said that Diallo, who is 63, had accepted, and that the FFF is due to ratify his candidacy on Tuesday.

Diallo was not the first choice. According to The Guardian, plans to recruit a decorated former player or manager did not materialise. Former players including Mikaël Silvestre and, later, Didier Drogba were reported to have shown interest that came to nothing. The paper also says there were late hopes that Arsène Wenger, Fifa's chief of global development, might stand, but that this would have required a major change of heart on his part.

Diallo has been openly critical of Infantino. The Guardian reports that in September he said Infantino had "seriously damaged the image and unity of football". He went on to argue that the president's initiatives during and after the World Cup had harmed the sport, and that trust had been broken.

The paper describes Diallo as a career administrator who has worked in French football since 1992. He chaired the clubs' lobby group, the Union des Clubs Professionnels de Football (UCPF), for almost 30 years before joining the FFF in 2021. He was elected president two years later, in what The Guardian says was regarded in France as a shock victory, having first taken over on an interim basis. The paper also notes that he sits on Uefa's executive committee.

The Guardian says it is unclear whether Uefa's backing for Diallo will end a separate campaign against Infantino led by Kozo Tashima, honorary president of the Japanese Football Association and a Fifa council member. Tashima has influential supporters within Uefa, but its leadership appears to believe Diallo has a better chance of winning votes in other parts of the world. According to the paper, Diallo is expected to win the backing of most Uefa member associations, and there are hopes he could also attract votes from Francophone members of the Confederation of African Football, which has consistently supported Infantino.

There are concerns, the paper reports, that several challengers could strengthen Infantino, who may present himself as the candidate of unity after months of infighting. Concacaf, the confederation for North and Central America and the Caribbean, has been reluctant to endorse an alternative candidate despite opposing Infantino. Some of its federations argue that a boycott would send the strongest message about the need for governance reform.

For now, the next steps are the ratification of Diallo's candidacy by the FFF on Tuesday and the deadline for candidates on Wednesday. All the details in this story come from The Guardian's reporting, and no other outlet has been used to confirm them.
