The agency also examined progress in 183 countries this year. Nearly nine in 10 now have a national action plan against antimicrobial resistance, compared with about four in 10 in 2017. However, only 42 countries, fewer than one in five, have a plan that is financed, implemented and monitored. Pravasha Prakash, an AMR technical officer at the WHO, said countries are moving forward at every step but that progress must speed up to reach the 2030 targets.

The latest global surveillance data, for 2023, show that one in every six laboratory-confirmed bacterial infections reported to the WHO was resistant to the antibiotics used to treat it. Sylvia Bertagnolo, head of AMR surveillance at the WHO, warned that without testing, clinicians must treat infections without knowing their cause or which antibiotic will work.

To help countries strengthen diagnostic capacity, the WHO released two new tools. The first defines which tests should be available at every level of the health system, from primary care centres to referral hospitals. The second compiles 73 laboratory procedures, from sample collection to identifying pathogens and testing their susceptibility to antibiotics.
