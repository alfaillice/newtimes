Shareholders have sued the US chipmaker Intel, accusing the company of hiding how severely sales of personal computers (PCs) were slowing. According to the BBC, the proposed class action was filed in San Jose, California, and links the lawsuit to a nearly 29% one-day fall in Intel's share price.

A class action is a lawsuit brought on behalf of a large group of people, in this case the company's shareholders. The BBC reports that the complaint was filed by the law firm Robbins Geller Rudman & Dowd for holders of Intel common shares, the ordinary shares that give investors a stake in the company, who held them between 23 July and 29 October this year. Named as defendants are Intel, its chief executive Tan Lip-Bu and other company leaders.

The lawsuit alleges that Intel made "materially false and/or misleading" statements that did not disclose the slowdown in PC sales or the impact of its data centre projects. It argues that the company was too late to warn investors that slowing sales could affect its earnings, and that this artificially inflated the share price. These are allegations made in the filing and have not been tested in court.

The trigger, according to the BBC, came in October, when Intel said its computer hardware sales had dropped sharply in the three months to September. That update undercut earlier signs from the company that demand was improving. Shares plunged on 30 October, the day after Intel forecast disappointing revenue in its coming earnings report, and the fall wiped tens of billions of dollars off its market value.

The lawsuit points to a 9% drop in quarterly earnings, or about $800m, to $8.1bn, which it links to what it calls "depressed demand for PC sales". The BBC also reports that Intel has invested heavily in manufacturing to meet the huge demand for AI computing power. As more semiconductors are channelled towards data centres, chips for PCs and other electronics have become more expensive.

Intel's chief executive had already described the situation to analysts. The BBC quotes Tan saying the industry faced an "unprecedented memory crunch", which has caused a shortage of chip supplies for PCs. "We need to address these challenges with urgency," Tan said in October.

The BBC reports that Intel's shares have fallen 37.5% over the past month, although they are still up 51% for the year as a whole. The article does not include a response from Intel to the complaint.
