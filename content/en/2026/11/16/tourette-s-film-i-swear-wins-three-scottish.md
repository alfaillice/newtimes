I Swear, a film about the Tourette's syndrome campaigner John Davidson, was the biggest winner at the Bafta Scotland awards on Sunday, taking Best Film, Best Director and Best Actor, the BBC reports. The film was nominated in six categories at the ceremony in Glasgow.

According to the BBC, Tourette's causes involuntary vocal and physical tics. In Davidson's case it also involves coprolalia, which makes him shout offensive and inappropriate phrases.

Davidson drew controversy at the Bafta film awards in London earlier this year. His outbursts, which included a racial slur, were heard in the hall and were broadcast in the BBC's coverage of the event. The BBC says Davidson, who is from Galashiels, said the experience had affected him deeply but that he was determined to attend the Glasgow ceremony. "I didn't want to give people the impression I was hiding away," he said.

He told the BBC he had asked Bafta Scotland how the evening could be made easier and less stressful than in London. A Bafta Scotland staff member stayed with him during any contact with members of the public. Davidson, his family and friends watched the awards by video link from a separate room in the Glasgow hotel where the ceremony was held.

Davidson said he hoped the film would raise awareness of the condition, which the BBC says affects about 300,000 people in the UK. Reflecting on the wins, he said: "Tonight's not really about me, although it is my story." He added that he had never watched the film again because it makes him cry.

Accepting the first of the three awards, Kirk Jones, the film's director, thanked the town of Galashiels and Tourette's groups in Scotland for their support. According to the BBC, Jones said he had hoped the film would help people understand Tourette's, but had not realised how many people with neurodiverse conditions would feel represented by it.

The ceremony also honoured Outlander, which ended earlier this year after 12 years and eight seasons. The series received the Outstanding Contribution to Television award, presented by Culture Secretary Mairi McAllan, who said the show had "changed Scotland forever". Bafta Scotland said the series had an "undeniable" impact on Scotland's screen industry, providing opportunities for local talent. The Traitors star Stephen Libby, from the Isle of Lewis, won the Audience Award for Favourite On Screen Scot, the BBC added.
