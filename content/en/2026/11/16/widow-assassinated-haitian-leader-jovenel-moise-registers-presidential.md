Martine Moïse, the widow of Haiti's former president Jovenel Moïse, has registered as a candidate in the country's presidential election, according to the Provisional Electoral Council (CEP), the body that organises the vote. Her name appeared on the council's website on Sunday. She is one of 33 registered candidates.

Jovenel Moïse led Haiti from 2017 until he was shot dead at the couple's home in Port-au-Prince in July 2021. Reports describe the attackers as a group of mainly Colombian mercenaries; others add that they posed as agents of the United States Drug Enforcement Administration. Martine Moïse was seriously wounded in the attack and was flown to a hospital in Florida.

Her legal position has been described differently. One account says she was charged in connection with the assassination in 2024, but that the indictment was overturned last year. Another describes her as a key witness in the Miami court case against dozens of suspects. According to reporting, 30 suspects have been indicted so far, and in September 18 people were flown from Haiti to Florida to stand trial.

Haiti last held a presidential election in 2016. Successive governments have repeatedly postponed the vote, citing insecurity. According to reporting, the election scheduled for December was pushed back last month to July. Reports say powerful, heavily armed gangs have expanded their influence over much of the country.

Among the other registered candidates are two former presidents, Michel Martelly and Jocelerme Privert, as well as former prime minister Claude Joseph and former foreign minister Dominique Dupuy. Reports say Martelly and Privert have been sanctioned internationally over accusations that they supported armed gangs, which both deny.

Martine Moïse has not publicly commented on her registration, according to reporting. Last week she wrote on X: "Haiti will rise again on its own two feet, because politics needs to be done differently." In the same post, she blamed the opposition for bringing the country to its knees, according to reporting.

The security situation remains severe. Reports say about 1.5 million people have been displaced and more than 22,000 killed since the assassination, many by security personnel. The UN Integrated Office in Haiti is cited as saying that 1,408 people were killed and 656 injured between April and June this year. Last week, Haitian security forces regained control of the national stadium, which gangs had held for two years, forcing the national football team to play elsewhere. The operation is part of a deployment of about 2,000 soldiers from El Salvador, ordered by President Nayib Bukele, according to reporting.
