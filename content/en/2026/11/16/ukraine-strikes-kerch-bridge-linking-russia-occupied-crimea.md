The Kremlin spokesperson, Dmitry Peskov, downplayed the incident in a call with reporters. "Minor damage is being assessed. Traffic across the bridge continues," he said.

The strike came during an overnight barrage of 1,336 drones, which the Kremlin says was one of the biggest attacks so far this year, the newspaper reports. About 600 were intercepted on their approach to Moscow, according to the capital's mayor, Sergei Sobyanin.

Ukraine describes its retaliatory strikes as justified retribution for Russian missile and drone attacks on its cities and energy grid. Those attacks have killed hundreds of civilians and regularly cut power and heating for millions of people, the newspaper says. In recent weeks Russia has repeatedly hit two critical bridges in Kyiv over the Dnipro river.

Diplomatic efforts to end the war, now almost five years old, have so far gone nowhere. After a fresh round of talks in Abu Dhabi last week, Putin reiterated his hardline territorial demands and said that any deployment of foreign troops would be seen in Moscow as a continuation of the war.

Ukraine's president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, said on Friday that the Trump administration had proposed that Kyiv give Russia the eastern Donbas region, including places Russia does not control, in exchange for Nato membership or Nato-like guarantees. Zelenskyy ruled out handing over territory. He said the details of possible US security guarantees were vague and did not include American troops on the ground, citing conversations with Trump's son-in-law, Jared Kushner, and the real-estate developer Steve Witkoff. The two envoys saw Putin in Moscow last week.
